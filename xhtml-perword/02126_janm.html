जन्म (janm)

noun A single core sense meaning 'birth', used both literally and in a cluster of common fixed expressions about life, rebirth, and lifelong bonds.
Sense 1 : birth
noun, masculine Common in the pattern X का जन्म होना (kaa janm honaa, 'X's birth to happen'), used to state that someone was born, and in जन्म लेना (janm lenaa, 'to take birth') as the everyday verb phrase for 'to be born.'
Synonyms: जन्मदिन (janmdin, birthday, narrower), पैदाइश (paidaaish)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Hindu thought जन्म (janm) is never a one-time event -- it's tied to the cycle of जन्म-मरण (janm-maran, 'birth and death') and पुनर्जन्म (punarjanm, 'rebirth'), so the word carries a philosophical weight beyond the biological fact of being born. You'll hear this in everyday blessings too, like जन्मों-जन्मों तक साथ रहना (janmon-janmon tak saath rahnaa, 'to stay together across lifetimes'), often said to newlyweds.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'to be born' with जन्मना (janmnaa) alone, but everyday speech overwhelmingly prefers the phrase जन्म लेना (janm lenaa) or जन्म होना (janm honaa). 2) The person born takes का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) before जन्म (janm), not ने (ne) -- e.g. मेरा जन्म (meraa janm, 'my birth'), not मैंने जन्म (mainne janm).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : a lifetime, an existence (as in a cycle of lives)
noun, masculine Used countably, as in इस जन्म में (is janm men, 'in this lifetime') or अगले जन्म में (agle janm men, 'in the next lifetime'), framing life as one instance in a series rather than a single continuous existence.
Synonyms: जीवन (jiivan, broader, means 'life' generally), ज़िंदगी (zindgii, broader)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is what makes lines like इस जन्म में नहीं, अगले जन्म में (is janm men nahiin, agle janm men, 'not in this life, in the next') so natural in Hindi -- the idea that a soul gets multiple जन्म (janm) is baked into everyday speech, not just religious texts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for ज़िंदगी (zindgii) or जीवन (jiivan) when talking about reincarnation, but जन्म (janm) is the natural word for 'a life' in that specific cyclical sense -- as in हर जन्म (har janm, 'every lifetime').
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : origin, birthplace/native (in compounds)
noun, masculine Appears as the first element of compounds like जन्मस्थान (janmasthaan, 'birthplace') and जन्मभूमि (janmbhoomi, 'homeland/native land'), and adjectivally in phrases like जन्म से (janm se, 'by birth', e.g. जन्म से भारतीय (janm se bhaartiiy, 'Indian by birth')).
Synonyms: मूल (mool, origin, broader)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जन्मभूमि (janmbhoomi) carries strong emotional and nationalistic overtones in Hindi -- it's the word used for one's sacred 'motherland,' distinct from the more neutral देश (desh, 'country').
