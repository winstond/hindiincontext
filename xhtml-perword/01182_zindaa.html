ज़िंदा (zindaa)

adjective A borrowed adjective meaning 'alive, living' -- used both literally (of people, animals) and figuratively (of feelings, memories, movements staying 'alive').
Sense 1 : alive, living (literal, physical)
adjective
Synonyms: जीवित (jiivit, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news headlines and everyday relief: ज़िंदा है (zindaa hai, 'he/she is alive') after an accident or rescue. It is a Persian loanword, and its warmth comes from how directly and simply it states the fact of survival, without any formal distance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like a native -आ adjective (ज़िंदी, ज़िंदे) -- it is invariant, so ज़िंदा (zindaa) stays the same for all genders and numbers. 2) Do not confuse with ज़िंदगी (zindgii, 'life'), a related but separate noun.

Sense 2 : alive, active, still vivid (figurative -- of feelings, memories, traditions, spirit)
adjective
Synonyms: जीवित (jiivit, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in phrases like यादें ज़िंदा हैं (yaaden zindaa hain, 'the memories are alive') or उम्मीद ज़िंदा है (ummiid zindaa hai, 'hope is alive') -- Hindi treats abstract things like hope and memory as things that can 'live' or 'die,' much as English does, which makes this figurative sense feel natural to English speakers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जीवित (jiivit) in figurative contexts, but native speakers overwhelmingly prefer ज़िंदा (zindaa) for feelings, memories, and spirit -- जीवित (jiivit) sounds stiff here.
