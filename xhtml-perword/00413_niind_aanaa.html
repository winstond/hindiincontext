नींद आना (niind aanaa)

verb A fixed noun-plus-verb pairing meaning 'to feel/fall sleepy' -- sleep is treated as something that arrives to you, not something you actively do. Closely related to नींद (niind, sleep) and आना (aanaa, to come).
Sense 1 : to feel sleepy, to fall asleep (sleep comes to someone)
verb, intransitive The experiencer is marked with को (ko): मुझे नींद आ रही है (mujhe niind aa rahii hai, 'I am feeling sleepy'). नींद (niind) is the grammatical subject and agrees with the verb आना (aanaa), not the person feeling sleepy.
Synonyms: सोने की इच्छा होना (sone kii ichchhaa honaa, longer descriptive phrase), झपकी आना (jhapkii aanaa, to get drowsy/doze off)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic example of Hindi's 'body/sensation-as-agent' pattern, in the same family as भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa, to feel hungry) and गुस्सा आना (gussaa aanaa, to get angry) -- the feeling arrives and you are simply its recipient, marked with को (ko) rather than being the active doer. English speakers have to unlearn the instinct to make 'I' the subject here.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try मैं सोना चाहता हूँ (main sonaa chaahtaa hoon) when they mean simply 'I'm sleepy' -- that phrase means 'I want to sleep' (a choice), while नींद आना (niind aanaa) describes the involuntary sensation itself. 2) The person who feels sleepy takes को (ko), not ने (ne) -- since नींद (niind) is the one doing the 'coming', not the person: मुझे नींद आई (mujhe niind aaii), never मैंने नींद आई.
