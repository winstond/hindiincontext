बरसाना (barsaanaa)

verb/noun The causative of बरसना (barasnaa, 'to rain'), meaning 'to cause to rain' or, figuratively, to shower something (blessings, love, blows) down on someone; also the name of a famous town near Mathura linked to Radha.
Sense 1 : to shower down (figurative: love, blessings, blows, etc.)
verb, transitive Takes the thing showered as direct object and पर (par) marking the recipient: प्यार बरसाना (pyaar barsaanaa, 'to shower love'), किसी पर गोलियाँ बरसाना (kisii par goliyaan barsaanaa, 'to rain bullets on someone').
Synonyms: बरसना (barasnaa, intransitive base verb, not a true synonym), लुटाना (lutaanaa, to lavish/shower generously)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the verb of choice in devotional and filmi language for describing blessings, love, or grace pouring down from above -- ईश्वर अपनी कृपा बरसाता है (iishvar apnii kripaa barsaataa hai, 'God showers his grace') -- carrying the same top-down, gift-like imagery as actual rain. It pairs naturally with its base verb बरसना (barasnaa), so learning them together helps fix the causative relationship in your mind.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use बरसना (barasnaa) where they mean the causative सक्रिय (barsaanaa); remember बरसना (barasnaa) is what the sky does on its own, while बरसाना (barsaanaa) requires an agent actively showering something down. 2) The recipient of the showering is marked with पर (par), not को (ko) -- दुश्मन पर गोलियाँ बरसाना (dushman par goliyaan barsaanaa, 'to rain bullets on the enemy'), not दुश्मन को (dushman ko).

Sense 2 : to cause (rain) to fall; to make it rain
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बरसना (barasnaa, intransitive base verb, not a true synonym)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Used literally less often than its figurative sense, since Hindi speakers don't usually talk about 'making it rain' in a literal weather-control sense outside of poetry or mythology, where clouds or gods are described as बरसाना (barsaanaa) rain upon the earth.

Sense 3 : Barsana, a town near Mathura associated with Radha
noun, masculine
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: बरसाना (barsaanaa) is Radha's legendary hometown in the Braj region near Mathura, famous for the boisterous Holi tradition called लठमार होली (lathmaar holii, 'stick-beating Holi'), where women playfully beat men with sticks in a re-enactment of local legend. This sense is unrelated in meaning to the verb, though a folk-etymological pun between 'showering' and this rain-soaked festival is often enjoyed rather than taken seriously.
