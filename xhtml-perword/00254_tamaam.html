तमाम (tamaam)

adjective/adverb तमाम (tamaam) works as an adjective meaning 'all/entire' and also as an adverb-like intensifier meaning 'completely, finished'; both senses come from the same Persian-Arabic root meaning 'complete'.
Sense 1 : all, entire, the whole lot of
adjective Placed before a plural or mass noun like सारा (saaraa) or पूरा (pooraa): तमाम लोग (tamaam log, 'all the people').
Synonyms: सारा (saaraa), पूरा (pooraa), सब (sab)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear तमाम (tamaam) a lot in news broadcasts and political speeches -- 'तमाम देशवासियों (tamaam deshvaasiyon, 'all countrymen')' has that slightly formal, rhetorical ring, whereas सारे लोग (saare log) is what you'd say at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सारा (saaraa) or पूरा (pooraa) by default and forget तमाम (tamaam) is a perfectly natural, slightly more formal-sounding alternative in the same slot, common in news and speeches. 2) तमाम (tamaam) tends to pair with plural/collective nouns (तमाम दुनिया (tamaam duniyaa, 'the whole world'), तमाम लोग (tamaam log, 'all the people')) more than with a single countable object, where पूरा (pooraa) sounds more natural.

Sense 2 : completely, entirely (finished/done)
adverb Common in the fixed phrase तमाम हो जाना (tamaam ho jaanaa, 'to be finished/come to an end'), where तमाम (tamaam) functions predicatively rather than as a pure adverb.
Synonyms: पूरी तरह (poorii tarah), बिल्कुल (bilkul)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in older Hindi cinema dialogue and dramatic narration -- 'कहानी तमाम हुई (kahaanii tamaam huii, 'the story is over')' has an almost storyteller's cadence to it, quite different from the flat, everyday ख़त्म (khatm).
Common Mistakes: 1) तमाम हो जाना (tamaam ho jaanaa) is not the everyday way to say 'finished' -- ख़त्म हो जाना (khatm ho jaanaa) is far more common in casual speech; this phrase has a slightly literary or dramatic flavor, often heard in older films or formal narration.
