सच्चा दोस्त (sachchaa dost)

noun A fixed noun phrase meaning 'true friend' -- सच्चा (sachchaa, 'true, genuine') modifying दोस्त (dost, 'friend'); it names a person, so it functions as a noun phrase rather than a free combination the learner needs to build from scratch.
Sense 1 : a loyal, genuine friend
noun, masculine सच्चा (sachchaa) agrees in gender/number with दोस्त (dost) only when दोस्त refers to a female friend and the speaker chooses to mark it -- सच्ची दोस्त (sachchii dost) -- since दोस्त itself does not change form for gender.
Synonyms: पक्का दोस्त (pakkaa dost, 'firm/reliable friend'), सच्चा यार (sachchaa yaar, colloquial), खरा दोस्त (kharaa dost, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase carries real emotional weight in Hindi -- it is what people say at weddings, in filmi dialogues, and in heartfelt toasts to praise a friend who stood by them in hard times ('mushkil waqt mein saath dena'). It pairs naturally with sayings about friendship, like the idea that a सच्चा दोस्त (sachchaa dost) is rarer than gold.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सच (sach, 'truth,' a noun) instead of सच्चा (sachchaa, the adjective 'true'), producing an ungrammatical phrase -- सच्चा (sachchaa) is the form needed before a noun. 2) सच्चा दोस्त (sachchaa dost) is about loyalty and sincerity, not about a friend who tells the truth literally -- confusing it with 'honest friend' in the literal sense can miss the emotional weight the phrase carries.
