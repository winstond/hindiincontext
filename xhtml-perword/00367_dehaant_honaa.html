देहांत होना (dehaant honaa)

verb A polite, respectful way to say 'to pass away, to die'; देहांत (dehaant) literally means 'end of the body' and combines with होना (honaa, 'to happen') to form this euphemistic expression used for humans.
Sense 1 : to pass away, to die (formal/respectful)
verb, intransitive The person who dies is marked with का/की/के (possessive), not से or को: उनका देहांत हो गया (unkaa dehaant ho gayaa, 'he/she passed away'), treating the death as an event belonging to the person rather than something done to them.
Synonyms: मरना (marnaa, plain, blunt), गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa), चल बसना (chal basnaa, literary), स्वर्गवास होना (svargvaas honaa, formal/devotional)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you reach for at a condolence visit or in a formal announcement -- it softens death the way English 'passed away' does compared to 'died.' You'll often hear it as उनका देहांत हो गया (unkaa dehaant ho gayaa, 'he/she has passed away'), with हो गया (ho gayaa) marking the completed, almost sudden nature of the event. In very devotional or old-fashioned contexts, स्वर्गवास होना (svargvaas honaa, 'to attain heaven') carries an even more elevated tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use this with को like other involuntary-event verbs (e.g. लगना lagnaa) -- but देहांत होना (dehaant honaa) takes का/की/के, not को: say उनका देहांत हुआ (unkaa dehaant huaa), not उनको देहांत हुआ. 2) Reserved for humans -- using it for an animal or object sounds odd or unintentionally comic; मरना (marnaa) or मर जाना (mar jaanaa) is used instead. 3) This phrase is inherently respectful, so pairing it with a casual or joking tone feels jarring to native speakers.
Sample Sentences:
कल रात उनका देहांत हो गया। He passed away last night.
(kal raat unkaa dehaant ho gayaa।)

(mujhe yah bataate hue bahut dukh ho rahaa hai ki shriimaan sharmaa jii kaa dehaant ho gayaa hai।)

(yah soochit karte hue khed hai ki aaj subah shriimtii varmaa kaa dehaant ho gayaa।)
