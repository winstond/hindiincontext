थोड़ा-थोड़ा करके (thodaa-thodaa karke)

adverb A reduplicated adverbial phrase meaning 'little by little, gradually, bit by bit' -- built from थोड़ा (thodaa, 'a little') doubled for emphasis and followed by करके (karke, the conjunctive-participle form of करना, 'having done'). Single sense.
Sense 1 : gradually, little by little, in small increments
adverb Functions as an adverbial phrase modifying how an action is carried out, usually with verbs of accumulation, consumption, saving, or learning -- e.g. पैसे जोड़ना (paise jodnaa, 'to save money'), सीखना (siikhnaa, 'to learn'), खाना (khaanaa, 'to eat').
Synonyms: धीरे-धीरे (dhiire-dhiire, focuses on slowness of pace rather than small quantity), क्रमशः (kramshah, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase captures a very Indian household ethic -- think of a mother telling a child to eat थोड़ा-थोड़ा करके (thodaa-thodaa karke) rather than gulping down food, or a family saving money थोड़ा-थोड़ा करके (thodaa-thodaa karke) for a wedding or a house. It reflects patience and incremental effort as a valued way of getting things done, rather than rushing or doing everything at once.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for धीरे-धीरे (dhiire-dhiire) instead, but that phrase emphasizes slow speed (e.g. walking slowly), while थोड़ा-थोड़ा करके (thodaa-thodaa karke) emphasizes doing something in small portions or installments over time -- the two overlap but are not interchangeable in every context. 2) The करके (karke) is not optional filler; dropping it to just थोड़ा-थोड़ा (thodaa-thodaa) changes it from an adverbial phrase of manner into a plain repeated quantifier ('a little bit of each'), which reads differently in a sentence.
