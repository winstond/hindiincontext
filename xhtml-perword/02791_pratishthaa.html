प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa)

noun A formal, weighty noun centered on 'prestige/reputation/standing'; also carries a religious-ritual sense of 'installation/consecration' (of a deity's image). Both senses share the idea of something being 'firmly established' -- socially or ritually.
Sense 1 : prestige, reputation, social standing
noun, feminine
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त (izzat), मान (maan), साख (saakh, especially credibility/standing in dealings), गरिमा (garimaa, dignity, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in news and formal speech -- a company's प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa) is at stake in a scandal, a family's प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa) is protected through a marriage, a country's प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa) is enhanced by a sporting win. It sits a notch above everyday इज़्ज़त (izzat) in formality, closer to 'prestige' or 'standing' than to 'honor' in the personal-insult sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for इज़्ज़त (izzat) in every context, but प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa) is more formal and institutional -- used for the standing of a person, family, company, or country, not for everyday 'honor' in a personal insult sense. 2) It commonly pairs with the verb बढ़ना (badhnaa, to increase) or verbs like बनाना (banaanaa) / बचाना (bachaanaa) -- e.g. प्रतिष्ठा बढ़ना (pratishthaa badhnaa, 'reputation to grow'), rather than होना (honaa) alone.

Sense 2 : consecration, ritual installation (of a deity's idol)
noun, feminine Used almost exclusively in the fixed phrase प्राण-प्रतिष्ठा (praan-pratishthaa, 'infusing life/breath -- the consecration ceremony of an idol') or standalone मूर्ति की प्रतिष्ठा (moorti kii pratishthaa, 'installation of the idol').
Synonyms: स्थापना (sthaapnaa, installation, broader/secular use possible too)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up heavily in news coverage of temple ceremonies -- the word प्राण-प्रतिष्ठा (praan-pratishthaa) became especially prominent nationwide around the Ram Mandir consecration in Ayodhya. The underlying idea links back to the first sense: an idol, once 'installed,' gains a fixed, honored standing, just as a person's reputation is something 'established.'
