सुर (sur)

noun Primarily a musical term meaning 'note/pitch/tune', with an extended figurative sense about being 'in tune' with someone or something (mood, agreement, alignment).
Sense 1 : figurative: tune, mood, tone, alignment (being in sync)
noun, masculine Very commonly used in the fixed idiom सुर मिलाना (sur milaanaa, 'to fall in line/agree with') and सुर बदलना (sur badalnaa, 'to change one's tone/attitude').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is where सुर (sur) leaves the music room and enters everyday Delhi conversation and political commentary: 'नेता ने सुर बदल लिया' (netaa ne sur badal liyaa, 'the leader changed his tune') is a staple of news anchors and dinner-table political chat. It reflects how Hindi often borrows musical metaphors to talk about attitude and consensus, much like English 'change one's tune' or 'sing the same song'.
Common Mistakes: 1) सुर मिलाना (sur milaanaa) does not literally mean 'to match musical notes' in most everyday contexts -- it means to agree with or go along with someone, often with a slightly critical edge implying flattery or opportunism. 2) सुर बदलना (sur badalnaa) means someone has changed their attitude or tone (often after a shift in circumstances), not literally changed a song.

Sense 2 : musical note, pitch, tone
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वर (svar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask any harmonium or tabla player in Delhi and they will tell you 'सुर में गाओ' (sur men gaao, 'sing in tune') is the first correction every music teacher gives. सुर (sur) is at the heart of Hindustani classical music vocabulary, sitting alongside ताल (taal, rhythm) and लय (lay, tempo/flow) as one of the three pillars students learn to name early on.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse सुर (sur) with सुर के पीछे ताल (taal, 'rhythm/beat') -- सुर (sur) is about pitch/melody, ताल (taal) is about the rhythmic cycle; they are not interchangeable even though both come up constantly in discussions of Indian classical music.
