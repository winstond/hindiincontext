जल्दबाज़ी (jaldbaazii)

noun A noun meaning haste or hurry, often carrying a mildly negative sense of rashness. Built from जल्दी (jaldii, quickly/soon) plus the Persian agentive suffix -बाज़ (baaz), so it literally frames someone as 'one who plays at speed.'
Sense 1 : haste, hurry (often implying carelessness)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: तेज़ी (tezii), हड़बड़ी (hadabdii, suggests flustered rushing), आतुरता (aaturtaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian elders love warning the young with जल्दबाज़ी अच्छी नहीं होती (jaldbaazii achchhii nahiin hotii, 'haste is not a good thing') -- it's practically a cultural reflex, paired with the popular saying जल्दी का काम शैतान का (jaldii kaa kaam shaitaan kaa, 'hasty work is the devil's work'). The word carries a built-in judgment: it's not neutral speed, but speed you'll likely regret.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use जल्दी (jaldii) where जल्दबाज़ी (jaldbaazii) is meant, or vice versa: जल्दी (jaldii) just means 'quickly/soon' and is neutral, while जल्दबाज़ी (jaldbaazii) is a noun implying rash or careless speed, as in जल्दबाज़ी मत करो (jaldbaazii mat karo, 'don't be hasty'). 2) It is common in the phrase जल्दबाज़ी में (jaldbaazii men, 'in one's haste'), marking the circumstance under which something (often a mistake) happened.

जल्दबाज़ी में मत करो, ठीक से सोचो। Don't do it in haste, think it through properly.
(jaldbaazii men mat karo, thiik se socho।)
