चौकीदार (chaukiidaar)

noun A watchman or guard, whether posted at a residential building, market, or (colloquially and politically) the country itself. One straightforward sense with an extended, well-known metaphorical use.
Sense 1 : watchman, guard
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गार्ड (gaard), पहरेदार (pahredaar), दरबान (darbaan, usually at a gate or door)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Almost every residential colony (mahalla) or apartment block in North India has a चौकीदार (chaukiidaar) who does rounds at night, sometimes blowing a whistle or banging a stick on the ground so residents know he's awake and alert. The word comes from चौकी (chaukii, a guard post or checkpoint) plus the Persian agentive suffix -दार (daar, 'holder of'), the same pattern seen in जमींदार (jamiindaar, landholder) and दुकानदार (dukaandaar, shopkeeper).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume चौकीदार (chaukiidaar) is only a formal or official title; in everyday Delhi-NCR usage it is the plain, common word people use for the neighborhood or building night guard, not a fancy term. 2) दरबान (darbaan) is closer to a doorman at an entrance (hotel, office lobby), while चौकीदार (chaukiidaar) suggests patrolling and watching over a wider area, including at night -- the two are not perfectly interchangeable.

Sense 2 : guardian/protector (figurative, especially political)
noun, masculine Used in a broader, non-literal sense to mean someone who claims to protect or safeguard something larger and more abstract, such as a nation, its wealth, or its interests.
Synonyms: रक्षक (rakshak, more formal/literary), संरक्षक (sanrakshak, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This extended sense took off in Indian public life when politicians began calling themselves चौकीदार (chaukiidaar) to project themselves as personally standing guard over the nation's money and interests -- it's a good example of how a humble, everyday job title can be borrowed for a much bigger, symbolic claim.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense became widely known in political slogans and campaign rhetoric (e.g. 'मैं भी चौकीदार' (main bhii chaukiidaar, 'I too am a watchman')); learners should recognize it as a rhetorical, self-appointed 'guardian of the nation' framing rather than a literal job title.
