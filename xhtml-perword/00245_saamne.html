सामने (saamne)

postposition/adverb Mainly a postposition/adverb meaning 'in front of, opposite, facing' -- covers both physical position and the extended sense of being 'faced with' a situation or person. Inline Hindi as Devanagari (placeholder).
Sense 1 : in front of / opposite (physical location)
postposition Takes a genitive-marked noun before it, e.g. घर के सामने (ghar ke saamne, 'in front of the house') -- the के (ke) link is required, just like with other compound postpositions.
Synonyms: आगे (aage, overlaps for 'ahead' but not always 'facing'), समक्ष (samaksh, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture standing in a Delhi market and someone says मेरे सामने वाली दुकान (mere saamne vaalii dukaan, 'the shop facing me') -- सामने वाला (saamne vaalaa) is a very common construction for 'the one across/opposite', used constantly for shops, houses, and people across a street or table.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop the के (ke) and just say घर सामने (ghar saamne) -- the linking के (ke) is needed before सामने (saamne) just as with के पास (ke paas) or के अंदर (ke andar). 2) Confusing सामने (saamne) with आगे (aage): आगे (aage) means 'ahead/forward' along a path, while सामने (saamne) means 'facing, opposite' -- a shop can be आगे (aage) on the road but not necessarily सामने (saamne) unless it directly faces you.

Sense 2 : in the presence of / faced with (figurative, before a person or situation)
postposition Common in expressions like मेरे सामने यह मत कहो (mere saamne yah mat kaho, 'do not say this in front of me') and समस्या के सामने (samasyaa ke saamne, 'faced with the problem') -- extends the spatial sense to social presence or confrontation with a difficulty.
Synonyms: समक्ष (samaksh, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A parent scolding a child might say मेरे सामने ऐसी बात मत करो (mere saamne aisii baat mat karo, 'don't talk like that in front of me') -- सामने (saamne) here carries a sense of being watched or answerable, which is why it shows up so often in family and authority contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for सामने (saamne) when they mean 'in my opinion' or 'to me' -- but सामने (saamne) means literal or social presence, not perspective; for opinion, मेरे हिसाब से (mere hisaab se) or मेरे ख़याल से (mere khayaal se) is correct instead.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : ahead, up front, in view (without a following noun)
adverb Used alone, without a genitive-marked object, as in सामने देखो (saamne dekho, 'look ahead/in front') -- functions adverbially rather than governing a noun.
Synonyms: आगे (aage)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly from auto and cab drivers -- सामने मोड़ लेना (saamne mod lenaa, 'take the turn up ahead') is a standard way to give directions in Delhi traffic.
