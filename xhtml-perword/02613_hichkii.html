हिचकी (hichkii)

noun A single everyday sense: 'hiccup.' Almost always paired with the verb आना (aanaa) in the frame X को हिचकी आना (X ko hichkii aanaa).
Sense 1 : hiccup
noun, feminine Used with a dative-subject frame: मुझे हिचकी आ रही है (mujhe hichkii aa rahii hai, 'I have hiccups') -- the hiccup 'comes to' the person, it is not something the person actively does.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: There's a common folk belief across North India that getting hiccups means someone is thinking or talking about you -- you'll often hear someone joke, 'लगता है कोई याद कर रहा है' (lagtaa hai koii yaad kar rahaa hai, 'seems like someone's remembering me') right after a bout of हिचकी (hichkii). This fits the broader Hindi pattern where bodily sensations (नींद (niind, sleep), भूख (bhookh, hunger), प्यास (pyaas, thirst)) are things that 'come to' you rather than things you actively do.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I am hiccupping' with the person as a grammatical subject doing an action, e.g. wrongly building it like an intransitive verb of the person. Hindi instead treats हिचकी (hichkii) as a noun that 'arrives,' so the person is marked with को (ko): उसे हिचकी आ रही है (use hichkii aa rahii hai, 'he/she has hiccups'), not a person-as-subject construction. 2) The count/plural form हिचकियाँ (hichkiyaan) is used when referring to a series of hiccups happening repeatedly, which learners sometimes forget to pluralize in natural speech.
