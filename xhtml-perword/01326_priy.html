प्रिय (priy)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning 'dear/beloved', with a related noun sense referring to a loved person; also survives in compounds and forms of address.
Sense 1 : dear, beloved, favorite
adjective
Synonyms: प्यारा (pyaaraa), चाहता (chaahtaa, less common as adjective), प्रियतम (priytam, literary, more intense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word right at the top of a formal letter or speech -- प्रिय महोदय (priy mahoday, 'dear sir') or प्रिय साथियों (priy saathiyon, 'dear colleagues') -- much like 'dear' opens an English letter. In everyday chat among friends and family, प्यारा (pyaaraa) is what people actually reach for; प्रिय (priy) has a slightly more formal, written-page feel.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this in casual speech where प्यारा (pyaaraa) would sound more natural; प्रिय (priy) leans slightly formal/written, common in letters, speeches, and set phrases like प्रिय मित्र (priy mitr, 'dear friend'). 2) As an adjective it is often invariant in fixed address phrases (प्रिय मित्रों priy mitron, 'dear friends') even though it can inflect elsewhere -- learners sometimes over-inflect or under-inflect it inconsistently.

Sense 2 : beloved one, dear one (person)
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun referring to a loved person, especially in poetry, songs, and romantic or devotional contexts.
Synonyms: प्रियतम (priytam, literary, intensified), महबूब (mahboob, more common in speech/song)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear in old film songs and shayari addressing a beloved -- ऐ मेरे प्रिय (ai mere priy, 'oh my beloved') -- carrying a soft, romantic, slightly old-fashioned charm rather than something you'd say to your partner over chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) This noun use is mostly literary/poetic -- in ordinary conversation about a boyfriend/girlfriend or spouse, learners should reach for महबूब (mahboob), प्यार (pyaar), or just a name/relation term rather than प्रिय (priy).
