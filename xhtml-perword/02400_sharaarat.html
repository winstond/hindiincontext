शरारत (sharaarat)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'mischief' or 'prank' -- almost always used for the harmless naughtiness of children, and by extension the playful teasing of adults.
Sense 1 : mischief, naughtiness (a child's playful misbehavior)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: नटखटपन (natkhatpan), उद्दंडता (uddandtaa, formal, closer to 'unruliness')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Any Delhi aunty watching a kid pull the cat's tail will sigh and say बहुत शरारती है (bahut sharaartii hai, 'he/she is very mischievous') -- the adjective शरारती (sharaartii) is the word's constant companion and worth learning alongside it. The tone is almost always affectionate, never harshly critical.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बदमाशी (badmaashii) as a direct synonym, but बदमाशी (badmaashii) leans toward willful bad behavior or even thuggishness, while शरारत (sharaarat) keeps a fond, indulgent tone -- it is what you say about a toddler who has scribbled on the wall, not a criminal. 2) The common verb pairing is शरारत करना (sharaarat karnaa, 'to do mischief'), not बनाना (banaanaa) or खेलना (khelnaa) -- learners sometimes reach for the wrong light verb.

Sense 2 : a prank, a mischievous act (countable instance)
noun, feminine Used in the plural, शरारतें (sharaarten), to refer to a pattern of pranks or a child's antics collectively.
Synonyms: मस्ती (mastii, broader, more like 'fun/high spirits')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will often hear grandparents recount बचपन की शरारतें (bachpan kii sharaarten, 'childhood pranks') with real nostalgia -- in Hindi-speaking households, remembering one's own शरारतें (sharaarten) is a beloved genre of family storytelling.
