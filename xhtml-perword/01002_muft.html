मुफ़्त (muft)

adjective/adverb A common loanword from Persian meaning 'free of cost'; it also extends into an adverbial sense of 'for nothing, in vain, without purpose.'
Sense 1 : free of charge, costing nothing
adjective
Synonyms: बिना पैसे का (binaa paise kaa), फ्री (phrii, colloquial, English loan)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear मुफ़्त की रेवड़ियाँ (muft kii revdiyaan, 'free treats/handouts') used in political commentary about freebies handed out before elections -- a very Delhi-NCR chai-shop conversation topic. The phrase मुफ़्त का माल (muft kaa maal, 'free stuff/goods') carries a slightly mocking tone, implying something got for free is treated carelessly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add का (kaa) or की (kii) after मुफ़्त (muft) as if it were a noun-derived adjective needing agreement, e.g. saying मुफ़्त की चीज़ (muft kii chiiz, 'a free thing') -- this is actually correct and common, but मुफ़्त (muft) itself never inflects, so don't add endings directly onto the word like मुफ़्ता (muftaa) or मुफ़्ती (muftii) to try to match gender/number.

Sense 2 : for nothing, without payment; also: in vain, pointlessly
adverb In the 'in vain' sense, मुफ़्त (muft) is often paired with में (men) as मुफ़्त में (muft men) to mean 'for no reason at all' or 'needlessly,' e.g. describing wasted effort or an undeserved scolding.
Synonyms: बेकार (bekaar, 'in vain' sense only), व्यर्थ (vyarth, 'in vain' sense only, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The 'in vain' sense shows up in everyday complaints, like मुफ़्त में बदनाम हो गए (muft men badnaam ho gae, 'got a bad name for nothing') -- a classic line when someone feels unfairly blamed.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुफ़्त में (muft men) is ambiguous between 'for free' (no cost) and 'for no reason/in vain' -- context decides which one is meant, so learners should listen for tone: मुफ़्त में मिल गया (muft men mil gayaa, 'got it for free') versus मुफ़्त में डाँट पड़ी (muft men daant padii, 'got scolded for nothing').
