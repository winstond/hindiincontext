साढ़े (saadhe)

numeral A fixed word used only in front of a number to mean 'and a half' (as in three and a half, five and a half). It always precedes the number it modifies -- साढ़े तीन (saadhe tiin, 'three and a half') -- and does not change form.
Sense 1 : and a half (used before a number)
numeral Placed immediately before the number it modifies: साढ़े चार (saadhe chaar, 'four and a half'), साढ़े नौ बजे (saadhe nau baje, 'at half past nine'). Note the special forms डेढ़ (dedh, 'one and a half') and ढाई (dhaaii, 'two and a half') replace साढ़े for one and two -- there is no साढ़े एक (saadhe ek) or साढ़े दो (saadhe do) in standard usage.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around clock time and prices in daily Delhi life -- साढ़े आठ बजे (saadhe aath baje, 'at half past eight') for a meeting time, or साढ़े तीन सौ रुपये (saadhe tiin sau rupye, 'three hundred fifty rupees') while bargaining at a market. Once you have डेढ़ (dedh) and ढाई (dhaaii) down for one and a half and two and a half, साढ़े is simply the regular pattern for everything above that.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try साढ़े एक (saadhe ek) or साढ़े दो (saadhe do) for 'one and a half' and 'two and a half' -- these are wrong; Hindi uses the irregular words डेढ़ (dedh) and ढाई (dhaaii) instead, and साढ़े only kicks in from three onward. 2) Word order surprises learners at first: साढ़े comes before the number, not after, matching neither literal English word order nor a postposition pattern.
