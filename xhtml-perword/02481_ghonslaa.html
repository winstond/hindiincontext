घोंसला (ghonslaa)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'nest' (of a bird or, by extension, small animal), with a natural figurative extension to a cozy or sheltered home. One core sense, used both literally and metaphorically.
Sense 1 : bird's nest
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in children's rhymes and school textbooks -- चिड़िया का घोंसला (chidiyaa kaa ghonslaa, 'the bird's nest') is a classic phrase kids learn early, often paired with a lesson about the bird flying off in the morning and returning at dusk, a common motif for teaching daily routine vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use घर (ghar) for 'nest' since it also means 'home' -- but घर (ghar) is reserved for human dwellings, while घोंसला (ghonslaa) is specifically the structure a bird (or small creature) builds to live and lay eggs in.
Sample Sentences:
चिड़िया ने पेड़ पर घोंसला बनाया। The bird built a nest on the tree.
(chidiyaa ne ped par ghonslaa banaayaa।)

Sense 2 : cozy/sheltered home (figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आशियाना (aashiyaanaa, literary), घर (ghar)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just like in English 'nest egg' or 'empty nest,' Hindi speakers extend घोंसला (ghonslaa) to a warm, protected home base -- you might hear a parent describe raising children as बनाना अपना घोंसला (apnaa ghonslaa banaanaa, 'building one's own nest'), carrying the same tender, protective feeling as the English idiom.

उन्होंने बरसों की मेहनत से यह छोटा-सा घोंसला बनाया है। They have built this little nest of a home through years of hard work.
(unhonne barson kii mehnat se yah chhotaa-saa ghonslaa banaayaa hai।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
