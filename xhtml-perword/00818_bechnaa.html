बेचना (bechnaa)

verb A single core sense: to sell. Extends naturally into a common figurative idiom about selling out one's dignity or principles.
Sense 1 : to sell (goods, property, etc.)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बेचना (bechnaa) pairs with its intransitive twin बिकना (biknaa) the way English 'sell' shifts between 'she sold the car' and 'the car sold quickly' -- except Hindi marks that shift with a completely different verb rather than the same one used both ways. You will hear this constantly in bazaar contexts: सब्ज़ी बेचना (sabzii bechnaa), फल बेचना (phal bechnaa), things a रेहड़ी (rehdii) vendor does all day.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a transitive verb, so in the perfective it takes ने (ne): उसने घर बेचा (usne ghar bechaa, 'he/she sold the house'), not उसने घर बेचा with wrong agreement -- the past participle agrees with the object (घर, masculine), not the subject. 2) Learners sometimes confuse बेचना (bechnaa, 'to sell') with बिकना (biknaa, 'to be sold'), which is the intransitive counterpart used when talking about the goods themselves as the grammatical subject: यह किताब अच्छी बिकती है (yah kitaab achchhii biktii hai, 'this book sells well').

Sense 2 : to sell out, betray (one's honor, principles, conscience) -- figurative
verb, transitive Occurs almost exclusively in fixed phrases with abstract nouns as object, e.g. ईमान बेचना (iimaan bechnaa, 'to sell one's integrity'), ज़मीर बेचना (zamiir bechnaa, 'to sell one's conscience').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension shows up in political and moral commentary: कहा जाता है कि उसने अपना ज़मीर बेच दिया (kahaa jaataa hai ki usne apnaa zamiir bech diyaa, 'it is said that he sold his conscience'). The compound-verb form बेच देना (bech denaa) often intensifies this sense, underscoring the completeness of the betrayal.
