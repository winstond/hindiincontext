श्रेय (shrey)

noun A noun meaning 'credit' -- the recognition given to someone for a good deed or achievement. Mostly seen in the fixed phrase pattern with देना (denaa) or जाना (jaanaa).
Sense 1 : credit, praise for an achievement
noun, masculine Almost always appears with देना (denaa) 'to give credit' or जाना (jaanaa) in passive-style constructions like श्रेय जाता है (shrey jaataa hai, 'the credit goes'), with को (ko) marking the person credited.
Synonyms: तारीफ़ (taariif, covers praise more broadly, not just credit for achievement), साख (saakh, leans toward reputation/credibility rather than one-time credit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Indian speeches, award functions, and cricket post-match interviews: टीम को श्रेय जाता है (tiim ko shrey jaataa hai, 'credit goes to the team') is a classic humble-hero line. It has a slightly formal, public-speech flavor rather than casual chit-chat register.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use श्रेय (shrey) as a verb or adjective; it is strictly a noun and needs देना (denaa) or जाना (jaanaa) to do anything. 2) The person receiving credit is marked with को (ko), not से (se) or ने (ne) -- e.g. उसे श्रेय मिलता है (use shrey miltaa hai, 'he gets the credit').
Sample Sentences:
इस जीत का श्रेय पूरी टीम को जाता है। The credit for this victory goes to the whole team.
(is jiit kaa shrey poorii tiim ko jaataa hai।)
