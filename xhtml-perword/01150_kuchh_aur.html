कुछ और (kuchh aur)

pronoun/adverb A two-word combination of कुछ (kuchh, 'some/something') and और (aur, 'more/other') meaning 'something else' or 'a bit more' -- used constantly in everyday requests, offers, and comparisons.
Sense 1 : something else, anything else
pronoun
Synonyms: कोई और चीज़ (koii aur chiiz, longer/explicit variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: At a dhaba or in a shop, कुछ और? (kuchh aur?, 'anything else?') is what a waiter or shopkeeper will ask you almost automatically once you've ordered or picked something -- it's the default follow-up question in service settings all over Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say कुछ दूसरा (kuchh doosraa) when they mean 'something else' -- this sounds odd; कुछ और (kuchh aur) is the natural everyday phrase. 2) In questions like 'anything else?', Hindi uses कुछ और? (kuchh aur?, 'anything else?') alone -- adding चाहिए (chaahie) or a verb is optional and often dropped in casual speech.

Sense 2 : a bit more, some more (quantity)
adverb Used after a noun or verb to ask for or offer an additional amount, e.g. चाय कुछ और? (chaay kuchh aur?, 'some more tea?').
Synonyms: थोड़ा और (thodaa aur, overlapping sense, more common for quantity)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase behind the classic hospitable offer at any Indian home: कुछ और लीजिए (kuchh aur liijie, 'please have some more') -- refusing once and being offered again is practically a ritual.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for ज़्यादा (zyaadaa, 'more/excess') here, but ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) implies a large or excessive amount, while कुछ और (kuchh aur) is a modest, polite 'a little more'.
Sample Sentences:
