बकरी (bakrii)

noun Means 'goat' (female goat, or the animal generically in everyday speech); बकरी (bakrii) also appears in a couple of common idioms about meekness.
Sense 1 : goat (the animal)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Goats are a common sight tied up outside homes or grazing on roadside patches in both villages and city neighborhoods across North India, so बकरी (bakrii) is a word children learn very early, often in nursery rhymes and animal-sound games (बकरी क्या बोलती है? -- bakrii kyaa boltii hai?, 'what sound does the goat make?').
Common Mistakes: 1) बकरी (bakrii) is grammatically feminine and is used for the animal in general in casual speech, even when the specific animal's sex is not being emphasized; बकरा (bakraa) specifically means 'male goat' (and is the word used for goat meat/sacrifice contexts), so swapping them changes the meaning, not just the gender.
Sample Sentences:

(bakrii ghaas khaa rahii hai।)

Sense 2 : meek, timid person (figurative)
noun, feminine Used predicatively or as a comparison, often in the set phrase बकरी जैसा (bakrii jaisaa, 'like a goat/sheep' -- meek).
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Calling someone बकरी (bakrii) or saying वह तो बकरी है (vah to bakrii hai, 'he/she is such a pushover') paints them as someone who never argues back or gets bullied around -- a mildly teasing, affectionate jab rather than a harsh insult.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense leans on the goat's image as a docile, easily-led animal, similar to how English uses 'sheep' rather than 'goat' for meekness -- so a direct word-for-word translation from English idioms about sheep will not land the same way.

(vah to bakrii hai, kabhii kisii se nahiin ladtaa।)
