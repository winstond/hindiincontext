सीटी (siitii)

noun A whistle -- both the object/sound (noun) and the act of whistling shows up mainly through the compound verb सीटी बजाना (siitii bajaanaa, 'to whistle').
Sense 1 : whistle (sound or object)
noun, feminine Almost always paired with the verb बजाना (bajaanaa) rather than used alone with a verb of its own -- सीटी बजाना (siitii bajaanaa, 'to blow/play a whistle') is the standard collocation for both the physical object and the sound it makes.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a Delhi kitchen, कुकर की सीटी (kukar kii siitii, 'the cooker's whistle') going off is practically a dinner-time clock -- everyone in the house recognizes that shrill sound as 'food's almost ready.' It is also the word for a referee's whistle in a cricket or football match, and for the low, playful सीटी (siitii) boys sometimes give when a hero enters in a film -- so the same small object/sound covers kitchens, stadiums, and movie halls.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to pair सीटी (siitii) with बोलना (bolnaa) or करना (karnaa); the fixed collocation is बजाना (bajaanaa), the same verb used for playing instruments. 2) Note the referee's or policeman's सीटी (siitii) and a pressure cooker's सीटी (siitii) are the same word in Hindi, whereas English might reach for 'whistle' versus 'valve' -- a cooker's सीटी बजना (siitii bajnaa) is a very common household sound cue, e.g. for knowing rice or daal is nearly done.
