सुनाना (sunaanaa)

verb The causative of सुनना (sunnaa, 'to hear/listen') -- means 'to tell/narrate/recite' (to make someone hear something), with an extended sense of 'to give someone a piece of one's mind.'
Sense 1 : to narrate, recite, or tell (a story, song, poem, news)
verb, transitive Takes an indirect object marked with को (ko) for the listener: उसे कहानी सुनाना (use kahaanii sunaanaa, 'to tell him/her a story').
Synonyms: कहना (kahnaa, to say/tell, narrower), बताना (bataanaa, to inform, narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic verb for bedtime stories -- दादी कहानी सुनाती थी (daadii kahaanii sunaatii thii, 'grandma used to tell a story') is a phrase almost every Hindi-speaking kid grew up hearing. It literally means 'to make someone hear,' capturing how Hindi treats storytelling as an act done to an audience's ears rather than just 'telling.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बताना (bataanaa) when सुनाना (sunaanaa) is more natural -- सुनाना (sunaanaa) is used specifically for stories, poems, songs, jokes, and news meant to be performed or narrated aloud, while बताना (bataanaa) is for plain information. 2) The listener takes को (ko), not से (se): मुझे कहानी सुनाओ (mujhe kahaanii sunaao, 'tell me a story'), not मुझसे (mujhse).

Sense 2 : to sing or perform (a song, ghazal) for listeners
verb, transitive Emphasizes performing for someone's benefit, as opposed to गाना (gaanaa) which can be sung alone or for no one in particular.
Synonyms: गाना (gaanaa, to sing, broader/neutral)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this at gatherings when someone is coaxed to perform: एक गाना सुनाओ (ek gaanaa sunaao, 'sing us a song') -- a common, warm request at Indian family functions and antakshari sessions.
Common Mistakes: 1) गाना (gaanaa) and सुनाना (sunaanaa) overlap for songs, but सुनाना (sunaanaa) specifically frames it as a performance for listeners: उसने गाना सुनाया (usne gaanaa sunaayaa, 'he sang a song (for us)') implies an audience in a way गाना (gaanaa) alone doesn't.

Sense 3 : to scold or give someone a piece of one's mind
verb, transitive Common in the idiom खरी-खोटी सुनाना (kharii-khotii sunaanaa, 'to tell off'), and also used alone: मैंने उसे सुना दी (mainne use sunaa dii, 'I gave him an earful') using the compound verb form.
Synonyms: डांटना (daantnaa, to scold, more direct/harsher), खरी-खोटी सुनाना (kharii-khotii sunaanaa, idiom, to give a strong tongue-lashing)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a wonderfully Hindi twist: the same verb used for a loving bedtime story can also mean 'to tell someone off,' as in जाकर उसे खरी-खोटी सुना आया (jaakar use kharii-khotii sunaa aayaa, 'went and gave him a piece of my mind'). Context and tone are everything here.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense flips the literal meaning ironically -- 'making someone hear' becomes 'making someone hear what they don't want to.' Learners who only know the storytelling sense may miss this negative meaning entirely in context. 2) Often appears as a compound verb, सुना देना (sunaa denaa), which adds a sense of decisiveness or finality to the scolding.
