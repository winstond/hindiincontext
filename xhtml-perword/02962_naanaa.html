नाना (naanaa)

noun/adjective Primarily a kinship noun for one's maternal grandfather (naanaa); there is also a much rarer adjective sense meaning 'various, of many kinds', now mostly confined to set phrases.
Sense 1 : maternal grandfather
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian families a mother's parents' house, नाना-नानी का घर (naanaa-naanii kaa ghar, 'maternal grandparents' house'), is a beloved childhood destination for summer holidays, distinct in feel from the father's side (दादा-दादी, daadaa-daadii). The idiom नानी याद आना (naanii yaad aanaa, 'to remember one's grandmother', i.e. to be in serious trouble or pain) is extremely common in speech, even though it uses नानी (naanii) rather than नाना (naanaa) itself -- worth knowing as the family-word connects the two.
Common Mistakes: 1) Hindi distinguishes maternal from paternal grandfather where English just says 'grandfather': नाना (naanaa) is mother's father, दादा (daadaa) is father's father -- learners often default to दादा (daadaa) for both. 2) The feminine counterpart is नानी (naanii, maternal grandmother), not a form of नाना (naanaa) itself -- they are paired terms, not gendered forms of one word.

Sense 2 : various, of many kinds
adjective Almost always appears in the fixed pair नाना प्रकार (naanaa prakaar, 'many kinds/types') or नाना प्रकार के/की/का (naanaa prakaar ke, 'of various kinds'); rarely used as a free-standing adjective outside this collocation.
Synonyms: विभिन्न (vibhinn), तरह-तरह का (tarah-tarah kaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense survives mainly in slightly formal or written registers, in the set phrase नाना प्रकार के (naanaa prakaar ke, 'of various kinds') -- you will meet it in essays, speeches, or older narration more than in casual chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) This नाना (naanaa) is unrelated in meaning to the grandfather sense -- it is a homophone, not a related meaning, so do not look for a family connection.
