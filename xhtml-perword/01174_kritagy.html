कृतज्ञ (kritagy)

adjective A single-sense formal adjective meaning 'grateful, thankful.' Sanskrit-derived and literary in flavor compared to everyday ways of expressing thanks.
Sense 1 : grateful, thankful
adjective Typically used with के प्रति (ke prati, 'towards') or का (kaa) to name who one is grateful to, e.g. आपका कृतज्ञ हूँ (aapkaa kritagy hoon, 'I am grateful to you').
Synonyms: आभारी (aabhaarii), एहसानमंद (ehsaanmand, colloquial), शुक्रगुज़ार (shukrguzaar, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll see engraved on plaques, printed in formal thank-you letters, or used in a felicitation speech -- it carries real weight and sincerity, almost like saying 'deeply indebted' rather than just 'thanks.' In daily conversation, most Delhi speakers reach for आभारी (aabhaarii) or the everyday शुक्रिया (shukriyaa, 'thank you') instead, saving कृतज्ञ (kritagy) for moments that call for real solemnity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कृतज्ञ (kritagy) in casual speech where a native speaker would say आभारी (aabhaarii) or शुक्रगुज़ार (shukrguzaar) instead; कृतज्ञ (kritagy) tends to show up in formal writing, speeches, and letters rather than everyday chat. 2) Because it ends in a consonant, learners sometimes wrongly add gendered vowel endings to it as if it were a variable adjective like अच्छा (achchhaa); it stays unchanged for all genders and numbers.

मैं आपका जीवन भर कृतज्ञ रहूँगा। I will remain grateful to you for my whole life.
(main aapkaa jiivan bhar kritagy rahoongaa।)
