आज्ञा देना (aagyaa denaa)

verb A formal, respectful way to say 'to permit/give permission' -- literally 'to give an order/command.' Compare the more everyday इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa) and अनुमति देना (anumti denaa).
Sense 1 : to grant permission, to allow (formal)
verb, transitive The person granted permission is marked with को (ko): माँ ने मुझे जाने की आज्ञा दी (maan ne mujhe jaane kii aagyaa dii, 'Mother gave me permission to go').
Synonyms: इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa), अनुमति देना (anumti denaa), मंज़ूरी देना (manzoorii denaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: आज्ञा (aagyaa) evokes the world of traditional hierarchy -- a guru giving आज्ञा (aagyaa) to a disciple, a king to a subject, parents to children in old joint-family settings. In modern Delhi speech, इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa) is what you'll actually hear at home or work; आज्ञा देना (aagyaa denaa) shows up more in formal, literary, or devotional registers, and in respectful address to elders.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for आज्ञा देना (aagyaa denaa) in casual contexts where इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa) sounds far more natural -- आज्ञा (aagyaa) carries a formal, almost old-fashioned or authoritative flavor, like a king, elder, or superior granting leave. 2) The thing permitted is usually expressed with a की (kii) + infinitive construction (जाने की आज्ञा, jaane kii aagyaa, 'permission to go'), not a plain infinitive.

(guru ne shishy ko aashram chhodne kii aagyaa dii।)

Sense 2 : to command, to order
verb, transitive
Synonyms: हुक्म देना (hukm denaa), आदेश देना (aadesh denaa)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This is the older, more literal sense behind आज्ञा (aagyaa, 'order, command'), still visible in phrases like आज्ञा का पालन करना (aagyaa kaa paalan karnaa, 'to obey an order/command'). It carries the same respectful-hierarchy flavor as the permission sense, since in traditional settings a superior's 'permission' and 'command' were often one and the same act.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps closely with the 'permission' sense above -- context (a superior instructing a subordinate vs. someone granting leave to do something) is what distinguishes 'command' from 'permit' here, not any change in grammar.
