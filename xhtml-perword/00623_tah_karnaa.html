तह करना (tah karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to fold' (clothes, paper, etc.) -- built from the noun तह (tah, 'fold/layer') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do/make').
Sense 1 : to fold (clothes, paper, blankets, etc.)
verb, transitive तह (tah) is the noun object here ('layer/fold'), and करना (karnaa) supplies the verb action -- the whole phrase behaves as a standard transitive verb-noun combination, not a fixed idiom.
Synonyms: मोड़ना (modnaa, general 'to bend/fold')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around the house in Delhi -- mothers telling kids कपड़े तह करो (kapde tah karo, 'fold the clothes') after laundry comes off the line, or someone folding a रज़ाई (razaaii, 'quilt') and tucking it away in a संदूक (sandook, 'trunk'). It's a very domestic, everyday phrase rather than a formal or literary one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मोड़ना (modnaa) instead, but मोड़ना (modnaa) is used for bending or turning something (a corner, a road, a wire), while तह करना (tah karnaa) specifically means folding something flat into layers, like clothes or a letter. 2) Because तह (tah) is the object noun, it takes ने-marking on the subject in the past tense just like any other transitive verb-noun phrase (e.g. उसने कपड़े तह किए, usne kapde tah kie, 'she folded the clothes') -- learners sometimes forget this since the phrase doesn't feel like a typical single-word transitive verb.

(maan roz shaam ko kapde tah kartii hai।)
