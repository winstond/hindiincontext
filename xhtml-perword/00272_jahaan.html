जहाँ (jahaan)

adverb/conjunction Relative adverb meaning 'where' -- used in correlative pairs with वहाँ (vahaan, 'there'), and extended idiomatically to mean 'wherever' or 'as soon as.'
Sense 1 : relative locative -- where
adverb Typically pairs with वहाँ (vahaan) in a correlative frame: जहाँ...वहाँ... (jahaan...vahaan..., 'where...there...'), naming a place and then referring back to it.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of जहाँ (jahaan) and कहाँ (kahaan) as a matched pair the way 'where' (relative) and 'where?' (question) split into two different words in Hindi -- this mapping trips up a lot of learners at first because English uses one word for both jobs.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop the correlative वहाँ (vahaan) and just use जहाँ (jahaan) alone the way English 'where' works in a simple sentence; in Hindi the two-part frame is far more natural and expected. 2) जहाँ (jahaan) is not used to ask a direct question ('where is it?') -- that job belongs to कहाँ (kahaan); confusing the two is a very common beginner slip.

Sense 2 : wherever (universal/indefinite sense)
adverb Often reinforced with भी (bhii) as जहाँ भी (jahaan bhii, 'wherever'), giving the indefinite/universal sense rather than the plain relative one.
Synonyms: जहाँ कहीं (jahaan kahiin, emphatic variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Adding भी (bhii) is the natural way Hindi turns a relative word into an indefinite one -- the same pattern shows up across जो भी (jo bhii, 'whoever'), जब भी (jab bhii, 'whenever'), and जैसा भी (jaisaa bhii, 'however'), so once you notice it with जहाँ (jahaan) you start seeing it everywhere.

Sense 3 : as soon as / the moment that (temporal idiomatic use)
conjunction In this use जहाँ (jahaan) shifts from marking place to marking a point in time, roughly 'the moment [that happens]' -- best learned from example sentences since it overlaps with जैसे ही (jaise hii).
Synonyms: जैसे ही (jaise hii, more common in this sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up more in writing and slightly formal narration than in everyday chatter, where जैसे ही (jaise hii) usually does the job instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) This temporal use is less common than जैसे ही (jaise hii) in casual speech, so leaning on जहाँ (jahaan) for 'as soon as' in every context can sound slightly literary or old-fashioned to a listener expecting the more everyday phrase.
