ही (hii)

particle An emphatic particle meaning 'only, just, exactly, precisely' -- it narrows focus onto whatever word it follows. One core sense, but its flavor shifts with context (exclusivity, emphasis on identity/timing, or softening/insistence in fixed phrases).
Sense 1 : emphatic focus particle: only/just/exactly
particle Attaches directly after the word it emphasizes, with no case change to that word; the emphasized word almost always sits right before ही (hii).
Synonyms: मात्र (maatr, formal), सिर्फ़ (sirf, often pairs with exclusion rather than pinpoint emphasis)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This little word is everywhere in spoken Hindi and does a lot of quiet work -- अभी (abhii, 'right now'), यही (yahii, 'this very one'), वही (vahii, 'that very one'), तभी (tabhii, 'only then') are all built by fusing ही (hii) onto a base word, which is why Hindi has this whole family of 'precisely this/that/then' words that English has to build with extra phrases. If you ask a Delhi shopkeeper 'kitne ka hai' and he says 'yahi sau rupaye ka hai', that ही (hii) is quietly saying 'exactly this, no more haggling.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Placement is the big one: ही (hii) must sit immediately after the exact word being emphasized, not just somewhere near the end of the clause -- मैं ही जाऊँगा (main hii jaaoongaa, 'I myself will go') and मैं जाऊँगा ही (main jaaoongaa hii, 'I will indeed go') mean different things because ही (hii) is emphasizing different words. 2) Learners often skip ही (hii) entirely since English doesn't have a direct one-word equivalent, but its absence can make a Hindi sentence sound flatter or even change the meaning (यही ठीक है (yahii thiik hai, 'this exact one is fine') vs. यह ठीक है (yah thiik hai, 'this is fine')). 3) Don't confuse with भी (bhii, 'also/even') -- they are near-opposites in function: भी (bhii) adds/includes, ही (hii) narrows/restricts.
Sample Sentences:
यह किताब सौ रुपये की ही है। This book costs exactly a hundred rupees, no more.
(yah kitaab sau rupye kii hii hai।)

Sense 2 : emphasis on immediacy/sequence: as soon as, right when
particle Common in the correlative frame ज्यों ही ... त्यों ही (jyon hii ... tyon hii, 'as soon as ... then') and in ही (hii) following a perfective verb form to mean 'the very moment X happened.'
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in storytelling and instructions: घर पहुँचते ही खाना खा लो (ghar pahunchte hii khaanaa khaa lo, 'eat as soon as you reach home') -- the ही (hii) here compresses 'the very moment you arrive' into one syllable, which is very characteristic of how Hindi likes to be economical with time clauses.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate 'as soon as' word-for-word with a separate conjunction instead of recognizing that ही (hii) attached to the verb or time word is already doing that job, e.g. पहुँचते ही (pahunchte hii, 'right upon arriving']) uses ही (hii) directly after the verb, not a standalone connector.
