पति (pati)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'husband.' Contrast with its close pair पत्नी (patnii, 'wife').
Sense 1 : husband
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पतिदेव (patidev, formal/respectful), शौहर (shauhar), वर (var, literary), स्वामी (svaamii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पति (pati) and पत्नी (patnii) are the standard, respectful words for 'husband' and 'wife' used in everyday Delhi-NCR speech, forms, and formal introductions alike. In warmer or more old-fashioned families you will also hear पतिदेव (patidev, literally 'husband-lord'), a half-affectionate, half-respectful term some women use, especially of an older generation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mix up पति (pati, 'husband') with पत्नी (patnii, 'wife') because the words look and sound similar -- the ending vowel is the only distinguishing cue. 2) आदमी (aadmii) can colloquially mean 'husband' in casual speech (e.g. मेरा आदमी (meraa aadmii, 'my man/husband')), but it primarily just means 'man,' so it is not a safe substitute for पति (pati) in neutral or formal contexts.
