शिष्य (shishy)

noun A student or disciple, especially one formally learning under a guru or master; carries a respectful, traditional flavor rather than the casual sense of 'student in a classroom.'
Sense 1 : disciple, pupil (of a guru or master)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: छात्र (chhaatr, classroom student), विद्यार्थी (vidyaarthii, classroom student), चेला (chelaa, colloquial, often for a guru's devoted follower)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries the weight of the guru-shishya (guru-shishy) tradition -- the old Indian model of learning where a student lives with, serves, and absorbs wisdom from a master over years, not just attends classes. You'll hear it used for disciples of classical musicians, yoga teachers, spiritual gurus, or even someone who considers themselves the intellectual heir of a famous thinker.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शिष्य (shishy) to mean any 'student,' but it's mainly used for someone learning under a specific guru, teacher, or master in a mentorship relationship (music, martial arts, spirituality, classical arts) -- for a school or college student, छात्र (chhaatr) or विद्यार्थी (vidyaarthii) is the natural choice. 2) The feminine form शिष्या (shishyaa) is used for a female disciple; don't default to शिष्य (shishy) for women in careful speech.
