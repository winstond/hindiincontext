नीति (niiti)

noun Mainly one core idea -- an organized course of action or set of guiding principles -- which stretches from formal 'policy' to personal 'ethics/conduct.' Both senses share the same lexical root, just applied to institutions versus individuals.
Sense 1 : policy (governmental, organizational, official plan of action)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: नियम (niyam, rule, narrower scope), रणनीति (ranniiti, strategy, narrower scope)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this word constantly in newspaper headlines and news broadcasts -- विदेश नीति (videsh niiti, 'foreign policy'), आर्थिक नीति (aarthik niiti, 'economic policy') -- it's the standard word for any official domain of policy in Indian public life.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for योजना (yojnaa) when they mean नीति (niiti) -- योजना (yojnaa) is a specific plan or scheme, while नीति (niiti) is the broader guiding principle behind many plans, e.g. सरकार की शिक्षा नीति (sarkaar kii shikshaa niiti, 'the government's education policy') versus पंचवर्षीय योजना (panchvarshiiy yojnaa, 'five-year plan').

Sense 2 : ethics, moral conduct, prudent wisdom (personal or philosophical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आचार (aachaar, formal, conduct/practice), सदाचार (sadaachaar, literary, virtuous conduct)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense connects नीति (niiti) to the tradition of नीति-कथा (niiti-kathaa, 'moral fable') and the famous नीतिशास्त्र (niitishaastr, 'science of ethics/statecraft') associated with figures like Chanakya -- so the word carries an old echo of wise, principled living, not just abstract morality.
