चना (chanaa)

noun Refers to the chickpea/gram, most often met in the plural चने (chane) as the snack food or the roasted/boiled dish, and in the compound चना दाल (chanaa daal) for split gram.
Sense 1 : chickpea / gram (the legume, and food made from it)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: काला चना (kaalaa chanaa, black chickpea variety)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Roasted चने (chane) sold by street vendors in a folded newspaper cone are a classic winter-evening snack in Delhi, often eaten with a squeeze of lemon and salt. उबले चने (uble chane, 'boiled chickpeas') are also a common high-protein breakfast, especially for people who work out -- you'll hear gym-going friends say भीगे चने (bhiige chane, 'soaked chickpeas') as a health food.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use the singular चना (chanaa) when talking about a handful or plate of the snack; native speakers almost always say चने (chane) in the plural for that, reserving the singular for the crop/grain in general or the dal made from it. 2) चना (chanaa) is the whole/split gram itself; don't confuse it with छोला (chholaa)/छोले (chhole), the cooked chickpea curry -- related but a different dish.

Sense 2 : idiom: something trivial/insignificant (as in 'chana chabaana' type expressions)
noun, masculine Lives almost entirely inside fixed idiomatic phrases rather than as a free-standing meaning; the sentences in Pass 2 will show the actual idiom in context.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative use grows out of the same humble, everyday grain as the literal sense -- something as small and ordinary as a chickpea becomes a natural stand-in for anything trifling, the way English might reach for 'peanuts.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense doesn't stand alone -- you won't hear चना (chanaa) used by itself to mean 'trivial thing' outside of set phrases, so don't try to build new sentences with it in this sense.
