याद (yaad)

noun याद (yaad) is a feminine noun meaning 'memory' or 'remembrance', but its everyday power comes from the fixed phrases it forms with आना (aanaa), करना (karnaa), and रखना (rakhnaa) -- these idiomatic verb pairings are the real heart of this word for a learner.
Sense 1 : the state of remembering something; something coming to mind
noun, feminine Used with याद आना (yaad aanaa) in a dative-subject frame: मुझे उसकी याद आई (mujhe uskii yaad aaii, 'I remembered him/her', literally 'his/her memory came to me'). The person or thing remembered takes की (kii)/का (kaa) before याद (yaad), and the rememberer is marked with को (ko).
Synonyms: स्मृति (smriti, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi's classic 'body/mind as receiver' constructions: memory is not something you actively do, it is something that arrives at you, much like नींद आना (niind aanaa, sleep) or प्यार होना (pyaar honaa, falling in love). A mother might say तेरी याद आती है (terii yaad aatii hai, 'I miss you', literally 'your memory keeps coming') -- this passive framing of longing is deeply embedded in how Hindi speakers talk about missing someone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the rememberer the subject of an active verb, saying something like मैं याद आया (main yaad aayaa) when they mean 'I remembered' -- but याद आना (yaad aanaa) always puts the person in the को (ko) slot and the thing remembered as the grammatical subject: मुझे याद आया (mujhe yaad aayaa). 2) Confusing याद आना (yaad aanaa) (something spontaneously comes to mind) with याद करना (yaad karnaa) (to deliberately recall or memorize) -- these are not interchangeable.

Sense 2 : missing someone; longing for someone's presence
noun, feminine Expressed via याद आना (yaad aanaa) in continuous/habitual aspect: तुम्हारी याद आती रहती है (tumhaarii yaad aatii rahtii hai, 'I keep missing you'). This sense overlaps with the 'memory' sense above but is split because in English it maps to a distinct verb, 'to miss', rather than 'to remember'.
Synonyms: विरह (virah, literary, more about separation-longing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is probably the most emotionally loaded use of याद (yaad) -- it shows up constantly in songs, letters, and phone calls home. Saying तेरी याद आ रही है (terii yaad aa rahii hai) to a friend or family member carries real tenderness, more so than the borrowed 'miss you' that younger urban speakers sometimes slip into English for.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners reach for a literal translation of 'I miss you' and try मैं तुम्हें मिस करता हूँ (main tumhen mis kartaa hoon) -- this is heard in casual Hinglish but the native, warmer construction is तुम्हारी याद आती है (tumhaarii yaad aatii hai). 2) Forgetting that the person missed is marked with का (kaa)/की (kii)/के (ke), not से (se) or को (ko).

Sense 3 : keeping something in mind; not forgetting (a task, fact, or promise)
noun, feminine Common in the frame याद रखना (yaad rakhnaa, 'to keep in mind, remember') and याद रहना (yaad rahnaa, 'to stay remembered/not forget', dative-subject: मुझे याद रहेगा (mujhe yaad rahegaa, 'I'll remember it'). Also याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa, the causative 'to remind').
Synonyms: ध्यान (dhyaan, broader: attention/mindfulness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa) is the everyday word for 'remind' and shows up constantly in practical requests -- मुझे याद दिला देना (mujhe yaad dilaa denaa, 'remind me') is something you will hear dozens of times a day in any Indian household or office, since these three verbs (रखना (rakhnaa), रहना (rahnaa), दिलाना (dilaanaa)) turn one noun into a small toolkit for talking about memory tasks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse याद रखना (yaad rakhnaa) (active: you deliberately hold something in memory) with याद रहना (yaad rahnaa) (it stays remembered on its own, no agency implied) -- the choice changes whether the sentence takes an agent subject or a dative one. 2) For 'remind', learners sometimes try a direct calque instead of the correct causative याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa, literally 'to cause memory to be given').
