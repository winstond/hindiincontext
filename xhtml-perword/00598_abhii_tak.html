अभी तक (abhii tak)

adverb A common time adverbial combining अभी (abhii, 'now') with तक (tak, 'until'); it means 'until now / so far' and, in negative sentences, 'not yet.'
Sense 1 : up to now, so far
adverb Used with the perfective or habitual to describe a state or action continuing from the past into the present moment.
Synonyms: अब तक (ab tak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this everywhere in daily complaints and updates -- अभी तक बिजली नहीं आई (abhii tak bijlii nahiin aaii, 'the power still hasn't come') is a classic Delhi-NCR grumble during a power cut. It pairs naturally with the perfective tense to check on the status of something up to the present.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use सिर्फ अभी (sirph abhii) or just अभी (abhii) when they mean 'so far,' losing the sense of a span of time leading up to now; अभी तक (abhii tak) specifically marks that stretch from some earlier point up to the present. 2) In questions like क्या तुमने खाना खा लिया? (kyaa tumne khaanaa khaa liyaa?, 'have you eaten?'), learners forget to add अभी तक (abhii tak) to make it clearly about a status check up to now, e.g. क्या तुमने अभी तक खाना खा लिया? (kyaa tumne abhii tak khaanaa khaa liyaa?, 'have you eaten yet?').

Sense 2 : not yet (in negative constructions)
adverb Used with a negated verb (often नहीं (nahiin) plus perfective) to mean 'still hasn't happened by now.'
Synonyms: अब तक (ab tak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase behind endless mild frustration in Indian daily life -- अभी तक जवाब नहीं आया (abhii tak javaab nahiin aayaa, 'no reply has come yet') is exactly how you'd nudge a friend who hasn't responded to a message.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place नहीं (nahiin) before अभी तक (abhii tak) instead of after, producing unnatural word order; the natural pattern is अभी तक नहीं आया (abhii tak nahiin aayaa, 'hasn't come yet'), not नहीं अभी तक आया. 2) Confusing this with अभी नहीं (abhii nahiin, 'not right now'), which refers to the present moment only, not a stretch of elapsed time.
