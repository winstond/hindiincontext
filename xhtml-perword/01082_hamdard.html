हमदर्द (hamdard)

noun/adjective A Persian-origin compound meaning 'fellow-sufferer' -- someone who shares in your pain. Used both as an adjective/noun for a sympathetic person and, less commonly, as an adjective for feelings of sympathy itself.
Sense 1 : a sympathetic person, well-wisher who shares your pain
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: शुभचिंतक (shubhchintak), हितैषी (hitaishii, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: हम (ham, 'same/together') plus दर्द (dard, 'pain') gives you the literal sense 'one who shares your pain' -- a lovely example of how Hindi/Urdu often builds emotional vocabulary from body-and-feeling roots. You will hear this word a lot in ghazals and old Bollywood dialogues about true friendship, where a हमदर्द (hamdard) is valued above a mere दोस्त (dost, 'friend') because they feel your sorrow, not just keep you company.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use हमदर्द (hamdard) where English would say 'supporter' in a political or competitive sense -- it specifically implies shared emotional pain, not just backing someone. 2) It is not used for professional sympathy (like a doctor's bedside manner) -- it carries a personal, heartfelt tone, closer to a friend or family member who genuinely feels for you.

Sense 2 : sympathetic, compassionate (describing a person or their nature)
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. हमदर्द इंसान (hamdard insaan, 'a compassionate person'), or predicatively with होना (honaa).
Synonyms: संवेदनशील (sanvedanshiil), दयालु (dayaalu)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This adjectival use overlaps heavily with the noun sense -- in practice, native speakers often don't sharply distinguish 'a sympathizer' from 'a sympathetic person' when using this word, since it describes both the quality and the person who has it.
