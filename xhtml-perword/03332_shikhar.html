शिखर (shikhar)

noun A noun meaning 'peak/summit', used both literally (mountain peak) and figuratively (the height or pinnacle of something like success or fame).
Sense 1 : figurative peak, pinnacle of achievement
noun, masculine Commonly used in the phrase शिखर पर होना (shikhar par honaa, 'to be at the peak/height of') or शिखर पर पहुँचना (shikhar par pahunchnaa, 'to reach the peak').
Synonyms: उच्चतम बिंदु (uchchtam bindu, meaning scope: the highest point), चरम (charam)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear in sports commentary and political speeches: सफलता के शिखर पर (saphaltaa ke shikhar par, 'at the peak of success'). It carries the same ambitious, aspirational energy as English 'reaching the top' -- a cricketer at the शिखर (shikhar) of his career, or a leader at the शिखर (shikhar) of power.

Sense 2 : mountain peak, summit
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चोटी (chotii), शीर्ष (shiirsh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the Himalayas: a news report will say पहाड़ का शिखर (pahaad kaa shikhar, 'the mountain's peak'), giving the word a slightly grand, scenic feel. It also shows up as the first part of temple architecture vocabulary -- the tapering tower over a temple's inner sanctum is called a शिखर (shikhar) in temple design.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse शिखर (shikhar) with चोटी (chotii) -- both mean 'peak', but चोटी (chotii) is the more everyday word for a hilltop or mountain top someone might actually climb, while शिखर (shikhar) leans slightly more formal or descriptive, as in nature writing or news reports about mountaineering.
