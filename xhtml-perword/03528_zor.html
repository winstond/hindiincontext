ज़ोर (zor)

noun Covers physical force/strength, emphasis/insistence, and its life in common compound phrases like ज़ोर से (zor se) and ज़ोर देना (zor denaa).
Sense 1 : physical force, strength, power
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ताकत (taakat), शक्ति (shakti, slightly formal), बल (bal, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear ज़ोर से बोलो (zor se bolo, 'speak loudly') and ज़ोर से धक्का दो (zor se dhakkaa do, 'push hard') using the exact same phrase -- Hindi doesn't split 'loud' and 'forceful' the way English does, it treats both as the same underlying 'force.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say ताकत से (taakat se) when a native speaker would default to ज़ोर से (zor se, 'forcefully/loudly') in everyday speech -- both are correct but ज़ोर से is far more common in casual conversation. 2) ज़ोर से (zor se) doubles for both 'forcefully' (push hard) and 'loudly' (speak loudly) -- context decides which, this is not two separate words.
Sample Sentences:
दरवाज़ा फँस गया है, ज़ोर से धक्का दो। The door is stuck, push it hard.
(darvaazaa phans gayaa hai, zor se dhakkaa do।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

(piichhe baithe logon ko sunaaii nahiin de rahaa, zor se bolo।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

Sense 2 : emphasis, insistence, stress on a point
noun, masculine Most commonly appears inside the light-verb construction ज़ोर देना (zor denaa, 'to lay emphasis/insist') and ज़ोर पड़ना (zor padnaa, 'pressure falls on someone/something').
Synonyms: बल (bal, formal, 'stress on a point'), ज़ोर-शोर (zor-shor, 'with fanfare/enthusiasm')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Teachers and parents in Delhi love this phrase: पढ़ाई पर ज़ोर दो (padhaaii par zor do, 'put emphasis on your studies') is something nearly every Indian kid has heard at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) ज़ोर देना (zor denaa) takes पर (par) for the thing being emphasized, e.g. इस बात पर ज़ोर देना (is baat par zor denaa, 'to emphasize this point') -- learners sometimes reach for पे or को by analogy with other verbs. 2) Don't confuse this with sense 1 (physical force); ज़ोर देना (zor denaa) here means 'to stress/insist,' not 'to apply force,' even though the noun is literally the same word.
Sample Sentences:
टीचर हमेशा पढ़ाई पर ज़ोर देती हैं। The teacher always puts emphasis on studies.
(tiichar hameshaa padhaaii par zor detii hain।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

(mammii ne is baat par bahut zor diyaa ki main samay par so jaaoon।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : control, dominance, sway (as in 'to have a hold over someone/something')
noun, masculine Appears in the fixed idiomatic frame किसी का ज़ोर न चलना (kisii kaa zor n chalnaa, 'to have no power/sway over something') and ज़ोर चलना (zor chalnaa, 'to have sway/control').
Synonyms: बस (bas, 'control, as in किसी के बस में'), वश (vash, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this when someone is venting about being powerless in a situation: मौत के आगे किसी का ज़ोर नहीं चलता (maut ke aage kisii kaa zor nahiin chaltaa, 'no one has any power in the face of death') -- a common philosophical-sounding line in everyday complaints and songs alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) The idiom is negated with न or नहीं and the verb चलना (chalnaa) -- उसका ज़ोर नहीं चलता (uskaa zor nahiin chaltaa, 'he has no sway/control') -- learners sometimes try to force a literal 'force doesn't work' translation instead of learning it as a set phrase.
Sample Sentences:
