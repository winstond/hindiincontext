आतंक (aatank)

noun A single core sense: intense fear or terror, often at a societal or collective scale rather than a personal fright. Closely tied to आतंकवाद (aatankvaad, 'terrorism') and आतंकवादी (aatankvaadii, 'terrorist'), which are separate lemmas built on this root.
Sense 1 : terror, reign of fear, dread (collective/large-scale)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भय (bhay, more general/personal fear), दहशत (dahshat), खौफ़ (khauf, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word constantly in Hindi news broadcasts -- डाकू का आतंक (daakoo kaa aatank, 'the terror of the bandit') or इलाके में आतंक का माहौल (ilaake men aatank kaa maahaul, 'an atmosphere of terror in the area') are classic phrasings. It has a slightly formal, media-register weight to it compared to the more everyday डर (dar), so native speakers reach for आतंक (aatank) when describing something gripping a whole community, not just one person's nerves.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this word to mean ordinary personal fear (like fear of an exam or a dog), but आतंक (aatank) is reserved for a heavier, larger-scale dread -- a terrorized city, a criminal's reign of fear, a disease's grip on a region. For everyday personal fear, डर (dar) is the natural choice. 2) The common verb pairing is आतंक फैलाना (aatank phailaanaa, 'to spread terror') or आतंक फैलना (aatank phailnaa, 'terror to spread'), not a direct verb form of आतंक itself -- the word doesn't verb on its own.

पूरे इलाके में डाकुओं का आतंक फैला हुआ है। The terror of the bandits has spread across the whole area.
(poore ilaake men daakuon kaa aatank phailaa huaa hai।)
