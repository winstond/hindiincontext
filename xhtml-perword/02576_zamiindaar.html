ज़मींदार (zamiindaar)

noun A historical/social term for a landowner or landlord, especially the large landholders of the pre-independence agrarian system; it also lives on as a surname and a casual way to describe someone who owns a lot of land.
Sense 1 : landowner, landlord (historical zamindari system)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जागीरदार (jaagiirdaar, historical, feudal estate-holder), भूस्वामी (bhoosvaamii, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The ज़मींदारी (zamiindaarii) system was the backbone of land revenue collection under Mughal and then British rule, where ज़मींदार (zamiindaar) families held huge estates and collected taxes from peasants below them -- it was abolished in independent India through land reform laws in the 1950s. Even today in Hindi cinema and literature, a ज़मींदार (zamiindaar) character usually signals old feudal wealth and power, often with a whiff of exploitation of poorer villagers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use ज़मींदार (zamiindaar) as a generic word for anyone who owns a house or flat -- in everyday urban speech that person is called मकान मालिक (makaan maalik) or फ़्लैट का मालिक (flait kaa maalik), not ज़मींदार (zamiindaar), which specifically evokes agricultural land and the old estate system. 2) It is not interchangeable with किसान (kisaan, farmer) -- a ज़मींदार (zamiindaar) traditionally owned the land and collected rent/revenue from tenant farmers who actually worked it.

Sense 2 : a big landowner, used loosely/figuratively for someone wealthy in land or property
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ज़मीन वाला (zamiin vaalaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will still hear someone jokingly called ज़मींदार (zamiindaar) in villages or small towns if their family has a lot of ancestral land, even without any real feudal history -- it carries a sense of old-money rural prestige rather than modern urban wealth.
