थकाना (thakaanaa)

verb The causative of थकना (thaknaa, 'to become tired'): थकाना (thakaanaa) means 'to tire someone/something out, to exhaust.'
Sense 1 : to tire someone out, to exhaust (physically)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: थका देना (thakaa denaa, compound verb variant), निढाल कर देना (nidhaal kar denaa, to leave limp/exhausted)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the base pair थकना/थकाना (thaknaa/thakaanaa) the way you'd think of 'to get tired' vs. 'to tire someone out' in English -- one is about your own state, the other is about causing that state in someone else. You'll often hear it in the compound form थका देना (thakaa denaa), as in दिनभर की गर्मी ने थका दिया (dinbhar kii garmii ne thakaa diyaa, 'the day's heat wore me out'), where the compound adds a sense of completion to the exhaustion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for थकना (thaknaa) with a causative meaning in mind, but थकना (thaknaa) itself is intransitive ('to become tired') -- to say someone/something tired *you* out, you need this separate causative verb थकाना (thakaanaa), with the tired person as its object: इस काम ने मुझे थका दिया (is kaam ne mujhe thakaa diyaa, 'this work tired me out').
Sample Sentences:
इतनी ज्यादा सीढ़ियाँ चढ़ने से बच्चे थक जाते हैं। Climbing so many stairs tires the children out.
(itnii jyaadaa siidhiyaan chadhne se bachche thak jaate hain।)

Sense 2 : to wear someone down, exhaust mentally/emotionally
verb, transitive
Synonyms: परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa, to trouble/harass), ऊबाना (oobaanaa, to bore)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use extends the physical tiredness of थकना (thaknaa) to mental or emotional fatigue -- a boring meeting, a nagging relative, or endless arguments can सबको थका देना (sabko thakaa denaa, 'wear everyone out'), just as a long walk would.
Sample Sentences:
