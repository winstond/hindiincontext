इस/उस तरफ़ (is/us taraf)

adverb A directional phrase meaning 'this way/that way' or 'in this direction/that direction' -- built from the oblique demonstratives इस (is) / उस (us) plus तरफ़ (taraf, 'side, direction'). One core spatial sense, with a common extended use for 'in this regard/matter.'
Sense 1 : in this/that direction, this way/that way
adverb Functions as a directional adverb modifying motion or orientation verbs like आना, जाना, देखना.
Synonyms: इस/उस ओर (is/us or), इधर/उधर (idhar/udhar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly from auto-rickshaw drivers and shopkeepers giving directions -- इस तरफ़ आइए (is taraf aaie, 'come this way') is the everyday way to usher someone along, much warmer and more natural in context than a bare इधर (idhar).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for इधर (idhar)/उधर (udhar) as the only way to say 'this way,' not realizing इस तरफ़ (is taraf) is equally common and slightly more specific about direction, e.g. pointing a person toward something. 2) तरफ़ (taraf) is feminine, but since it's frozen here after the oblique demonstrative, learners sometimes wrongly try to inflect it further -- the phrase itself is invariant.
Sample Sentences:
इस तरफ़ आइए, बाथरूम वहाँ है। Come this way, the bathroom is over there.
(is taraf aaie, baathroom vahaan hai।)

Sense 2 : in this/that regard, on this/that matter (extended, non-spatial use)
adverb Used to shift from literal direction to topical direction, similar to how English extends 'this way' into 'in this respect.'
Synonyms: इस/उस मामले में (is/us maamle men), इस/उस बारे में (is/us baare men)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same metonymic leap English makes with 'on that front' -- Hindi speakers use उस तरफ़ (us taraf) the same way when steering a conversation toward a particular topic, e.g. discussing a problem's angle rather than a literal place.
