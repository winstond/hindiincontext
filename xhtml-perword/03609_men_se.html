में से (men se)

postposition A postposition combination meaning 'from among/out of' -- used to pick out one or more items from a group or to indicate the source location something comes from within.
Sense 1 : from among / out of (a group)
postposition Follows an oblique-case noun phrase denoting a group or set; the whole phrase answers 'which one(s), selected from what group'.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of में से (men se) as literally 'in, from' stacked together -- you're locating something inside a set (में, men) and then extracting it (से, se). You'll hear this constantly in daily choices: इन दोनों में से कौन सा चाहिए? (in donon men se kaun saa chaahie, 'which one of these two do you want?') is the classic shopkeeper-customer exchange in any Delhi market.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop से (se) and just say में (men), which changes the meaning to simple location ('in') rather than selection ('from among'). 2) दो में से एक (do men se ek, 'one out of two') is a common pattern learners forget when choosing between options -- they instead say केवल एक (keval ek) losing the 'out of X' framing.

Sense 2 : out of (a substance or whole), indicating physical origin
postposition Used when something is physically extracted or emerges from within a mass, container, or substance rather than a countable group.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same grammatical glue as the 'selecting from a group' sense, just applied to substances rather than countable items -- मिट्टी में से पानी निकलना (mittii men se paanii nikalnaa, 'water seeping out of the soil') uses में से (men se) the same way as picking a mango from a basket. The underlying image is always 'inside something, then out of it.'

(mittii men se paanii ris rahaa hai।)
