बना-बनाया (banaa-banaayaa)

adjective A reduplicated adjective meaning 'ready-made' or 'already prepared' -- describing something that comes pre-formed rather than built from scratch, often with a connotation (literal or figurative) of a set plan, mold, or excuse.
Sense 1 : ready-made, already prepared (literal, of objects/food)
adjective Formed by doubling बना (banaa, 'made') with its own perfective participle बनाया (banaayaa); the pattern itself signals 'already in finished form.'
Synonyms: तैयार (taiyaar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of buying kapde bane-banaaye (kapde bane-banaaye, 'ready-made clothes') off the rack at a shop in Karol Bagh, as opposed to getting them stitched to order -- this is the everyday, literal use of the phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes write it as two unrelated words; it should stay hyphenated as one compound adjective, बना-बनाया (banaa-banaayaa). 2) Since it inflects like a regular adjective, the feminine/plural form is बनी-बनाई (banii-banaaii), not a doubled बनाया (banaayaa) left unchanged.

Sense 2 : ready-made, pat (figurative, of excuses/opinions/plans)
adjective
Synonyms: घड़ा-घड़ाया (ghadaa-ghadaayaa, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this about excuses and opinions alike: एक बना-बनाया बहाना (ek banaa-banaayaa bahaanaa, 'a ready-made/pat excuse') is the classic complaint of a parent or boss who suspects the same tired line is being recycled rather than a genuine reason being offered.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost always carries a mildly critical or dismissive tone -- calling someone's excuse or opinion बना-बनाया (banaa-banaayaa) implies it is lazy or insincere, not simply 'convenient.'
