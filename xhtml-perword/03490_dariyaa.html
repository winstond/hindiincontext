दरिया (dariyaa)

noun A poetic/everyday word for a large river; also used figuratively for a vast flowing abundance of something, as in दरिया-ए-दिल (dariyaa-e-dil).
Sense 1 : river
noun, masculine
Synonyms: नदी (nadii), नद (nad, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Delhi you will hear यमुना (yamunaa) called a नदी (nadii) in a geography class but a दरिया (dariyaa) in a Ghalib-flavored couplet or a filmy dialogue -- the word carries a slightly grander, more literary weight than its plain cousin नदी (nadii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume दरिया (dariyaa) and नदी (nadii) are perfectly interchangeable in all contexts, but दरिया (dariyaa) tends to be reserved for genuinely big rivers or used in a more poetic register, while नदी (nadii) is the everyday, neutral word for any river.

यमुना दिल्ली का सबसे मशहूर दरिया है। The Yamuna is Delhi's most famous river.
(yamunaa dillii kaa sabse mashahoor dariyaa hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative: a flood/abundance of something
noun, masculine Used in phrases like खुशियों का दरिया (khushiyon kaa dariyaa, 'a river of joys') to describe an overflowing abundance of an emotion or quality.
Synonyms: सागर (saagar, figurative use), समुद्र (samudr, figurative use)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the river sense extended outward -- Hindi loves to describe an overwhelming feeling as something you could drown in, so दरिया-ए-दिल (dariyaa-e-dil, 'a river/sea of heart', meaning generous-hearted) is a common poetic compliment for someone big-hearted.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is almost always used with a genitive/possessive construction (X का दरिया (kaa dariyaa)) rather than standing alone, so dropping the 'of X' part sounds incomplete to a native ear.

(usne to khushiyon kaa dariyaa bahaa diyaa।)
