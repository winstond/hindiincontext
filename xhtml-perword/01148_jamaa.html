जमा (jamaa)

noun/adjective Covers deposit/accumulation as a noun and adjective ('deposited, collected'), and appears heavily in the compound verb जमा करना (jamaa karnaa, 'to deposit/collect') and जमा होना (jamaa honaa, 'to be deposited/gathered').
Sense 1 : deposit (money, savings)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जमापूंजी (jamaapoonjii, savings), निधि (nidhi, formal/fund)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday bank talk you will hear जमा (jamaa) constantly: फिक्स्ड डिपॉजिट (phiksd dipojit) is often just called एफ़डी (efdii), but the passbook and forms still say जमा (jamaa) for 'deposit' and निकासी (nikaasii) for 'withdrawal' -- a useful pair to learn together.
Common Mistakes: 1) जमा (jamaa) itself does not inflect like a typical feminine noun -- it stays invariant across singular/plural and is often used almost adjectivally, as in जमा राशि (jamaa raashi, 'deposit amount'). 2) Learners sometimes use जमा (jamaa) alone where जमा करना (jamaa karnaa) is needed to express the action 'to deposit' -- जमा (jamaa) by itself is the noun/state, not the verb.
Sample Sentences:
मैंने बैंक में पाँच हज़ार रुपये जमा किए। I deposited five thousand rupees in the bank.
(mainne baink men paanch hazaar rupye jamaa kie।)

Sense 2 : deposited, collected, accumulated
adjective Almost always appears predicatively with होना (honaa) or as part of the compound जमा होना (jamaa honaa, 'to accumulate, gather') -- e.g. पानी जमा हो गया (paanii jamaa ho gayaa, 'water has collected/pooled').
Synonyms: इकट्ठा (ikatthaa, gathered/accumulated), संचित (sanchit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly during monsoon season -- सड़क पर पानी जमा हो गया (sadak par paanii jamaa ho gayaa, 'water has collected on the road') is a very Delhi-NCR complaint every July and August.
Common Mistakes: 1) As an adjective जमा (jamaa) does not change form for gender or number -- learners sometimes wrongly inflect it like कड़ा (kadaa) type adjectives.

Sense 3 : addition (in arithmetic)
noun, masculine Used in basic school-level math to mean 'plus' or 'addition,' as in दो जमा दो (do jamaa do, 'two plus two').
Synonyms: जोड़ (jod, addition/sum), योग (yog, formal/mathematical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Older schoolbooks and many parents still say दो जमा दो बराबर चार (do jamaa do baraabar chaar, 'two plus two equals four') rather than switching to English 'plus' -- a nice example of how basic arithmetic vocabulary has stayed thoroughly Hindi even as much other school talk mixes in English.
