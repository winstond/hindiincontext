कक्षा (kakshaa)

noun Primarily means 'classroom' or 'grade/class' (as in a school year); it also has a technical astronomy sense, 'orbit'.
Sense 1 : grade/class (school year)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: क्लास (klaas, colloquial, English loanword)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Indian schools, कक्षा (kakshaa) is followed by a number to indicate grade level, e.g. कक्षा पाँच (kakshaa paanch, 'class five') or पाँचवीं कक्षा (paanchviin kakshaa, 'fifth class') -- you will see this exact phrasing on report cards, school gates, and admission forms across Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to क्लास (klaas) in speech since it is so common, but कक्षा (kakshaa) is the standard word in writing, school notices, and formal contexts -- both are widely understood, but कक्षा (kakshaa) sounds more proper. 2) To say 'which grade,' Hindi uses कौन सी कक्षा (kaun sii kakshaa, 'which class'), not किस कक्षा (kis kakshaa) alone as a standalone question -- the oblique form किस (kis) is only used when followed directly by a postposition like में (men).

Sense 2 : classroom (physical room)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कक्ष (kaksh, formal, refers to any room)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Context usually disambiguates this from the 'grade' sense -- कक्षा में जाओ (kakshaa men jaao, 'go to the classroom') is about the physical room, while कौन सी कक्षा में हो? (kaun sii kakshaa men ho, 'which grade are you in') is about the grade level.

Sense 3 : orbit (astronomy/physics)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: परिक्रमा पथ (parikrmaa path, technical)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This technical sense shows up mainly in school science textbooks discussing planets and satellites, e.g. पृथ्वी की कक्षा (prithvii kii kakshaa, 'the earth's orbit') -- useful to recognize if you read Hindi science content, but rare in everyday conversation.
