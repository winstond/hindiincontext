गढ़ना (gadhnaa)

verb Primarily 'to shape/mold/forge' something (physically, by a craftsman), extended figuratively to 'fabricate/invent' a story, excuse, or word. Both senses share the image of deliberately shaping raw material into a finished form.
Sense 1 : to shape, mold, forge (a physical object)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa), तराशना (taraashnaa, to carve/sculpt), ढालना (dhaalnaa, to cast/mold)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a गाँव (gaanv) blacksmith at his भट्टी (bhattii, forge) hammering out a tool -- that image of deliberate, skilled shaping is the heart of this word, which is why it later got borrowed for 'crafting' a story or an idea, the same way a smith crafts iron.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बनाना (banaanaa) as a catch-all 'to make', but गढ़ना (gadhnaa) specifically implies working raw material (metal, stone, clay) into shape with effort and skill -- it sounds odd for something simply assembled or bought.
Sample Sentences:
लोहार भट्टी में लोहे को गढ़ता है। The blacksmith shapes iron in the forge.
(lohaar bhattii men lohe ko gadhtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to fabricate, invent, concoct (a story, excuse, word)
verb, transitive Common object nouns with this sense include बहाना (bahaanaa, excuse), कहानी (kahaanii, story), and बात (baat) -- as in बात गढ़ना (baat gadhnaa, to concoct a story).
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa), रचना (rachnaa, to compose/create, literary), बनाना-गढ़ना (banaanaa-gadhnaa, colloquial doubling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Parents and teachers often accuse kids of गढ़ना (gadhnaa) when they invent an excuse for missing homework -- कहानी मत गढ़ो (kahaanii mat gadho, 'don't make up a story') is a very familiar scolding line in Delhi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense always carries a whiff of dishonesty or artificiality -- गढ़ी हुई कहानी (gadhii huii kahaanii, 'a made-up/concocted story') is not neutral like 'made up' can be in English; it implies the speaker doesn't believe it.
