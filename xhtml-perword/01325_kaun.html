कौन (kaun)

pronoun An interrogative meaning 'who', used to ask about people; it also appears in a few fixed expressions of vague identity or dismissal (English inline: placeholder not needed here since only Devanagari appears below).
Sense 1 : who (question word for people)
pronoun
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: कौन (kaun) is one of the first words every learner needs for basic introductions -- कौन हो आप? (kaun ho aap?, 'who are you?') is blunter than the more polite आप कौन हैं? (aap kaun hain?, 'who are you?'), so tone of voice and word order matter as much as the word itself. It pairs naturally with कौन सा (kaun saa, 'which one') and किसका (kiskaa, 'whose'), which are built on the same oblique stem किस- (kis).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use कौन (kaun) for 'which' when choosing among objects or options -- that job belongs to कौन सा (kaun saa); कौन (kaun) alone asks about a person's identity. 2) Because कौन (kaun) does not change form for the plural, learners sometimes add a plural marker; instead the verb and context show number, as in कौन आए हैं? (kaun aae hain?, 'who [plural] have come?').

Sense 2 : someone or other, whoever (vague/dismissive identity)
pronoun Used in dismissive or indefinite rhetorical phrasing, often doubled or paired with पता नहीं (pataa nahiin), rather than as a direct question.
Synonyms: कोई (koii, someone)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear pata nahii kaun (pataa nahiin kaun) all over Delhi markets and offices when someone wants to wave off a stranger's importance -- 'pata nahii kaun tha' (pataa nahiin kaun thaa, 'no idea who that was') carries a little shrug of indifference that a flat translation misses.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners read कौन जाने (kaun jaane, 'who knows') as a real question and expect an answer, when it is actually a rhetorical shrug meaning 'nobody really knows.' 2) पता नहीं कौन (pataa nahiin kaun, 'no idea who') is a set dismissive phrase about an unnamed or unimportant person, not a literal request for identification.
