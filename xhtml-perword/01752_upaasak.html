उपासक (upaasak)

noun A worshipper or devotee -- someone who performs upaasnaa (upaasnaa, worship/devotion), whether of a deity or, by extension, an idea or cause.
Sense 1 : worshipper, devotee (of a deity)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भक्त (bhakt), पुजारी (pujaarii, performs ritual worship as a role/profession), साधक (saadhak, one pursuing spiritual practice)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word has a slightly formal, textbook or discourse-y flavor -- you're more likely to meet it in a description of a religious community (like सूर्य के उपासक (soory ke upaasak, 'worshippers of the sun')) or in writing about bhakti traditions than in casual chat, where भक्त (bhakt) is far more common.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पुजारी (pujaarii) as a synonym, but पुजारी (pujaarii) usually implies an official or professional role (like a temple priest), while उपासक (upaasak) simply means someone devoted to worship -- any ordinary devotee can be called this. 2) The feminine form उपासिका (upaasikaa) exists and is used for a female worshipper -- this word does not stay invariant across genders the way some borrowed nouns do.

Sense 2 : devoted follower or adherent (of an idea, ideology, or practice)
noun, masculine Takes का (kaa) to mark what is being devoted to, e.g. अहिंसा के उपासक (ahinsaa ke upaasak, 'devotees/adherents of non-violence').
Synonyms: अनुयायी (anuyaayii), समर्थक (samarthak, supporter, broader/weaker sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the metaphorical extension of the religious sense -- just as English speaks of a 'worshipper of truth' or 'devotee of art,' Hindi extends उपासक (upaasak) beyond gods to abstract ideals like सत्य (saty, truth) or अहिंसा (ahinsaa, non-violence), often in a somewhat elevated or rhetorical register.
