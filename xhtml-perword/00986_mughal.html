मुग़ल (mughal)

noun/adjective Refers to the Mughals, the Central Asian-descended dynasty that ruled much of the Indian subcontinent from the 1500s to the 1800s; used both as a noun for the people/dynasty and as an adjective for things associated with them (architecture, cuisine, style).
Sense 1 : the Mughal dynasty/people
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Delhi, you can hardly go a day without brushing up against the मुग़ल (mughal) legacy -- the Red Fort, Humayun's Tomb, and Jama Masjid are all their handiwork. Locals often speak of them with a mix of historical pride and complexity, since their rule is such a layered chapter in Indian history.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write मुगल (mugal) -- this is a common casual spelling but the ग़ (gh) with nuqta reflects the original Persian/Central Asian pronunciation and is preferred in careful writing. 2) मुग़ल (mughal) refers specifically to this one dynasty/ethnic group -- it is not a generic word for 'emperor' or 'ruler', which would be बादशाह (baadshaah) or सम्राट (samraat).

Sense 2 : of or relating to the Mughals (style, cuisine, architecture)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear most in daily life, especially around food -- मुग़लई (mughlaii) and मुग़ल (mughal) both show up on Delhi restaurant menus for rich, Mughal-court-inspired dishes like कोरमा (kormaa) and बिरयानी (biryaanii). Note the related word मुग़लई (mughlaii), a separate adjectival form specifically tied to cuisine and courtly style.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this adjectival use, the word usually stays invariant before a noun, e.g. मुग़ल खाना (mughal khaanaa, Mughal food) or मुग़ल शैली (mughal shailii, Mughal style) -- learners sometimes try to inflect it like a regular -आ (aa) adjective, which is incorrect here.
