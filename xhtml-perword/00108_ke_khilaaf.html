के ख़िलाफ़ (ke khilaaf)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'against' -- used for opposition, resistance, or being contrary to something, whether physically, legally, or in argument/opinion. Built from ख़िलाफ़ (khilaaf, an Arabic-origin word) plus the oblique postposition के (ke).
Sense 1 : against (opposition, contrary to)
postposition Takes the oblique form of the noun/pronoun it follows, like other compound postpositions ending in के: उसके ख़िलाफ़ (uske khilaaf, 'against him/her').
Synonyms: विरुद्ध (viruddh, formal/literary), विपरीत (vipriit, contrary to)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news reports about protests and court cases -- सरकार के ख़िलाफ़ (sarkaar ke khilaaf, 'against the government') or कानून के ख़िलाफ़ (kaanoon ke khilaaf, 'against the law') -- but it works just as easily in a family argument, like बच्चों के ख़िलाफ़ नहीं जाना चाहिए (bachchon ke khilaaf nahiin jaanaa chaahie, 'one shouldn't go against the children's wishes'). Its Arabic-via-Urdu origin (ख़िलाफ़, khilaaf) means it sits comfortably in both formal Hindi and everyday Hindustani speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop के (ke) and just say ख़िलाफ़ (khilaaf) alone, but the के is required with a noun/pronoun object -- 'मेरे ख़िलाफ़' (mere khilaaf, 'against me'), not just 'मैं ख़िलाफ़'. 2) It is easy to confuse with विरोध करना (virodh karnaa, 'to oppose/protest') -- ख़िलाफ़ (khilaaf) itself is not a verb, it needs होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa) with a helper verb to form a full predicate, as in 'के ख़िलाफ़ होना' (ke khilaaf honaa, 'to be against') or 'के ख़िलाफ़ बोलना' (ke khilaaf bolnaa, 'to speak against').

यह फ़ैसला कानून के ख़िलाफ़ है। This decision is against the law.
(yah faislaa kaanoon ke khilaaf hai।)
