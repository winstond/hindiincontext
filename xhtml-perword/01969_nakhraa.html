नखरा (nakhraa)

noun Mostly used in its plural form नखरे (nakhre) meaning fussy demands, affectation, or attitude; also has a more specific sense of coquettish/flirtatious airs.
Sense 1 : fussiness, attitude, demanding behavior
noun, masculine Almost always heard in the plural नखरे (nakhre) with verbs like करना (karnaa) or दिखाना (dikhaanaa) -- 'नखरे करना' (nakhre karnaa, 'to make a fuss/be fussy').
Synonyms: बहाना (bahaanaa, excuse-making, meaning overlaps but not identical), नाटक (naatak, drama, colloquial), तेवर (tevar, attitude/temper)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic word you'll hear from mothers, shopkeepers, and friends alike -- 'इतने नखरे मत करो' (itne nakhre mat karo, 'don't be so fussy') is a common scolding line when someone is being difficult about food, clothes, or plans. It carries a light, often affectionate or teasing edge rather than harsh criticism, especially between family members.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use the singular नखरा (nakhraa) where native speakers would default to the plural नखरे (nakhre), even when talking about a general trait rather than one specific incident. 2) This word is used with करना (karnaa) as a compound expression ('नखरे करना'), not as a direct object of a generic verb like 'होना' (honaa) -- learners sometimes try to say 'उसका नखरा है' when 'वह नखरे करता है' (vah nakhre kartaa hai, 'he is fussy/throws tantrums') is far more natural.

Sense 2 : coquettish airs, flirtatious mannerisms
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अदा (adaa, graceful/coquettish charm), चोंचला (chonchlaa, affected airs, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense in the context of classical dance, courtesan culture in old Bollywood films, or someone being playfully dramatic in a romantic sense -- 'नखरे दिखाना' (nakhre dikhaanaa) here suggests coy, theatrical charm rather than annoyance.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with the 'fussiness' sense in form (both usually plural, both with करना (karnaa) or दिखाना (dikhaanaa)), so context -- often romantic or performative -- is what signals this meaning rather than any grammatical marker.
