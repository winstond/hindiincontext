गढ़ (gadh)

noun/verb A noun meaning 'fort/stronghold' in both a literal military sense and a figurative sense (a place or field where someone/something dominates); also lives on as a verb root meaning 'to shape/fashion/mold' (and by extension, 'to fabricate/concoct').
Sense 1 : fort, fortress, stronghold
noun, masculine
Synonyms: किला (kilaa), दुर्ग (durg, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Look at any map of North India and you will spot dozens of towns ending in -गढ़ (gadh) -- अलीगढ़ (aliigadh), चंडीगढ़ (chandiigadh) -- a living trace of how many settlements grew up around a fort. The word carries the same rugged, defensive image as English 'fort,' but with much deeper roots in everyday place names than किला (kilaa) has.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for किला (kilaa) by default and forget गढ़ (gadh) is equally common, especially in historical place names like चित्तौड़गढ़ (chittaudgadh) or the many towns ending in -गढ़ (gadh).

Sense 2 : stronghold, bastion (figurative -- a domain of dominance)
noun, masculine Used with genitive/possessive constructions, e.g. भाजपा का गढ़ (bhaajpaa kaa gadh, 'BJP's stronghold') for a political base.
Synonyms: किला (kilaa, used figuratively too)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in election-season news: किसी पार्टी का गढ़ (kisii paartii kaa gadh) means a constituency or region where that party always wins, the way we'd say 'safe seat' or 'bastion' in English. It also works for non-political dominance, like कला का गढ़ (kalaa kaa gadh) for a city known as a center of art.

Sense 3 : to shape, mold, fashion (a physical object)
verb, transitive Typically used for shaping something out of raw material -- clay, metal, wood -- with the material taking a से (se)-marked role, e.g. मिट्टी से गढ़ना (mittii se gadhnaa, 'to mold from clay').
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa), ढालना (dhaalnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a कुम्हार (kumhaar, potter) working clay on his wheel -- that hands-on, deliberate shaping is exactly the image गढ़ना (gadhnaa) evokes, distinct from the more generic बनाना (banaanaa, 'to make').

Sense 4 : to fabricate, concoct, invent (a story, excuse, or lie)
verb, transitive Common object nouns are बहाना (bahaanaa, 'excuse'), कहानी (kahaanii, 'story'), or बात (baat, 'thing/tale') -- e.g. बहाना गढ़ना (bahaanaa gadhnaa, 'to concoct an excuse').
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa), झूठ बोलना (jhooth bolnaa, only for outright lies)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The link to the physical 'molding' sense is very much alive: just as a potter shapes raw clay into a pot, someone who गढ़ता है (gadhtaa hai) a story is shaping raw invention into something that sounds solid and convincing.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a figurative extension of the 'mold/shape' sense -- learners sometimes miss that गढ़ना (gadhnaa) applied to a story carries a distinct whiff of deliberate fabrication, stronger than just 'making up' casually.
