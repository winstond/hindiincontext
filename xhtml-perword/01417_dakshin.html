दक्षिण (dakshin)

noun/adjective Primarily the cardinal direction 'south' (dakshin); also functions as an adjective meaning 'southern' and appears in the compound name दक्षिण भारत (dakshin bhaarat, 'South India').
Sense 1 : the direction south
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday speech, especially in the north, दक्षिण (dakshin) often carries a specific cultural weight beyond just the compass point -- दक्षिण भारत (dakshin bhaarat) or just 'दक्षिण' (dakshin) colloquially conjures South India as a whole, its languages, food, and culture, similar to how 'the South' can mean a whole region to an American. The word is Sanskrit-derived and feels a touch more formal/written than the everyday spoken alternative साउथ (saauth, borrowed from English), which many Delhi speakers use casually instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use दक्षिण (dakshin) with की तरफ़ (kii taraf, 'towards') the same way as अंदर (andar) or बाहर (baahar) -- the natural phrasing is दक्षिण की ओर (dakshin kii or, 'towards the south') or दक्षिण में (dakshin men, 'in the south'), with ओर (or) sounding more natural than तरफ़ (taraf) in formal/geographic contexts, though both are heard.

Sense 2 : southern
adjective Commonly used invariantly before a noun in compounds like दक्षिण भारत (dakshin bhaarat, 'South India') or दक्षिण दिशा (dakshin dishaa, 'southern direction'), functioning much like an English noun-adjective.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this adjectival use constantly in news and geography contexts -- दक्षिण अफ्रीका (dakshin aphriikaa, 'South Africa'), दक्षिण एशिया (dakshin eshiyaa, 'South Asia') -- it is the standard formal way to build compass-direction place names, parallel to उत्तर (uttar, 'north'), पूर्व (poorv, 'east'), and पश्चिम (pashchim, 'west').
