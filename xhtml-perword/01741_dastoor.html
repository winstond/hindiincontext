दस्तूर (dastoor)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'custom' or 'practice' -- what is customarily done, whether in a family, region, or generally in life.
Sense 1 : custom, established practice
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रिवाज़ (rivaaz), रीति (riiti, formal), परंपरा (parampraa, tradition, broader in scope), चलन (chalan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear दस्तूर (dastoor) a lot in the context of shaadi-byaah (shaadii-byaah, weddings) and festival customs -- someone explaining a ritual might simply say यह हमारा दस्तूर है (yah hamaaraa dastoor hai, 'this is our custom'), closing the topic without needing further justification. It carries a gentle, accepting tone, as if customs don't need to be logical, only followed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for परंपरा (parampraa) when दस्तूर (dastoor) would sound more natural -- दस्तूर (dastoor) fits everyday practices (like who greets whom first, or how a family celebrates a festival) while परंपरा (parampraa) leans toward bigger, inherited traditions. 2) It commonly appears in the fixed phrase यही दस्तूर है (yahii dastoor hai, 'that's just how it's done') -- learners sometimes translate this too literally instead of recognizing it as a shrug-like explanation for why something is the way it is.

यह हमारा दस्तूर है। This is our custom.
(yah hamaaraa dastoor hai।)
