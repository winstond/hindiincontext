रुचि (ruchi)

noun A noun meaning 'interest' or 'liking' -- the mental pull toward something you enjoy or want to know more about; it also has a secondary sense of 'taste' in the sensory/culinary meaning.
Sense 1 : interest, inclination
noun, feminine Commonly used with होना (honaa) in the pattern X को Y में रुचि होना (X ko Y men ruchi honaa), 'X has an interest in Y' -- the person who is interested takes को (ko), not the subject marker a learner might expect.
Synonyms: दिलचस्पी (dilchaspii), उत्सुकता (utsuktaa, leans toward eagerness/curiosity), शौक (shauk, leans toward hobby-like enthusiasm)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word a lot on school report cards and job forms -- रुचियाँ (ruchiyaan, 'interests/hobbies') is the standard heading, similar to how English forms ask for 'hobbies and interests.' In relaxed conversation among friends, दिलचस्पी (dilchaspii) or शौक (shauk) usually feels more natural than रुचि (ruchi).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the interested person the subject with a normal verb agreement pattern, forgetting the को (ko)-marked construction: मुझे संगीत में रुचि है (mujhe sangiit men ruchi hai, 'I am interested in music'), not मैं रुचि हूँ। 2) रुचि (ruchi) is more bookish/measured than दिलचस्पी (dilchaspii), which is the everyday spoken choice -- using रुचि (ruchi) constantly in casual chat can sound a little formal.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे संगीत में बहुत रुचि है। I have a lot of interest in music.
(mujhe sangiit men bahut ruchi hai।)

Sense 2 : taste, liking (for food/sensory experience)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: स्वाद (svaad, more specifically flavor/taste of food), पसंद (pasand, everyday word for liking/preference)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shares its root with the 'interest' sense above -- both come from the idea of something 'appealing' or 'tasting good' to the mind or palate, so you'll sometimes see रुचि (ruchi) used for refined taste, like साहित्यिक रुचि (saahityik ruchi, 'literary taste'), bridging both senses.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with पसंद (pasand) but रुचि (ruchi) leans slightly more formal/reflective, e.g. describing someone's general palate or aesthetic taste (खाने की रुचि (khaane kii ruchi, 'taste in food')) rather than a specific momentary preference.
