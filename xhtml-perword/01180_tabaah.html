तबाह (tabaah)

adjective An invariant adjective meaning 'devastated, ruined' -- used for physical destruction (cities, homes, crops) and, very commonly, for emotional or financial ruin. Pairs constantly with the verb होना (honaa) and the compound तबाह करना (tabaah karnaa).
Sense 1 : devastated, ruined (physically)
adjective
Synonyms: बर्बाद (barbaad), नष्ट (nasht, more formal/literary), खत्म (khatm, broader, 'finished/over')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news reports about floods, earthquakes, or storms -- शहर तबाह हो गया (shahar tabaah ho gayaa, 'the city was devastated') -- but it is just as at home in everyday exaggeration, the way English speakers say 'wrecked' or 'destroyed' about a bad day.
Common Mistakes: 1) तबाह (tabaah) is invariant -- learners sometimes try to inflect it like a variable adjective (adding -ई or -े); it never changes form regardless of gender or number. 2) It is almost always used predicatively with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa), rarely as a plain attributive adjective before a noun in casual speech, though that use exists too (तबाह शहर (tabaah shahar, 'devastated city')).

Sense 2 : ruined, wrecked (emotionally, financially, or in life circumstances)
adjective
Synonyms: बर्बाद (barbaad), बदहाल (badahaal, more about a wretched state than the act of ruin)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you hear in filmi dialogue and heartbreak songs -- someone's ज़िंदगी (zindgii, 'life') or दिल (dil, 'heart') is 'तबाह' after a betrayal or loss. It carries real dramatic weight, so native speakers reach for it when 'upset' or 'sad' feels too weak.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for बर्बाद (barbaad) in emotional contexts and miss that तबाह (tabaah) is just as common and often stronger -- ज़िंदगी तबाह हो गई (zindgii tabaah ho gaii, 'life was ruined') is a very natural, dramatic thing to say.
