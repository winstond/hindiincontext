दर (dar)

noun A common noun meaning 'rate' -- used for prices, interest, speed, and similar measures. It also survives as a bound element in the fixed phrase दर-दर (dar-dar), where it means 'door', but that is a separate, much rarer usage.
Sense 1 : rate, price ratio
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भाव (bhaav), मूल्य (mooly, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in the news and in banks: ब्याज दर (byaaj dar, 'interest rate'), विकास दर (vikaas dar, 'growth rate'), विनिमय दर (vinimay dar, 'exchange rate'). It has a formal, almost bureaucratic flavor compared to the market-stall word भाव (bhaav) you would use haggling for vegetables.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse दर (dar) with दाम (daam) or भाव (bhaav); दर (dar) is used specifically for a fixed, often official or calculated ratio (interest rate, exchange rate, growth rate), while दाम (daam)/भाव (bhaav) refer to the everyday selling price of goods.

Sense 2 : door, threshold (in the fixed expression दर-दर)
noun, feminine Almost always seen in the doubled idiom दर-दर (dar-dar, 'door to door'), as in दर-दर भटकना (dar-dar bhataknaa, 'to wander from door to door, to be reduced to begging/searching desperately'). It is not used as a freestanding word for 'door' in ordinary speech.
Synonyms: दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa, everyday word for 'door')
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: दर-दर भटकना (dar-dar bhataknaa, 'to wander from door to door') paints a vivid image of someone going house to house in desperation, whether looking for work, justice, or shelter. It carries real pathos and shows up often in film dialogue and poetry about hardship.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not use दर (dar) alone to mean 'door' in a normal sentence like 'close the door' -- that is always दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa). This sense of दर (dar) survives only inside the fixed phrase दर-दर (dar-dar) and similar idioms.
