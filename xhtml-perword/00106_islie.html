इसलिए (islie)

conjunction A single-sense connector meaning 'so, therefore, for this reason' -- links a cause to its consequence. Literally built from इस (is, 'this') + लिए (lie, 'for'), so it means something like 'for this (reason)'.
Sense 1 : so, therefore, for this reason
conjunction Typically appears at the start of the consequence clause, often paired with a preceding क्योंकि (kyonki, 'because') clause: क्योंकि...इसलिए... (kyonki...isliye..., 'because...therefore...').
Synonyms: इस कारण (is kaaran, for this reason), अतः (atah, literary), लिहाज़ा (lihaazaa, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday explanations -- a mother explaining why dinner is late, a friend justifying why they're skipping a wedding. The क्योंकि...इसलिए (kyonki...islie) pairing is the default Hindi way of narrating cause and effect, much more common in speech than trying to cram both ideas into one English-style clause.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place इसलिए (islie) mid-sentence like English 'so' can be, but in Hindi it almost always opens the clause it introduces. 2) Confusing it with क्योंकि (kyonki): क्योंकि introduces the reason, इसलिए introduces the result -- they point in opposite directions and are often used together in the same sentence.
