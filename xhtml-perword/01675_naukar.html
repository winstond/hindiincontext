नौकर (naukar)

noun A single core sense: a servant or domestic employee. The related word नौकरी (naukrii, 'job/employment') shares the same root but means the job itself, not the person.
Sense 1 : servant, domestic help, employee
noun, masculine
Synonyms: नौकरानी (naukraanii, female servant, not interchangeable), सेवक (sevak, formal/literary), दास (daas, literary, 'slave/servant'), कर्मचारी (karmchaarii, employee, broader/more formal use for office staff)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In many Indian households, especially in cities like Delhi, having a नौकर (naukar) or domestic help for cooking, cleaning, or errands is common across the middle class, not just the wealthy -- so the word carries none of the rare, luxury connotation 'servant' might have in English. The related नौकरी (naukrii, 'job') is one of the most common words in daily conversation, as in नौकरी ढूँढना (naukrii dhoondhnaa, 'to look for a job'), and it is worth learning alongside नौकर (naukar) to keep the two straight.
Common Mistakes: 1) नौकर (naukar) refers to a male servant or is used generically for 'domestic help' in a household; for a female servant, नौकरानी (naukraanii) is the distinct word, not a feminine inflection of नौकर (naukar). 2) Learners sometimes use नौकर (naukar) loosely for any employee, but in everyday speech it specifically evokes household help (cook, cleaner, driver) rather than office colleagues, for whom कर्मचारी (karmchaarii) or स्टाफ़ (staaf) is more natural.
