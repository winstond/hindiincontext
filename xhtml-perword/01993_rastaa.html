रस्ता (rastaa)

noun A common variant spelling of रास्ता (raastaa, 'road/path/way'); both spellings are heard constantly in everyday speech, and the meanings and usage are identical to रास्ता (raastaa).
Sense 1 : road, path, street
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रास्ता (raastaa, variant form), मार्ग (maarg, formal), सड़क (sadak, specifically a paved road/street), पथ (path, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly when asking for directions in Delhi -- रस्ता कहाँ है? (rastaa kahaan hai, 'where is the way/road?') is everyday market talk. Note that रास्ता (raastaa) is the more common spelling in print and formal contexts, while रस्ता (rastaa) is just as natural in speech; both are used interchangeably by native speakers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for सड़क (sadak) when they mean a paved road, but रस्ता (rastaa) is broader and covers any path, route, or way, including footpaths and dirt tracks. 2) रस्ता दिखाना (rastaa dikhaanaa, 'to show the way') and रस्ता भूलना (rastaa bhoolnaa, 'to lose one's way') are set phrases worth learning as chunks rather than building word by word.
Sample Sentences:
यह रस्ता सीधा बाज़ार तक जाता है। This road goes straight to the market.
(yah rastaa siidhaa baazaar tak jaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : way, method, means (figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: उपाय (upaay), तरीका (tariikaa), मार्ग (maarg, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense mirrors English 'the way forward' -- कोई रस्ता नहीं है (koii rastaa nahiin hai, 'there's no way/option') is something you will hear said with a sigh in any family discussion about a tricky problem.
Common Mistakes: 1) रस्ता निकालना (rastaa nikaalnaa, 'to find a way out') is a common idiom for solving a problem -- learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'to take out a road' instead of hearing it as 'to work something out.'
Sample Sentences:
अब कोई रस्ता नहीं है, हमें यह फैसला लेना ही होगा। Now there's no way out, we'll have to make this decision.
