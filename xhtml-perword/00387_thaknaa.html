थकना (thaknaa)

verb A single-sense verb meaning 'to get tired, to become exhausted' -- describes the physical/mental state of fatigue setting in.
Sense 1 : to become tired, exhausted
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: थक जाना (thak jaanaa, common compound-verb form used in most everyday speech)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear थक गया (thak gayaa) constantly at the end of a long day, a workout, or a crowded Delhi metro ride -- it's the default way to announce 'I'm exhausted.' Note the adjective थका हुआ (thakaa huaa, 'tired') is built directly from this verb's perfective participle, so once you know थकना you can recognize that description everywhere.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the subject with ने (ne) by reflex since it is a past-tense verb, but थकना (thaknaa) is intransitive and never takes ने -- say मैं थक गया (main thak gayaa, 'I got tired'), not मैंने थका। 2) In natural speech this verb almost always appears with जाना (jaanaa) as थक जाना (thak jaanaa) to mark the completed onset of tiredness; the bare form थका (thakaa) alone can sound like an adjective (tired) rather than the event of becoming tired.
Sample Sentences:

(aaj main bahut thak gayaa hoon।)
