परेशान (pareshaan)

adjective/verb One core meaning: troubled, worried, or upset -- used constantly in daily conversation to describe emotional distress or being bothered/harassed by something or someone.
Sense 1 : worried, distressed, upset
adjective Commonly used with होना (honaa) to describe a state ('मैं परेशान हूँ' main pareshaan hoon, 'I am worried') or with करना (karnaa) to describe causing that state in someone else.
Synonyms: चिंतित (chintit), फ़िक्रमंद (fikrmand), व्याकुल (vyaakul, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of those words you'll hear constantly in Delhi households -- a mother might say 'मुझे बहुत परेशान कर दिया' (mujhe bahut pareshaan kar diyaa, 'you've really troubled/worried me') to a child who's been up to mischief, blending 'you worried me' and 'you're driving me crazy' into one phrase. It's an emotional Swiss-army word: mild irritation, deep worry, and being pestered can all be परेशान (pareshaan) depending on tone and situation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use चिंतित (chintit) as a direct swap in every context, but परेशान (pareshaan) is the far more common, everyday word -- चिंतित (chintit) sounds a touch more formal/written. 2) परेशान (pareshaan) covers both 'worried' and 'annoyed/bothered' -- context decides which; English speakers sometimes reach for a separate word for 'annoyed' when Hindi speakers would just say परेशान (pareshaan).

Sense 2 : to trouble, bother, harass (someone)
verb, transitive Used as परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa) -- a compound formed with करना (karnaa) that turns the adjective into a transitive action, 'to make [someone] troubled.'
Synonyms: तंग करना (tang karnaa), सताना (sataanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kids pestering a parent for a toy, a mosquito buzzing around at night, an annoying colleague who won't stop calling -- all of these get described with परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa). It is the everyday go-to for 'stop bothering me,' as in 'मुझे परेशान मत करो' (mujhe pareshaan mat karo, 'don't bother me').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to conjugate परेशान (pareshaan) itself as if it were a verb root; it always needs करना (karnaa) (to trouble someone) or होना (honaa) (to become troubled) attached to actually function as a verb.
