ईद (iid)

noun ईद (iid) refers to the Islamic festival of Eid, most commonly Eid al-Fitr marking the end of Ramadan fasting; it is a single-sense noun.
Sense 1 : Eid (the Islamic festival)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ईद-उल-फ़ितर (iid-ul-fitar, full/formal name for the post-Ramadan festival)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, ईद (iid) is a major civic event, not just a private religious one: you'll see markets like Jama Masjid's lane packed for सेवइयां (sevaiyaan, vermicelli dessert) shopping the night before, and neighbors of all faiths often exchange greetings and sweets. There's also a common colloquial saying, 'ईद का चाँद होना' (iid kaa chaand honaa, 'to be like the Eid moon') used for someone who is seen very rarely -- a nice window into how deeply the festival's imagery has entered everyday Hindi-Urdu speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume ईद (iid) always means the festival at the end of Ramadan; without a qualifier context usually makes clear which ईद (iid) is meant, but there is also ईद-उल-अज़्हा (iid-ul-azhaa), the 'other Eid' (Bakr-Id), so don't assume it always refers to the fast-breaking one. 2) The greeting is ईद मुबारक (iid mubaarak, 'happy Eid'), not a literal translation of 'happy Eid' word for word.
