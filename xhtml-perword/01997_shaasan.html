शासन (shaasan)

noun A formal, Sanskritic word for governing or rule -- covers both the abstract act of governance and the concrete apparatus (a government/administration/regime) that does it.
Sense 1 : governance, rule, administration (abstract)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शासन-व्यवस्था (shaasan-vyavasthaa, compound form), राज (raaj, everyday/informal), हुकूमत (hukoomat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see शासन (shaasan) constantly in newspapers and civics textbooks -- लोकतांत्रिक शासन (loktaantrik shaasan, 'democratic governance') is a stock phrase. It sits a notch above राज (raaj) in formality, the way 'governance' sits above 'rule' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for राज (raaj) in casual speech and शासन (shaasan) in writing/news, but the two overlap heavily -- शासन (shaasan) is not 'more correct,' just more formal-register. 2) Don't confuse with शासन करना (shaasan karnaa, 'to rule/govern'), which needs the light verb करना (karnaa) -- शासन (shaasan) alone is the noun, not a verb.

Sense 2 : a government, regime, or administration (as an entity/period)
noun, masculine Often marked with a possessor or time-frame, e.g. मुगल शासन (mughal shaasan, 'Mughal rule/regime') or किसी के शासन में (kisii ke shaasan men, 'under someone's regime').
Synonyms: सरकार (sarkaar), शासनकाल (shaasankaal, specifically 'reign/period of rule')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up heavily in history lessons -- मुगल शासन (mughal shaasan) and ब्रिटिश शासन (british shaasan) are practically fixed phrases every Indian schoolchild learns, referring to entire eras of rule rather than a single sitting government.
Common Mistakes: 1) सरकार (sarkaar) is the everyday word for 'the government' (as in, who's currently in power); शासन (shaasan) leans toward the broader regime or era of rule -- e.g. historians say ब्रिटिश शासन (british shaasan, 'British rule') rather than ब्रिटिश सरकार (british sarkaar) when talking about the colonial period as a whole.
