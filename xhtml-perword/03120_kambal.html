कंबल (kambal)

noun A simple, everyday noun meaning 'blanket' -- the thick woolen or cotton one you pull over yourself on a cold Delhi winter night. Only one common sense.
Sense 1 : blanket
noun, masculine
Synonyms: लिहाफ़ (lihaaf, a quilted/padded blanket), रजाई (rajaaii, a quilt, usually cotton-stuffed)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian winters, ओढ़ना (odhnaa, 'to wrap/cover oneself') is the verb that naturally pairs with कंबल (kambal) -- you don't just 'have' a blanket, you drape it over yourself. Thick, heavy कंबल (kambal) are still commonly distributed as charity to the poor during cold spells, so the word carries a mild association with winter relief drives, not just home comfort.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use कंबल (kambal) and रजाई (rajaaii) interchangeably, but they are physically different: कंबल (kambal) is a woven woolen blanket, while रजाई (rajaaii) is a stitched, stuffed quilt -- native speakers keep these distinct in daily speech.
