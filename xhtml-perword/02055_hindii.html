हिन्दी (hindii)

noun/adjective The name of the Hindi language itself; also used as an adjective meaning 'Hindi-related' or 'in Hindi.' Also spelled हिंदी (hindii) with the anusvaar instead of the chandrabindu-plus-halant, that spelling is in fact more common in print today.
Sense 1 : the Hindi language
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi you will constantly hear हिन्दी (hindii) and अंग्रेज़ी (angrezii) mentioned side by side, since so much daily speech mixes the two -- shopkeepers, teachers, and news anchors alike slide between them mid-sentence. Note also that हिन्दी (hindii) as a language name is grammatically feminine, which shows up when you modify it, as in शुद्ध हिन्दी (shuddh hindii, 'pure Hindi').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say मैं हिन्दी हूँ (main hindii hoon) trying to say 'I am Hindi' (nationality); that sentence actually claims you literally are the language. For nationality/identity, use भारतीय (bhaartiiy) or हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) instead. 2) The verb used with this word for 'to speak Hindi' is बोलना (bolnaa) -- हिन्दी बोलना (hindii bolnaa) -- not करना (karnaa).
Sample Sentences:

(main hindii boltaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : of or relating to Hindi
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this adjectival use everywhere in Delhi -- हिन्दी अख़बार (hindii akhbaar, 'Hindi newspaper'), हिन्दी फ़िल्म (hindii film, 'Hindi film'), हिन्दी माध्यम (hindii maadhyam, 'Hindi medium', referring to Hindi-medium schools as opposed to English-medium ones), a distinction that carries real social weight in how Indians talk about schooling and class.
