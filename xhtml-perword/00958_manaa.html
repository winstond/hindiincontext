मना (manaa)

verb/adjective मना (manaa) is mainly used in the fixed phrase मना करना (manaa karnaa, 'to refuse/forbid') and the state-phrase मना होना (manaa honaa, 'to be forbidden'); it functions as an invariant adjective/predicate word, not a standalone verb.
Sense 1 : to refuse, to say no, to forbid
verb, transitive Always occurs as the compound मना करना (manaa karnaa); मना (manaa) itself does not conjugate -- only करना (karnaa) carries tense/aspect/agreement.
Synonyms: इनकार करना (inkaar karnaa), अस्वीकार करना (asviikaar karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मना करना (manaa karnaa) is the everyday, polite way to decline something -- at a family dinner you might hear मना मत करो, थोड़ा और खा लो (manaa mat karo, thodaa aur khaa lo, 'don't refuse, eat a little more'), which captures the classic Indian-host insistence on hospitality.
Common Mistakes: 1) मना करना (manaa karnaa) can mean either 'to refuse (for oneself)' or 'to forbid (someone else)' depending on context -- मैंने मना कर दिया (mainne manaa kar diyaa, 'I refused') vs. उसने मुझे जाने से मना किया (usne mujhe jaane se manaa kiyaa, 'he forbade me from going'); the second pattern takes a से-marked verb (oblique infinitive + से) that learners often get wrong. 2) Because करना (karnaa) is transitive here, this sense takes ने (ne) marking in the perfective, which is easy to forget since मना (manaa) itself looks like it should be the main verb.

Sense 2 : forbidden, prohibited
adjective Used predicatively with होना (honaa) -- मना होना (manaa honaa, 'to be forbidden') -- and takes a को-marked person: उसे यहाँ आना मना है (use yahaan aanaa manaa hai, 'he is forbidden from coming here').
Synonyms: निषिद्ध (nishiddh, formal), वर्जित (varjit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see मना है (manaa hai) everywhere in Delhi -- on signs like धूम्रपान मना है (dhoomrpaan manaa hai, 'smoking is forbidden') or थूकना मना है (thooknaa manaa hai, 'spitting is forbidden'). It has a clipped, official, notice-board feel, distinct from the more personal मना करना (manaa karnaa) sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to inflect मना (manaa) like a regular आ-ending adjective (मनी, मने); it never changes form regardless of gender or number. 2) मना है (manaa hai) describes a general prohibition/rule (like a sign saying 'not allowed'), while मना करना (manaa karnaa) describes a person actively refusing or forbidding something -- learners sometimes use one where the other fits better.
