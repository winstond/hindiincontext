भरना (bharnaa)

verb A versatile verb centered on the idea of filling or becoming full: literally filling a container (transitive) or something filling up/becoming full on its own (intransitive), extending to paying dues or fees, and to time 'filling up' i.e. elapsing.
Sense 1 : to fill (something) with something
verb, transitive Takes से (se) for the filling material: गिलास पानी से भरना (gilaas paanii se bharnaa, 'to fill the glass with water').
Synonyms: भर देना (bhar denaa, compound-verb form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a mother telling a child पेट भर के खाओ (pet bhar ke khaao, 'eat until your stomach is full') -- भरना (bharnaa) is the verb behind that everyday image of filling up.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try को (ko) for the material instead of से (se); say पानी से भरना (paanii se bharnaa), not पानी को भरना (paanii ko bharnaa).
Sample Sentences:
माँ रोज़ मेरा टिफ़िन खाने से भरती है। Mom fills my tiffin with food every day.
(maan roz meraa tifin khaane se bhartii hai।)

Sense 2 : to become full, to fill up (on its own)
verb, intransitive The intransitive counterpart: the container or space itself is the subject, e.g. कमरा धुएँ से भर गया (kamraa dhuen se bhar gayaa, 'the room filled up with smoke').
Synonyms: भर जाना (bhar jaanaa, compound-verb form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Eyes welling up with tears is often described this way: आँखें भर आईं (aankhen bhar aaiin, 'her eyes welled up') -- the emotion is framed as something that happens to the eyes, not something the person actively does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because the transitive and intransitive forms look almost identical in the dictionary, learners sometimes force an object onto this sense; here the thing that fills is the grammatical subject, not an object.

Sense 3 : to pay (a fee, fine, tax, installment)
verb, transitive Common with फीस (phiis), जुर्माना (jurmaanaa), टैक्स (taiks), किस्त (kist) as objects.
Synonyms: जमा करना (jamaa karnaa, for deposits/fees), चुकाना (chukaanaa, for dues/debts)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around school admissions and utility bills: फीस भरना (phiis bharnaa, 'to pay the fee') or बिल भरना (bil bharnaa, 'to pay the bill') is the everyday phrase, more common in speech than the more formal चुकाना (chukaanaa).

Sense 4 : to fill out (a form)
verb, transitive Used with फॉर्म (phorm) or आवेदन (aavedan) as the object; a natural extension of the literal 'fill' sense.
Synonyms: भरना (bharnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: फॉर्म भरना (phorm bharnaa, 'to fill out a form') is the phrase every Indian student and job-seeker knows by heart, from exam applications to government paperwork.

Sense 5 : (of time) to elapse, to be up
verb, intransitive Time itself is the subject: समय भर गया (samay bhar gayaa, 'time is up'), मियाद भरना (miyaad bharnaa, 'for a term/sentence to be up').
Synonyms: गुजरना (gujarnaa, of time passing), पूरा होना (pooraa honaa, for a term/period being complete)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in the context of a prison sentence or a fixed term: उसकी सज़ा की मियाद भर गई (uskii sazaa kii miyaad bhar gaii, 'his sentence term is up') -- time is imagined as a vessel that gradually fills.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar only with the 'fill container' sense are surprised to see this used with abstract time nouns like समय (samay) or मियाद (miyaad) with no container in sight.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी सज़ा की मियाद भर गई His sentence term is up.
