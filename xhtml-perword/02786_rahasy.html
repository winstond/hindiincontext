रहस्य (rahasy)

noun A single core meaning: 'mystery/secret,' used both for a puzzling thing that lacks explanation and for information deliberately kept hidden.
Sense 1 : mystery, enigma (something unexplained)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गुत्थी (gutthii, a puzzle/knotty problem), पहेली (pahelii, riddle-like mystery)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Detective novels and TV shows love the phrase रहस्य और रोमांच (rahasy aur romaanch, 'mystery and thrill') as a genre label, roughly Hindi's version of 'mystery and suspense.' You will also hear ब्रह्मांड का रहस्य (brahmaand kaa rahasy, 'the mystery of the universe') in a more philosophical register.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पहेली (pahelii) when they mean a genuine unsolved mystery -- पहेली leans toward a riddle or brain-teaser, while रहस्य (rahasy) suits deeper, often unsettling mysteries (a murder, the universe, a person's past). 2) रहस्य (rahasy) is masculine, so watch agreement: यह रहस्य है (yah rahasy hai, 'this is a mystery'), not feminine agreement.

Sense 2 : secret (deliberately withheld information)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राज़ (raaz, everyday word for a personal secret), भेद (bhed, secret/distinction, slightly literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of रहस्य (rahasy) as the word for a secret with some weight or gravity to it -- a family's hidden past, a locked room, a conspiracy -- whereas राज़ (raaz) covers the small, personal secrets of daily life, like a crush or a surprise gift.
Common Mistakes: 1) In casual conversation about personal secrets ('don't tell anyone my secret'), native speakers overwhelmingly reach for राज़ (raaz) rather than रहस्य (rahasy); रहस्य sounds a notch more formal or dramatic, fitting a 'deep dark secret' more than everyday gossip. 2) The common verb pairing is राज़ खोलना (raaz kholnaa, 'to reveal a secret') more often than रहस्य खोलना (rahasy kholnaa), though both are understood.
