ग्रामीण (graamiin)

adjective/noun A word built on ग्राम (graam, 'village') that covers both the adjective 'rural/village' and the noun 'villager' -- the same word shifts role depending on whether it modifies a noun or stands alone.
Sense 1 : rural, village (as a descriptor)
adjective
Synonyms: देहाती (dehaatii, colloquial), गँवई (ganvaii, colloquial, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in news and government contexts -- ग्रामीण बैंक (graamiin baink, 'rural bank'), ग्रामीण क्षेत्र (graamiin kshetr, 'rural area') -- it is the standard bureaucratic and journalistic term for 'rural,' paired against शहरी (shahrii, 'urban').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for देहाती (dehaatii) in formal writing or news contexts where ग्रामीण (graamiin) is the expected word -- देहाती (dehaatii) carries a more casual, sometimes slightly mocking, village-bumpkin flavor, while ग्रामीण (graamiin) is neutral and even official-sounding, as in ग्रामीण विकास (graamiin vikaas, 'rural development').

Sense 2 : villager, rural person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: देहाती (dehaatii, colloquial), गाँव वाला (gaanv vaalaa, everyday phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Used as a plain noun in sentences like ग्रामीणों को सुविधा मिलेगी (graamiinon ko suvidhaa milegii, 'villagers will get the facility') -- notice the oblique plural ग्रामीणों (graamiinon) that shows up whenever this word takes a postposition like को (ko).
