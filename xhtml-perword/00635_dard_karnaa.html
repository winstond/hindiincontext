दर्द करना (dard karnaa)

verb A common conjunct-verb pattern meaning 'to hurt/ache' -- built from दर्द (dard, 'pain') plus करना (karnaa), but used intransitively/impersonally with a को-marked experiencer rather than as a normal transitive 'do' construction.
Sense 1 : to hurt, to ache (a body part)
verb, intransitive Used with a को-marked experiencer and the body part as subject: मुझे सिर दर्द कर रहा है (mujhe sir dard kar rahaa hai, 'my head is hurting'). Despite the verb करना (karnaa), the construction behaves impersonally -- the pain is the grammatical subject, not something the person 'does'.
Synonyms: दर्द होना (dard honaa, more common alternative), दुखना (dukhnaa, single-word verb for body parts aching)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech you will hear both पेट दर्द कर रहा है (pet dard kar rahaa hai, 'my stomach is aching') and पेट में दर्द हो रहा है (pet men dard ho rahaa hai) used almost interchangeably by the same speaker in the same conversation -- Hindi is comfortable letting pain be described either as an action the body part performs or a state that simply occurs to you. This fits the broader pattern where physical sensations (nींद आना niind aanaa for sleep, भूख लगना bhookh lagnaa for hunger) are framed as things that happen to you rather than things you do.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं दर्द कर रहा हूँ (main dard kar rahaa hoon) treating 'I' as the subject who 'does' the pain -- instead the body part or दर्द (dard) itself is the subject, and the person takes को (ko): मुझे पैर में दर्द कर रहा है (mujhe pair men dard kar rahaa hai). 2) In casual speech, दर्द होना (dard honaa) is heard at least as often as दर्द करना (dard karnaa) for the same meaning, and learners sometimes assume only one is correct.
