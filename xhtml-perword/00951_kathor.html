कठोर (kathor)

adjective An adjective covering both physical hardness and, more commonly in everyday talk, hardness of character or manner -- strict, harsh, or unyielding.
Sense 1 : harsh, strict, unyielding (of a person, tone, or rule)
adjective
Synonyms: सख्त (sakht), कड़ा (kadaa), निर्दयी (nirdyii, focuses on cruelty rather than strictness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian parents and teachers are often described (affectionately or not) as कठोर (kathor) when strict about discipline -- 'कठोर अनुशासन' (kathor anushaasan, 'strict discipline') is a very common phrase in the context of schooling and upbringing. Note the near-homophone कठिन (kathin, 'difficult') -- the two are unrelated in meaning despite the similar sound and spelling, and learners frequently confuse them.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सख्त (sakht) by default since it is more common in casual speech -- कठोर (kathor) sounds slightly more formal or literary and is common in writing, lectures, and serious scoldings. 2) कठोर (kathor) describes a person's nature, words, or discipline (कठोर स्वभाव (kathor svabhaav, 'harsh temperament'), कठोर शब्द (kathor shabd, 'harsh words')) -- it is not used for physical difficulty of a task, where you would say कठिन (kathin) instead.
Sample Sentences:

(mere pitaajii bahut kathor aadmii hain।)

Sense 2 : physically hard, tough, rigid (of a material or surface)
adjective
Synonyms: सख्त (sakht), कड़ा (kadaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shares its root with the personality sense (rank 1) -- Hindi, like English, extends 'hardness' from physical objects to character, so a कठोर व्यक्ति (kathor vyakti, 'hard/harsh person') is imagined as someone with an unyielding, rock-like nature.
Common Mistakes: 1) For everyday hard objects (hard bread, a hard mattress, hard water), native speakers overwhelmingly prefer सख्त (sakht) or कड़ा (kadaa) -- कठोर (kathor) in this physical sense leans literary/technical (e.g. कठोर चट्टान (kathor chattaan, 'hard rock') in geography) and can sound a bit bookish in casual conversation.
