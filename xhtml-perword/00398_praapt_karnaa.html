प्राप्त करना (praapt karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to obtain, receive, achieve' -- built from the Sanskritic adjective प्राप्त (praapt, 'obtained/attained') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'). One core sense, but it spans registers from formal paperwork to lofty achievement.
Sense 1 : to obtain, receive (something tangible or official)
verb, transitive Takes a direct object marking the thing obtained; the person is the subject and takes ने (ne) in the perfective, since करना (karnaa) is transitive.
Synonyms: पाना (paanaa), मिलना (milnaa, intransitive, dative-subject construction), हासिल करना (haasil karnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you see on government forms, certificates, and news reports -- 'डिग्री प्राप्त की' (digrii praapt kii, 'obtained the degree') sounds like an official announcement, whereas 'मुझे डिग्री मिली' (mujhe digrii milii, 'I got the degree') is what you'd actually say to your family over dinner. Knowing when to reach for the formal register versus मिलना (milnaa)/पाना (paanaa) is itself a marker of fluency.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this when मिलना (milnaa) or पाना (paanaa) would sound far more natural in casual speech -- प्राप्त करना (praapt karnaa) is noticeably more formal/written, closer to 'to obtain' than everyday 'to get'. 2) Because the subject actively 'does' the obtaining (करना), it takes ने (ne) in the past -- unlike मिलना (milnaa), where the recipient is marked with को (ko) and the thing obtained is the grammatical subject.

Sense 2 : to achieve, attain (a goal, status, or abstract result)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: हासिल करना (haasil karnaa), अर्जित करना (arjit karnaa, formal, often for merit/earnings)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in speeches and news headlines: 'भारत ने आज़ादी प्राप्त की' (bhaarat ne aazaadii praapt kii, 'India attained independence') has a ceremonial weight that a plain verb like पाना (paanaa) would not carry in the same sentence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this for small everyday achievements (like winning a game with friends), where जीतना (jiitnaa) or पाना (paanaa) fits better -- this sense is reserved for weightier, often abstract accomplishments like सफलता (saphaltaa, success) or सम्मान (sammaan, honor).
