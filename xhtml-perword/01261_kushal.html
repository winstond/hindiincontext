कुशल (kushal)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning skilled or competent; also used as a noun meaning welfare or well-being, mostly seen in the set phrase कुशल-मंगल (kushal-mangal).
Sense 1 : skilled, competent, expert
adjective
Synonyms: निपुण (nipun), माहिर (maahir), दक्ष (daksh, somewhat formal), कुशाग्र (kushaagr, rare, more about sharp intellect)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear कुशल (kushal) applied to all kinds of practical expertise -- कुशल कारीगर (kushal kaariigar, 'a skilled craftsman'), कुशल डॉक्टर (kushal doktar, 'a competent doctor') -- it has a slightly formal, respectful ring, the kind of word you'd use in a recommendation or a news report rather than casual chat about a friend being good at something.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat कुशल (kushal) as if it inflects like an ordinary -आ adjective; it does not change form for gender or number, so कुशल औरत (kushal aurat, 'a skilled woman') and कुशल आदमी (kushal aadmii, 'a skilled man') both use the same form. 2) कुशल (kushal) is used for practical skill or expertise (a craftsman, a driver, a doctor), not for being merely clever or witty, which would call for a different word.

Sense 2 : welfare, well-being (as in inquiring after someone)
noun, masculine Almost always appears in the compound कुशल-मंगल (kushal-mangal, 'welfare and well-being'), especially in the greeting-style question कुशल-मंगल पूछना (kushal-mangal poochhnaa, 'to ask after someone's welfare').
Synonyms: मंगल (mangal), भलाई (bhalaaii), खुशहाली (khushahaalii)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense survives mainly in the phrase कुशल-मंगल पूछना (kushal-mangal poochhnaa), the kind of thing an elder relative does when you visit -- asking not just 'how are you' but genuinely checking that all is well with your health, family, and affairs. It has a warm, old-fashioned courtesy to it, often heard from grandparents or in more traditional letters and phone calls.
