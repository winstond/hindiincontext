लकीर (lakiir)

noun A physical line, mark, or streak; extends figuratively into idioms about rigid habit and tradition (see the 'लकीर का फकीर' sense).
Sense 1 : a line, streak, or mark drawn or naturally occurring on a surface
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रेखा (rekhaa, formal), निशान (nishaan, mark/streak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a crack in a wall, a scratch on a table, or the lines on your palm -- लकीर (lakiir) is the everyday, tactile word for these, while रेखा (rekhaa) leans more textbook or geometric. Palmists and fortune-tellers at Delhi's roadside stalls will talk about हाथ की लकीरें (haath kii lakiiren, the lines of the hand) reading your fate.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for रेखा (rekhaa) by default because it is the textbook/geometry word; in everyday speech about a scratch, crease, or hand-drawn line, लकीर (lakiir) is far more natural. 2) हाथ की लकीरें (haath kii lakiiren, the lines of the palm) is the standard way to talk about palm-reading lines -- रेखा (rekhaa) also works here but लकीर (lakiir) is the colloquial choice.

Sense 2 : a fixed, unquestioned path or tradition; rigid convention (mainly in the idiom लकीर का फकीर)
noun, feminine Lives almost entirely in the fixed idiom लकीर का फकीर (lakiir kaa phakiir, 'a beggar/ascetic of the line') describing someone who blindly follows old ways without thinking for themselves.
Synonyms: रवायत (ravaayat, tradition, broader meaning), परंपरा (parampraa, tradition, broader meaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: लकीर का फकीर (lakiir kaa phakiir) literally paints a picture of a फकीर (phakiir, mendicant/ascetic) who never strays from a line drawn in the sand -- it is what you call someone (often affectionately, sometimes critically) who insists on doing things exactly as they have always been done, refusing new methods even when they make more sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is not used freely outside the idiom लकीर का फकीर (lakiir kaa phakiir) -- learners should not try to use लकीर (lakiir) alone to mean 'tradition' or 'rut' the way they might use रवायत (ravaayat) or परंपरा (parampraa).
