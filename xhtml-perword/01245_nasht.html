नष्ट (nasht)

adjective A learned adjective meaning 'destroyed' or 'ruined,' almost always used with the light verb करना (karnaa) or होना (honaa) to form compound expressions ('to destroy' / 'to be destroyed'); it rarely stands alone as a simple descriptive adjective.
Sense 1 : destroyed, ruined, wrecked
adjective Almost always occurs paired with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa) rather than as a standalone attributive adjective before a noun.
Synonyms: बर्बाद (barbaad), तबाह (tabaah), खत्म (khatm, informal), विनष्ट (vinasht, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word constantly in the news and in school textbooks -- फसल नष्ट हो गई (phasal nasht ho gaii, 'the crop was destroyed') is a staple headline during floods or droughts in North India. In casual chat about a ruined day or a broken heart, most Delhi speakers reach for बर्बाद (barbaad) instead, keeping नष्ट (nasht) for a slightly more clinical or official register.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use नष्ट (nasht) alone as a verb; it needs करना (karnaa) (transitive: 'to destroy') or होना (honaa) (intransitive: 'to be destroyed/get destroyed') attached to actually function as an action in a sentence. 2) Confusing नष्ट होना (nasht honaa) with बर्बाद होना (barbaad honaa) -- both work for 'to be ruined,' but नष्ट (nasht) leans more formal/written (used for crops, records, ecosystems, evidence) while बर्बाद (barbaad) is the everyday spoken choice, especially for ruined lives, plans, or relationships.

(baadh men hazaaron ekad phasal nasht ho gaii।)
