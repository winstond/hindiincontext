चीज़ (chiiz)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'thing/object' in the concrete sense, and also used figuratively to mean 'a remarkable person or thing.'
Sense 1 : thing, object, item
noun, feminine
Synonyms: वस्तु (vastu, formal), सामान (saamaan, goods/belongings, broader scope)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first words learners reach for when they don't know the specific noun -- much like English 'thing' or 'stuff.' You'll hear it constantly in markets and kitchens: यह चीज़ कितने की है? (yah chiiz kitne kii hai, 'how much is this thing?') is a classic shopping line.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget the nuqta and write/say चीज (chiij) -- both spellings are heard, but चीज़ (chiiz) with the ज़ sound is standard. 2) चीज़ (chiiz) is used for any countable or uncountable item in casual speech (food, objects, even abstract 'stuff'), so it is much broader than English 'thing' feels at first -- for example, खाने की चीज़ (khaane kii chiiz, 'something to eat') is a very natural phrase.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : a remarkable or striking person/thing (figurative)
noun, feminine Used predicatively, often with बड़ी (badii) or कोई (koii) -- कोई चीज़ है (koii chiiz hai, 'is quite something') -- to comment admiringly or teasingly on a person or situation.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the flavor you hear in Bollywood banter and playful teasing among friends -- चीज़ (chiiz) shifts from a neutral noun to something almost like slangy praise (or mock-praise) for a person's personality or looks, similar to calling someone 'a piece of work' in English, though the Hindi version leans more admiring than critical.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only works in an evaluative frame like तुम बड़ी चीज़ हो (tum badii chiiz ho, 'you're quite something') -- using चीज़ (chiiz) plainly to describe a person without this frame just sounds like calling them an 'object,' which can land oddly or rudely.
Sample Sentences:
