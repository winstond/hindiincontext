देखभाल (dekhbhaal)

noun A compound noun literally combining 'see' (dekh) and 'catch/hold' (bhaal from bhaalnaa), meaning care or looking after. It has one core meaning used broadly across health, household, and relationships.
Sense 1 : care, looking after, upkeep
noun, feminine
Synonyms: देख-रेख (dekh-rekh), निगरानी (nigraanii, more about supervision/monitoring), संभाल (sambhaal, handling/upkeep)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly around family life in India, where looking after elders, children, and even houseplants is a shared duty, not a hired-out task: माँ-बाप की देखभाल (maan-baap kii dekhbhaal, 'care of one's parents') carries strong cultural weight, tied to ideas of duty and gratitude. It also shows up in more clinical contexts, like स्वास्थ्य देखभाल (svaasthy dekhbhaal, 'healthcare'), so it stretches comfortably from the tender to the bureaucratic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use देखभाल (dekhbhaal) as a verb directly; it needs a helper verb, usually करना (karnaa) -- देखभाल करना (dekhbhaal karnaa, 'to take care of') -- and the person or thing cared for is marked with की (kii), not को (ko): बच्चे की देखभाल करना (bachche kii dekhbhaal karnaa, 'to take care of the child'). 2) Because देखभाल करना (dekhbhaal karnaa) is transitive, remember ने (ne) marking in the past: उसने देखभाल की (usne dekhbhaal kii, 'she took care [of it]') -- note the verb agrees with the feminine देखभाल (dekhbhaal), not with the subject.
