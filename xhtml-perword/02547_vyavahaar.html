व्यवहार (vyavahaar)

noun Covers behavior/conduct toward others, and by extension practical/routine use or dealing (business or otherwise). Both senses share the idea of 'how something is handled in practice.'
Sense 1 : behavior, conduct, manner of treating people
noun, masculine Often paired with अच्छा (achchhaa) or बुरा (buraa) to describe how someone treats others, and takes के साथ (ke saath) to mark the person on the receiving end, e.g. 'X के साथ व्यवहार' (X ke saath vyavahaar, 'behavior toward X').
Synonyms: बर्ताव (bartaav), रवैया (ravaiyaa), आचरण (aachran, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi households and schools alike, अच्छा व्यवहार (achchhaa vyavahaar, 'good behavior/manners') is a phrase you will hear constantly -- elders praising or scolding children with it. It carries a slightly more formal, character-judging weight than the everyday बर्ताव (bartaav), which describes the same thing but sounds more casual, more 'in the moment.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बात (baat) or बातचीत (baatchiit) when they mean 'how someone treats you' -- व्यवहार (vyavahaar) is the right word for conduct/treatment, not for the content of speech. 2) The person toward whom the behavior is directed is marked with के साथ (ke saath), not को (ko) -- 'उसके साथ अच्छा व्यवहार करो' (uske saath achchhaa vyavahaar karo, 'behave well toward him'), not उसको (usko).
Sample Sentences:
उसका व्यवहार बहुत अच्छा है। His behavior is very good.
(uskaa vyavahaar bahut achchhaa hai।)

Sense 2 : practical use, dealing, or transaction (including business/financial dealings)
noun, masculine Common in the fixed pattern व्यवहार में लाना (vyavahaar men laanaa, 'to put into practice/use') and व्यवहार में होना (vyavahaar men honaa, 'to be in use'). Also used for financial or commercial dealings between people or firms.
Synonyms: इस्तेमाल (istemaal, for practical use), लेन-देन (len-den, for transactions/dealings)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense in slightly more formal or written contexts -- a shopkeeper describing his लेन-देन (len-den) with a supplier might just as easily say व्यवहार (vyavahaar), especially in more traditional or Hindi-medium business circles.
Common Mistakes: 1) In the 'business dealing' sense, learners often default to बिज़नेस (biznes) or काम (kaam) -- व्यवहार (vyavahaar) specifically implies an ongoing relationship of dealing/exchange with someone, not just 'work.' 2) Don't confuse this sense with the 'behavior' sense just because the word is identical -- context (money/practice vs. people/manners) tells you which is meant.
Sample Sentences:
हमारा उनसे पुराना व्यवहार है। We have an old business relationship with them.
(hamaaraa unse puraanaa vyavahaar hai।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
इस दुकान का व्यवहार नकद में ही होता है। This shop's dealings are conducted only in cash.
(is dukaan kaa vyavahaar nakad men hii hotaa hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
उन्होंने कई सालों तक इस कंपनी के साथ व्यवहार किया। He conducted business with this company for many years.
(unhonne kaii saalon tak is kampnii ke saath vyavahaar kiyaa।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
कृपया सभी व्यवहार लिखित रूप में किए जाएँ। Please let all transactions be conducted in writing.

व्यापारी अपने व्यवहार का हिसाब बही में रखता था। The merchant used to keep an account of his transactions in a ledger.
(vyaapaarii apne vyavahaar kaa hisaab bahii men rakhtaa thaa।)

क्या आपका इस फर्म से कोई व्यवहार रहा है? Have you had any dealings with this firm?

दोनों पक्षों के बीच व्यवहार अनुबंध के अनुसार होगा। The dealings between the two parties will be according to the contract.
