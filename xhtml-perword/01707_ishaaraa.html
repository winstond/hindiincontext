इशारा (ishaaraa)

noun A noun meaning a gesture, hint, or signal -- covers both physical signaling (a wave, a nod) and verbal/situational hinting. Used heavily in the idiom इशारा करना (ishaaraa karnaa, 'to signal/hint').
Sense 1 : physical gesture or signal
noun, masculine
Synonyms: संकेत (sanket), इंगित (ingit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a crowded Delhi bazaar -- a rickshaw-wallah doesn't shout, he just gives an इशारा (ishaaraa) with his hand to say 'move' or 'wait.' The word carries that flavor of quick, wordless communication that's second nature in daily Indian life.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for संकेत (sanket) by default from textbooks, but इशारा (ishaaraa) is the word people actually reach for in speech, especially with हाथ का इशारा (haath kaa ishaaraa, 'a hand gesture') or आँख का इशारा (aankh kaa ishaaraa, 'a wink/glance signal').

Sense 2 : hint, indirect suggestion
noun, masculine
Synonyms: संकेत (sanket), आशय (aashay, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers often prize इशारा (ishaaraa) over blunt speech, especially with elders or in-laws -- dropping a hint rather than stating something outright is considered polite. If someone says कुछ इशारा तो दो (kuchh ishaaraa to do, 'at least give me a hint'), they're asking you to ease into a topic rather than blurt it out.
Common Mistakes: 1) इशारे में बात करना (ishaare men baat karnaa, 'to speak in hints') is a common set phrase for someone being deliberately indirect, e.g. avoiding a topic in front of elders or in a delicate conversation -- learners tend to translate this too literally instead of recognizing it as an idiom for tact/indirectness.
