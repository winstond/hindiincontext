उपाधि (upaadhi)

noun A formal-register word meaning a title, degree, or honorific designation -- covers academic degrees, official titles, and honorary epithets. Mostly encountered in written/institutional contexts rather than casual chat.
Sense 1 : academic degree
noun, feminine
Synonyms: डिग्री (digrii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word most on a convocation stage or in a news headline: 'विश्वविद्यालय ने उन्हें डॉक्टरेट की उपाधि प्रदान की' (vishvvidyaalay ne unhen doktret kii upaadhi pradaan kii, 'the university conferred a doctorate degree on him'). In daily conversation about one's own studies, people almost always say डिग्री (digrii) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to डिग्री (digrii) for 'degree' since it's the everyday loanword; उपाधि (upaadhi) is the formal Hindi equivalent seen on certificates, in university announcements, and in news reports, not in casual speech. 2) Doesn't take ने (ne) itself, but is commonly the object of दी जाना (dii jaanaa, 'to be conferred') or प्रदान करना (pradaan karnaa, 'to confer'), which learners sometimes render with a plainer verb like देना (denaa) -- not wrong, just less formal-sounding.

(vishvvidyaalay ne unhen doktret kii upaadhi pradaan kii।)

Sense 2 : honorary title or epithet
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पदवी (padvii, less common), खिताब (khitaab, often for awards/honors)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Think of old titles like 'महात्मा' (mahaatmaa, 'great soul') or 'राष्ट्रपिता' (raashtrpitaa, 'father of the nation') given to Gandhi -- these are called उपाधियाँ (upaadhiyaan, 'titles', plural) in textbooks and historical writing. It carries a slightly formal, almost textbook flavor compared to more everyday words.
Common Mistakes: 1) खिताब (khitaab) is the go-to word for a sports or competition 'title' (like a championship); उपाधि (upaadhi) leans toward formal, often historical or honorary titles (e.g. royal or literary epithets) and academic/official designations -- mixing the two sounds odd to a native ear.
