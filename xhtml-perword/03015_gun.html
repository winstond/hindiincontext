गुण (gun)

noun A word with two related everyday senses: a person's inherent quality/virtue (character trait), and a property/attribute of a thing; it also appears in the common idiom गुण-दोष (gun-dosh, 'pros and cons').
Sense 1 : virtue, good quality (of character)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खूबी (khoobii), विशेषता (visheshtaa), सद्गुण (sadgun, formal, emphasizes 'good' quality specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian parents and teachers love talking about a child's गुण (gun) and दोष (dosh) together, as if character is a ledger to be balanced -- you'll often hear किसी में गुण भी हैं और दोष भी (kisii men gun bhii hain aur dosh bhii, 'a person has both good and bad qualities') in everyday moral reasoning, not just in formal essays.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use गुण (gun) where अच्छाई (achchhaaii) or खूबी (khoobii) would sound more natural in casual speech -- गुण (gun) leans slightly more formal/reflective, as in describing someone's character in a considered way rather than just chatting about what's nice about them. 2) The opposite pairing is दोष (dosh, 'fault/flaw'), not किसी और शब्द से -- learners should learn गुण-दोष (gun-dosh) as a set pair.

Sense 2 : property, attribute, characteristic (of a thing, substance, or concept)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: विशेषता (visheshtaa), खासियत (khaasiyat, colloquial), लक्षण (lakshan, leans toward 'symptom/trait' in medical or descriptive contexts)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense constantly around Indian kitchens and in Ayurvedic talk -- अदरक के गुण (adrak ke gun, 'the properties of ginger') is the kind of phrase a grandmother or a health show host uses in the same breath, treating food as something with an inherent nature to be respected, not just a flavor.
Common Mistakes: 1) In science or health contexts (e.g. herbs, foods, medicines), गुण (gun) is the standard word for 'medicinal property' or 'beneficial effect' -- learners coming from English often reach for असर (asar, 'effect') instead, which is not wrong but shifts the emphasis from an inherent property to a resulting effect.
