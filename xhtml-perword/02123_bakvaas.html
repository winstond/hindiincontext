बकवास (bakvaas)

noun/adjective One core idea, 'nonsense/rubbish talk,' used as a noun ('nonsense'), an adjective ('nonsensical, worthless'), and as part of a very common light-verb phrase बकवास करना (bakvaas karnaa, 'to talk nonsense').
Sense 1 : nonsense, rubbish talk
noun, feminine Very frequently used with करना (karnaa) -- बकवास करना (bakvaas karnaa, 'to talk nonsense') -- as a light-verb construction rather than alone as a bare object.
Synonyms: फ़ालतू बातें (faaltoo baaten), गप (gap, colloquial), बेकार की बातें (bekaar kii baaten)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बकवास (bakvaas) is one of those wonderfully punchy words that Delhi folks use constantly in daily arguments -- friends interrupt each other with बकवास बंद कर! (bakvaas band kar, 'stop talking nonsense!') far more casually than English speakers would say the equivalent, so it lands as blunt but not necessarily deeply offensive among close friends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say केवल बकवास (keval bakvaas) as a standalone verb-like word; remember it needs करना (karnaa) or a form of होना (honaa) to function as a full sentence, e.g. यह बकवास है (yah bakvaas hai, 'this is nonsense'). 2) Used loosely, it can sound rude or dismissive -- in polite company, फ़ालतू बातें (faaltoo baaten) is a softer alternative.

Sense 2 : nonsensical, worthless, rubbish (describing a thing)
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. बकवास फ़िल्म (bakvaas film, 'a rubbish film'), or predicatively with है (hai).
Synonyms: बेकार (bekaar), फ़ालतू (faaltoo)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This adjectival use is extremely common in casual reviews and opinions -- यह किताब बिल्कुल बकवास है (yah kitaab bilkul bakvaas hai, 'this book is total rubbish') is the kind of blunt verdict you'll hear friends give about movies, food, or plans gone wrong.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like a regular -आ adjective (बकवासा, बकवासे); it never changes form regardless of the noun's gender or number.
