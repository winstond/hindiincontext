दौरा (dauraa)

noun Two main senses: a tour/official visit or circuit (दौरे पर जाना (daure par jaanaa, to go on a tour)), and a medical attack or seizure/spell (दिल का दौरा (dil kaa dauraa, heart attack)).
Sense 1 : an official tour, visit, or circuit
noun, masculine
Synonyms: यात्रा (yaatraa, broader trip/journey), भ्रमण (bhraman, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this word constantly in Indian news: नेता का दौरा (netaa kaa dauraa, a politician's tour) of a flood-hit area, or a foreign दौरा (dauraa) by a cricket team -- it carries a slightly official, purposeful flavor rather than a relaxed vacation feel.
Common Mistakes: 1) दौरा (dauraa) implies an official, work-related, or inspection-style visit (a minister's दौरा (dauraa), a doctor's rounds), not a casual personal trip -- for a leisure trip, यात्रा (yaatraa) or सैर (sair) fits better. 2) The common verb frame is दौरा करना (dauraa karnaa, to tour/visit) or दौरे पर जाना (daure par jaanaa, to go on tour) -- learners sometimes force दौरा जाना (dauraa jaanaa) which sounds off.

Sense 2 : a sudden attack, seizure, or spell (medical or emotional)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हमला (hamlaa, more general 'attack', also used for assault)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear in a real emergency -- family members shouting उसे दौरा पड़ गया (use dauraa pad gayaa, 'he's had an attack/seizure')! It reflects the same dative-subject logic as things happening *to* you rather than you doing them, much like नींद आना (niind aanaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Most commonly heard in दिल का दौरा (dil kaa dauraa, heart attack) and मिर्गी का दौरा (mirgii kaa dauraa, epileptic seizure) -- learners should recognize this fixed pattern X का दौरा (X kaa dauraa) rather than trying to translate 'attack' word-for-word in other contexts. 2) The verb used is usually पड़ना (padnaa) -- उसे दौरा पड़ा (use dauraa padaa, 'he had an attack') -- with a को-marked experiencer, not a normal subject doing the action.
