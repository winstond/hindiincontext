शिष्टाचार (shishtaachaar)

noun A formal noun meaning 'etiquette, courtesy, decorum' -- the accepted code of polite behavior in a social or professional setting.
Sense 1 : etiquette, decorum, polite social conduct
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सभ्यता (sabhytaa, broader: civility/culture), तमीज़ (tamiiz, everyday: manners), विनय (vinay, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in news and diplomatic contexts -- शिष्टाचार भेंट (shishtaachaar bhent, 'courtesy visit') is a stock phrase in Indian political reporting, describing a formal, largely ceremonial meeting between officials. In daily life, though, Delhi households talk about raising kids with तमीज़ (tamiiz) or अच्छे संस्कार (achchhe sanskaar) rather than शिष्टाचार, which feels more like a term for institutional or public-facing conduct.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use this word for everyday small talk, but शिष्टाचार (shishtaachaar) is a formal, somewhat bookish register word -- in casual speech, तमीज़ (tamiiz) or अच्छे मैनर्स (achchhe mainars, 'good manners') is far more natural. 2) It refers to the code of conduct itself, not a single polite act -- you would not say 'उसने एक शिष्टाचार किया' for a single kind gesture; that would need something like 'शिष्टाचार दिखाया' (shishtaachaar dikhaayaa, 'showed courtesy').
Sample Sentences:
राष्ट्रपति ने प्रधानमंत्री से शिष्टाचार भेंट की। The president paid a courtesy visit to the prime minister.
(raashtrapti ne pradhaanmantrii se shishtaachaar bhent kii।)
