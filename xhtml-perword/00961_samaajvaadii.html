समाजवादी (samaajvaadii)

adjective/noun A political term built from समाज (samaaj, 'society') plus वाद (vaad, '-ism') plus the agentive suffix ई (ii); it works both as an adjective ('socialist') and as a noun referring to a person who holds this ideology ('a socialist').
Sense 1 : socialist (relating to socialism)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in Indian political discourse -- India's constitution's preamble famously calls the country a 'सोशलिस्ट' or समाजवादी (samaajvaadii) republic, and several real political parties (like the समाजवादी पार्टी, samaajvaadii paartii, 'Socialist Party', a major force in Uttar Pradesh) carry this word right in their name.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a variable adjective and try to inflect it like कड़ा (kadaa) -- it does not change form for gender or number: समाजवादी नेता (samaajvaadii netaa, 'socialist leader') and समाजवादी सरकार (samaajvaadii sarkaar, 'socialist government') both use the identical form.

Sense 2 : a socialist (a person who believes in socialism)
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday political conversation, यह आदमी समाजवादी है (yah aadmii samaajvaadii hai, 'this man is a socialist') uses the same word as the adjective sense -- context and sentence position tell you whether it is describing a person's identity (noun) or modifying another noun (adjective), much like 'socialist' in English.
