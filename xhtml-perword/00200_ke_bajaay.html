के बजाय (ke bajaay)

postposition A postposition phrase meaning 'instead of, rather than' -- used to set up a contrast between two options with the second (or implied) one being chosen. Related to बजाय (bajaay) alone, which is less common in everyday speech than the full के बजाय (ke bajaay) form.
Sense 1 : instead of, rather than
postposition Takes the oblique/genitive-marked noun or verb stem before it, e.g. जाने के बजाय (jaane ke bajaay, 'instead of going'); the noun or infinitive it follows is marked with के (ke) as if governed by a postposition.
Synonyms: की जगह (kii jagah), के स्थान पर (ke sthaan par, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday complaints and suggestions -- चाय के बजाय कॉफी (chaay ke bajaay kophii, 'coffee instead of tea']) is the kind of thing said at every Delhi breakfast table. It pairs naturally with बल्कि (balki, 'rather') in more elaborate contrastive sentences: वह पढ़ने के बजाय, बल्कि सोता रहा (vah padhne ke bajaay, balki sotaa rahaa, 'instead of studying, he rather kept sleeping').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that a verb before के बजाय (ke bajaay) must be in its oblique infinitive form (ending in -ने), not the plain dictionary form -- so 'instead of eating' is खाने के बजाय (khaane ke bajaay), not खाना के बजाय (khaanaa ke bajaay). 2) Some learners try to use के बजाय (ke bajaay) to mean 'in exchange for' (like an actual trade), but it only expresses a preference/contrast between choices, not a literal exchange.
Sample Sentences:
चाय के बजाय आज कॉफी पी लो। Have coffee instead of tea today.
(chaay ke bajaay aaj kophii pii lo।)

वह पढ़ने के बजाय, बल्कि सोता रहा। Instead of studying, he rather kept sleeping.
(vah padhne ke bajaay, balki sotaa rahaa।)

(kripyaa nakad ke bajaay kaard se bhugtaan karen।)

(is maamle men baatchiit ke bajaay muqadmaa darz kiyaa gayaa।)
