ऋण (rin)

noun/adjective Two related senses: a financial loan/debt, and (mainly in fixed technical/school contexts) 'negative' as in negative numbers or electric charge.
Sense 1 : loan, debt
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कर्ज़ (karz), उधार (udhaar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see ऋण (rin) constantly in bank and government contexts -- गृह ऋण (grih rin, 'home loan'), शिक्षा ऋण (shikshaa rin, 'education loan') -- but if you ask a friend for money you would say मुझे थोड़ा उधार चाहिए (mujhe thodaa udhaar chaahie, 'I need a little loan/some cash'), not ऋण (rin). There is also an older cultural idea of ऋण (rin) as a moral debt, as in मातृ-ऋण (maatri-rin, 'debt to one's mother') or देव-ऋण (dev-rin, 'debt to the gods') -- a debt you owe not in money but in duty.
Common Mistakes: 1) ऋण (rin) is the formal/written word for a loan -- in everyday speech, especially spoken Hindi, कर्ज़ (karz) or उधार (udhaar) is far more common; ऋण (rin) shows up on bank forms, in news, and in set phrases like ऋण लेना (rin lenaa, 'to take a loan') or ऋण चुकाना (rin chukaanaa, 'to repay a loan'). 2) Learners sometimes try को-marking the lender; the standard frame is X से ऋण लेना (X se rin lenaa, 'to take a loan from X'), with से (se) marking the source.

Sense 2 : negative (of numbers, charge)
adjective Used almost exclusively in math and science contexts, e.g. ऋण संख्या (rin sankhyaa, 'negative number') or ऋण आवेश (rin aavesh, 'negative charge'), contrasting with धन (dhan, 'positive') in the same technical register.
Synonyms: नकारात्मक (nakaaraatmak, broader sense, not just math/science)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the word Indian schoolchildren learn in math class for the minus sign and negative numbers, paired with धन (dhan) for positive -- if you remember ऋण (rin) as 'loan/debt,' the math sense makes intuitive sense too: a negative number is like being 'in debt.'
