ग़म करना (gham karnaa)

verb A conjunct expression built on ग़म (gham, 'sorrow, grief') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'), meaning 'to grieve, to worry, to feel sad about something.' Almost always met in the negative, telling someone not to worry.
Sense 1 : to grieve, worry, feel sad (about something)
verb, transitive Takes का (kaa) marking the thing worried/grieved about: किसी चीज़ का ग़म करना (kisii chiiz kaa gham karnaa, 'to grieve over something').
Synonyms: फ़िक्र करना (fikr karnaa), चिंता करना (chintaa karnaa), दुखी होना (dukhii honaa, intransitive variant, 'to become sad')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase carries a certain filmi, soulful weight -- ग़म (gham) is a favorite word in Urdu-influenced Hindi poetry and Bollywood lyrics, evoking heartbreak and melancholy more than everyday 'worry.' You'll often hear elders or friends console someone with ग़म न करो, सब ठीक हो जाएगा (gham n karo, sab thiik ho jaaegaa, 'don't grieve, everything will be fine').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दुखी होना (dukhii honaa) or उदास होना (udaas honaa) in every context, but ग़म करना (gham karnaa) specifically pairs with ना (naa) in the famous comforting phrase ग़म न करो (gham n karo, 'don't worry/grieve') -- it carries a slightly more dramatic, heartfelt register than plain चिंता करना (chintaa karnaa). 2) Because ग़म (gham) is grammatically the object here, the verb agrees with ग़म (gham) (masculine), not with the subject -- so a female subject still takes करता है (kartaa hai) -style agreement pattern, e.g. वह ग़म करती है (vah gham kartii hai, 'she worries') actually does agree with the subject since करना (karnaa) is transitive and ग़म (gham) is inanimate/non-specific, so this is less surprising than it seems -- the real trap is expecting ने (ne) marking in the past tense, e.g. लोगों ने ग़म किया (logon ne gham kiyaa, 'people grieved').
