मिटना (mitnaa)

verb An intransitive verb meaning to be erased, wiped out, or vanish -- used for marks, memories, distinctions, or even things being destroyed entirely. Closely related to its transitive partner मिटाना (mitaanaa, 'to erase, wipe out'), which is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to be destroyed, wiped out, annihilated (people, civilizations, existence)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: नष्ट होना (nasht honaa), ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa), फ़ना होना (fanaa honaa, literary/poetic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in patriotic and devotional contexts -- लाइनें जैसे मिट जाएगा हिंदुस्तान, ऐसा सोचने वालों के लिए (mit jaaegaa hindustaan, 'India will be wiped out') carry a dramatic, almost mythic weight. It's the same root you meet in phrases about a name or a dynasty being erased from history.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is much stronger than 'to fade' -- मिट जाना (mit jaanaa) here implies total erasure or annihilation, so learners should reserve it for high-stakes contexts (a civilization, a name, a species) rather than everyday small losses.

(agar dushman ne hamlaa kiyaa to hindustaan nahiin mitegaa।)

Sense 2 : to be erased/wiped away (literal, of marks/writing/stains)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: धुलना (dhulnaa, of stains/washing away), मिट जाना (mit jaanaa, compound form, same meaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a chalkboard: जब बारिश हुई, तो सारी लिखाई मिट गई (jab baarish huii, to saarii likhaaii mit gaii, 'when it rained, all the writing was washed away') -- the rain is the cause but grammatically the writing itself just 'erases.' This is the same body-as-agent, world-does-things-to-you logic you see elsewhere in Hindi verb pairs.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मिटाना (mitaanaa) when they mean the mark disappeared by itself -- if there's no agent doing the erasing, मिटना (mitnaa) is correct: दाग़ मिट गया (daagh mit gayaa, 'the stain faded/disappeared'), not दाग़ ने मिटाया।
Sample Sentences:
जब बारिश हुई, तो सारी लिखाई मिट गई। When it rained, all the writing was washed away.
(jab baarish huii, to saarii likhaaii mit gaii।)

Sense 3 : to fade away, disappear (of feelings, memories, differences, distinctions)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa), दूर होना (door honaa, of doubts/fears), घुल जाना (ghul jaanaa, of differences blending away)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind lines like दिल का ग़म मिट गया (dil kaa gham mit gayaa, 'the heart's sorrow faded away') -- common in Hindi film lyrics and ghazals, where sorrow, distance, or distinctions between people 'erase' themselves rather than being actively removed by someone.
Common Mistakes: 1) With abstract nouns like फ़र्क़ (farq, 'difference') or ग़म (gham, 'sorrow'), मिटना (mitnaa) is the natural idiomatic choice where English might use several different verbs (fade, dissolve, be dispelled) -- don't overthink translating it literally each time.
Sample Sentences:
उसके दिल का ग़म धीरे-धीरे मिट गया। The sorrow in his heart faded away slowly.
