मिटाना (mitaanaa)

verb The causative counterpart of मिटना (mitnaa, 'to be erased/vanish'); मिटाना (mitaanaa) means to actively erase, wipe out, or eliminate something -- literally (writing, stains) or figuratively (memories, hunger, differences).
Sense 1 : to erase, rub out, wipe off
verb, transitive
Synonyms: मिटा देना (mitaa denaa, compound verb variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a teacher at the board saying बोर्ड मिटा दो (bord mitaa do, 'wipe the board clean') -- this is the most everyday, literal use, and it's the sense every learner meets first in a classroom or office context.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मिटना (mitnaa) when they mean the transitive act of erasing something themselves -- मिटना (mitnaa) is what happens to the writing (it fades/gets erased on its own or as a result), while मिटाना (mitaanaa) is you doing the erasing. 2) With chalk/pencil marks, रगड़ना (ragadnaa, 'to rub/scrub') is a common near-synonym learners confuse -- मिटाना (mitaanaa) focuses on the result (the mark is gone), रगड़ना (ragadnaa) on the physical action.
Sample Sentences:
बोर्ड मिटा दो। Wipe the board clean.
(bord mitaa do।)

Sense 2 : to eliminate, destroy, wipe out (figurative)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: खत्म करना (khatm karnaa), नष्ट करना (nasht karnaa, stronger/more formal), मिटा देना (mitaa denaa, compound verb variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the मिटाना (mitaanaa) of slogans and speeches -- गरीबी मिटाओ (gariibii mitaao, 'eradicate poverty') is the classic political rallying-cry register, echoing the famous Congress-era slogan pattern; it also shows up warmly in personal contexts like दूरियां मिटाना (dooriyaan mitaanaa, 'to erase the distances/differences between people').
Common Mistakes: 1) In this figurative sense (poverty, hunger, differences, enemies), the compound form मिटा देना (mitaa denaa) is very common in speech to add finality -- learners who only use the plain verb sound slightly flatter, though both are correct.
