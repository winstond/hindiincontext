भीड़-भाड़ (bhiid-bhaad)

noun A reduplicated intensifier form of भीड़ (bhiid, 'crowd'), used to evoke the general bustle and congestion of a crowded place rather than just a single crowd.
Sense 1 : crowd, bustle, congestion
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भीड़ (bhiid), हुजूम (hujoom, less common), रेला (relaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the context of Delhi markets and festival season -- बाज़ार में बहुत भीड़-भाड़ है (baazaar men bahut bhiid-bhaad hai, 'the market is very crowded/bustling') is a classic complaint before Diwali shopping. This kind of echo-word reduplication (भीड़-भाड़, bhiid-bhaad) is a common Hindi pattern for turning a plain noun into a richer, more atmospheric one -- compare चीज़-वस्तु (chiiz-vastu, 'things and belongings') or रोज़-मर्रा (roz-marraa, 'day-to-day').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as just a fancier synonym for भीड़ (bhiid), but भीड़-भाड़ (bhiid-bhaad) specifically evokes the general atmosphere of congestion (traffic, noise, crowded streets) rather than one specific crowd of people gathered for a purpose -- भीड़ (bhiid) works better for the latter. 2) The second element भाड़ (bhaad) has no independent meaning of its own here; it exists only to intensify भीड़ (bhiid) in this fixed pair, so it can't be swapped out or used alone with this sense.

दिवाली से पहले बाज़ार में बहुत भीड़-भाड़ होती है। Before Diwali, the market has a lot of crowd and bustle.
(divaalii se pahle baazaar men bahut bhiid-bhaad hotii hai।)
