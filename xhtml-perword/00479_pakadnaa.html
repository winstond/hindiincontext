पकड़ना (pakadnaa)

verb A common physical-action verb meaning 'to catch, hold, grab' -- extends naturally into catching transport, catching someone in wrongdoing, and a few idiomatic uses like taking hold of a habit or direction.
Sense 1 : to catch, grab, hold (physically)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: थामना (thaamnaa), जकड़ना (jakadnaa, to grip tightly), लपकना (lapaknaa, to snatch/grab quickly)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb you will hear shouted in a market when someone's kid wanders off -- हाथ पकड़ो! (haath pakdo!, 'hold hands!') -- or when a monkey grabs a bag of chips near a temple. It is very physical and immediate, not abstract.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for थामना (thaamnaa) as a straight synonym, but पकड़ना (pakadnaa) is the everyday default -- थामना (thaamnaa) sounds a touch more deliberate or literary, like steadying something. 2) With ने-marking in the perfective, remember पकड़ना (pakadnaa) is transitive, so उसने बिल्ली पकड़ी (usne billii pakdii, 'he/she caught the cat') agrees with बिल्ली (billii), not the subject.

(meraa haath pakdo, yahaan bahut bhiid hai।)

Sense 2 : to catch (a bus, train, flight)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: लेना (lenaa, less specific, 'to take')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Delhi commuter says ट्रेन पकड़नी है (tren pakadnii hai, 'I have to catch the train') when rushing out the door -- it carries the same urgency as in English, that sense of a small window closing.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers sometimes default to लेना (lenaa) for 'catch the bus,' but पकड़ना (pakadnaa) is the natural choice here -- बस पकड़ना (bas pakadnaa, 'to catch the bus') is what people actually say.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे सुबह जल्दी उठकर ट्रेन पकड़नी है। I have to get up early in the morning and catch the train.

Sense 3 : to catch someone doing something wrong; to apprehend
verb, transitive
Synonyms: गिरफ़्तार करना (giraftaar karnaa, formal, 'to arrest'), धरना (dharnaa, to seize/catch)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the context of police and mischief alike -- चोर पकड़ा गया (chor pakdaa gayaa, 'the thief was caught') is a stock news-report line, and a mother catching a child with hand in the biscuit tin might say तुम पकड़े गए! (tum pakde gae!, 'you got caught!'). The passive पकड़ा जाना (pakdaa jaanaa) is extremely common in exactly this sense.

Sense 4 : to take up, adopt (a direction, habit, or course of action)
verb, transitive Common in phrases like राह पकड़ना (raah pakadnaa, 'to take a path') or आदत पकड़ना (aadat pakadnaa, 'to pick up a habit').
Synonyms: अपनाना (apnaanaa, to adopt)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This extended sense treats an abstract path or habit as something physical you take hold of -- घर की राह पकड़ी (ghar kii raah pakdii, 'headed off toward home') is a nice everyday example of Hindi's love of concrete verbs for abstract movement.
