भारीपन (bhaariipan)

noun A noun meaning 'heaviness' -- built from the adjective भारी (bhaarii, 'heavy') plus the abstract-noun suffix -पन (pan). Covers physical heaviness as well as extended senses like a heavy/dull feeling in the body or a heavy, oppressive mood.
Sense 1 : physical heaviness (weight)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वज़न (vazan, weight, not quite the same as the felt quality of heaviness), बोझिलता (bojhiltaa, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in very physical, everyday contexts -- a mother testing a sack of atta (aataa) or a shopkeeper hefting a suitcase might comment on its भारीपन (bhaariipan). It describes the quality of being heavy, not a specific weight value.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for वज़न (vazan) when they mean 'heaviness' as a felt quality rather than a measurable weight -- भारीपन (bhaariipan) is the quality itself, not the number on a scale.

(is thaile kaa bhaariipan uthaane se hii pataa chal jaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : a heavy, dull, or clogged feeling in the body
noun, masculine Common with सिर (sir, 'head') and पेट (pet, 'stomach') to describe a dull, weighed-down sensation, often paired with dative-subject constructions like सिर में भारीपन लगना (sir men bhaariipan lagnaa, 'to feel heaviness in the head').
Synonyms: जड़ता (jadtaa, leans more toward sluggishness/numbness), सुस्ती (sustii, closer to lethargy, not identical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear at the clinic: a doctor asking about सिर में भारीपन (sir men bhaariipan, 'heaviness in the head') as a symptom, or someone describing the foggy, weighed-down feeling after a poor night's sleep or a heavy meal. It treats the body somewhat like a container that can feel loaded down, a common pattern in Hindi health-talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes describe this feeling with भारी (bhaarii) alone ('मेरा सिर भारी है', meraa sir bhaarii hai, 'my head is heavy') which is also correct and arguably more common in speech -- भारीपन (bhaariipan) as a noun tends to show up when naming the sensation itself, as in doctor-patient talk.

आज सुबह से मेरे सिर में भारीपन है। Since this morning I've had a heaviness in my head.

Sense 3 : heaviness/oppressiveness of mood, atmosphere, or emotion
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बोझिलता (bojhiltaa, works well for a heavy atmosphere or heavy prose), उदासी (udaasii, sadness -- narrower, an emotional cause rather than the felt heaviness itself)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: After bad news in a family, people might describe the mood in the house as having a certain भारीपन (bhaariipan) -- a heaviness hanging in the air. It extends the physical sense metaphorically to emotional or social atmosphere, much like English 'heaviness' does, but is used quite naturally in Hindi conversation about mood.
