दीन (diin)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning 'poor, wretched, humble' -- often heard in devotional or charitable contexts like दीन-दुखियों (diin-dukhiyon, 'the poor and suffering'); a separate, unrelated homograph दीन (diin) means 'religion/faith' (from Arabic/Persian, as in दीन-ईमान (diin-iimaan)).
Sense 1 : poor, humble, wretched
adjective
Synonyms: गरीब (gariib), दरिद्र (daridr, literary), विनीत (viniit, meaning scope: humble in manner rather than poverty), बेचारा (bechaaraa, pitiable)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will meet this word most often in the fixed phrase दीन-दुखी (diin-dukhii, 'the poor and suffering') or दीन-हीन (diin-hiin, 'destitute and lowly'), especially in charity appeals, bhajans, or NGO language. It carries a compassionate, almost devotional weight -- think of a temple donation box or a sermon urging kindness to दीन-दुखियों (diin-dukhiyon) rather than everyday chat about money troubles.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use दीन (diin) as a casual synonym for गरीब (gariib, 'poor'), but it carries a stronger note of helplessness or wretchedness -- it is closer to 'the destitute and downtrodden' than plain economic poverty. 2) It is rarely used to describe oneself casually; it appears more in religious, literary, or formal charitable speech than everyday conversation.

मंदिर में दीन-दुखियों की सेवा को सबसे बड़ा पुण्य माना जाता है। In the temple, serving the poor and suffering is considered the greatest virtue.
(mandir men diin-dukhiyon kii sevaa ko sabse badaa puny maanaa jaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : religion, faith (unrelated homograph)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धर्म (dharm), मज़हब (mazahab)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense comes from Arabic via Persian/Urdu and shows up in the common expression दीन-ईमान (diin-iimaan, 'faith and integrity'), often used when someone questions another's honesty or moral compass. It is far more common in Urdu-influenced speech and Islamic contexts than in general Hindi conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) This दीन (diin, 'faith') is a completely different word from the adjective दीन (diin, 'poor/wretched') despite identical spelling -- context (often paired with ईमान (iimaan, 'faith/integrity') as in दीन-ईमान (diin-iimaan)) is the only way to tell them apart.
Sample Sentences:
उसने अपना दीन-ईमान बेच दिया। He sold his faith and integrity.
