टिकना (tiknaa)

verb One core meaning: to stay put, hold up, or last -- used for people/things enduring in a place, in a condition, or over time (tiknaa).
Sense 1 : to stay, remain in place
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: रुकना (ruknaa), ठहरना (thaharnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a traveler who cannot find anywhere to टिकना (tiknaa) for the night -- the word carries a mild sense of finding stable ground, which is why it also extends naturally to things that hold up structurally or endure over time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for रुकना (ruknaa) here, which is fine for a short halt, but टिकना (tiknaa) carries a stronger sense of settling in or lasting there for a while, not just pausing. 2) It takes पर (par) or में (men) for the place, not को (ko): घर पर टिकना (ghar par tiknaa, 'to stay put at home').

Sense 2 : to hold up, withstand, endure (of things/situations)
verb, intransitive Common with चीज़ें (chiizen), रिश्ते (rishte), दाम (daam), मौसम (mausam) etc. as subjects rather than people.
Synonyms: बना रहना (banaa rahnaa), कायम रहना (kaayam rahnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in market haggling -- दाम नहीं टिकेंगे (daam nahiin tikenge, 'the price won't hold') -- and in everyday complaints about cheap goods that don't टिकना (tiknaa), reflecting a very Delhi-bazaar concern with durability and value for money.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used for things that endure stress or time -- a phone, a relationship, a price -- where English might say 'hold up' or 'last', not 'stay'. Learners sometimes default to रहना (rahnaa) here, which just means 'to remain' without टिकना's (tiknaa) implication of surviving pressure or a test.
