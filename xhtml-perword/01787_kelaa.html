केला (kelaa)

noun A simple, everyday noun meaning 'banana' -- the fruit itself and, by extension, the plant it grows on.
Sense 1 : banana (the fruit)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see केले (kele) piled high on carts (ठेला, thelaa) at almost every street corner in Delhi -- vendors often sell them by the दर्जन (darjan, dozen) rather than by weight. It is also a common, cheap breakfast or post-meal fruit in North Indian households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget that केला (kelaa) is masculine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take masculine forms, e.g. यह केला मीठा है (yah kelaa miithaa hai, 'this banana is sweet'), not मीठी (miithii).

Sense 2 : banana plant/tree
noun, masculine The same word केला (kelaa) can refer to the whole plant, not just the fruit, when context makes it clear (e.g. talking about something growing in a garden).
Synonyms: केले का पेड़ (kele kaa ped, 'banana tree', more explicit compound form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In many North Indian homes and temples, a केले का पेड़ (kele kaa ped, 'banana tree') is planted near the entrance or courtyard, since banana leaves and the plant itself are used in पूजा (poojaa, worship) and festival decorations.
