कहीं नहीं (kahiin nahiin)

adverb A combination of कहीं (kahiin, 'somewhere/anywhere') and नहीं (nahiin, 'not') that means 'nowhere' -- the standard way to negate location in Hindi, since there is no single word for 'nowhere.'
Sense 1 : nowhere, not anywhere
adverb Functions as a clausal negator of location: कहीं (kahiin) 'anywhere' is paired with नहीं (nahiin) 'not', mirroring how Hindi builds 'no one' (कोई नहीं, koii nahiin) and 'nothing' (कुछ नहीं, kuchh nahiin) from an indefinite plus नहीं (nahiin) rather than using a single dedicated word.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pairing pattern is worth internalizing as a family: कहीं नहीं (kahiin nahiin, 'nowhere'), कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahiin, 'nothing'), कोई नहीं (koii nahiin, 'no one'), and कभी नहीं (kabhii nahiin, 'never') all take an indefinite word and negate it with नहीं (nahiin) rather than relying on a single fused negative word the way English does. Once a learner sees this pattern, all four become easy to produce on the fly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate 'nowhere' word-for-word and search for a single Hindi word for it -- there isn't one; कहीं नहीं (kahiin nahiin) is always the two-word construction. 2) Word order is flexible: वह कहीं नहीं गया (vah kahiin nahiin gayaa, 'he did not go anywhere') and वह कहीं नहीं है (vah kahiin nahiin hai, 'he is nowhere [to be found]') both keep कहीं (kahiin) immediately before नहीं (nahiin) -- learners sometimes misplace नहीं (nahiin) elsewhere in the sentence out of habit from other negations.
