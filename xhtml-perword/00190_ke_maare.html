के मारे (ke maare)

postposition A postposition phrase meaning 'because of, on account of' -- almost always used for a negative or overwhelming cause, e.g. cold, fear, poverty, crowd. Related to मारना (maarnaa, 'to hit') but functions purely as a fixed grammatical frame here.
Sense 1 : because of, on account of (a negative/overwhelming cause)
postposition Takes the oblique/genitive form of the noun before it: भूख के मारे (bhookh ke maare, 'because of hunger'), ठंड के मारे (thand ke maare, 'because of the cold'). Functions like के कारण (ke kaaran) but carries a stronger sense of being overwhelmed or afflicted by the cause.
Synonyms: की वजह से (kii vajah se), के कारण (ke kaaran, slightly more formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this a lot in complaints about weather or crowds: ठंड के मारे हाथ सुन्न हो गए (thand ke maare haath sunn ho gae, 'my hands went numb because of the cold'). It has a slightly dramatic, folksy flavor compared to the more neutral के कारण (ke kaaran), so it fits everyday spoken complaints better than formal writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use के मारे (ke maare) for any neutral cause, but native speakers reserve it for causes that are painful, distressing, or overwhelming -- you would not say खुशी के मारे (khushii ke maare) for a mild pleasant reason unless the joy itself is overwhelming (e.g. crying from happiness). 2) It is a fixed phrase; do not try to conjugate मारे (maare) as if it were an active verb form here -- it no longer means 'hit' in this usage.
Sample Sentences:
ठंड के मारे हाथ सुन्न हो गए। My hands went numb because of the cold.
(thand ke maare haath sunn ho gae।)
