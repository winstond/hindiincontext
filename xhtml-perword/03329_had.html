हद (had)

noun/interjection A feminine noun meaning 'limit' or 'boundary,' whose main life for a learner is in fixed everyday phrases -- हद है (had hai) as an exclamation of exasperation, and हद तक (had tak) to mean 'to an extent.'
Sense 1 : limit, boundary
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सीमा (siimaa), सरहद (sarahad, border sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear हद (had) constantly in Delhi households and traffic jams alike -- यह तो हद है (yah to had hai, 'this is really too much') is a classic line when someone's patience runs out. It pairs naturally with पार करना (paar karnaa, 'to cross') in हद पार करना (had paar karnaa, 'to cross the line'), a vivid image of someone overstepping a boundary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सीमा (siimaa) in every context, but हद (had) is the word native speakers actually use in everyday exasperated or exclamatory speech -- सीमा (siimaa) sounds more formal or geographic. 2) In the sense of 'to a certain extent,' the fixed phrase is एक हद तक (ek had tak, 'to a certain extent'), not a literal word-for-word translation of 'extent.'

Sense 2 : exclamation of exasperation/disbelief
interjection Used as a short standalone exclamation, typically हद है! (had hai!, 'that's too much!') or हद हो गई! (had ho gaii!, 'that's gone too far!'), reacting to someone's behavior or a situation.
Synonyms: हद है (had hai), हद हो गई (had ho gaii, stronger/completed version)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture an aunty watching a neighbor's kid misbehave for the tenth time and muttering हद है! (had hai!) under her breath -- it's the verbal equivalent of throwing your hands up. This use is so common it almost feels like its own word, distinct from the literal noun sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not used to describe a physical limit in this exclamatory form -- it is purely emotional, expressing shock, annoyance, or disbelief at someone's audacity or a ridiculous situation.
