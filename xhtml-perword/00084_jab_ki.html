जब कि (jab ki)

conjunction A two-word conjunction meaning 'whereas' or 'even though/while', used to draw a contrast between two clauses. Commonly also written as one word, जबकि (jabki).
Sense 1 : whereas, while (contrastive)
conjunction Introduces a clause that contrasts with or contradicts the main clause; the main clause is often stated first.
Synonyms: जबकि (jabki, one-word variant), हालांकि (haalaanki)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot in complaints and comparisons -- a mother might say, 'तुम टीवी देख रहे हो, जब कि तुम्हें पढ़ना चाहिए' (tum tiivii dekh rahe ho, jab ki tumhen padhnaa chaahie, 'you're watching TV, whereas you should be studying'), pointing out a contradiction between what's happening and what should be happening.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat जब कि (jab ki) like जब (jab) 'when' alone and miss the contrastive sense -- the कि (ki) is what turns 'when' into 'whereas'. 2) Confusing it with हालांकि (haalaanki): हालांकि (haalaanki) typically opens the sentence with a concessive clause ('although'), while जब कि (jab ki) usually sits between the two clauses to mark contrast.
Sample Sentences:
तुम टीवी देख रहे हो, जब कि तुम्हें पढ़ना चाहिए। You're watching TV, whereas you should be studying.
(tum tiivii dekh rahe ho, jab ki tumhen padhnaa chaahie।)
