प्रगतिवादी (pragtivaadii)

adjective/noun A literary/intellectual term built from प्रगति (pragti, 'progress') plus the -वादी (vaadii) suffix meaning 'one who believes in/follows an -ism'; used both as a noun ('a progressivist') and as an adjective ('progressivist, progressive') mainly in literary, political, and social-commentary contexts.
Sense 1 : progressive, progressivist (relating to a movement favoring social/literary reform)
adjective
Synonyms: उन्नतिशील (unntishiil, literary), आधुनिकतावादी (aadhuniktaavaadii, meaning scope: modernist, not identical)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This word is closely tied to प्रगतिवाद (pragtivaad), the Progressive Writers' Movement in Hindi/Urdu literature from the 1930s-40s that pushed for socially conscious, often Marxist-inflected writing (think of poets and writers reacting against pure romanticism). Even outside literature, calling a politician or thinker प्रगतिवादी (pragtivaadii) signals they favor reform and social change over tradition.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with आधुनिक (aadhunik, 'modern') -- प्रगतिवादी (pragtivaadii) specifically implies alignment with a progressivist ideology or movement (often the Hindi literary Progressive Movement), not just being up to date.

Sense 2 : a progressivist, an adherent of the progressive/reformist ideology or movement
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun referring to a person, most often in discussions of literature, politics, or social thought; gender is typically determined by context/agreement with other words in the sentence rather than the noun itself changing form.
Synonyms: सुधारवादी (sudhaarvaadii, meaning scope: reformist, related but distinct)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: In everyday conversation this word rarely comes up outside of discussions about literature, history, or politics -- it belongs to the register of a college seminar or a newspaper op-ed rather than casual chat, so learners will meet it more in reading than in speech.
