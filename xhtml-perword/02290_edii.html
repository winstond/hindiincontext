एड़ी (edii)

noun A concrete body-part noun meaning 'heel' (of the foot), with a common extended use for the heel of a shoe; also lives inside a few everyday idioms about effort and standing tall.
Sense 1 : heel (of the foot)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear एड़ी उठाना (edii uthaanaa, 'to raise one's heels', i.e. to stand on tiptoe) used literally when someone reaches for a high shelf, and एड़ी-चोटी का ज़ोर लगाना (edii-chotii kaa zor lagaanaa, 'to strain from heel to crown, i.e. try one's absolute hardest') is a very common idiom pairing this word with चोटी (chotii, 'crown of the head') to mean the whole body straining with effort.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse एड़ी (edii) with टखना (takhnaa, 'ankle') -- एड़ी (edii) is the back/underside of the foot, टखना (takhnaa) is the joint above it. 2) In the idiom एड़ी-चोटी का ज़ोर लगाना (edii-chotii kaa zor lagaanaa, 'to put in one's utmost effort'), एड़ी (edii) is fixed inside the phrase and doesn't inflect independently.
Sample Sentences:
मेरी एड़ी में बहुत दर्द हो रहा है। My heel hurts a lot.
(merii edii men bahut dard ho rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : heel (of a shoe)
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is simply the anatomical sense extended to footwear, the same way English uses 'heel' for both -- a shoe-seller in a Delhi market will happily say एड़ी टूट गई (edii toot gaii, 'the heel broke') about a sandal without anyone finding it odd.
