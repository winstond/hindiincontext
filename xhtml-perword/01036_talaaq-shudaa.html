तलाक़-शुदा (talaaq-shudaa)

adjective A Persian-derived compound meaning 'divorced'; तलाक़ (talaaq) is 'divorce' and शुदा (shudaa) is a Persian past-participle suffix meaning roughly 'having become', so the whole word literally means 'having become divorced'.
Sense 1 : divorced
adjective
Synonyms: तलाक़याफ़्ता (talaaqyaaftaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word in everyday conversation, on matrimonial profiles, and in casual gossip about someone's marital history; it carries a slightly formal, almost bureaucratic flavor (much like 'divorcee' in English) compared to describing the situation with a full sentence like उसका तलाक़ हो गया (uskaa talaaq ho gayaa, 'her/his divorce happened'). The शुदा (shudaa) suffix shows up in a handful of other borrowed adjectives too, like ज़ख़्मी-शुदा-type constructions in older Urdu-influenced speech, though तलाक़-शुदा (talaaq-shudaa) is by far the most common survivor in daily use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect this like a native -आ adjective (talaaq-shudii for feminine), but it is invariant -- the same form तलाक़-शुदा (talaaq-shudaa) is used for men and women alike. 2) It describes a person's marital status directly (आदमी तलाक़-शुदा है, aadmii talaaq-shudaa hai, 'the man is divorced'), unlike the verb फ़ेस meaning to get divorced, which uses तलाक़ लेना (talaaq lenaa) or तलाक़ होना (talaaq honaa).
