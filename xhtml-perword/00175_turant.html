तुरंत (turant)

adverb A single-sense adverb meaning 'immediately, at once' -- one of the most common time-adverbs for talking about quick action. Also spelled तुरन्त (turant) with the full nasal consonant instead of the anusvaar; both are common.
Sense 1 : immediately, at once
adverb
Synonyms: फ़ौरन (fauran), तत्काल (tatkaal, formal), झट से (jhat se, colloquial), अभी (abhii, right now)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear तुरंत (turant) constantly in daily life -- on hospital signs (तुरंत डॉक्टर से मिलें, turant doktar se milen, 'see a doctor immediately'), in customer service lines, and in scoldings like तुरंत आओ (turant aao, 'come here right now'). It has a brisk, no-delay feel that पड़ता है (padtaa hai) type reluctant obligations lack -- तुरंत (turant) is about speed, not compulsion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place तुरंत (turant) at the very end of the sentence as English does with 'immediately' -- Hindi speakers usually put it right before the verb it modifies, e.g. वह तुरंत आया (vah turant aayaa, 'he came immediately'), not वह आया तुरंत (vah aayaa turant). 2) तुरंत (turant) describes speed of action, not proximity in time like अभी (abhii); saying तुरंत आओ (turant aao, 'come at once') carries urgency that अभी आओ (abhii aao, 'come now') does not.
Sample Sentences:
तुरंत आओ! Come here right now!
(turant aao!)
