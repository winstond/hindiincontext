शक्ति (shakti)

noun A feminine noun meaning power, strength, or energy in both physical and abstract senses; also carries a devotional sense as the divine feminine force/goddess energy in Hindu thought.
Sense 1 : power, strength, energy (physical/abstract)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ताकत (taakat), बल (bal), ज़ोर (zor, colloquial), ऊर्जा (oorjaa, scientific energy)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see शक्ति (shakti) everywhere in formal registers: news broadcasts talk of सैनिक शक्ति (sainik shakti, military strength), scientists discuss सौर ऊर्जा (saur oorjaa) and शक्ति (shakti) almost interchangeably for energy. It sits a notch above ताकत (taakat) in formality, the way 'strength' and 'vigor' differ in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ताकत (taakat) in casual speech and शक्ति (shakti) in more formal or written contexts -- both are correct, but शक्ति (shakti) sounds noticeably more formal/bookish for everyday physical strength (e.g. arm-wrestling talk favors ताकत (taakat)). 2) In compounds like सौर शक्ति (saur shakti, solar power) or परमाणु शक्ति (parmaanu shakti, nuclear power), शक्ति (shakti) is the only natural choice -- ताकत (taakat) would sound odd here.

(parmaanu shakti kaa upyog bijlii banaane men hotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : divine feminine power/energy; the goddess as embodiment of cosmic force
noun, feminine Often capitalized in English translation as a proper noun referring to the goddess or the feminine cosmic principle, distinct from the common-noun sense of 'power.'
Synonyms: देवी (devii, goddess, not identical but overlapping), माँ शक्ति (maan shakti, devotional address)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: In Hindu devotional and philosophical thought, शक्ति (shakti) is the primal cosmic energy personified as the Goddess (often as Durga (durgaa) or Kali (kaalii)), the active feminine force that animates Shiva (shiv), who is otherwise pure consciousness without her. Navratri (navraatri) is essentially a festival honoring Shakti in her many forms -- if you've heard people say 'माँ की शक्ति' (maan kii shakti, 'the mother goddess's power'), this is the sense at work.
