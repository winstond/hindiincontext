महोदय (mahoday)

noun A formal, respectful term of address meaning 'Sir', used mainly in written and official contexts; its feminine counterpart महोदया (mahodyaa) is treated as its own related lemma.
Sense 1 : Sir (formal term of address, mainly written)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: श्रीमान (shriimaan), साहब (saahab, everyday spoken equivalent)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you see at the top of a formal letter or application in India -- आदरणीय महोदय (aadarniiy mahoday, 'Respected Sir') is the classic opening line taught in school Hindi composition classes. In spoken life, though, Delhi-NCR folks reach for साहब (saahab) or just सर (sar); महोदय (mahoday) stays mostly on paper, in speeches, and in bureaucratic address.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use महोदय (mahoday) in casual spoken conversation, where it sounds stiff and out of place; साहब (saahab) or सर (sar) is what people actually say out loud. 2) It is typically placed after the name or title, as in शर्मा जी महोदय (sharmaa jii mahoday) is not idiomatic -- it usually stands alone as a salutation, e.g. at the start of a letter.
Sample Sentences:
आदरणीय महोदय, मैं इस पत्र के माध्यम से अपनी शिकायत दर्ज करना चाहता हूँ। Respected Sir, through this letter I wish to register my complaint.
(aadarniiy mahoday, main is patr ke maadhyam se apnii shikaayat darj karnaa chaahtaa hoon।)
