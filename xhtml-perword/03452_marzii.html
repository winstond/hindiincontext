मरज़ी (marzii)

noun A single-sense word meaning 'will, wish, choice' -- central to expressing consent, personal preference, and autonomy in everyday Hindi.
Sense 1 : will, wish, personal choice
noun, feminine
Synonyms: इच्छा (ichchhaa), चाहत (chaahat), रज़ामंदी (razaamandii, consent/agreement specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: तुम्हारी मरज़ी (tumhaarii marzii, 'as you wish') is one of the most common ways Hindi speakers hand a decision back to someone else, often with a shrug -- it can mean genuine deference or mild passive-aggressive resignation depending on tone, much like English 'suit yourself.' अपनी मरज़ी का मालिक (apnii marzii kaa maalik, 'master of one's own will') is a common phrase praising or criticizing someone who does exactly as they please. Note the nuqta in मरज़ी (marzii) reflecting its Persian/Urdu origin, though it's fully naturalized in everyday Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say किसी की इच्छा (kisii kii ichchhaa) when they mean casual everyday preference, but मरज़ी (marzii) is far more common in spoken Hindi for 'it's up to you' type statements -- इच्छा (ichchhaa) can sound slightly more formal or literary in comparison. 2) The fixed phrase अपनी मरज़ी से (apnii marzii se, 'of one's own free will') uses से (se), not का/की/के -- learners sometimes reach for का (kaa) here by analogy with possession.
