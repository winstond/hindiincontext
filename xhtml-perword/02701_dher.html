ढेर (dher)

adjective/noun Primarily a noun meaning 'a heap/pile,' but its everyday power comes from the adverbial/quantifier sense meaning 'a lot, tons of.'
Sense 1 : a lot of, tons of (informal quantifier)
adjective Used before a noun like a quantifier, e.g. ढेर सारा काम (dher saaraa kaam, 'a ton of work'); often paired with सारा (saaraa) for extra emphasis.
Synonyms: बहुत (bahut), बहुत सारा (bahut saaraa), बहुत ज़्यादा (bahut zyaadaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the ढेर (dher) you hear constantly in casual Delhi speech -- ढेर सारा प्यार (dher saaraa pyaar, 'tons of love') is a classic way to sign off a warm message, much warmer and more colloquial than बहुत सारा (bahut saaraa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that ढेर (dher) in this sense is usually followed by सारा (saaraa) before the noun -- ढेर सारा (dher saaraa) is the natural collocation, not ढेर (dher) alone in most casual speech. 2) Because सारा (saaraa) inflects for gender/number while ढेर (dher) itself stays fixed in this quantifier role, learners sometimes wrongly try to inflect ढेर (dher) too.
Sample Sentences:
उसने मुझे ढेर सारा प्यार भेजा। She sent me tons of love.
(usne mujhe dher saaraa pyaar bhejaa।)

Sense 2 : a heap, pile, mound
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राशि (raashi, formal), समूह (samooh, meaning scope: group/cluster)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of कचरे का ढेर (kachre kaa dher, 'a heap of garbage') -- a very common sight-based phrase in everyday Indian city life, or किताबों का ढेर (kitaabon kaa dher, 'a pile of books') on a student's desk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse ढेर (dher) 'heap' with ढेला (dhelaa) 'lump/clod,' which is a different, less common word.
