धार (dhaar)

noun Mainly a noun meaning the sharp edge/blade of a cutting tool, or a flowing stream/jet of liquid; both senses share the idea of a continuous line of force or flow.
Sense 1 : the sharp edge of a blade
noun, feminine
Synonyms: किनारा (kinaaraa, edge, less specific to blades)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around knife-sharpeners (सिकलीगर, sikliigar) who walk through Delhi neighborhoods calling out छुरी-चाकू पर धार लगवा लो (chhurii-chaakoo par dhaar lagvaa lo, 'get an edge put on your knives'). Testing a blade's धार by feel or by a light touch of the thumb is a very common household gesture.
Common Mistakes: 1) धार (dhaar) refers to the sharpness/edge itself, not the whole knife or blade -- छुरी (chhurii) or चाकू (chaakoo) is the object; 2) the common phrase धार तेज़ करना (dhaar tez karnaa, 'to sharpen the edge') pairs धार with तेज़ (tez) rather than with a word like 'sharp' used alone.
Sample Sentences:

(is chaakoo kii dhaar bahut tez hai।)

Sense 2 : a flowing stream or jet of liquid
noun, feminine Often used for a continuous jet, like milk being drawn from an udder or water pouring from a source: दूध की धार (doodh kii dhaar, 'a stream of milk').
Synonyms: बहाव (bahaav, general flow, less specific to a thin stream)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in rural and pastoral imagery -- milking a cow or buffalo and watching the दूध की धार (doodh kii dhaar, 'stream of milk') hit the pail is a classic descriptive image in Hindi songs and stories. It extends easily to any thin forceful flow, like blood or rain.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बहाव (bahaav) or नदी (nadii) here, but धार (dhaar) specifically evokes a thin, continuous line of liquid in motion, not a body of water or general current.

Sense 3 : figurative: intensity, force, or onslaught (as in a torrent)
noun, feminine Used metaphorically for something (rain, words, emotion) coming in a forceful, unbroken stream, often in phrases like आँसुओं की धार (aansuon kii dhaar, 'a stream of tears').
Synonyms: बहाव (bahaav, general flow, less vivid), प्रवाह (pravaah, literary, flow/current)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same 'continuous line of flow' image as the water sense, just stretched onto emotion or weather -- बारिश की धार (baarish kii dhaar, 'the driving stream of rain') or आँसुओं की धार (aansuon kii dhaar, 'a stream of tears') both borrow the same mental picture of something pouring out without a break.
