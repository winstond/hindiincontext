भरोसा (bharosaa)

noun A single core idea -- trust, faith, or reliance -- expressed as a noun; the word also anchors several very common idiomatic phrases built with करना (karnaa) and होना (honaa).
Sense 1 : trust, faith, confidence (in a person, thing, or outcome)
noun, masculine Used with पर (par) for the person/thing trusted (उस पर भरोसा है, us par bharosaa hai, 'there is trust in him') and often as a को-marked experiencer construction with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa): मुझे भरोसा है (mujhe bharosaa hai, 'I trust/believe').
Synonyms: विश्वास (vishvaas), यकीन (yakiin), भरोसा (bharosaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: भरोसा (bharosaa) is less formal and more emotionally warm than विश्वास (vishvaas) -- you'd say मुझे तुम पर भरोसा है (mujhe tum par bharosaa hai, 'I trust you') to a friend or family member, carrying a sense of relational faith rather than abstract belief. It is also the everyday word Indian parents invoke when reassuring or testing loyalty: 'मुझे तुम पर भरोसा है' is a line every kid has heard at some point before being given responsibility.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for विश्वास (vishvaas) by default from textbooks, but भरोसा (bharosaa) is the word Delhi speakers actually reach for in daily conversation about relying on people -- both are correct but भरोसा (bharosaa) feels warmer and more personal. 2) The person or thing trusted takes पर (par), not को (ko) or में (men) -- साहिल पर भरोसा है (saahil par bharosaa hai), not साहिल को भरोसा।

Sense 2 : reliance, dependence (on something being certain or dependable)
noun, masculine Commonly used in the negative to express unreliability or uncertainty: मौसम का कोई भरोसा नहीं (mausam kaa koii bharosaa nahiin, 'the weather is unpredictable'), using का (kaa) to mark the thing that can't be depended on.
Synonyms: आसरा (aasraa, reliance/dependence for support), उम्मीद (ummiid, hope, related but distinct)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the भरोसा (bharosaa) of philosophical resignation -- मौसम का भरोसा नहीं, वक़्त का भरोसा नहीं, जिंदगी का भरोसा नहीं (mausam kaa bharosaa nahiin, vaqt kaa bharosaa nahiin, jindgii kaa bharosaa nahiin, 'you can't count on the weather, time, or life') is a very common register of everyday Hindi fatalism, often said with a shrug.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense भरोसा (bharosaa) attaches to the unreliable thing with का/की/के (kaa), not पर (par) -- जिंदगी का कोई भरोसा नहीं (jindgii kaa koii bharosaa nahiin, 'life is uncertain'), a different frame from the trust-in-a-person sense above.
