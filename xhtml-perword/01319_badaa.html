बड़ा (badaa)

adjective/noun Core adjective meaning 'big/large' or 'elder/senior', with an extended noun sense referring to an elder/senior person, and a culinary noun sense (a fried lentil-based savory).
Sense 1 : big, large (size)
adjective
Synonyms: विशाल (vishaal, huge, more emphatic), बृहत (brihat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बड़ा (badaa) is one of the first adjectives every learner picks up, right alongside छोटा (chhotaa, 'small'), and the pair shows up constantly in daily comparisons -- from house sizes to portion sizes at a dhaba.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget to inflect बड़ा (badaa) to match the noun's gender and number: बड़ी किताब (badii kitaab, 'big book') not बड़ा किताब (badaa kitaab). 2) Do not confuse with बड़ी (badii) as a separate word -- it is the same adjective agreeing with a feminine noun, not a different lemma.

(yah ghar bahut badaa hai।)

Sense 2 : elder, older, senior (age or rank)
adjective
Synonyms: वरिष्ठ (varishth, formal, used for rank/seniority), ज्येष्ठ (jyeshth, literary/formal, eldest sibling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Hindi family vocabulary, बड़ा (badaa) and छोटा (chhotaa) are baked right into sibling terms -- बड़ा भाई (badaa bhaaii), छोटी बहन (chhotii bahan) -- because relative age within the family carries real social weight, unlike the more age-neutral English 'brother' and 'sister'.
Common Mistakes: 1) बड़ा भाई (badaa bhaaii, 'elder brother') and बड़ी बहन (badii bahan, 'elder sister') are fixed family terms -- learners sometimes reach for 'older' translations like पुराना (puraanaa) by mistake, but पुराना (puraanaa) means 'old' as in aged/worn, not senior in age.

Sense 3 : great, important, distinguished
adjective
Synonyms: महान (mahaan, formal, 'great' as in illustrious), महत्वपूर्ण (mahatvpoorn, important)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear बड़ा आदमी (badaa aadmii, 'a big man' or 'an important man') and बड़ी बात (badii baat, 'a big deal/significant thing') used constantly to talk about status and importance, not just physical size -- Hindi leans on this same word for both.
Common Mistakes: 1) बड़ा आदमी (badaa aadmii) can mean either 'a big/tall man' or 'an important/influential man' -- context (often tone or the rest of the sentence) decides which, and learners often miss the figurative reading.

Sense 4 : elder person, senior (as a noun, from the adjective)
noun, masculine Used as a noun typically in the plural, बड़े (bade), referring to elders collectively, e.g. respect owed to seniors in the family.
Synonyms: बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg, elder, more respectful/formal term for older people)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: घर के बड़े (ghar ke bade, 'the elders of the house') is a phrase you'll hear often in discussions of family respect and decision-making -- touching your elders' feet, or पैर छूना (pair chhoonaa), is a common gesture of respect toward बड़े (bade) in many Hindi-speaking households.
Common Mistakes: 1) बड़ों का आदर करो (badon kaa aadar karo, 'respect your elders') uses the oblique plural बड़ों (badon) -- learners sometimes leave it in the direct form बड़े (bade) before का (kaa).
