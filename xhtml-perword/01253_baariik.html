बारीक (baariik)

adjective An invariant adjective meaning 'fine, thin, or subtle' -- used for physically fine things (a fine powder, a thin slice) as well as figuratively sharp or subtle things (a keen eye, a subtle point).
Sense 1 : fine, finely-ground or thinly-cut (of physical texture)
adjective
Synonyms: पतला (patlaa, for thin slices/strands), महीन (mahiin)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask for onions chopped बारीक बारीक (baariik baariik, 'very finely') in a Delhi kitchen and everyone knows exactly the size you mean -- almost minced. Doubling an adjective like this for emphasis is a very common, colloquial move in spoken Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पतला (patlaa) for everything 'thin,' but बारीक (baariik) is the natural word for texture -- fine salt, finely chopped onions, a thin thread -- while पतला (patlaa) suits things like thin rotis or thin liquids.

Sense 2 : subtle, minute, sharp (of detail, sense, or distinction)
adjective Often used with abstract nouns like नज़र (nazar, 'eye/gaze'), फ़र्क़ (farq, 'difference'), or बात (baat, 'point') to mean a keen or subtle quality.
Synonyms: सूक्ष्म (sookshm, more formal/literary), महीन (mahiin)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A बारीक नज़र (baariik nazar, 'a keen eye') is high praise for someone who notices what others miss -- a good editor, a sharp shopkeeper checking for fake notes, or a detective in a TV serial.
Common Mistakes: 1) In English 'fine' covers both texture and subtlety, but Hindi speakers reach for बारीक (baariik) for both too -- so this sense is not as much of a stretch as it might seem, but learners should note it applies to perception and detail, not just physical fineness.
