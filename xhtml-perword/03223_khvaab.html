ख़्वाब (khvaab)

noun A Persian-origin word for 'dream,' used both literally (sleep-dreams) and figuratively (aspirations, hopes) -- much like सपना (sapnaa), its everyday Hindi counterpart, but with a more poetic, Urdu-tinged flavor.
Sense 1 : dream (aspiration, hope)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सपना (sapnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in Hindi film songs and dialogues -- ख़्वाबों (khvaabon) का शहर (kaa shahar) style phrasing shows up often when talking about Mumbai or big-city ambitions. It sits comfortably in casual conversation too, so do not treat it as reserved for shayari alone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for सपना (sapnaa) and forget ख़्वाब (khvaab) exists as a completely natural, everyday alternative -- it is not obscure or purely poetic, especially in Delhi-NCR speech influenced by Urdu. 2) The verb pairing is ख़्वाब देखना (khvaab dekhnaa, 'to see a dream') for both literal dreaming and having aspirations -- learners sometimes try to use करना (karnaa) instead.

Sense 2 : dream (literal, during sleep)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सपना (sapnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Same word as the aspirational sense above -- Hindi/Urdu, like English, does not distinguish night-dreams from life-dreams with separate words, so context alone tells you which is meant.
Sample Sentences:
कल रात मुझे एक अजीब ख़्वाब आया। Last night I had a strange dream.
(kal raat mujhe ek ajiib khvaab aayaa।)

मैं रोज़ अजीब ख़्वाब देखता हूँ। I have strange dreams every day.
