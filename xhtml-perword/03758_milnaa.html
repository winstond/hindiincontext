मिलना (milnaa)

verb A dative-subject verb (with को (ko)) covering several related ideas: meeting someone, receiving something, something being available, things matching/fitting together, and -- following an oblique infinitive -- getting the chance to do something.
Sense 1 : to meet (a person)
verb, intransitive Takes a dative subject with को (ko): मुझे उससे मिलना है (mujhe usse milnaa hai, 'I have to meet him/her') -- the person met is marked with से (se).
Synonyms: भेंट होना (bhent honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, मिलते हैं (milte hain, 'see you' / 'let's meet') is the standard warm sign-off among friends, much like 'catch you later' -- it's used even when no concrete plan exists yet.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the person met with को (ko) instead of से (se) -- say उससे मिलना (usse milnaa), not उसको मिलना (usko milnaa) for 'to meet him/her'. 2) मिलना (milnaa) is intransitive here, so the subject stays in the dative/nominative, never gets ने (ne), even in the past tense.
Sample Sentences:
कल हम बाज़ार में मिले थे। We met at the market yesterday.
(kal ham baazaar men mile the।)

Sense 2 : to get, receive (something)
verb, intransitive The thing received is the grammatical subject and the recipient takes को (ko): उसे इनाम मिला (use inaam milaa, 'he/she got a reward').
Synonyms: प्राप्त होना (praapt honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind common phrases like सज़ा मिलना (sazaa milnaa, 'to get punished') and डाँट मिलना (daant milnaa, 'to get scolded') -- Hindi frequently frames receiving even unpleasant things (punishment, a scolding) with this same 'arrives to you' logic rather than an active verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because the received item is the real subject, the verb agrees with it, not with the recipient -- उसे किताबें मिलीं (use kitaaben miliin) not मिला (milaa), since किताबें (kitaaben) is feminine plural.

Sense 3 : to be available, obtainable
verb, intransitive Common in market/shop contexts: यहाँ ताज़ी सब्ज़ियाँ मिलती हैं (yahaan taazii sabziyaan miltii hain, 'fresh vegetables are available here').

Sense 4 : to match, fit, agree (of things, colors, ideas, accounts)

Sense 5 : to get the chance/opportunity to do (something) -- auxiliary after an oblique infinitive
verb, intransitive Follows a verb stem plus the oblique infinitive ending -ने (ne): V-ने + मिलना, with the person marked by को (ko) -- बच्चों को खेलने नहीं मिलता (bachchon ko khelne nahiin miltaa, 'the children don't get to play'). This is structurally distinct from the 'get/receive' sense, which takes a plain noun object rather than an infinitive.
Synonyms: पाना (paanaa, parallel V-ने पाना construction)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This mirrors पाना (paanaa) in its own V-ने पाना (V-ne paanaa) auxiliary use -- both frame getting to do something as an external grant rather than a personal achievement, fitting Hindi's broader habit of using dative subjects (को (ko)) for things that happen 'to' you rather than things you actively cause.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse this with सकना (saknaa, 'to be able to') -- सकना (saknaa) is about ability, while V-ने मिलना (milnaa) is about being granted permission or opportunity by circumstance. 2) The infinitive before मिलना (milnaa) must be in its oblique form ending in -ने (ne), not the dictionary form -- जाने मिला (jaane milaa), not जाना मिला (jaanaa milaa).
