या तो x या (yaa to x yaa)

pattern A paired-choice pattern meaning 'either x or y' -- या तो (yaa to) marks the first option and या (yaa) alone introduces the second.
Sense 1 : either...or (exclusive alternative)
pattern Fills two clause-sized or phrase-sized slots: या तो x या y. Both slots can hold single words, phrases, or full clauses; when clauses, each typically has its own verb, e.g. या तो तुम आओ या मैं जाऊँ (yaa to tum aao yaa main jaaoon, 'either you come or I'll go').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily bargaining and planning talk in Delhi -- या तो पूरा पैसा दो या सामान वापस लो (yaa to pooraa paisaa do yaa saamaan vaapas lo, 'either pay the full amount or take the item back') is the exact tone of a shopkeeper laying down an ultimatum. It pairs naturally with नहीं तो (nahiin to, 'otherwise') in follow-up clauses, e.g. या तो अभी बोल दो, नहीं तो बाद में मत कहना (yaa to abhii bol do, nahiin to baad men mat kahnaa, 'either speak up now, or don't complain later').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop तो (to) and just say या (yaa) twice; this is understood but sounds less natural than the full या तो (yaa to) ... या (yaa) frame. 2) The two options should be grammatically parallel (both nouns, both clauses, etc.) -- mixing a noun with a full clause reads awkwardly, just as in English. 3) Don't confuse this with चाहे x चाहे y (chaahe x chaahe y), which leans more toward 'whether x or y, it doesn't matter' -- या तो x या y (yaa to x yaa y) is a straightforward exclusive choice, not a concessive one.

(yaa to chaay pii lo yaa kofii mangvaa lo।)
