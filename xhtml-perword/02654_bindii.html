बिंदी (bindii)

noun Refers primarily to the decorative dot worn on the forehead; also has an extended, less common sense as a small dot/mark or dot-shaped diacritic. Also spelled बिन्दी (bindii) with the full nasal consonant instead of the anusvaar -- both are common.
Sense 1 : the forehead dot worn by women
noun, feminine
Synonyms: टीका (tiikaa, often the red ceremonial dot applied for tilak/rituals rather than everyday fashion), टिकुली (tikulii, regional/colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi you will see every kind of बिंदी (bindii) -- the tiny red dot a married woman wears daily, and the big glittery stick-on ones that go with a lehenga for a wedding or festival. It is one of those items that carries both deep traditional meaning (marking the 'third eye' or a married woman's status in some communities) and pure fashion, often at the very same time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume बिंदी (bindii) is only a Hindu religious marker; in everyday urban use it is very often just a fashion accessory, worn or not worn by choice, independent of any ritual meaning. 2) Don't confuse with टीका (tiikaa), which usually refers to the specific dot applied during a puja or ceremony (often with kumkum/sindoor), rather than the general decorative sticker-bindi worn daily.

Sense 2 : a small dot or point (general/extended use)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बिंदु (bindu, more formal/mathematical), नुक्ता (nuktaa, specifically the diacritic dot under a letter)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up less in daily chit-chat and more in contexts like describing a pattern, a map marking, or a printed dot -- for the precise mathematical 'point,' most speakers reach for बिंदु (bindu) instead.
