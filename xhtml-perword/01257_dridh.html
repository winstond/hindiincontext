दृढ़ (dridh)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning firm, resolute, or steadfast -- used for both physical firmness and mental/moral resolve.
Sense 1 : firm, resolute, steadfast
adjective
Synonyms: मज़बूत (mazboot), अटल (atal, firm and unwavering, often for resolve/decisions), पक्का (pakkaa, everyday word for firm/sure), स्थिर (sthir, stable, steady)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word most often paired with निश्चय (nishchay, resolve) or इरादा (iraadaa, intention) in mildly formal speech -- दृढ़ निश्चय (dridh nishchay, firm resolve) is a set phrase used in speeches, motivational talk, and school essays about willpower. In daily bazaar or kitchen talk, people reach for पक्का (pakkaa) or मज़बूत (mazboot) instead, so दृढ़ (dridh) carries a slightly bookish, earnest flavor -- the kind of word a teacher or a parent giving advice might use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मज़बूत (mazboot) in every context, but दृढ़ (dridh) leans more toward mental or moral firmness (दृढ़ निश्चय (dridh nishchay, firm resolve)) whereas मज़बूत (mazboot) is the default for physical sturdiness (मज़बूत रस्सी (mazboot rassii, strong rope)). 2) दृढ़ (dridh) is a Sanskrit-origin word and feels slightly more formal/written than पक्का (pakkaa) or मज़बूत (mazboot) in casual speech.
