बोर होना (bor honaa)

verb A colloquial hybrid expression borrowed from English 'bore' plus होना (honaa, 'to be/become'), used to say someone is or gets bored. Very common in casual conversation among younger and urban speakers.
Sense 1 : to become/be bored
verb, intransitive बोर (bor) functions as an invariant predicative adjective borrowed from English; होना (honaa) supplies the tense/agreement, agreeing with the subject in the usual way (बोर होना, बोर हुई, बोर हो गया, etc.).
Synonyms: ऊबना (oobnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic Hindi-English hybrid you'll hear constantly in Delhi -- friends complaining यार, बहुत बोर हो रहा हूँ (yaar, bahut bor ho rahaa hoon, 'dude, I'm so bored') on a lazy Sunday afternoon. It sits comfortably in casual speech alongside the more 'pure' Hindi ऊबना (oobnaa), which feels a touch more literary or formal by comparison.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to conjugate बोर (bor) itself (like an adjective agreeing directly) instead of just changing होना (honaa) -- the borrowed word बोर (bor) never changes form, only होना (honaa) inflects. 2) Many learners default to plain होना (honaa) when native speakers would prefer the compound हो जाना (ho jaanaa) to mark the moment boredom sets in, e.g. मैं बोर हो गया (main bor ho gayaa, 'I got bored') rather than मैं बोर हुआ (main bor huaa).
