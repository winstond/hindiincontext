बग़ल में (baghal men)

postposition/adverb A postpositional phrase built on बग़ल (baghal, 'side/flank') plus में (men, 'in'), meaning 'beside, next to, alongside'. Also written बगल में (bagal men) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : beside, next to (spatial location)
postposition Takes a noun in the oblique case followed by की/के before बग़ल में (baghal men), e.g. मेरी बग़ल में (merii baghal men, 'beside me'), घर के बग़ल में (ghar ke baghal men, 'next to the house').
Synonyms: पास (paas), नज़दीक (nazdiik), साथ में (saath men, less precise, means 'along with')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बग़ल (baghal) literally refers to the armpit/side of the body, so बग़ल में (baghal men) carries a physical, body-based sense of 'right alongside' -- picture someone sitting so close their arm brushes yours. You will hear it constantly in directions, like 'दुकान के बग़ल में है' (dukaan ke baghal men hai, 'it is next to the shop').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use पास (paas) for everything; बग़ल में (baghal men) is more specific, pointing to immediate physical adjacency (right next to, flank-to-flank) rather than general proximity. 2) The connecting particle before बग़ल (baghal) must agree with the gender/number logic of की/के, not with the noun's own gender -- it is always के बग़ल में (ke baghal men) or की बग़ल में (kii baghal men) depending on convention with the possessor, which trips up learners used to simpler postpositions.
Sample Sentences:
मेरा घर स्कूल के बग़ल में है। My house is next to the school.
(meraa ghar skool ke baghal men hai।)

Sense 2 : under one's arm, tucked at one's side
adverb Used with verbs like दबाना (dabaanaa, 'to press/tuck') or लेना (lenaa, 'to take'), as in बग़ल में दबाना (baghal men dabaanaa, 'to tuck under one's arm').
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense stays close to the literal body part -- think of someone tucking a folder or an umbrella बग़ल में (baghal men) as they hurry out the door. It is a vivid, physical image rather than an abstract location phrase.
