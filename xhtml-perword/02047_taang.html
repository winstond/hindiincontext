टाँग (taang)

noun/verb Primarily a body-part noun meaning 'leg', with a common idiomatic use ('टाँग अड़ाना' taang adaanaa, 'to interfere') built on it; also has a colloquial verb form meaning 'to hang up'.
Sense 1 : leg (body part)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पैर (pair, often used loosely for the whole leg or foot)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: टाँग (taang) is the everyday, slightly more casual word for leg -- you'll hear it in homes and among friends more than in a doctor's clinic, where पैर (pair) or the more formal medical terms tend to show up instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use पैर (pair) for everything from foot to leg; टाँग (taang) specifically emphasizes the leg (thigh to ankle) and is the word you want when talking about limping, kicking, or crossing legs, e.g. टाँग तोड़ना (taang todnaa, 'to break a leg').

(merii taang men bahut dard ho rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : interference (in idiom 'टाँग अड़ाना')
noun, feminine Almost always seen inside the fixed compound verb टाँग अड़ाना (taang adaanaa, 'to interfere/meddle') -- literally 'to stick one's leg in'.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like English 'to stick your nose in', Hindi imagines meddling as sticking a body part where it doesn't belong -- here it's the leg, not the nose. You'll hear this a lot in family arguments about who's interfering in whose business: दूसरों के काम में टाँग मत अड़ाओ (doosron ke kaam men taang mat adaao, 'don't interfere in others' work').
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense barely exists outside the idiom टाँग अड़ाना (taang adaanaa); don't try to use टाँग (taang) alone to mean 'interference'.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to hang (something) up
verb, transitive This is the verb टाँगना (taangnaa) appearing in its stem/imperative-adjacent form; as a plain headword it functions as the verb root meaning 'to hang (transitive)', as in कपड़े टाँगना (kapde taangnaa, 'to hang clothes').
Synonyms: टांगना (taangnaa, standard spelling without chandrabindu), लटकाना (latkaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this verb constantly around household chores -- कपड़े टाँगना (kapde taangnaa, 'to hang up clothes') on a line after washing, or तस्वीर टाँगना (tasviir taangnaa, 'to hang up a picture') on the wall.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse this verb टाँगना (taangnaa) with the noun टाँग (taang, 'leg') -- they're unrelated in meaning despite looking similar; the verb is about hanging objects (like washing or a picture), not legs.
