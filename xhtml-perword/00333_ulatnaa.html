उलटना (ulatnaa)

verb Covers both 'to turn over / capsize / topple' (intransitive) and 'to overturn / turn something inside out' (transitive); closely related to the transitive causative उलटाना (ultaanaa) and the adjective/adverb उलटा (ultaa).
Sense 1 : to overturn, capsize, topple (something falls or flips over on its own)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: पलटना (palatnaa), गिरना (girnaa, less precise, 'to fall')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly with कश्ती (kashtii) or नाव (naav) capsizing in a river, or a कड़ाही (kadaahii) or गिलास (gilaas) tipping over in the kitchen -- it is the everyday word for something flipping onto its other face.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गिरना (girnaa) for anything that falls, but उलटना (ulatnaa) specifically implies flipping over onto the other side (a boat capsizing, a cup tipping), not just dropping. 2) As an intransitive verb it does not take ने (ne) in the past: नाव उलट गई (naav ulat gaii, 'the boat capsized'), not नाव ने उलटी (naav ne ultii).
Sample Sentences:

(naav achaanak ulat gaii।)

Sense 2 : to turn something over, turn inside out, invert
verb, transitive Takes a direct object and behaves as an ordinary transitive verb, requiring ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने कुर्सी उलट दी (usne kursii ulat dii, 'he turned the chair over').
Synonyms: पलटना (palatnaa), उलटाना (ultaanaa, more common causative form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of उलटना (ulatnaa) here the way you'd use 'flip' for a pancake or 'turn inside out' for a sock -- कमीज़ उलटना (kamiiz ulatnaa, 'to turn a shirt inside out') is a very concrete, physical image Hindi speakers reach for.
Common Mistakes: 1) Many speakers prefer the derived causative उलटाना (ultaanaa) for this transitive meaning in everyday speech, and reserve plain उलटना (ulatnaa) mostly for the intransitive sense -- so learners should not be surprised to hear उलटाना (ultaanaa) more often when someone deliberately flips or inverts something.

Sense 3 : to vomit (colloquial)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: उलटी करना (ultii karnaa, more common phrase), कै करना (kai karnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense is the root of the everyday noun-plus-verb phrase उलटी आना (ultii aanaa, 'to feel/be sick') that you'll hear far more often than the plain verb by itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense native speakers overwhelmingly say उलटी करना (ultii karnaa) or उलटी आना (ultii aanaa) rather than the bare verb उलटना (ulatnaa); using उलटना (ulatnaa) alone for vomiting sounds dated or regional to many listeners today.
