आराम करना (aaraam karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to rest, to relax' -- built from the noun आराम (aaraam, 'comfort/rest') plus the light verb करना (karnaa, 'to do'). Only one core sense in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : to rest, to relax, to take it easy
verb, intransitive Functions as a single unit: आराम (aaraam) is the object-like noun and करना (karnaa) is the light verb carrying the tense/agreement; the noun itself does not take ने-marking exceptions beyond the normal transitive pattern.
Synonyms: सुस्ताना (sustaanaa, to take a short rest), विश्राम करना (vishraam karnaa, formal), चैन करना (chain karnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase every Indian mother says when you're sick: थोड़ा आराम कर लो (thodaa aaraam kar lo, 'rest a little'). आराम (aaraam) carries a warmer, more holistic sense than just 'sleep' -- it covers lying down, taking a break from work, or simply not exerting yourself, and is often prescribed as the cure-all for everything from a headache to heartbreak.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say आराम लेना (aaraam lenaa) by analogy with 'take rest' in English -- Hindi speakers say आराम करना (aaraam karnaa) instead. 2) Do not confuse with आराम से (aaraam se, 'comfortably/at ease'), which describes the manner of doing another action rather than resting itself.
