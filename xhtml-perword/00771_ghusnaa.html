घुसना (ghusnaa)

verb One core sense: to enter, push in, or intrude into a space, often with a connotation of force, cramming, or unwelcome entry -- distinct from the neutral अंदर जाना (andar jaanaa).
Sense 1 : to enter forcefully or intrusively
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: प्रवेश करना (pravesh karnaa, formal), अंदर जाना (andar jaanaa, neutral, lacks the forceful connotation), दाखिल होना (daakhil honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture squeezing onto a packed Delhi Metro at rush hour -- that scramble, shove, and squeeze is exactly the feel of घुसना (ghusnaa). It is also the word you reach for when a thief चोर घर में घुस गया (chor ghar men ghus gayaa, 'the thief got into the house') -- entry that is unwanted, sudden, or against resistance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अंदर जाना (andar jaanaa) or जाना (jaanaa) when they mean simple entry, but घुसना (ghusnaa) carries a built-in sense of squeezing, forcing, or sneaking in -- using it for a polite, invited entry (like a guest arriving) sounds odd or even rude. 2) With a place name, the destination is often marked with में (men) rather than को (ko): भीड़ में घुसना (bhiid men ghusnaa, 'to push into the crowd'), not भीड़ को घुसना.
Sample Sentences:
भीड़ में हर कोई धक्का देकर मेट्रो में घुसने की कोशिश कर रहा था। In the crowd, everyone was trying to push their way into the metro.
(bhiid men har koii dhakkaa dekar metro men ghusne kii koshish kar rahaa thaa।)

Sense 2 : to get involved in or meddle with (figurative)
verb, intransitive Used with में (men) marking the matter being intruded upon, e.g. दूसरों के मामले में घुसना (doosron ke maamle men ghusnaa, 'to poke into other people's business').
Synonyms: दखल देना (dakhal denaa), टांग अड़ाना (taang adaanaa, idiomatic, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear elders scold with तुम बीच में क्यों घुस गए? (tum biich men kyon ghus gae?, 'why did you butt into this?') when someone interrupts a conversation or dispute that was not theirs to join -- the physical 'squeezing in' image carries over neatly into social intrusion.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative use is always about intruding into someone else's affairs, conversation, or business -- it is not used for benign 'joining in.' Learners sometimes overextend it to mean simply 'participate,' which sounds off; शामिल होना (shaamil honaa) is the neutral word for that.
