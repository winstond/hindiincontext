हिलना (hilnaa)

verb An intransitive verb covering physical movement/shaking, and by extension emotional/mental disturbance; its causative counterpart हिलाना (hilaanaa, 'to shake something') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to move, shake, sway, wobble
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: काँपना (kaampnaa, to tremble), हिलना-डुलना (hilnaa-dulnaa, to stir/budge, common paired form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a loose tooth (दाँत हिल रहा है, daant hil rahaa hai, 'the tooth is wobbly'), a table that wobbles, or a leaf shaking in the wind -- this is the everyday physical sense every learner meets early. It contrasts neatly with its causative pair हिलाना (hilaanaa), 'to shake something (else)' -- e.g. सिर हिलाना (sir hilaanaa, 'to nod/shake the head') is something you do to your own head using the transitive verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for हिलाना (hilaanaa) when they mean 'don't move' -- but the command is मत हिलो (mat hilo, 'don't move') using हिलना (hilnaa) itself, since the subject is moving on its own, not moving something else. 2) In the negative/imperative sense of 'stay still,' native speakers almost always pair it with the doubled form हिलना-डुलना (hilnaa-dulnaa) rather than हिलना (hilnaa) alone, e.g. मत हिलो-डुलो (mat hilo-dulo, 'don't budge').

Sense 2 : to be shaken, disturbed, unsettled (emotionally/mentally)
verb, intransitive Often used with मन (man, 'mind/heart') or इरादा (iraadaa, 'resolve') as the subject: मन हिल जाना (man hil jaanaa, 'to be shaken/unsettled at heart').
Synonyms: विचलित होना (vichlit honaa, more formal/literary), घबराना (ghabraanaa, to be flustered/panic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same physical image of wobbling or shaking extended inward -- your resolve (इरादा, iraadaa) or heart (मन, man) 'wobbles' under pressure, much like English 'shaken' after bad news. It is a good example of how Hindi often maps physical verbs onto emotional states rather than coining separate abstract vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is almost always seen with the compound form हिल जाना (hil jaanaa) rather than bare हिलना (hilnaa) -- the जाना (jaanaa) marks the sudden, complete nature of being shaken.
