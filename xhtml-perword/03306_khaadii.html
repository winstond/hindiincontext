खादी (khaadii)

noun A single, culturally loaded sense: hand-spun, hand-woven cloth, and by extension the fabric's association with Gandhian self-reliance and Indian politics.
Sense 1 : hand-spun, hand-woven cotton cloth
noun, feminine
Synonyms: खादी का कपड़ा (khaadii kaa kapdaa, full phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: खादी (khaadii) is inseparable from Gandhi's Swadeshi movement -- he promoted spinning it on the चरखा (charkhaa, 'spinning wheel') as a symbol of self-reliance and resistance to British textile imports. Today it's still worn by many Indian politicians and officials as a mark of simplicity and nationalist identity, so the word carries political and moral weight beyond just describing cloth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat खादी (khaadii) as just a generic word for 'cotton'; it specifically means hand-spun and hand-woven cloth, not any cotton fabric (सूती कपड़ा (sootii kapdaa) is the general term for cotton cloth). 2) It is often used attributively before another noun without any postposition, as in खादी कुर्ता (khaadii kurtaa, 'khadi kurta'), which can look odd to learners expecting a linking 'का (kaa)'.
