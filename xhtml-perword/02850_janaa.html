जना (janaa)

noun/verb Primarily a counting word for 'person(s)' used after numerals (जैसे दो जना, do janaa, 'two people'); also survives as a literary/dialectal verb meaning 'to give birth to' or 'to be born.' The two are historically related but function completely differently in modern speech.
Sense 1 : person, individual (as a counting unit)
noun, masculine Used almost exclusively right after a number to count people, e.g. तीन जने (tiin jane, 'three people'). In the modern colloquial register, जन (jan) or आदमी (aadmii) is far more common standalone; जना (janaa) mainly survives in this fixed numeral+जना pattern.
Synonyms: आदमी (aadmii), व्यक्ति (vyakti, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in markets and family counts: कितने जने आएंगे? (kitne jane aaenge, 'how many people will come?') is a very natural, homely way to ask, more casual-sounding than कितने लोग (kitne log).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use जना (janaa) as a general standalone word for 'person' the way they'd use आदमी (aadmii); in everyday speech it almost always appears glued to a number (दो जने, do jane, 'two people'), not on its own. 2) The plural/oblique form जने (jane) is what you'll actually hear after numbers greater than one, not जना (janaa) itself -- so listen for that shift.

Sense 2 : to give birth to, to bear (a child)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: जन्म देना (janm denaa)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This verb sense is old-fashioned and mostly shows up in folk songs, proverbs, or rural/dialectal speech rather than in the Hindi you'll hear in Delhi drawing rooms; जन्म देना (janm denaa) is what people actually say today.
Sample Sentences:
