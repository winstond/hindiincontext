प्रतिबिंब (pratibimb)

noun A literary/formal noun meaning 'reflection' or 'image', used both for a physical reflection (in water, a mirror) and figuratively for an impression or echo of something in another form.
Sense 1 : reflection (physical image, e.g. in water or a mirror)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: परछाई (parchhaaii), छाया (chhaayaa, literary), प्रतिच्छाया (pratichchhaayaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word shows up often in poetry and school-level Hindi essays describing moonlight on water (चांद का प्रतिबिंब, chaand kaa pratibimb, 'reflection of the moon') -- it carries a slightly literary shimmer that परछाई (parchhaaii) or छाया (chhaayaa) don't quite have in daily speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for परछाई (parchhaaii) in casual speech instead, since प्रतिबिंब (pratibimb) leans formal/written and can sound stiff in an everyday conversation about, say, a mirror or a puddle. 2) Don't confuse with प्रतिबंध (pratibandh, 'restriction') -- the words look and sound similar but are unrelated in meaning.

Sense 2 : figurative reflection or impression (of an idea, era, mood, etc. in something else)
noun, masculine Common in phrases like समाज का प्रतिबिंब (samaaj kaa pratibimb, 'a reflection of society'), where an abstract quality is said to be 'mirrored' in a person, work, or event.
Synonyms: झलक (jhalak, glimpse/partial sense), छवि (chhavi, image/likeness)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense constantly in Hindi literary criticism and journalism -- a novel or film is called समाज का प्रतिबिंब (samaaj kaa pratibimb, 'a reflection of society'), much like English critics say a work 'mirrors' its era.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is common in essays, news commentary, and book reviews (e.g. a novel being called 'a reflection of its times') but sounds overly bookish if used for small everyday comparisons -- झलक (jhalak) is the more natural everyday choice there.
