ठेस (thes)

noun A feminine noun with a literal sense of a physical knock or stumble, and a very common figurative sense of hurt feelings -- the emotional 'knock' someone gives you. Almost always seen with the verb लगना (lagnaa).
Sense 1 : hurt feelings, emotional wound
noun, feminine Occurs almost exclusively in the frame X को ठेस लगना (X ko thes lagnaa) -- 'X feels hurt', with the person affected marked by को (ko), following the dative-subject pattern.
Synonyms: चोट (chot), आघात (aaghaat, somewhat formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear far more often in daily life -- a mother might say उसकी बात से मुझे बहुत ठेस लगी (uskii baat se mujhe bahut thes lagii, 'his words really hurt me'), capturing that quiet, wounded feeling rather than open anger. It reflects a broader Hindi pattern of framing emotions as things that 'arrive' at you (को लगना (ko lagnaa)) rather than things you actively feel.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दर्द (dard) for emotional pain, but ठेस (thes) specifically implies a hurt caused by someone's words or behavior, not physical or generic pain. 2) The verb is almost always लगना (lagnaa) here, not होना (honaa) -- मुझे ठेस लगी (mujhe thes lagii, 'I felt hurt'), not मुझे ठेस हुई (mujhe thes huii).
Sample Sentences:
उसकी बात से मुझे बहुत ठेस लगी। His words really hurt me.
(uskii baat se mujhe bahut thes lagii।)

Sense 2 : physical knock, stumble, jolt
noun, feminine Also used with लगना (lagnaa): पैर को ठेस लगी (pair ko thes lagii, 'the foot got knocked/hurt'), describing a minor physical bump or stubbing, often against a stone or step.
Synonyms: झटका (jhatkaa), टक्कर (takkar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this on uneven Delhi pavements -- ठोकर (thokar) and ठेस (thes) overlap here, but ठेस (thes) leans toward the resulting jolt or minor hurt rather than the act of stumbling itself.
