तरह (tarah)

noun/postposition A single high-frequency noun meaning 'way, manner, kind' -- almost always seen inside the postposition-like phrase की तरह (kii tarah, 'like') or as तरह तरह का (tarah tarah kaa, 'of various kinds').
Sense 1 : manner, way, style
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ढंग (dhang), तौर (taur), प्रकार (prakaar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of those small words that shows up constantly once you start listening for it -- इस तरह (is tarah) and उस तरह (us tarah) are the everyday way to say 'like this' and 'like that' in casual explanation, much more common in speech than a formal 'in this manner.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'in this way' with a preposition-like structure borrowed from English; the natural pattern is इस तरह (is tarah, 'in this way/thus') with तरह (tarah) taking the oblique demonstrative directly, no extra postposition needed. 2) तरीका (tariikaa) is a close cousin meaning 'method' -- तरह (tarah) leans toward 'sort/manner' while तरीका (tariikaa) leans toward 'technique,' and the two are not always interchangeable.

Sense 2 : like, similar to (in the phrase X की तरह)
postposition Functions as a compound postposition in the frame X की तरह (kii tarah, 'like X'), with X in the genitive/oblique: वह मेरी माँ की तरह है (vah merii maan kii tarah hai, 'she is like my mother').
Synonyms: जैसा (jaisaa), समान (samaan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear की तरह (kii tarah) everywhere in comparisons, compliments, and scoldings alike -- तुम बच्चों की तरह मत करो (tum bachchon kii tarah mat karo, 'don't act like children') is a classic parental line. It pairs naturally with the noun sense above; the postposition use grew directly out of 'in the manner of X.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop की (kii) and just say X तरह, which sounds broken -- the genitive की (kii) is required before तरह (tarah) in this comparative use. 2) Confusing की तरह (kii tarah) with जैसा (jaisaa): both mean 'like,' but जैसा (jaisaa) agrees in gender/number with what follows while की तरह (kii tarah) stays invariant, so learners overuse one where the other fits the sentence rhythm better.
