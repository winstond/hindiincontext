समझाना (samjhaanaa)

verb The causative of समझना (samajhnaa, 'to understand') -- means 'to make someone understand,' covering both explaining facts and persuading/reasoning with someone emotionally.
Sense 1 : to explain
verb, transitive Takes the person being taught as a direct object and the topic marked with के बारे में (ke baare men) or as a subordinate clause; often used with a dative-marked listener too: उसे समझाओ (use samjhaao, 'explain to him').
Synonyms: स्पष्ट करना (spasht karnaa, more formal, 'to clarify'), बताना (bataanaa, meaning scope: overlaps only when explaining information, not persuading)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A teacher explaining a lesson, a mother explaining why a decision was made, a friend breaking down a tricky form -- समझाना (samjhaanaa) is the verb for all of it. It's the causative partner of समझना (samajhnaa), so think of it literally as 'making someone's understanding happen.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बताना (bataanaa) when they mean समझाना (samjhaanaa) -- बताना (bataanaa) just conveys information, while समझाना (samjhaanaa) implies the listener didn't get it and needs help understanding. 2) The object of explanation can be marked with either को (ko) or no marker at all depending on whether a person or a topic is being explained, which surprises learners expecting one fixed pattern.

Sense 2 : to persuade, reason with, talk someone into/out of something
verb, transitive Common in contexts of calming someone down or talking them out of a decision, often paired with a purpose clause: उसे समझाया कि ऐसा मत करो (use samjhaayaa ki aisaa mat karo, 'I explained to him not to do that').
Synonyms: मनाना (manaanaa, meaning scope: focuses on convincing/coaxing rather than reasoning), राजी करना (raajii karnaa, meaning scope: specifically 'to get someone to agree')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic word for when elders sit a family member down to talk sense into them -- a father समझाता है (samjhaataa hai) his stubborn son, a friend समझाती है (samjhaatii hai) another out of a bad decision. It carries real warmth: the effort to help someone see reason, not just lecture them.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate this sense too literally as 'explain' when English would say 'talk (someone) into/out of' or 'reason with' -- the emotional persuasion angle is easy to miss. 2) Confusing this with मनाना (manaanaa), which leans more toward coaxing or appeasing (like calming a crying child) rather than reasoning through an argument.
