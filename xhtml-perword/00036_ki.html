कि (ki)

conjunction कि (ki) is one of Hindi's workhorse conjunctions: mainly the complementizer 'that' introducing reported speech or a following clause, but also heard colloquially as 'or' in quick alternative questions, and inside a fixed frame that marks a sudden interruption ('was just... when').
Sense 1 : complementizer 'that' introducing a subordinate clause
conjunction Links a main clause (often with कहना (kahnaa), सोचना (sochnaa), पता (pataa), मालूम (maaloom)) to the clause it introduces; unlike English 'that', it is almost never dropped in speech.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the कि (ki) you will hear constantly in news reports, gossip, and storytelling -- किसी ने बताया कि... (kisii ne bataayaa ki, 'someone told [me] that...') is practically a stock opener for passing on information in Delhi conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing कि (ki) with की (kii, possessive 'of') -- they sound close but are grammatically unrelated. 2) Trying to drop it the way English drops 'that' ('he said he'd come'); in Hindi उसने कहा कि वह आएगा (usne kahaa ki vah aaegaa, 'he said that he would come') normally keeps कि (ki). 3) Placing the कि-clause before the main clause -- word order stays fixed with कि (ki) after the reporting verb.
Sample Sentences:
किसी ने बताया कि आज बारिश होगी। Someone told me that it will rain today.
(kisii ne bataayaa ki aaj baarish hogii।)

Sense 2 : 'or' in short alternative questions
conjunction Used informally in place of या (yaa) inside brisk either-or questions, especially about immediate choices: चाय कि कॉफ़ी? (chaay ki kofii, 'tea or coffee?').
Synonyms: या (yaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear a shopkeeper or friend toss out चलना है कि नहीं? (chalnaa hai ki nahiin, 'are we going or not?') -- this quick, clipped कि (ki) has a different flavor from the more formal-sounding या (yaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to या (yaa) everywhere and miss that कि (ki) sounds natural and snappier in casual back-and-forth choices, like at a chai stall or when ordering food.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : 'when suddenly' -- marks an abrupt interruption after 'just'
conjunction Appears in a fixed frame with अभी (abhii) or जैसे ही (jaise hii) plus the perfective + ही (hii): अभी बैठा ही था कि फोन बज उठा (abhii baithaa hii thaa ki phon baj uthaa, 'I had just sat down when the phone suddenly rang').
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This dramatic 'no sooner had... than' कि (ki) is a favorite in storytelling for building suspense -- it mimics the way a good story-teller pauses mid-sentence before the twist.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners reach for जब (jab) here, but plain जब (jab) reads as neutral 'when', while this कि (ki) frame specifically signals surprise or interruption -- something happening right in the middle of something else.

(main abhii ghar se niklaa hii thaa ki baarish shuroo ho gaii।)
