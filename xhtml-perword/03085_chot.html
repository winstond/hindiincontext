चोट (chot)

noun Primarily a physical 'injury/hurt' word, but it stretches naturally into emotional hurt and into a couple of common idioms built on the same image of a blow or wound.
Sense 1 : physical injury, wound, or hurt
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ज़ख्म (zakhm), घाव (ghaav)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चोट लगना (chot lagnaa) is the everyday way Hindi speakers report injuries -- a mother asking a child मुझे दिखाओ, कहाँ चोट लगी? (mujhe dikhaao, kahaan chot lagii?, 'show me, where did you get hurt?') is a very ordinary scene. Note the dative-subject-like feel: the injury 'lands on' you (लगना, lagnaa) rather than you 'getting' it as a direct object.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दर्द (dard) when they mean चोट (chot): दर्द (dard) is the pain itself (a symptom), while चोट (chot) is the injury or the act of hurting that causes it -- you can have चोट (chot) without much दर्द (dard), or दर्द (dard) (like a headache) with no चोट (chot) at all. 2) The common verb pairing is चोट लगना (chot lagnaa, to get hurt/injured), not चोट होना (chot honaa), for describing that someone got injured.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : emotional hurt, blow to feelings/pride
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आघात (aaghaat, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi loves this body-as-site-of-emotion move: दिल पर चोट लगना (dil par chot lagnaa, 'a wound lands on the heart') or स्वाभिमान को चोट पहुँचना (svaabhimaan ko chot pahunchnaa, 'a hurt reaches one's self-respect') both use चोट (chot) exactly like a physical blow, just redirected at the heart or the ego -- a nice window into how the language treats emotional pain as something that strikes you from outside.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense also pairs with लगना (lagnaa) -- दिल पर चोट लगना (dil par chot lagnaa, for it to hurt the heart) -- rather than any form of feel-verb learners might default to from English.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी बातों से मेरे दिल पर गहरी चोट लगी। His words dealt a deep hurt to my heart.
(uskii baaton se mere dil par gahrii chot lagii।)

Sense 3 : a strike, blow, or stroke (in idiomatic/set expressions)
noun, feminine Appears mainly inside fixed expressions rather than as a freestanding 'blow' word for ordinary hitting -- for a plain physical strike, मार (maar) or वार (vaar) is more typical.
Synonyms: प्रहार (prahaar, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in contexts like व्यापार को चोट पहुँची (vyaapaar ko chot pahunchii, 'the business took a hit') -- चोट (chot) here functions almost like English 'a blow to...' in headlines, extending the injury image to abstract setbacks.
Common Mistakes: 1) चोट खाना (chot khaanaa, to suffer a blow/setback) is a set idiom (business चोट खा गया, chot khaa gayaa, 'took a hit') -- learners sometimes try to build this meaning with लगना (lagnaa) instead, but खाना (khaanaa) is the fixed verb here.
