उधार देना (udhaar denaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to lend' (money or goods) -- literally 'to give on credit.' Pairs with उधार लेना (udhaar lenaa), 'to borrow,' which is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to lend (money or goods)
verb, transitive The person lent to takes को (ko): उसे उधार दिया (use udhaar diyaa, 'lent to him'). The thing lent can also be marked, e.g. पैसे उधार दिए (paise udhaar die, 'gave money on credit').
Synonyms: कर्ज़ देना (karz denaa, for money specifically), ऋण देना (rin denaa, formal, for money)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: उधार (udhaar) is deeply woven into neighborhood life in India -- the corner shopkeeper (दुकानदार, dukaandaar) who lets regular customers buy on a running tab, or a neighbor who lends a cup of sugar, all live in this word. उधार देना (udhaar denaa) is the shopkeeper's or lender's side of that exchange; उधार लेना (udhaar lenaa) is the borrower's side -- together they frame borrowing/lending as a single shared social transaction rather than a formal banking term.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for कर्ज़ देना (karz denaa) as a direct swap, but कर्ज़ (karz) is specifically money/debt, while उधार (udhaar) covers both money and everyday goods (sugar, a book, a tool) borrowed informally. 2) Since देना (denaa) is the main verb here, ने-marking follows the regular transitive pattern for देना (denaa) in the perfective -- learners occasionally forget it because उधार (udhaar) feels like the 'main' word and देना (denaa) like an afterthought.

(dukaandaar apne puraane graahkon ko udhaar de detaa hai।)
