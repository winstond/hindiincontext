पिलाना (pilaanaa)

verb The causative of पीना (piinaa, 'to drink') -- it means 'to give something to drink' or 'to make someone drink'; it also carries a specific slang sense about giving someone a beating.
Sense 1 : to give (someone) something to drink
verb, transitive Takes a dative/को-marked recipient plus an object for the liquid: X ko Y pilaanaa (X ko Y pilaanaa).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word comes up constantly around chai and water -- offering to पिलाना (pilaanaa) someone tea or water is a basic act of hospitality in any Indian home or office. You'll also hear माँ बच्चे को दूध पिलाती है (maan bachche ko doodh pilaatii hai, 'the mother breastfeeds/nurses the child') where पिलाना (pilaanaa) is the standard verb for nursing/breastfeeding, since milk is something 'drunk'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use पीना (piinaa) with a को-marked person when they mean 'give someone a drink' -- but पीना (piinaa) only works for the drinker's own act of drinking, while पिलाना (pilaanaa) is required when a second person is causing/serving the drinking. 2) The recipient is marked with को (ko), not से (se) -- माँ बच्चे को दूध पिलाती है (maan bachche ko doodh pilaatii hai, 'the mother gives the child milk to drink'), not बच्चे से (bachche se).
Sample Sentences:
माँ अपने बच्चे को दूध पिलाती है। The mother breastfeeds her child.
(maan apne bachche ko doodh pilaatii hai।)

Sense 2 : to get someone drunk, to ply with alcohol
verb, transitive
Synonyms: शराब पिलाना (sharaab pilaanaa, 'to make someone drink liquor')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: उसने दोस्तों को पिलाई (usne doston ko pilaaii, 'he treated his friends to drinks') is a very common phrase among friend groups celebrating something -- a raise, a birthday, a cricket win. It carries a casual, masculine, adda-culture flavor.
Common Mistakes: 1) Without an explicit object, पिलाना (pilaanaa) in a social context (especially दोस्तों को पिलाना (doston ko pilaanaa)) is often understood as alcohol by default, similar to how पीना (piinaa) alone can imply drinking alcohol -- learners are sometimes surprised the liquid doesn't need to be named.
Sample Sentences:
आज मैं दोस्तों को पिलाऊँगा, रेज़ बोनस मिला है। Today I'll treat my friends to drinks, I got a raise bonus.
(aaj main doston ko pilaaoongaa, rez bonas milaa hai।)

Sense 3 : to give a beating to (slang)
verb, transitive Used in the same causative-sounding frame but with no literal liquid involved; the object is the person being beaten.
Synonyms: पीटना (piitnaa), धुनना (dhunnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi street-slang bravado -- एक थप्पड़ पिलाऊँगा (ek thappad pilaaoongaa, 'I'll slap you one') is the kind of line you hear in playful arguments or Bollywood tough-guy dialogue, extending the 'make someone take something' logic of the drinking sense to a slap or punch.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only appears in informal/slang speech, often in mock-threatening or joking contexts like एक पिलाऊँगा (ek pilaaoongaa, 'I'll land one on you') -- learners shouldn't use it in neutral or polite registers expecting it to mean 'to give a drink'.
Sample Sentences:
एक थप्पड़ पिलाऊँगा तुझे! I'll slap you one!
(ek thappad pilaaoongaa tujhe!)
