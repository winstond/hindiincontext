बासी (baasii)

adjective An adjective describing food (or, by extension, ideas/news) that is stale or no longer fresh -- the opposite of ताज़ा (taazaa, 'fresh').
Sense 1 : stale (food)
adjective
Synonyms: पुराना (puraanaa, for leftover food specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Indian homes, बासी रोटी (baasii rotii, 'stale roti') from last night is not thrown away -- it is often eaten for breakfast, sometimes soaked in milk or fried again, so the word carries a very everyday, practical association rather than a purely negative one like 'spoiled' would.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पुराना (puraanaa, 'old') to describe stale food, but बासी (baasii) is the specific word native speakers use for food that has gone stale overnight or lost freshness -- पुराना (puraanaa) sounds odd applied to, say, रोटी (rotii). 2) बासी (baasii) is used for food and closely related domestic contexts; it does not extend to inanimate objects like clothes or furniture the way 'stale' sometimes can in English.

Sense 2 : stale, old (figurative: news, jokes, ideas)
adjective
Synonyms: पुराना (puraanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as in English 'stale news,' Hindi extends बासी (baasii) from food to information or humor that has lost its punch -- बासी खबर (baasii khabar, 'stale/old news') or बासी मज़ाक (baasii mazaak, 'stale joke') are common ways to say something is no longer fresh or interesting.
Sample Sentences:
यह तो बासी खबर है, यह तो सबको पता है। This is stale news, everyone already knows it.
(yah to baasii khabar hai, yah to sabko pataa hai।)
