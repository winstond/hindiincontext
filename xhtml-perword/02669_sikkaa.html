सिक्का (sikkaa)

noun Primarily 'coin' (physical currency); also used figuratively to mean someone's dominant influence or reputation, as in the idiom सिक्का चलना (sikkaa chalnaa).
Sense 1 : coin (metal currency)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मुद्रा (mudraa, formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian kid learns to sort सिक्के (sikke) by size and shape long before they understand their value -- the 1, 2, 5, and 10 rupee coins all look and feel distinct, which is a small tactile pleasure of Indian currency. Shopkeepers often grumble good-naturedly about being given too many coins at once for change, since सिक्के (sikke) are heavier to handle than notes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सिक्का (sikkaa) for paper money too; it refers only to metal coins, while नोट (not) is the word for banknotes. 2) The plural सिक्के (sikke) is easy to miss when counting change, e.g. दस रुपये के सिक्के (das rupye ke sikke, 'ten-rupee coins').

Sense 2 : dominant influence, reputation, sway (figurative)
noun, masculine Almost always appears in the fixed idiom सिक्का चलना (sikkaa chalnaa, 'for one's influence/authority to prevail') or सिक्का जमना (sikkaa jamnaa, 'for one's reputation to become established').
Synonyms: दबदबा (dabadbaa), प्रभाव (prabhaav, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense grows naturally out of the coin meaning: just as a ruler's coin (sikkaa) being accepted across the land signaled recognized authority in old kingdoms, today saying किसी का सिक्का चलता है (kisii kaa sikkaa chaltaa hai, 'someone's word/authority holds sway') carries that same historical flavor of a name being 'currency' that others honor.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only really surfaces inside set phrases like सिक्का चलना (sikkaa chalnaa) or सिक्का जमाना (sikkaa jamaanaa) -- learners shouldn't try to use सिक्का (sikkaa) alone to mean 'influence.'
