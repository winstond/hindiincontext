किन (kin)

adjective/pronoun This is the oblique plural form of कौन (kaun, 'who/which'), used before postpositions when asking 'which (people/things)' or 'whom' in the plural. It functions as an interrogative adjective/pronoun and does not stand alone as a separate lemma sense beyond this grammatical role.
Sense 1 : which (plural, oblique, before a postposition)
adjective Used directly before a noun plus postposition, e.g. किन लोगों से (kin logon se, 'from which people'). This is the oblique plural of कौन सा (kaun saa) style questions when the noun is plural and followed by a postposition.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of किन (kin) as what happens to कौन (kaun) when it has to 'bend' (go oblique) for a plural noun and a postposition -- the same shift you already know from log (log) becoming logon (logon). Hearing किन लोगों ने (kin logon ne, 'which people') in a question is a good signal that oblique plural marking is at play.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to कौन से (kaun se) even when a postposition follows a plural noun -- but किन (kin) is required there, e.g. किन किताबों में (kin kitaabon men, 'in which books'), not कौन से किताबों में. 2) किन (kin) is only for plural/oblique contexts; for a singular noun before a postposition, कौन से (kaun se) or कौन सी (kaun sii) is still used, not किन (kin).

Sense 2 : whom (plural, oblique, before a postposition)
pronoun Used without an accompanying noun when the referents are understood, e.g. किनसे बात की? (kinse baat kii, 'whom did you talk to?').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This pronoun use of किन (kin) pairs with कौन (kaun) the way मुझे (mujhe) pairs with मैं (main) -- a case-shifted relative you'll recognize once you know the base word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try किसको (kisko) or किसे (kise) (singular oblique) when asking about multiple people -- the plural context calls for किन्हें (kinhen) or किनको (kinko), both built on this same किन (kin) base.
