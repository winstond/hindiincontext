गद्दी (gaddii)

noun Basic sense is a thick cushion or mattress-seat (gaddii); from there it extends to a throne/seat of authority and, in business contexts, a shopkeeper's or moneylender's seat of trade.
Sense 1 : throne, seat of power/authority
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सिंहासन (sinhaasan, throne, literary/formal), राजगद्दी (raajgaddii, royal throne)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense comes straight from the image of a ruler sitting on a raised cushioned seat rather than a Western-style throne -- Mughal and Rajput courts used richly embroidered गद्दी (gaddii) as the actual seat of rule, so गद्दी पर बैठना (gaddii par baithnaa, 'to ascend the throne') still carries that historical flavor even when used for a modern CEO or political successor.
Common Mistakes: 1) गद्दी पर बैठना (gaddii par baithnaa, 'to sit on the throne/to take charge') is the fixed idiom for assuming power or headship -- learners sometimes translate 'take over' literally instead of using this phrase.

Sense 2 : cushion seat, thick padded seat
noun, feminine
Synonyms: तकिया (takiyaa, cushion/pillow, meaning scope), गद्दा (gaddaa, mattress, closely related)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture the low, cushioned seating still common in many Indian homes and shops -- a shopkeeper sitting cross-legged on a गद्दी (gaddii) behind the counter is a very familiar Delhi-NCR bazaar image, and it feeds directly into the 'seat of business' sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse with गद्दा (gaddaa) -- गद्दा (gaddaa) is the mattress itself, while गद्दी (gaddii) is more often the smaller cushioned seat, like the padded seat on a chair, sofa, or traditional low seating.

Sense 3 : shop counter, business seat/establishment
noun, feminine Especially common in trading and moneylending communities, where गद्दी (gaddii) refers to the family firm or its physical seat of business, not just any shop.
Synonyms: दुकान (dukaan, shop, meaning scope), प्रतिष्ठान (pratishthaan, establishment, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Old trading families in markets like Chandni Chowk still talk about 'बैठना गद्दी पर' (baithnaa gaddii par, 'to sit at the business seat') meaning to run the family firm from its traditional seat, often literally a cushioned spot behind ledgers and cash boxes.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense गद्दी (gaddii) implies a traditional, often family-run trading business (cloth merchants, jewelers, moneylenders) rather than a generic modern shop or store -- using it for a supermarket or mall outlet sounds odd.
