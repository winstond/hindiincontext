जोकर (jokar)

noun A loanword from English 'joker,' used literally for a circus/clown figure (or the playing card) and, very commonly, as a figurative insult for someone acting foolish.
Sense 1 : fool, buffoon (insult)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), मसखरा (masakhraa, can be gentler/less harsh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you hear most in daily life, often thrown at someone who has said or done something silly: तुम तो बड़े जोकर हो (tum to bade jokar ho, 'you're such a joker/fool'). It shares the same word as the circus sense because the underlying image -- someone whose behavior invites laughter or ridicule -- is the same, just redirected as an insult.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use जोकर (jokar) as a neutral synonym for 'funny person,' but in everyday speech it more often lands as a put-down -- calling someone जोकर (jokar) implies they are acting ridiculous or not to be taken seriously, closer to 'clown' used insultingly in English than to 'comedian.'
Sample Sentences:
तुम तो बड़े जोकर हो! You're such a joker/fool!
(tum to bade jokar ho!)

Sense 3 : joker (playing card)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Straight from card games like रम्मी (rammii, 'rummy') -- if you have played cards with a Hindi-speaking family, you have likely already heard जोकर (jokar) called out at the table exactly as in English.
