आनंद (aanand)

noun/interjection A warm, literary-leaning noun meaning deep joy or bliss, used both in everyday exclamations and in more reflective/spiritual contexts.
Sense 1 : joy, bliss, delight
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खुशी (khushii), मज़ा (mazaa, everyday, more like 'fun'), प्रसन्नता (prasanntaa, formal), सुख (sukh, leans toward contentment/comfort)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear आनंद (aanand) in phrases like जीवन का आनंद (jiivan kaa aanand, 'the joy of life') or बारिश का आनंद (baarish kaa aanand, 'the pleasure of the rain') -- it has a slightly contemplative, savoring quality, like the joy you notice and sit with rather than just feel in passing. It's also a very common given name for boys in North India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for खुशी (khushii) as the default word for happiness and forget आनंद (aanand) exists as a deeper, richer alternative -- आनंद (aanand) suggests a fuller, more savored joy, not just being pleased about something. 2) आनंद (aanand) is commonly used with आना (aanaa) in the pattern 'X में आनंद आता है' (X men aanand aataa hai, 'there's joy/pleasure in X') -- treating it only as a standalone noun misses this very common construction.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे बारिश में भीगने का बहुत आनंद आता है। I take great joy in getting drenched in the rain.
(mujhe baarish men bhiigne kaa bahut aanand aataa hai।)

Sense 2 : wonderful! / bliss! (exclamation)
interjection Used as a standalone one-word exclamation, often doubled as आनंद आनंद (aanand aanand, 'sheer bliss!') in casual speech.
Synonyms: वाह (vaah, more general exclamation of delight/appreciation)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Said on its own, आनंद (aanand) has a slightly theatrical, old-fashioned charm -- picture someone leaning back after a great meal or a satisfying nap and sighing आनंद (aanand), almost like saying 'bliss!' in English. It's affectionate and a little playful rather than a common everyday exclamation like वाह (vaah).
