भेंट (bhent)

noun Covers two related everyday ideas: (1) a meeting or formal encounter with someone, and (2) a gift or offering presented on such an occasion.
Sense 1 : meeting, encounter (often formal)
noun, feminine Commonly used with होना (honaa) as in किसी से भेंट होना (kisii se bhent honaa, 'to have a meeting with someone') -- the person met is marked with से (se), not को (ko).
Synonyms: मुलाक़ात (mulaaqaat), मुलाकात (mulaakaat, variant spelling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly in news headlines -- प्रधानमंत्री की राष्ट्रपति से भेंट (pradhaanmantrii kii raashtrapti se bhent, 'the Prime Minister's meeting with the President') -- because it carries a formal, dignified weight that plain मुलाक़ात (mulaaqaat) or मिलना (milnaa) lacks in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मिलना (milnaa) in every context, but भेंट (bhent) is the noun used for the meeting itself, especially in slightly formal or written contexts like news reports or official visits -- casual chats among friends are rarely called भेंट (bhent). 2) The person encountered takes से (se): उनसे भेंट हुई (unse bhent huii, 'a meeting with them took place'), not उनको (unko).

प्रधानमंत्री की राष्ट्रपति से भेंट आज सुबह हुई। The Prime Minister's meeting with the President took place this morning.
(pradhaanmantrii kii raashtrapti se bhent aaj subah huii।)

Sense 2 : gift, offering (presented formally)
noun, feminine Used with चढ़ाना (chadhaanaa) in religious contexts (an offering to a deity) or देना (denaa)/भेंट करना (bhent karnaa) for a formal presentation to a person.
Synonyms: उपहार (upahaar), तोहफ़ा (tohfaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up at temples (भगवान को भेंट चढ़ाना (bhagvaan ko bhent chadhaanaa, 'to offer something to God')) and at formal functions, where a chief guest is given a भेंट (bhent) -- often a shawl or memento -- on stage; it carries a ceremonial dignity that a casual gift wouldn't.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense feels more formal or ceremonial than तोहफ़ा (tohfaa) or उपहार (upahaar) -- learners sometimes use it for an everyday birthday present among friends, where तोहफ़ा (tohfaa) sounds more natural. 2) भेंट करना (bhent karnaa) ('to present as a gift') should not be confused with the intransitive meeting sense भेंट होना (bhent honaa) -- the verb pairing changes the meaning entirely.
