दाल-भात (daal-bhaat)

noun A compound noun literally meaning 'lentils-and-rice,' used both for the literal everyday dish and, more broadly, as shorthand for simple, humble, staple food.
Sense 1 : the everyday meal of lentils and rice
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दाल-चावल (daal-chaaval)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: दाल-भात (daal-bhaat) is the bedrock home-cooked meal across much of North India -- simple, quick, and comforting, the kind of thing you eat on a day when nobody feels like cooking anything elaborate. It's the Hindi-belt cousin of what Bengali and Odia households call भात-डाल (bhaat-daal) or similar, just reordered.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as two separate words in a sentence; it functions as a single compound noun and takes singular agreement, e.g. दाल-भात बना है (daal-bhaat banaa hai, 'dal-rice has been made') not बने हैं (bane hain).

Sense 2 : plain, basic, no-frills (fare or life)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as a modifier-like noun to describe something unpretentious, e.g. a dal-bhaat kind of life or dal-bhaat kind of arrangement, contrasted with something fancy.
Synonyms: साधारण खाना (saadhaaran khaanaa, descriptive phrase, not a true synonym)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Beyond the plate, दाल-भात (daal-bhaat) is used the way English speakers might say 'bread and butter' -- to mean something basic and unglamorous but reliable. Someone might say उसकी तो दाल-भात वाली ज़िंदगी है (uskii to daal-bhaat vaalii zindgii hai, 'his is a simple, no-frills life'), invoking modesty and contentment rather than deprivation.
