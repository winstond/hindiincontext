सलाम (salaam)

interjection/noun A greeting/salutation word borrowed from Persian-Arabic via Urdu; primarily used as a respectful or affectionate greeting, and also as a noun meaning a formal salute or greeting gesture.
Sense 1 : greeting exchanged on meeting someone
interjection
Synonyms: नमस्ते (namaste), आदाब (aadaab, formal, especially in Urdu-influenced speech), राम राम (raam raam, regional/rustic variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Delhi you'll hear सलाम (salaam) especially in Muslim neighborhoods and among Urdu-speaking families, often exchanged between an younger person and an elder as a mark of respect. It has a warmer, more old-world courtly flavor than the everyday नमस्ते (namaste), and shows up a lot in Hindi film dialogue set in Lucknow or old Delhi to evoke that refined tehzeeb (etiquette).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes think सलाम (salaam) is interchangeable with नमस्ते (namaste) in all contexts, but it carries a Muslim/Urdu cultural association and can sound out of place in a strictly Hindu religious setting. 2) It is often paired with a name or title in respectful address, as in सलाम, चाचा जी (salaam, chaachaa jii, 'greetings, uncle'), rather than used as a bare standalone word among strangers.
Sample Sentences:

(salaam, chaachaa jii! kaise hain aap?)

Sense 2 : a formal salute, bow, or gesture of respect
noun, masculine Common in the fixed phrase सलाम करना (salaam karnaa, 'to salute/pay respects'), where सलाम (salaam) is the object of करना (karnaa).
Synonyms: प्रणाम (pranaam, more devotional/formal), नमस्कार (namaskaar, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear सलाम ठोकना (salaam thoknaa, 'to snap a salute') used informally and a bit sarcastically about bureaucratic or exaggerated displays of deference, poking fun at over-the-top formality -- a very Delhi way of commenting on office politics.
Common Mistakes: 1) सलाम (salaam) as a noun refers to the act of saluting or the gesture itself, not the word of greeting -- learners sometimes conflate the two senses when translating 'he gave a salaam' versus 'he said salaam.' 2) In formal/military or ceremonial contexts, सलाम (salaam) is used the way 'salute' is in English, e.g. a soldier's सलाम (salaam) to a flag.
