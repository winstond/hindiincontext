के बिना (ke binaa)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'without'; governs the oblique case of the preceding noun/pronoun just like other के-postpositions (के लिए (ke lie), के साथ (ke saath)).
Sense 1 : without
postposition Takes the oblique case of the preceding noun: पानी के बिना (paanii ke binaa, 'without water'). Also commonly pairs with a verb's oblique infinitive to mean 'without doing X': बताए के बिना (bataae ke binaa, 'without telling') -- though बिना (binaa) alone before the infinitive is more common in that construction.
Synonyms: बिना (binaa, one-word variant, does not require के)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear उसके बिना (uske binaa, 'without him/her') and तुम्हारे बिना (tumhaare binaa, 'without you') constantly in Hindi film songs and romantic dialogue -- it's the standard way to express longing or incompleteness, much more emotionally loaded than the plain English 'without' might suggest.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use के बिना (ke binaa) and बिना (binaa) interchangeably in every slot, but बिना (binaa) alone is far more common before a verb form (बिना बताए, binaa bataae, 'without telling'), while के बिना (ke binaa) is more common after a plain noun (उसके बिना, uske binaa, 'without him/her'). 2) The noun before के बिना (ke binaa) must be in the oblique form when it has one, e.g. लड़के के बिना (ladke ke binaa) not लड़का के बिना (ladkaa ke binaa).
