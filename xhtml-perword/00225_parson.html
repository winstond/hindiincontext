परसों (parson)

adverb One word, two mirror-image meanings depending on tense context: 'day before yesterday' when talking about the past, and 'day after tomorrow' when talking about the future.
Sense 1 : day after tomorrow
adverb Context (future-tense verb, or another future time word nearby) is what signals this reading rather than the 'day before yesterday' one.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi collapses 'day before yesterday' and 'day after tomorrow' into one word, and even native speakers occasionally pause mid-sentence to clarify which one they mean -- listen for the verb tense, not the word itself, to know which direction in time is being pointed at.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume परसों (parson) always means 'day before yesterday' because that is the sense usually taught first; the future reading is just as common and depends entirely on the tense of the sentence, not on any change in the word itself. 2) Do not reach for a two-word phrase here -- English needs 'day after tomorrow' but Hindi covers it with this single word.

Sense 2 : day before yesterday
adverb Signaled by past-tense verbs elsewhere in the sentence.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense pairs naturally with कल (kal, 'yesterday/tomorrow') in a little escalating chain Hindi speakers use casually: आज, कल, परसों (aaj, kal, parson, 'today, yesterday/tomorrow, day before/after') -- worth learning as a set.
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this with कल (kal), which covers both 'yesterday' and 'tomorrow' -- परसों (parson) is specifically two days away, never one.
Sample Sentences:
परसों मैं बाज़ार गया था। I went to the market the day before yesterday.
(parson main baazaar gayaa thaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
