कंपनी (kampnii)

noun A loanword from English 'company', used for a business firm and, less commonly, for the idea of companionship or fellow-travelers.
Sense 1 : business firm, corporation
noun, feminine
Synonyms: फ़र्म (farm), संस्था (sansthaa, more formal, used for institutions generally)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly in job talk -- कौन सी कंपनी में काम करते हो? (kaun sii kampnii men kaam karte ho, 'which company do you work at?') is a very standard icebreaker question in Delhi-NCR, where so much conversation revolves around IT and corporate jobs.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use संस्था (sansthaa) as a direct swap, but that word leans toward institutions/organizations (NGOs, trusts) rather than commercial firms -- कंपनी (kampnii) is the everyday word for a business. 2) The plural कंपनियाँ (kampniyaan) is used for 'companies' in general statements (e.g., about the market or industry), which learners often forget to inflect.

Sense 2 : companionship, company (of people)
noun, feminine Common in the fixed phrase कंपनी देना (kampnii denaa, 'to keep someone company') and कंपनी करना (kampnii karnaa, 'to accompany, hang out with')
Synonyms: साथ (saath), संगत (sangat, more about the influence of the company one keeps)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this especially among friends: चलो, थोड़ी कंपनी हो जाएगी (chalo, thodii kampnii ho jaaegii, 'come on, we'll have some company') when inviting someone along so nobody is alone. It carries a warm, informal feel, quite different from the business sense above.
Common Mistakes: 1) कंपनी देना (kampnii denaa) means 'to keep someone company', not 'to give a company' -- learners occasionally take it too literally. 2) साथ (saath) is the more neutral, native way to express 'together/company' in most sentences; कंपनी (kampnii) in this sense feels a touch more colloquial and is often used affectionately or jokingly among friends.
