चुकना (chuknaa)

verb Two connected uses: (1) as a completive auxiliary attached to a verb stem meaning 'to have already done X', and (2) as an independent intransitive verb meaning 'to run out / be used up / be settled (of money, debt, energy, time)'. Its causative counterpart, meaning 'to pay off/settle', is the separate lemma चुकाना (chukaanaa).
Sense 1 : completive auxiliary: 'to have already done (something)'
verb Attaches directly to a verb stem (stem + चुकना, e.g. खा चुकना (khaa chuknaa, 'to have already eaten')) and is almost always used in perfective forms: खा चुका हूँ (khaa chukaa hoon, 'I have already eaten'). It marks that an action is fully completed, often before or in contrast to another event.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi's neat ways of marking 'already-ness' without a separate word like English 'already' -- मैं खाना खा चुका हूँ (main khaanaa khaa chukaa hoon, 'I have already eaten my food') quietly does the work that 'already' does in English. You'll hear it a lot in polite refusals at the dinner table, e.g. जब आप बुलाते हैं तो लोग कहते हैं, 'मैं खा चुका हूँ, शुक्रिया' (main khaa chukaa hoon, shukriyaa, 'I've already eaten, thanks').
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this with सकना (saknaa): both attach to a bare verb stem and look similar in shape, but खा सकता हूँ (khaa saktaa hoon) means 'I can eat' while खा चुका हूँ (khaa chukaa hoon) means 'I have already eaten' -- ability versus completion. 2) Learners sometimes try to use it in the plain present (चुकता है) to mean 'finishes,' when in natural speech it almost always shows up in perfective/past forms describing a completed action.

मैं खाना खा चुका हूँ, शुक्रिया। I've already eaten, thanks.
(main khaanaa khaa chukaa hoon, shukriyaa।)

Sense 2 : to run out, be used up, be settled/cleared (of money, debt, patience, time)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa), समाप्त होना (samaapt honaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the lexical root behind the completive auxiliary sense: something 'chuk jaataa hai' when it has been fully used up or discharged. Its transitive partner चुकाना (chukaanaa) is what you do to a debt -- कर्ज़ चुकाना (karz chukaanaa, 'to pay off a debt') -- while कर्ज़ चुकना (karz chuknaa) describes the debt itself becoming settled.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa) in every context, but चुकना (chuknaa) is the natural choice specifically for money, debts, or limited resources running out or being settled, as in पैसे चुक गए (paise chuk gae, 'the money ran out') or कर्ज़ चुक गया (karz chuk gayaa, 'the debt is cleared').
Sample Sentences:
मेरा सारा कर्ज़ चुक गया है। All my debt has been cleared.
(meraa saaraa karz chuk gayaa hai।)
