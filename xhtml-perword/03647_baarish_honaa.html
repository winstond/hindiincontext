बारिश होना (baarish honaa)

verb A compound verb meaning 'to rain' -- बारिश (baarish, 'rain') paired with the intransitive verb होना (honaa, 'to happen/be'), the standard way Hindi expresses weather events.
Sense 1 : to rain, for rain to occur
verb, intransitive Used impersonally, with no grammatical subject doing the raining -- बारिश (baarish) itself is the grammatical subject and होना (honaa) agrees with it (feminine singular): बारिश हो रही है (baarish ho rahii hai, 'it is raining').
Synonyms: पानी बरसना (paanii barasnaa, colloquial), वर्षा होना (varshaa honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi's monsoon (मानसून, maansoon) is a huge conversational topic every July-August -- बारिश हो रही है (baarish ho rahii hai) or बारिश आने वाली है (baarish aane vaalii hai, 'rain is coming') are things you'll hear constantly as people check the sky before stepping out. Note the related idiom बारिश में भीगना (baarish men bhiignaa, 'to get soaked in the rain'), a nostalgic, almost romantic image in Hindi film and song.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to build an English-style 'it rains' with a dummy subject like यह (yah); Hindi just makes बारिश (baarish) the subject, so 'यह बारिश होती है' is wrong -- say बारिश होती है (baarish hotii hai). 2) Because होना (honaa) is intransitive here, no ने (ne) is ever used, even in the perfective: बारिश हुई (baarish huii, 'it rained'), never 'बारिश ने हुई'. 3) For 'it started raining,' learners often say 'बारिश होना शुरू' instead of the natural compound बारिश होने लगी (baarish hone lagii, 'it started to rain') or बारिश शुरू हो गई (baarish shuroo ho gaii).
