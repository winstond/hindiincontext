गाँठ (gaanth)

noun Primarily a physical knot or tied lump (gaanth), with a well-worn figurative extension to an emotional 'knot' -- a grudge, tension, or unresolved complication between people.
Sense 1 : a knot (in string, rope, cloth, etc.)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of tying a दुपट्टे (dupatte) corner or a rope around a bundle -- that physical action is the anchor for this word, and it's also the image Hindi reaches for when talking about emotional knots (see the next sense).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse गाँठ (gaanth) with जोड़ (jod, 'joint/link') -- गाँठ (gaanth) specifically means a knot tied by twisting/looping, not any connection point. 2) The common verb pairing is गाँठ लगाना (gaanth lagaanaa, 'to tie a knot') and गाँठ खोलना (gaanth kholnaa, 'to undo a knot') -- लगाना (lagaanaa) and खोलना (kholnaa) are the natural verbs here, not बनाना (banaanaa) or करना (karnaa).
Sample Sentences:

(rassii men gaanth pad gaii hai।)

Sense 2 : a lump, node, or swelling (e.g. in a body part, wood, or bamboo)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गिल्टी (giltii, swelling/lump specifically on the body)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this at the doctor's or in casual health talk -- someone might say गले में गाँठ है (gale men gaanth hai, 'there's a lump in the throat') meaning an actual physical swelling, distinct from the English idiom about emotion.

Sense 3 : a grudge, unresolved tension, or emotional 'knot' between people
noun, feminine Almost always appears with दिल में (dil men) or मन में (man men) plus गाँठ पड़ना (gaanth padnaa, 'a knot forms') or गाँठ बाँधना (gaanth baandhnaa, 'to tie a knot' = to hold onto a grudge/resolve firmly).
Synonyms: मनमुटाव (manmutaav, estrangement/friction), शिकायत (shikaayat, complaint/grievance)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image as sense 1, just moved inside the heart: a relationship or memory that has literally 'knotted up' and won't smooth out on its own. दिल में गाँठ पड़ना (dil men gaanth padnaa, 'a knot forms in the heart') is exactly how a Delhi aunty might describe lingering resentment after a family dispute -- something that needs deliberate untying (खोलना, kholnaa), not just time.
Common Mistakes: 1) गाँठ बाँधना (gaanth baandhnaa) can mean either 'to bear a grudge' or, in a different context, 'to firmly resolve/commit something to memory' (gaanth baandh lo, 'mark my words') -- the intended sense depends entirely on context, which surprises learners expecting one fixed meaning. 2) The grudge sense takes दिल में (dil men) or मन में (man men), not पर (par) or को (ko) -- learners sometimes reach for the wrong postposition by analogy with English 'have a grudge against someone'.
