कहीं (kahiin)

adverb An adverb of place meaning 'somewhere/anywhere', which extends into two very common idiomatic uses: as an intensifying 'by any chance' in questions and conditionals, and as an anxious 'what if' marker of unwanted possibility.
Sense 1 : somewhere / anywhere (place)
adverb
Synonyms: किसी जगह (kisii jagah)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the basic locative use: कहीं जाना है? (kahiin jaanaa hai, 'want to go somewhere?') is a very ordinary way to ask if someone has plans, no destination specified.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जगह (jagah) to say 'somewhere', but कहीं (kahiin) alone already carries that meaning as an indefinite location adverb -- no noun needed.
Sample Sentences:
क्या तुम कहीं जा रहे हो? Are you going somewhere?
(kyaa tum kahiin jaa rahe ho?)

Sense 2 : by any chance (intensifier in questions/requests)
adverb Placed early in a yes/no question or polite request to soften it or flag that the speaker suspects an unlikely possibility, e.g. कहीं आपके पास पेन है? (kahiin aapke paas pen hai, 'do you happen to have a pen?').
Synonyms: शायद (shaayad, not a perfect substitute -- shaayad suggests 'maybe' rather than 'by any chance')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the कहीं (kahiin) you hear when someone hesitantly checks a hunch, like a shopkeeper asking कहीं आपके पास छोटे नोट तो नहीं? (kahiin aapke paas chhote not to nahiin, 'you don't happen to have small notes, do you?') -- it softens the ask and signals low expectation.
Common Mistakes: 1) This कहीं (kahiin) does not mean 'somewhere' here -- learners sometimes translate it literally and get confused when there is no place being discussed at all.
Sample Sentences:

(kahiin aapke paas chhote not to nahiin?)

Sense 3 : what if (marker of anxious possibility)
adverb Often opens a clause expressing a feared outcome, frequently paired with न हो (n ho) or a plain subjunctive: कहीं वह नाराज़ न हो जाए (kahiin vah naaraaz n ho jaae, 'what if he gets upset').
Synonyms: अगर (agar, not equivalent -- agar is neutral 'if', while kahiin carries worry)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the worried-parent word -- कहीं बारिश न हो जाए (kahiin baarish n ho jaae, 'what if it rains') said while glancing at the sky is exactly the tone this sense carries: bracing for something you hope won't happen.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar with अगर (agar) for 'if' may skip कहीं (kahiin) entirely, but native speakers add it specifically to flag anxiety or unwanted risk, which अगर (agar) alone doesn't convey.
Sample Sentences:
कहीं बारिश न हो जाए, इसलिए छत पर कपड़े मत छोड़ो। What if it rains -- so don't leave the clothes on the roof.
(kahiin baarish n ho jaae, islie chhat par kapde mat chhodo।)
