बनना (bannaa)

verb Covers four senses: to become/turn into, to be made/built/prepared, to pretend/pose as, and (in a negated impersonal construction with a -ता/ते participle) to fail to come together for someone. Note also that बनना (bannaa) productively combines with nouns (बात बनना (baat bannaa), काम बनना (kaam bannaa)) to form idiomatic expressions with specialized, non-literal meanings -- these are treated as their own separate headwords, not senses of this word.
Sense 1 : to become, turn into
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: होना (honaa, to become/happen), तब्दील होना (tabdiil honaa, to be transformed into)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the most basic, all-purpose sense of बनना (bannaa) and the one to internalize first -- it is the mirror image of बनाना (banaanaa), 'to make,' the way English pairs 'become' with 'make.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for होना (honaa) where बनना (bannaa) is needed for a change of state -- वह डॉक्टर बना (vah doktar banaa, 'he became a doctor') sounds natural, while वह डॉक्टर हुआ (vah doktar huaa) sounds odd for this meaning. 2) The complement (what someone becomes) stays in the nominative, not oblique -- वह अफ़सर बना (vah afsar banaa), not a marked form.

(bade hokar tum kyaa bannaa chaahte ho?)

Sense 2 : to be made, built, prepared, formed
verb, intransitive Functions as the natural passive-like counterpart of बनाना (banaanaa) when the agent is unspecified or unimportant -- खाना बन गया (khaanaa ban gayaa, 'the food is ready/made') is far more common than any explicit passive construction.
Synonyms: तैयार होना (taiyaar honaa, especially for food), निर्मित होना (nirmit honaa, formal, for buildings/structures)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask anyone in a Delhi kitchen 'खाना बन गया?' (khaanaa ban gayaa?, 'is the food ready?') and this is exactly the sense in play -- बनना (bannaa) treats the finished dish as having come into being on its own, which is a very Hindi way of downplaying the cook's effort.
Common Mistakes: 1) For food, khaanaa is often the subject with बनना (bannaa) rather than pakanaa, which learners sometimes overuse -- चाय बन रही है (chaay ban rahii hai, 'tea is being made') is the everyday phrasing. 2) Do not confuse this with the 'become' sense: यह मकान पत्थर से बना है (yah makaan patthar se banaa hai, 'this house is made of stone') uses से (se) to mark material, not a change of identity.

Sense 3 : to pretend to be, pose as, act like
verb, intransitive Takes the pretended identity as a direct nominative complement, just like the 'become' sense, but the context (often with बनना (bannaa) in combination with जैसे (jaise) or an accusing tone) signals insincerity rather than real change.
Synonyms: ढोंग करना (dhong karnaa, to put on an act), नाटक करना (naatak karnaa, colloquial, to make a scene/pretend)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the बनना (bannaa) of playground and office gossip alike -- बड़ा आदमी बनता है (badaa aadmii bantaa hai, 'he acts like he's a big shot') is a classic put-down you will hear constantly in Delhi banter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Context alone distinguishes this from the literal 'become' sense -- वह अनजान बन गया (vah anjaan ban gayaa) means 'he acted like he didn't know,' not that he actually became ignorant. 2) Learners sometimes miss the mildly critical/sarcastic tone this sense usually carries in conversation.

Sense 4 : (impersonal, usually negated, after a -ता/ते participle) for an action to fail to come together for someone; to not manage to do something
verb, intransitive Impersonal construction: [subject]+से + [verb stem]+ता/ते + नहीं + बनना, e.g. मुझसे यह काम करते नहीं बनता (mujhse yah kaam karte nahiin bantaa, 'I can't manage to get this work done'). The logical subject is marked with से (se), not ने (ne) or को (ko); बनना (bannaa) itself stays third-person singular regardless of the doer, and the participle typically appears in its habitual/imperfective (-ता/ते) form rather than agreeing with any object.
Synonyms: न हो पाना (n ho paanaa, to not turn out to be possible), न सँभलना (n sambhalnaa, for something to not come under control)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This impersonal से...नहीं बनता (se...nahiin bantaa) frame is a distinctively Hindi way of expressing helplessness -- it removes the person as an active agent and instead says the action itself simply refused to happen for them, much like how लगना (lagnaa) makes feelings arrive at you rather than something you actively feel.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners instinctively reach for a को (ko)-marked subject as with लगना (lagnaa) or चाहिए (chaahie), but this construction takes से (se) instead -- उससे बोलते नहीं बनता (usse bolte nahiin bantaa, 'he can't manage to speak up'), not usko. 2) The verb बनना (bannaa) does not agree with the object of the embedded verb; it is frozen in the third-person singular masculine (बनता (bantaa)) regardless of what the sentence is about. 3) This is easily confused with the ordinary 'become' sense -- without the negated -ता/ते participle frame, बनना (bannaa) reverts to its literal meaning.
