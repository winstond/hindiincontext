घर (ghar)

noun/adverb Primarily 'house/home' (noun), but also anchors common idioms and adverbial phrases about domestic life; one core sense with several idiomatic extensions.
Sense 1 : house, home
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मकान (makaan, building/structure), घराना (gharaanaa, household/lineage), आवास (aavaas, formal), निवास (nivaas, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: घर (ghar) is one of the most emotionally loaded words in Hindi -- it means far more than a building; it carries family, belonging, and roots. Notice how अपना घर (apnaa ghar, 'one's own home') is used to talk about security and identity, not just property, and how a married woman's घर (ghar) can shift meaning depending on whether she means her parents' home or her husband's -- a distinction that carries real cultural weight in North Indian families.
Common Mistakes: 1) घर (ghar) covers both the physical building and the emotional idea of 'home' -- English speakers sometimes reach for मकान (makaan) when they mean 'home,' but मकान (makaan) is just the structure and feels cold or purely real-estate-ish for that context. 2) घर जाना (ghar jaanaa, 'to go home') does not take a postposition before घर (ghar) the way English needs 'to' -- learners often insert को (ko) unnecessarily.

Sense 2 : at home, homeward (in fixed adverbial phrases)
adverb Functions adverbially without a postposition in set phrases like घर जाना (ghar jaanaa, 'to go home') and घर पहुँचना (ghar pahunchnaa, 'to reach home'), unlike most nouns of place which take पर (par) or में (men).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This dual behavior -- postposition-free with motion verbs, but needing पर (par) with stative ones like होना (honaa) or रहना (rahnaa) -- is one of those small native-speaker instincts that just has to be absorbed through hearing the phrases repeatedly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add में (men) or पर (par) after घर (ghar) in phrases like घर हूँ (ghar hoon) when native speakers simply say घर पर हूँ (ghar par hoon, 'I am at home') -- पर (par) is actually required here, unlike with जाना (jaanaa) or पहुँचना (pahunchnaa), which drop any postposition entirely.
