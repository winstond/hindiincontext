धक्का (dhakkaa)

noun Basic meaning is a physical push or shove (dhakkaa); extends naturally to a jolt/shock (emotional or situational setback) and appears in a few very common set phrases like धक्का लगना (dhakkaa lagnaa) and धक्का देना (dhakkaa denaa).
Sense 1 : a physical push or shove
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ठेला (thelaa, colloquial), झटका (jhatkaa, overlaps for a jolt/jerk)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in Delhi's crowded buses and metro stations -- धक्का मत दो (dhakkaa mat do, 'don't push') is practically a public transport catchphrase. The word also shows up in धक्का-मुक्की (dhakkaa-mukkii), the reduplicated form describing general jostling and shoving in a crowd.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use धक्का (dhakkaa) only for deliberate shoving, but it equally covers an accidental bump or jostle, like being pushed in a crowded bus. 2) The common verb pairing is धक्का देना (dhakkaa denaa, 'to give a push') or धक्का लगना (dhakkaa lagnaa, 'to receive/experience a push'), not मारना (maarnaa) as a learner might guess from English 'to give a shove.'
Sample Sentences:
मेट्रो में इतनी भीड़ थी कि किसी ने पीछे से जोर का धक्का दे दिया। The metro was so crowded that someone gave a hard push from behind.
(metro men itnii bhiid thii ki kisii ne piichhe se jor kaa dhakkaa de diyaa।)

Sense 2 : a shock, jolt, or setback (figurative)
noun, masculine Common frame: [person/thing] को धक्का लगना (ko dhakkaa lagnaa) -- 'to be shocked/dealt a blow,' using a dative-subject construction the learner has already met with लगना (lagnaa).
Synonyms: झटका (jhatkaa), आघात (aaghaat, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word people use when describing an emotional gut-punch -- a sudden death in the family, a failed relationship, or unexpected bad news all get described with धक्का लगना (dhakkaa lagnaa). It captures the same 'something hit me' framing that Hindi loves, treating the shock as an external force acting on you rather than something you actively feel.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for शॉक (shok, the English loanword) when धक्का (dhakkaa) is the more natural, everyday Hindi choice for news that hits you hard, like bad exam results or a business loss. 2) The subject of the shock takes को (ko), not ने (ne) -- मुझे बहुत धक्का लगा (mujhe bahut dhakkaa lagaa, 'I was quite shocked'), following the same pattern as other लगना (lagnaa) constructions.

उसकी मौत की खबर सुनकर मुझे बहुत धक्का लगा। Hearing the news of his death gave me a huge shock.
