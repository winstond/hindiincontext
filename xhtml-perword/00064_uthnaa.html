उठना (uthnaa)

verb Covers physical rising -- getting up, standing up, waking up -- as well as extended senses like dust/smoke/prices/sound rising, a topic coming up in conversation, and a grammaticalized compound-verb use that marks sudden onset when attached to another verb stem. Its transitive causative counterpart उठाना (uthaanaa, 'to lift/pick up') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to get up / stand up
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: खड़ा होना (khadaa honaa, to stand up)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A parent's most common morning line is something like बस उठ जाओ (bas uth jaao, 'just get up already') -- said on repeat before school or work. The word covers everything from getting off a chair to getting out of bed, so it does a lot of daily work.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for खड़ा होना (khadaa honaa) in every context, but उठना (uthnaa) is the natural choice for getting out of bed, off a chair, or off the floor, while खड़ा होना (khadaa honaa) emphasizes the resulting standing posture.

(subah chhah baje utho, varnaa skool ke lie der ho jaaegii।)

Sense 5 : grammaticalized vector verb marking sudden onset (V-stem + उठना)
verb, intransitive Attaches to the stem of another verb (V + उठना (uthnaa)) as a compound/vector verb, marking a sudden, spontaneous onset -- most common with verbs of emotion, sound, or momentum: चिल्ला उठना (chillaa uthnaa, 'to suddenly scream'), चौंक उठना (chaunk uthnaa, 'to be suddenly startled'), लजा उठना (lajaa uthnaa, 'to suddenly turn shy'), झूम उठना (jhoom uthnaa, 'to be swept up, swaying with feeling'), गूँज उठना (goonj uthnaa, 'to suddenly resound'), बोल उठना (bol uthnaa, 'to suddenly speak up/cut in'). It layers a sense of abruptness -- being overtaken by a feeling or sound -- onto the main verb, and with speech verbs it can carry a mild flavor of impulsiveness or interruption.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi leans heavily on 'vector verbs' like this to add drama and immediacy, especially in storytelling and dialogue -- a character does not just चिल्लाना (chillaanaa, 'scream') but चिल्ला उठना (chillaa uthnaa, 'suddenly scream out'), which feels far more alive. It sits in the same compound-verb family as उठ जाना (uth jaanaa) and निकल पड़ना (nikal padnaa), a pattern worth watching for once you notice it.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not a separate word to memorize in isolation -- it is उठना (uthnaa) itself, grammaticalized onto another verb's stem, so learners should recognize the pattern rather than treat each combination as a new vocabulary item. 2) Learners sometimes use this construction for slow, deliberate actions; it specifically signals suddenness or being swept up, not a planned or gradual process.

(yah sunkar vah chillaa uthii।)
