तकिया (takiyaa)

noun Primarily the everyday word for 'pillow'; also has a lesser but real use meaning a small shrine or resting place of a Sufi saint (as in the place name तकिया).
Sense 1 : pillow, cushion
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गद्दी (gaddii, cushion, meaning overlap only), मसनद (masnad, literary, bolster/cushion)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A staple word from daily domestic life -- you will hear it constantly in the phrase तकिया लगाकर बैठना (takiyaa lagaakar baithnaa, 'to sit propped up against a pillow'), a very common way of relaxing on a diwan or bed in North Indian homes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse तकिया (takiyaa) with गद्दा (gaddaa), which is the mattress, not the pillow -- the two are separate items on a Hindi bed. 2) तकिया (takiyaa) is masculine, so possessive agreement is मेरा तकिया (meraa takiyaa, 'my pillow'), not मेरी तकिया (merii takiyaa).

Sense 2 : a habitual prop, crutch, mainstay (figurative, mainly in the idiom तकिया कलाम)
noun, masculine Almost exclusively encountered in the fixed compound तकिया कलाम (takiyaa kalaam), meaning a person's verbal tic or pet phrase -- literally 'pillow of speech,' something one leans on while talking.
Synonyms: सहारा (sahaaraa, support, broader meaning)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative sense survives mainly in the one compound तकिया कलाम (takiyaa kalaam) -- Hindi speakers love pointing out a friend's तकिया कलाम (takiyaa kalaam) as a bit of gentle teasing.
Common Mistakes: 1) तकिया कलाम (takiyaa kalaam) is a fixed noun phrase and should not be split or reordered -- it refers to a filler phrase someone overuses, like 'you know' or 'basically' in English.
