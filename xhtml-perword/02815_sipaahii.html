सिपाही (sipaahii)

noun A single core meaning: a soldier or, in everyday use, a policeman/constable -- the word stretches naturally from the battlefield to the beat cop on the corner.
Sense 1 : policeman, constable
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पुलिसवाला (pulisvaalaa), हवलदार (havaldaar, often a specific rank), कांस्टेबल (kaanstebal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Walk past any Delhi crossing and the khaki-clad constable directing traffic is the सिपाही (sipaahii) of daily life, not a soldier -- this is the sense most learners will actually hear used around them. It carries a whiff of the lowest rung of the police hierarchy, so it is respectful but not deferential.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume सिपाही (sipaahii) only means 'soldier' and are confused when it is used for a traffic or beat constable -- in everyday Delhi-NCR speech this is actually the more common referent. 2) It is a low-ranking title; using it for a senior police officer (an अफ़सर (afsar) or inspector) sounds off or even mildly dismissive.

Sense 2 : soldier
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जवान (javaan), सैनिक (sainik, slightly more formal/military register)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the older, historically rooted sense -- the word comes down from Mughal-era military vocabulary (compare the English loanword 'sepoy,' which is exactly this word) and still surfaces in stories about the freedom struggle and in words like विद्रोही सिपाही (vidrohii sipaahii, 'rebel sepoy'). In contemporary speech जवान (javaan) is often preferred for a soldier on active duty, with सिपाही (sipaahii) sounding a touch more old-fashioned or literary/historical in this sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) सिपाही (sipaahii) denotes a rank-and-file soldier, not an officer -- calling a commissioned officer सिपाही (sipaahii) understates their rank.
