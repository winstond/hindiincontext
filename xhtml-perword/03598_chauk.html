चौक (chauk)

noun Mainly a noun meaning a town square, crossroads, or open junction area in a city or market; also used as the name-element in intersection names (e.g. Connaught Place area landmarks). Has a secondary decorative-pattern sense (a checkered or diamond-grid design, and the ritual floor-drawing sense) related by the idea of a marked-off square shape.
Sense 1 : town square / crossroads / market junction
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चौराहा (chauraahaa), चौबारा (chaubaaraa, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Old Delhi is full of these -- चांदनी चौक (chaandnii chauk, 'Chandni Chowk') is the most famous चौक (chauk) in the country, a bustling market square that has given its name to the whole neighborhood. When a Delhi-ite says 'चौक पर मिलते हैं' (chauk par milte hain, 'let's meet at the chowk'), they mean a well-known local landmark junction, not just any crossing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse चौक (chauk) with चौराहा (chauraahaa) -- चौक (chauk) is the wider notion of a market square or named city junction (often part of a proper place name, like सदर बाज़ार चौक (sadar baazaar chauk, 'Sadar Bazaar Chowk')), while चौराहा (chauraahaa) is specifically a four-way road crossing.

Sense 2 : checkered/diamond pattern; ritual square design
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रंगोली (rangolii, related ritual-art term, not exact synonym)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in everyday phrases like चौक भरना (chauk bharnaa, 'to fill in a decorative square') for the auspicious floor patterns drawn during festivals and weddings, and in the checkered pattern seen on some traditional textiles and games boards -- the same root idea of a marked-off square shape as the town-square sense.
