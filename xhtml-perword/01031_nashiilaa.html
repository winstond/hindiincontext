नशीला (nashiilaa)

adjective An adjective describing something intoxicating or heady, related to the noun नशा (nashaa, intoxication/addiction) -- used both for literal substances and for figurative things like a voice, smell, or beauty that has an intoxicating effect.
Sense 1 : intoxicating (literal)
adjective
Synonyms: मादक (maadak, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word used for anything from शराब (sharaab, liquor) to the धुआँ (dhuaan, smoke) of a cigarette -- it describes the substance's power to intoxicate, not the drinker's state.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use नशीला (nashiilaa) as a noun meaning 'an intoxicated person' -- it only describes the intoxicating quality of a thing (a drink, a smell, a smoke), never the state of a person who has drunk it; for that, use नशे में (nashe men, intoxicated/drunk).

Sense 2 : intoxicating, heady (figurative -- of a voice, smell, beauty, etc.)
adjective
Synonyms: मादक (maadak, formal), सुरीला (suriilaa, meaning scope: only for a heady/melodious voice or sound)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense you meet constantly in Hindi film songs and shayari (shaayrii, poetry) -- a नशीली आवाज़ (nashiilii aavaaz, 'an intoxicating voice') or नशीली आँखें (nashiilii aankhen, 'intoxicating eyes') describes someone whose charm has a dizzying, almost drugging effect on the listener or viewer. It is a favorite word of romantic poets precisely because it borrows the physical pull of नशा (nashaa, intoxication) to describe an emotional or aesthetic one.
