धोखा देना (dhokhaa denaa)

verb A compound verb built from धोखा (dhokhaa, 'deceit/trick') plus देना (denaa, 'to give') -- literally 'to give deceit,' i.e. to cheat, deceive, or betray someone.
Sense 1 : to cheat, deceive, betray (a person, in relationships/trust/business)
verb, transitive Takes the person deceived marked with को (ko): उसने मुझे धोखा दिया (usne mujhe dhokhaa diyaa, 'he/she deceived me').
Synonyms: छलना (chhalnaa, literary), फ़रेब करना (fareb karnaa), बेवफ़ाई करना (bevfaaii karnaa, used specifically for romantic betrayal), ठगना (thagnaa, stronger, implies swindling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase carries real emotional weight in Hindi -- it's the go-to phrase in film and daily conversation for betrayal in love, friendship, or business, and pairs naturally with भरोसा (bharosaa, 'trust') and वफ़ा (vafaa, 'faithfulness'), as in भरोसा तोड़ना (bharosaa todnaa, 'to break trust'). Its mirror-image partner is धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to be cheated/deceived'), which every learner should learn alongside this one since Hindi treats the giver and receiver of deceit as two distinct verbal frames.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use से (se) instead of को (ko) for the person deceived -- it is धोखा किसी को देना (dhokhaa kisii ko denaa), not धोखा किसी से देना (dhokhaa kisii se denaa). 2) Don't confuse with धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to be deceived') -- देना (denaa) is for the one doing the deceiving, खाना (khaanaa) for the one deceived. 3) As a transitive compound verb with देना (denaa), it takes ने (ne) marking on the subject in the perfective, just as regular transitive verbs do.
