तारीख़ (taariikh)

noun Means 'date' (calendar date), and by extension a 'historic date' or occasionally 'history' in a formal sense. Also spelled तारीख ((taariikh)) without the nuqta -- both are common.
Sense 1 : calendar date
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दिनांक (dinaank, formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi-ite for the तारीख़ (taariikh) and they'll rattle off just the number plus month, like पंद्रह अगस्त (pandrah agast, '15 August') -- no need for 'the' or ordinal endings like in English. In everyday government-office or court speak, this word also carries a slightly weary sense of 'yet another date given, nothing resolved yet', a very familiar bureaucratic rhythm in India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often ask 'क्या तारीख़ है?' (kyaa taariikh hai?, 'what is the date?') correctly, but forget that months are usually just said as numbers plus the month name (e.g. दस तारीख़ (das taariikh, 'the 10th')) rather than using ordinal forms like English 'tenth'. 2) In court/legal or bureaucratic contexts, तारीख़ (taariikh) can mean a scheduled 'hearing date' or 'appointment date', not just any date -- a lawyer saying अगली तारीख़ (aglii taariikh, 'next date') means the next court date.

Sense 2 : historic date / history (formal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: इतिहास (itihaas, broader/more common word for 'history')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense in slightly grander or literary speech -- तारीख़ में दर्ज होना (taariikh men darj honaa, 'to be recorded in history') is a common phrase in news anchor style commentary about a momentous event, similar to English 'go down in history'.
