ग़रीब (ghariib)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'poor' (economically), with an extended noun sense referring to poor people collectively, and a common figurative use for someone humble or meek. Also spelled गरीब (gariib) without the nuqta, a very common simplification in casual writing.
Sense 1 : poor, economically disadvantaged
adjective
Synonyms: निर्धन (nirdhan, formal), दरिद्र (daridr, literary), कंगाल (kangaal, destitute)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first adjectives learners meet, often paired with अमीर (amiir, 'rich') as a stock opposite pair, much like Hindi film plots built around 'अमीर और ग़रीब' (amiir aur ghariib, 'the rich and the poor') love stories. The nuqta under the ग़ marks its Persian/Urdu origin, but in everyday spoken Hindi almost nobody distinguishes ग़ from ग in pronunciation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write गरीब (gariib); both spellings are in everyday use, so this is not really an error, just a style choice. 2) कंगाल (kangaal) implies total destitution and is stronger than ग़रीब (ghariib) -- don't treat them as interchangeable in normal conversation.

Sense 2 : a poor person; the poor (collectively)
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun especially in the plural or generic sense, e.g. ग़रीबों की मदद (ghariibon kii madad, 'help for the poor'), often in the context of charity, government schemes, or social commentary.
Synonyms: निर्धन (nirdhan, formal), दीन-हीन (diin-hiin, destitute and helpless, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this noun sense constantly in the context of दान (daan, 'charity') and सरकारी योजनाएँ (sarkaarii yojnaaen, 'government schemes') aimed at ग़रीबों (ghariibon) -- it's a staple of news broadcasts and political speeches in India.

Sense 3 : humble, meek, harmless (figurative, of a person's nature)
adjective Used affectionately or pityingly about a person's temperament rather than their finances, e.g. calling someone a 'ग़रीब आदमी' (ghariib aadmii, 'harmless/meek fellow') even if they are financially comfortable.
Synonyms: बेचारा (bechaaraa, poor thing, pitiable), मासूम (maasoom, innocent)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a lovely bit of everyday Hindi color: a mother might say उसे मत डराओ, वो तो ग़रीब है (use mat daraao, vo to ghariib hai, 'don't scare him, he's such a harmless soul') about a shy child, with zero reference to income. It overlaps closely with बेचारा (bechaaraa) in tone, sympathetic and a little protective.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense has nothing to do with money -- learners sometimes miss that a wealthy person can be called ग़रीब (ghariib) to mean 'meek' or 'timid', which can seem contradictory at first.
Sample Sentences:

(use mat daraao, vo to ghariib hai।)
