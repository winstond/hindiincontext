सारा (saaraa)

adjective An adjective meaning 'whole, entire, all of' -- used with singular nouns to mean the totality of a thing, and in the plural to mean 'all' (of a set). Closely related in meaning to पूरा (pooraa) but with its own shade of use.
Sense 1 : whole, entire (of a single mass or thing)
adjective
Synonyms: पूरा (pooraa), समस्त (samast, formal), संपूर्ण (sampoorn, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear सारा दिन (saaraa din, 'the whole day') and सारी रात (saarii raat, 'the whole night') constantly in complaints about work or exhaustion -- it is the classic Hindi way of stretching time to make a point, much like English 'all day long.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पूरा (pooraa) and सारा (saaraa) interchangeably, which mostly works, but सारा (saaraa) leans toward 'the entire extent of' (सारा दिन (saaraa din, 'the whole day')) while पूरा (pooraa) leans toward 'complete, not lacking anything' (पूरा काम (pooraa kaam, 'the complete/finished work')) -- native speakers feel the difference even though both are often acceptable.

Sense 2 : all (of a countable set, in the plural)
adjective In the plural (सारे, सारी), agrees with the noun and means 'all' of a group, functioning much like सब (sab) but placed before the noun as a modifier.
Synonyms: सब (sab), तमाम (tamaam)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: सारे लोग (saare log, 'all the people') and सब लोग (sab log, 'all the people') are both extremely common and largely interchangeable in casual speech -- don't overthink choosing between them.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place सारे (saare) after the noun the way सब (sab) can sometimes float; सारा (saaraa) and its forms are used strictly before the noun they modify.
