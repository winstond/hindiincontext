उगना (ugnaa)

verb A single, concrete sense: 'to grow / sprout / rise' -- used for plants sprouting and for the sun/moon/stars rising.
Sense 1 : to grow, sprout (of plants)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: पैदा होना (paidaa honaa), उपजना (upajnaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a seed pushing itself up out of the soil on its own -- that self-starting quality is why this verb is intransitive. Its causative partner उगाना (ugaanaa) is the word you reach for the moment a farmer or gardener is doing the growing.
Common Mistakes: 1) उगना (ugnaa) is intransitive -- the plant itself is the subject that sprouts. Do not confuse with उगाना (ugaanaa), the causative 'to grow (something), to cultivate,' which needs ने (ne) with a transitive subject: किसान ने गेहूँ उगाया (kisaan ne gehoon ugaayaa, 'the farmer grew wheat') versus गेहूँ उगा (gehoon ugaa, 'the wheat grew/sprouted').
Sample Sentences:
बारिश के बाद घास तेज़ी से उगती है। After the rain, grass grows quickly.
(baarish ke baad ghaas tezii se ugtii hai।)

Sense 2 : to rise (of sun, moon, stars)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: निकलना (nikalnaa), उदय होना (uday honaa, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi extends the same verb from a seed breaking through soil to the sun breaking over the horizon -- both are things that emerge upward into view, which is a nice bit of shared imagery to hold onto.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for निकलना (nikalnaa) for 'the sun rose,' which is also correct and more common in casual speech -- सूरज उगना (sooraj ugnaa) sounds a touch more descriptive/literary but is still everyday usable, especially with सूरज (sooraj) and चाँद (chaand).
Sample Sentences:

(sooraj poorab se ugtaa hai।)
