चूड़ी (choodii)

noun A single-sense everyday noun: a bangle, the round glass or metal ornament worn on the wrist, almost always used in the plural (चूड़ियाँ, choodiyaan).
Sense 1 : bangle
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कंगन (kangan, a thicker/sturdier bangle, often metal), कड़ा (kadaa, a thick solid bangle, esp. worn by men or as a religious symbol)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चूड़ियाँ (choodiyaan) are a loaded cultural symbol in North India -- a married woman traditionally wears them (often red and green, or gold), and the sound of glass bangles clinking (चूड़ियों की खनक, choodiyon kii khanak, 'the tinkling of bangles') is a classic poetic and filmi image for a woman's presence or grace. You'll also hear चूड़ियाँ पहनना (choodiyaan pahannaa, 'to wear bangles') used insultingly toward men to mock their courage, implying they're behaving timidly like a woman -- a sexist idiom still heard in colloquial speech, though increasingly called out.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use चूड़ी (choodii) in the singular when talking about someone wearing bangles, but native speakers almost always say चूड़ियाँ (choodiyaan) since they're worn as a set on the wrist, not one at a time. 2) Don't confuse with कंगन (kangan) or कड़ा (kadaa) -- चूड़ी (choodii) specifically evokes the thin, often colorful glass or lac bangles typically associated with women's everyday and festive wear, while कड़ा (kadaa) is thicker and can be worn by anyone, including as a Sikh religious article.
