जो (jo)

pronoun/adjective/conjunction The core relative pronoun/adjective of Hindi ('who, which, that'), almost always paired with a correlative वह (vah) or उस (us) later in the sentence; it also appears as a conditional/concessive conjunction ('if').
Sense 1 : relative pronoun -- who, which, that
pronoun Sets up a relative clause that is completed by a correlative pronoun (वह (vah), वे (ve), उसको (usko), etc.) later in the sentence -- the जो...वह (jo...vah) frame.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the backbone of Hindi's relative-correlative system, which works quite differently from English embedding: instead of tucking 'who/which' clauses inside a sentence, Hindi tends to state the जो (jo)-clause first as its own unit and then resolve it with वह (vah) -- 'jo ... so/vah ...'. Once you get comfortable hearing sentences open with जो (jo), the whole rhythm of Hindi complex sentences starts to click.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget to supply the correlative वह (vah)/वे (ve) in the main clause, leaving the sentence feeling incomplete to a native ear -- जो आदमी आया, वह मेरा दोस्त है (jo aadmii aayaa, vah meraa dost hai, 'the man who came is my friend') needs that वह (vah). 2) English speakers try to place जो (jo) mid-sentence right before the noun it modifies as English does with 'who/which'; Hindi instead often opens the whole sentence with the जो (jo)-clause, then resolves it with the correlative.

Sense 2 : relative adjective -- whichever, that (modifying a following noun)
adjective Directly modifies a noun in the relative clause (जो किताब, jo kitaab, 'whichever book') rather than standing alone as the head of the clause.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Functionally this is the same जो (jo) as the relative pronoun, just doing adjective work by sitting directly in front of a noun -- Hindi doesn't force a hard line between 'which' as a standalone word and 'which' as a modifier the way English sometimes does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use जो (jo) alone where a noun is expected, forgetting it needs a noun right after it in this use, e.g. जो लड़की (jo ladkii, 'whichever girl') not just जो (jo) meaning 'the girl'.

Sense 3 : if/whoever/whatever -- introducing a conditional or open-ended clause
conjunction Used in fixed frames like जो कोई (jo koii, 'whoever'), जो भी (jo bhii, 'whatever/whoever it may be'), and in some conditional-flavored proverbs and set phrases where it functions closer to अगर (agar) than to a plain relative pronoun.
Synonyms: अगर (agar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in sayings and everyday advice-giving, e.g. जो होगा देखा जाएगा (jo hogaa dekhaa jaaegaa, 'whatever happens, we'll deal with it') -- a very common bit of Hindi fatalistic-but-optimistic wisdom that leans on this open-ended sense of जो (jo).
Common Mistakes: 1) In the जो भी (jo bhii) and जो कोई (jo koii) frames, learners often translate word-for-word and miss that these are fixed idiomatic units meaning 'whatever/whoever,' not a relative clause needing its own separate correlative.
