झटका (jhatkaa)

noun Primarily a physical or emotional 'jerk/jolt/shock' (noun); also names the Sikh method of animal slaughter by a single strike, and appears as part of the compound verb frame jhatkaa denaa / jhatkaa lagnaa.
Sense 1 : a sudden jerk, jolt, or physical shock
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धक्का (dhakkaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the jolt you feel when a bus brakes suddenly, or the tug you give a stuck door -- झटका (jhatkaa) captures that abrupt, physical suddenness. It is the same word Hindi speakers reach for to describe a bumpy rickshaw ride jerking to a stop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use झटका (jhatkaa) as a verb directly; it is a noun and needs a light verb like लगना (lagnaa) or देना (denaa) -- e.g. झटका लगना (jhatkaa lagnaa, 'to receive a jolt') or झटका देना (jhatkaa denaa, 'to give a jerk/pull').

Sense 2 : a sudden emotional shock or blow (news, loss, betrayal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सदमा (sadmaa, stronger, used for grief or trauma), आघात (aaghaat, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for that gut-punch feeling when you hear sudden bad news -- a friend's accident, a failed exam result, a sudden price hike. Delhi speakers say मुझे बड़ा झटका लगा (mujhe badaa jhatkaa lagaa, 'I got a big shock') all the time in everyday conversation about disappointments big and small.
Common Mistakes: 1) झटका (jhatkaa) suggests a sharp, sudden emotional hit (like unexpected bad news), whereas सदमा (sadmaa) implies deeper, longer-lasting trauma -- learners sometimes use them interchangeably. 2) The frame is usually झटका लगना (jhatkaa lagnaa, 'to feel a shock') with a को-marked experiencer, e.g. मुझे झटका लगा (mujhe jhatkaa lagaa, 'I got a shock') -- not एक झटका किया (ek jhatkaa kiyaa), which sounds wrong.

Sense 3 : a method of slaughtering an animal with a single quick strike (as opposed to halal), and the meat prepared this way
noun, masculine Used attributively as in झटका मीट (jhatkaa miit, 'jhatka meat') or झटका मुर्गा (jhatkaa murgaa, 'jhatka chicken').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word on butcher shop signboards in Punjabi-dominated markets around Delhi-NCR, like झटका मीट यहाँ मिलता है (jhatkaa miit yahaan miltaa hai, 'jhatka meat available here') -- a practical, everyday religious-dietary distinction rather than an abstract one.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is specific to meat and religious practice (associated with Sikh tradition) and contrasts with हलाल (halaal); it should not be confused with the general 'jolt' sense even though the word is identical.
