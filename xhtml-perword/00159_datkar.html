डटकर (datkar)

adverb An adverbial participle formed from डटना (datnaa, 'to stand firm/hold one's ground') plus कर (kar); it describes doing something firmly, resolutely, or with full commitment.
Sense 1 : firmly, resolutely, with full commitment
adverb Formed from the conjunctive participle of डटना (datnaa) and typically pairs with verbs like खड़े होना (khade honaa, 'to stand'), मुकाबला करना (mukaablaa karnaa, 'to face/compete'), or पढ़ना (padhnaa, 'to study'), meaning to do that action with sustained effort or resolve.
Synonyms: मज़बूती से (mazbootii se), दृढ़ता से (dridhtaa se, more formal), पूरी ताकत से (poorii taakat se, with full strength)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear डटकर (datkar) a lot in the context of exams, sports, and hard work -- a mother telling her son डटकर पढ़ो (datkar padho, 'study hard, with full focus') is a very familiar Delhi household scene. It carries a sense of standing your ground under pressure, echoing the base verb डटना (datnaa), which is also used for soldiers holding a position.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate it too literally as 'sticking' or 'jamming,' missing that in modern usage it almost always means facing a challenge or working hard with resolve, not the physical sense of being stuck. 2) It is rarely used alone; it usually modifies a verb of standing, facing, competing, or working (e.g. डटकर मेहनत करना (datkar mehnat karnaa, 'to work hard with full dedication')), so using it in isolation sounds incomplete to a native ear.

बेटा, कल से डटकर पढ़ो, परीक्षा नज़दीक है। Son, study hard from tomorrow, the exam is near.
(betaa, kal se datkar padho, pariikshaa nazdiik hai।)
