सीना (siinaa)

verb/noun Two unrelated words sharing this spelling: the verb 'to sew/stitch' and the noun 'chest/breast' -- distinct lemmas historically, treated here together since they share the same written and spoken form.
Sense 1 : to sew, to stitch
verb, transitive
Synonyms: टाँकना (taanknaa, to tack/stitch loosely)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Every Indian household has someone -- often a grandmother or a local darzi (tailor) -- who can सीना (siinaa) a torn kurta in minutes; it is a very hands-on, everyday verb tied to mending and tailoring rather than large-scale sewing/textile work.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with बुनना (bunnaa, 'to knit/weave') -- सीना (siinaa) specifically means joining fabric with needle and thread, like hemming or stitching a tear, not knitting or weaving cloth from yarn. 2) The idiom मुँह सीना (munh siinaa, 'to sew someone's mouth shut') means to silence someone or force them to keep quiet, often used about oneself: अपना मुँह सी लिया (apnaa munh sii liyaa, 'I kept my mouth shut/said nothing').

Sense 2 : chest, breast (of the body)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: छाती (chhaatii), वक्ष (vaksh, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: सीना (siinaa) carries a strong emotional charge in Hindi -- puffing out your सीना (siinaa) signals pride, honor, or bravado, much like 'chest out' in English but used far more often, especially in patriotic or heroic contexts (soldiers, fathers boasting about their children, etc.).
Common Mistakes: 1) सीना (siinaa) is heavily used in figurative expressions of pride and courage -- सीना तानकर (siinaa taankar, 'chest thrust out, i.e. with pride/defiance') and सीना चौड़ा होना (siinaa chaudaa honaa, 'to swell with pride') -- learners who only know छाती (chhaatii) may miss these idioms since they don't map onto English 'chest' phrases directly.
Sample Sentences:
