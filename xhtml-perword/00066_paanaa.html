पाना (paanaa)

verb Core meaning is 'to get/obtain' (मौका पाना (maukaa paanaa, 'to get a chance')); it also means 'to find someone/something in a state' in a slightly literary register, and -- most importantly for daily speech -- attaches to a verb stem (V+पाना) to mean 'to manage to,' reporting success or failure against some obstacle.
Sense 1 : to obtain, get, receive, attain
verb, transitive
Synonyms: प्राप्त करना (praapt karnaa, formal), हासिल करना (haasil karnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पाना (paanaa) shows up constantly in devotional and filmi language for things you long for or strive toward -- मोक्ष पाना (moksh paanaa, 'to attain liberation'), प्यार पाना (pyaar paanaa, 'to receive/win love') -- so it carries a slightly more heartfelt, aspirational color than a flat 'get,' even though it's used for ordinary objects too.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मिलना (milnaa) and पाना (paanaa) interchangeably, but मिलना puts the recipient in the को-marked dative role ('मुझे मौका मिला', mujhe maukaa milaa, 'I got a chance,' literally 'a chance came to me'), while पाना makes the recipient the active subject who obtains something -- 'मैंने मौका पाया' (mainne maukaa paayaa) sounds more deliberate/earned.

Sense 2 : to manage to, succeed in (doing something despite an obstacle)
verb, intransitive Formed as verb-stem + पाना (paanaa) -- जा पाना (jaa paanaa), कर पाना (kar paanaa), चल पाना (chal paanaa) -- and reports whether the subject actually pulled off the action against some resistance (traffic, illness, other commitments, etc.), not mere permission or general capability like सकना (saknaa). It appears overwhelmingly in negative, past, or question forms, and sounds odd as a bare affirmative present-tense claim.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This construction is the backbone of daily excuses in Delhi -- मैं समय पर नहीं पहुँच पाया (main samay par nahiin pahunch paayaa, 'I couldn't manage to get there on time') is the classic traffic-jam apology. It carries a flavor of 'I wanted to, but something got in the way,' making it more emotionally loaded than the neutral सकना (saknaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners substitute सकना (saknaa) where पाना is more natural: वह नहीं आ पाया (vah nahiin aa paayaa, 'he couldn't manage to come') suggests something specifically got in his way, while वह नहीं आ सका (vah nahiin aa sakaa) just states a neutral inability -- native speakers reach for पाना when describing a real-life failure against circumstance. 2) Learners try flat affirmative present tense like मैं कर पाता हूँ, which sounds unnatural -- this construction lives mainly in negative, past, or interrogative sentences.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to find (someone or something in a particular state)
verb, transitive Takes a participial complement describing the state discovered, typically V+ता/ते हुआ or a perfective form: मैंने उसे रोते हुए पाया (mainne use rote hue paayaa, 'I found him crying'), मैंने उसे सोते पाया (mainne use sote paayaa, 'I found him sleeping').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This 'find' sense turns up often in storytelling -- novels, memoirs, even news reports -- where a narrator describes stumbling on a scene; it gives prose a slightly formal, descriptive touch rather than the plain-spoken feel of देखना (dekhnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to देखना (dekhnaa) for 'I found him crying,' but देखना is neutral 'saw/watched,' while पाना (paanaa) here implies an element of discovery or surprise, and reads as more literary/narrative than conversational.
