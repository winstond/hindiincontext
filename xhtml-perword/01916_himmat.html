हिम्मत (himmat)

noun A single core sense: inner courage or nerve to face something difficult or scary. Used constantly in everyday encouragement and scolding alike.
Sense 1 : courage, nerve, boldness to act despite fear
noun, feminine Very commonly paired with the verb करना (karnaa) in the frame हिम्मत करना (himmat karnaa, 'to muster courage/dare to'), and with होना (honaa) in constructions like मेरी हिम्मत नहीं हुई (merii himmat nahiin huii, 'I didn't have the nerve') where the person is marked with का (kaa) possessive rather than an agentive subject.
Synonyms: हौसला (hauslaa), जुर्रत (jurrat, formal), दिलेरी (dilerii, literary), साहस (saahas, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हिम्मत रखो (himmat rakho, 'keep your courage up') is something you'll hear from friends, parents, and even strangers consoling someone after bad news -- it's the go-to phrase for emotional support in Delhi households. Related expressions like हिम्मत टूटना (himmat tootnaa, 'courage to break/shatter') and हिम्मत बंधना (himmat bandhnaa, 'courage to be bound/take hold') treat courage as something fragile that can break or be tied down, reflecting a broader Hindi pattern of framing inner states as physical objects acted upon rather than things one simply 'has'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I have courage' with पास (paas) like other possessed nouns, but native speakers usually say मेरी हिम्मत है (merii himmat hai) or use हिम्मत होना (himmat honaa) with a possessive, not हिम्मत मेरे पास है (himmat mere paas hai) which sounds odd. 2) हिम्मत बढ़ाना (himmat badhaanaa, 'to boost someone's courage/morale') is a fixed collocation that beginners tend to replace with बढ़ना (badhnaa) alone, losing the causative sense.

(mujhmen abhii bhii usse baat karne kii himmat nahiin hai।)
