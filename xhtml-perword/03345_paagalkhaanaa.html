पागलख़ाना (paagalkhaanaa)

noun A compound noun meaning 'madhouse/mental asylum,' from पागल (paagal, 'mad') plus ख़ाना (khaanaa, 'house/place'), used literally and, very commonly, as an exaggerated figure of speech for any chaotic scene.
Sense 1 : chaotic, noisy, disordered place
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अफ़रा-तफ़री (afraa-tafrii, chaos/commotion, not a place-noun), भीड़-भाड़ (bhiid-bhaad, crowded mayhem, related but distinct)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you'll hear far more often at home: a mother walking into a room where all the kids are screaming and things are flying might sigh, 'यह घर तो पागलख़ाना बन गया है' (this house has become a madhouse (yah ghar to paagalkhaanaa ban gayaa hai, 'this house has become a madhouse')). It's affectionate exasperation, not literal diagnosis.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for the literal asylum meaning; in daily speech this figurative use (e.g., describing a chaotic office or house full of shouting kids) is actually the more frequent one. 2) It's an exclamatory/descriptive noun here, not something you'd use clinically or respectfully -- calling an actual hospital this would sound insensitive.

Sense 2 : mental asylum, psychiatric hospital
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मानसिक चिकित्सालय (maansik chikitsaalay, formal/clinical), मनोरोग अस्पताल (manorog asptaal, formal/clinical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Older Hindi speech and cinema use पागलख़ाना (paagalkhaanaa) freely for the literal institution, but the word has the same blunt, slightly stigmatizing edge in Hindi that 'madhouse' or 'asylum' carries in English -- worth knowing, but not the polite modern term.
Common Mistakes: 1) This word itself carries an old-fashioned, somewhat crude tone -- like calling it a 'nuthouse' in English rather than 'psychiatric hospital.' In careful or clinical contexts, native speakers reach for मानसिक चिकित्सालय (maansik chikitsaalay) or मनोरोग अस्पताल (manorog asptaal) instead.
