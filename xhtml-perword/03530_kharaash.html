ख़राश (kharaash)

noun A physical-sensation noun meaning a scratchy, raw irritation, most often felt in the throat.
Sense 1 : a scratchy/raw irritation, especially in the throat
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जलन (jalan, burning sensation), खुजली (khujlii, itching, less common for throat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Delhi household reaches for the moment someone's voice sounds rough -- गले में ख़राश है (gale men kharaash hai, 'there's a scratch in the throat') is usually followed immediately by advice to gargle with warm salt water or sip adrak-wali chai (adrak vaalii chaay, ginger tea). It's a mild, everyday complaint, not a medical term, so you'll hear it far more at home than in a doctor's clinic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दर्द (dard) to say 'my throat hurts,' but ख़राश (kharaash) is the more natural word for that scratchy, raw feeling specifically -- गले में ख़राश है (gale men kharaash hai, 'there's a scratchy feeling in the throat') is what a Hindi speaker says before a cold sets in, while दर्द (dard) suggests sharper, more general pain. 2) The word takes है (hai) with a locative गले में (gale men), not a verb like होना (honaa) used transitively -- you don't 'do' ख़राश (kharaash) to your throat, it 'is there.'
