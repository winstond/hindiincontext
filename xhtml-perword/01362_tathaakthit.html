तथाकथित (tathaakthit)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'so-called', used to cast doubt on a label or claim -- often with a skeptical or critical edge.
Sense 1 : so-called; alleged
adjective
Synonyms: कथित (kathit, often used in news/legal contexts), स्वघोषित (svaghoshit, self-proclaimed -- narrower, implies self-appointed status)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in Hindi news and political commentary -- तथाकथित बुद्धिजीवी (tathaakthit buddhijiivii, 'so-called intellectuals') or तथाकथित विशेषज्ञ (tathaakthit visheshagy, 'so-called experts') -- almost always as a subtle jab at someone's claimed credentials or status. It is a favorite word of newspaper editorials and TV debate panelists for signaling 'I don't buy this label' without saying so outright.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use तथाकथित (tathaakthit) as a neutral synonym for 'so-called' the way English sometimes uses it descriptively, but in Hindi it almost always carries a skeptical or dismissive undertone -- calling someone तथाकथित समाजसेवी (tathaakthit samaajsevii, 'so-called social worker') implies you doubt they really are one. 2) It is placed before the noun like an ordinary adjective, but because it is invariant, learners sometimes wrongly try to inflect it to agree with a feminine or plural noun.
Sample Sentences:
यह सब तथाकथित बुद्धिजीवियों की साजिश है। All this is a conspiracy of so-called intellectuals.
(yah sab tathaakthit buddhijiiviyon kii saajish hai।)
