डर (dar)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'fear', with a closely related intransitive verb sense डरना (darnaa, 'to be afraid') sharing the same root -- this entry covers the noun; the verb is its own lemma.
Sense 1 : fear, fright
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भय (bhay, formal), खौफ़ (khauf), भीति (bhiiti, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll most often meet this word in the phrase डर लगना (dar lagnaa, 'to feel scared'), used with a को-marked experiencer: मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe dar lagtaa hai, 'I feel scared') -- fear here is something that 'arrives' or is 'felt to' you, not something you actively do, which is a very Hindi way of framing involuntary emotion. Parents often warn children with डर मत (dar mat, 'don't be afraid') using the related verb, so the two forms live side by side in everyday speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मुझे डर है (mujhe dar hai, 'I am afraid') as डर is a noun and takes a दिखना-style dative-subject construction, not a subject-verb pattern like the English 'I fear.' 2) Confusing डर (dar) with the verb डरना (darnaa) -- मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe dar lagtaa hai, 'I feel afraid') and मैं डरता हूँ (main dartaa hoon, 'I am afraid/get scared') both work but come from different grammatical frames, and mixing them (e.g. मुझे डरता है) is a common error.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे अंधेरे से डर लगता है। I feel scared of the dark.
(mujhe andhere se dar lagtaa hai।)
