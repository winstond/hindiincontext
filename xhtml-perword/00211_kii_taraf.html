की तरफ़ (kii taraf)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'toward, in the direction of'; also extends to 'on behalf of/from the side of' in social contexts. Built from तरफ़ (taraf, 'side/direction') with the possessive marker की (kii).
Sense 1 : toward, in the direction of (physical/spatial)
postposition Attaches to a noun/pronoun in the oblique case: घर की तरफ़ (ghar kii taraf, 'toward home'), उसकी तरफ़ (uskii taraf, 'toward him/her').
Synonyms: की ओर (kii or, slightly more formal), की दिशा में (kii dishaa men, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly when someone points out a direction on the street: उस तरफ़ जाओ (us taraf jaao, 'go that way'). Note की (kii) agrees with तरफ़ (taraf) being feminine, so it is always की तरफ़ (kii taraf), never का/के तरफ़, regardless of the gender of the noun before it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop की (kii) and just say तरफ़ (taraf) directly after a noun without the connecting particle -- the की/के link is required, similar to other compound postpositions like के पास (ke paas). 2) With pronouns, the possessive form is used (मेरी तरफ़ merii taraf, not मैं की तरफ़ main kii taraf).
Sample Sentences:

(vah ghar kii taraf bhaag rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : toward (a person, emotionally or attitudinally)
postposition Used with feelings, attitudes, or attention directed at a person: उसकी तरफ़ ध्यान दो (uskii taraf dhyaan do, 'pay attention to him/her').
Synonyms: के प्रति (ke prati, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear in advice about relationships or family duty: बच्चों की तरफ़ ध्यान दो (bachchon kii taraf dhyaan do, 'pay attention toward the children'). के प्रति (ke prati) says almost the same thing but sounds more like something read in an essay than said at the dinner table.

Sense 3 : on the side of, on behalf of (in an argument, dispute, or allegiance)
postposition Used to mark loyalty or backing in a dispute: वह मेरी तरफ़ है (vah merii taraf hai, 'he/she is on my side').
Synonyms: के पक्ष में (ke paksh men, formal), की जानिब से (kii jaanib se, literary/Urdu-influenced)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Perfect for playful family arguments -- a kid complaining tू हमेशा दीदी की तरफ़ हो (too hameshaa diidii kii taraf ho, 'you're always on didi's side') captures exactly this everyday, half-joking use of taking sides.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may reach for के पक्ष में (ke paksh men) by default from textbook study, but in a casual argument between friends, native speakers overwhelmingly say तरफ़ (taraf) instead.
Sample Sentences:
