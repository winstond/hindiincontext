क़लम (qalam)

noun A reed/writing pen, mostly literary or old-fashioned now next to पेन (pen), but still alive in fixed phrases about writing and authority; also used figuratively for one's writing style or a writer's authorial voice.
Sense 1 : pen (writing instrument)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पेन (pen)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word comes from Persian/Arabic and originally meant a reed pen dipped in ink -- the image behind a lot of Urdu poetry about ink and paper. Today it survives less as a shopping-list word and more in set expressions about writing, signing, and authority, like क़लम चलाना (qalam chalaanaa, 'to wield the pen', i.e., to write) or क़लम तोड़ना (qalam todnaa, 'to write brilliantly').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for क़लम (qalam) as the everyday word for 'pen', but in ordinary conversation पेन (pen) is what people actually ask for at a shop or in class; क़लम (qalam) sounds old-fashioned or literary in that context. 2) The nuqta under the क़ (q) is often dropped in casual writing, giving कलम (kalam) -- both spellings are seen and understood.

(mez par ek qalam padii hai।)

Sense 2 : writing style, authorial voice
noun, feminine Used the way English uses 'pen' figuratively, as in 'a mighty pen' -- always about a person's writing, never the physical object in this sense.
Synonyms: लेखनी (lekhnii, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in praise of writers and journalists: उसकी क़लम में जान है (uskii qalam men jaan hai, 'his/her pen has life/soul in it') is a classic compliment for someone who writes powerfully. It pairs with the literary word लेखनी (lekhnii) in more formal or poetic registers.
