बुरी तरह (burii tarah)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'badly, terribly, to a severe degree' -- combines बुरी (burii, the feminine form of बुरा, 'bad') with तरह (tarah, 'manner/way'), and is used to intensify a verb rather than describe a noun.
Sense 1 : badly, terribly, severely (intensifying a negative outcome)
adverb Always appears in the fixed feminine form बुरी (burii) regardless of the gender/number of the subject, since it modifies the verb, not a noun -- e.g. वह बुरी तरह हार गया (vah burii tarah haar gayaa, 'he lost badly').
Synonyms: बहुत ज़्यादा (bahut zyaadaa), बेतरह (betrah, literary), सख्त (sakht, colloquial intensifier)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday complaints and gossip in Delhi -- वह बुरी तरह फँस गया (vah burii tarah phans gayaa, 'he got badly trapped/stuck') is the kind of line that comes up when someone's plan backfires spectacularly. It pairs naturally with verbs like हारना (haarnaa, 'to lose'), फँसना (phansnaa, 'to get stuck'), डरना (darnaa, 'to be afraid'), and पिटना (pitnaa, 'to get beaten').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to match बुरी (burii) to the subject's gender, changing it to बुरा तरह (buraa tarah) or बुरे तरह (bure tarah) -- the phrase is frozen and never changes form. 2) It is used almost exclusively with negative or painful outcomes (losing, failing, getting hurt, being scared) -- learners sometimes misuse it to intensify a positive statement, where बहुत (bahut) or ज़बरदस्त (zabardast) would be the natural choice instead.
