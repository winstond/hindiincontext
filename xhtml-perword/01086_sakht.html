सख़्त (sakht)

adjective An adjective meaning physically hard/firm or, by extension, harsh/strict in manner and severe in degree; also spelled सख्त (sakht) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : strict, harsh, severe (of a person, rule, or punishment)
adjective
Synonyms: कठोर (kathor), कड़ा (kadaa), सख़्त-गीर (sakht-giir, rare, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every strict schoolteacher or no-nonsense boss earns: सख़्त मास्टर (sakht maastar, 'strict teacher') or सख़्त निगरानी (sakht nigraanii, 'strict supervision') are everyday phrases in offices, schools, and homes across Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) सख़्त (sakht) describes a person's manner or a rule, not a task's difficulty -- for 'a hard exam' learners should use कठिन (kathin) or मुश्किल (mushkil), not सख़्त (sakht).

Sense 2 : hard, firm (physical texture)
adjective
Synonyms: कठोर (kathor), ठोस (thos, solid/dense), कड़ा (kadaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a rock, a wooden board, or stale bread gone hard -- सख़्त (sakht) is the word for something that resists pressure and doesn't give. It's the physical anchor from which the 'strict/harsh' sense (below) naturally grew.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for कड़ा (kadaa) and सख़्त (sakht) interchangeably, but सख़्त (sakht) leans more toward 'rigid/unyielding' while कड़ा (kadaa) can also mean 'strong-tasting' (कड़ी चाय, kadii chaay, 'strong tea') -- that food sense doesn't transfer to सख़्त (sakht).

Sense 3 : intense, severe (of pain, cold, need, etc.)
adjective Often pairs with abstract nouns like ज़रूरत (zaroorat, need), दर्द (dard, pain), or सर्दी (sardii, cold) to intensify them, similar to 'severe' or 'acute' in English.
Synonyms: तेज़ (tez), गहरा (gahraa, deep)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in phrases like सख़्त ज़रूरत (sakht zaroorat, 'urgent need') or सख़्त सर्दी (sakht sardii, 'bitter cold') -- the physical 'hardness' sense stretches here to mean an intensity that's hard to bear, much like English 'hard winter.'
