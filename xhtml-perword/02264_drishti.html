दृष्टि (drishti)

noun A somewhat formal/literary word covering both literal 'eyesight/vision' and figurative 'viewpoint, perspective'; also lives inside a few common fixed phrases like दृष्टि से (drishti se, from the point of view of).
Sense 1 : viewpoint, perspective, outlook
noun, feminine Very commonly used in the fixed frame X दृष्टि से (X drishti se, 'from the point of view of X'), e.g. आर्थिक दृष्टि से (aarthik drishti se, 'from an economic point of view').
Synonyms: नज़रिया (nazriyaa), दृष्टिकोण (drishtikon)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you meet constantly in essays, news analysis, and formal debate in Hindi -- newsreaders and columnists lean on phrases like इस दृष्टि से (is drishti se, 'from this point of view') to frame an argument. It pairs naturally with its cousin दृष्टिकोण (drishtikon), which means almost the same thing but is built explicitly from दृष्टि (drishti) plus कोण (kon, angle) -- literally 'angle of sight.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'point of view' word-for-word and miss the natural फ्रेम X दृष्टि से (X drishti se), which is how this sense is idiomatically expressed rather than as a full sentence with 'perspective' as subject.
Sample Sentences:
इस दृष्टि से यह फ़ैसला सही है। From this point of view, this decision is correct.
(is drishti se yah faislaa sahii hai।)

Sense 2 : eyesight, power of vision
noun, feminine
Synonyms: नज़र (nazar), निगाह (nigaah)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, नज़र (nazar) is what people actually reach for when talking about someone's eyesight or a quick glance; दृष्टि (drishti) shows up more in writing, formal speech, and educated registers -- doctors' reports, essays, or a teacher explaining eyesight in a classroom.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for नज़र (nazar) by default in speech; दृष्टि (drishti) is not wrong but sounds noticeably more formal or written, so using it constantly in casual conversation can feel stiff. 2) दृष्टि (drishti) does not carry the 'evil eye' superstition sense that नज़र (nazar) does (as in नज़र लगना, nazar lagnaa) -- that meaning is specific to नज़र (nazar), not दृष्टि (drishti).
