पालथी (paalthii)

noun A single concrete noun naming the cross-legged sitting posture, used almost always in the fixed phrase पालथी मारकर (paalthii maarkar) or पालथी लगाकर (paalthii lagaakar).
Sense 1 : the cross-legged sitting position (sitting on the floor with legs folded)
noun, feminine Almost always appears as the object of मारना (maarnaa) or लगाना (lagaanaa) rather than standing alone as a subject: पालथी मारकर बैठना (paalthii maarkar baithnaa, 'to sit down cross-legged').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the everyday floor-sitting posture you see at a satsang (satsang), around a chowki (chaukii) for a puja, or simply on a charpai (chaarpaaii) or durrie (darii) spread on the floor while eating or chatting -- it is how generations of Indians have sat before chairs became common in every home. The verb मारना (maarnaa) here is a good example of how Hindi pairs an otherwise forceful verb ('to hit') with a body-posture noun to just mean 'to assume' that posture, with no violence implied at all.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use बैठना (baithnaa) alone to mean 'sit cross-legged' -- but बैठना (baithnaa) just means 'to sit' in general; पालथी (paalthii) has to be added with मारना (maarnaa) or लगाना (lagaanaa) to specify the cross-legged style. 2) Learners sometimes reach for अंग्रेज़ी में 'lotus position' vocabulary from yoga class; in everyday Hindi, पालथी (paalthii) is the plain word used at home, not a technical yoga term.
