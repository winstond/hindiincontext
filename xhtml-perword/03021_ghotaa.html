ग़ोता (ghotaa)

noun A noun meaning 'a dive/plunge,' used almost always with the light verb खाना (khaanaa) or लगाना (lagaanaa) rather than standing alone as a plain object of physical diving.
Sense 1 : a dive, plunge, dip (into water)
noun, masculine Almost always appears as part of the compound expressions ग़ोता खाना (ghotaa khaanaa, to take a dive/plunge) or ग़ोता लगाना (ghotaa lagaanaa, to dive, to take a plunge), rather than used as a free-standing noun.
Synonyms: डुबकी (dubkii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word most in the context of kids at a swimming pool or river taking a ग़ोता (ghotaa), or divers in a documentary. It also extends metaphorically -- a person's mood or fortunes can figuratively 'take a dive' with this same word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use ग़ोता (ghotaa) with generic verbs like करना (karnaa); native speakers only pair it with खाना (khaanaa) or लगाना (lagaanaa). 2) The nuqta under ग is often dropped in casual writing as गोता (gotaa) -- both are seen, but ग़ोता (ghotaa) is the more careful spelling.
Sample Sentences:
बच्चों ने नदी में ग़ोता लगाया। The kids took a dive into the river.
(bachchon ne nadii men ghotaa lagaayaa।)

Sense 2 : a sudden dip/plunge (figurative, e.g. of prices, fortunes, health)
noun, masculine Used with खाना (khaanaa) in phrases like शेयर बाज़ार ने ग़ोता खाया (sheyar baazaar ne ghotaa khaayaa, 'the stock market took a plunge') to describe a sharp fall.
Synonyms: गिरावट (giraavat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use shows up constantly in business news -- शेयर बाज़ार ने ग़ोता खाया (sheyar baazaar ne ghotaa khaayaa, 'the stock market plunged') is a headline-style phrase you'll see often if you read Hindi news.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for गिरना (girnaa) alone when a native speaker would color the sentence with ग़ोता खाना (ghotaa khaanaa) to emphasize suddenness and depth of the fall.
