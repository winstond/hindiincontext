जनाब (janaab)

noun/interjection A respectful term of address for a man, roughly 'sir' or 'good sir' (janaab), often carrying a slightly old-fashioned, Urdu-inflected courtesy or, in modern speech, a teasing/sarcastic edge.
Sense 1 : respectful term of address for a man
noun, masculine Used directly as a vocative or attached before a name/title, e.g. जनाब अली (janaab alii, 'Sir Ali') -- functions almost like an honorific prefix.
Synonyms: साहब (saahab), महोदय (mahoday, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जनाब (janaab) has an old Mughal-court/Urdu courtly flavor, the kind of word you'd hear in a period drama or a ghazal, but in everyday Delhi speech today it's just as often used sarcastically among friends: 'अब जनाब को भूख लगी है' (ab janaab ko bhookh lagii hai, 'now His Highness is hungry') pokes gentle fun at someone acting a bit self-important.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat जनाब (janaab) as a neutral equivalent of 'sir,' missing that in casual modern speech it frequently carries a playful or sarcastic tone, as if teasing someone for acting important. 2) It is used for men only; it is not the polite address for a woman.

Sense 2 : polite/attention-getting address, 'sir!'
interjection Used alone as a call for attention, e.g. to a shopkeeper, waiter, or stranger, similar to calling out 'sir!' in English.
Synonyms: साहब (saahab)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear जनाब! (janaab) called out by an auto-rickshaw driver or a shopkeeper trying to get a customer's attention politely, it has that slightly formal, old-world street courtesy to it.
