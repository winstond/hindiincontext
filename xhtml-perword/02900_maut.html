मौत (maut)

noun A single core meaning -- 'death' -- but the word branches widely into idioms and figurative uses (near-misses, hopeless situations, harsh criticism) that a learner will meet often.
Sense 1 : death (the end of life)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मृत्यु (mrityu, formal), निधन (nidhan, formal), देहांत (dehaant, formal, respectful), मौत (maut)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मौत (maut) is the everyday, blunt word for death -- the one you hear in news reports (सड़क हादसे में मौत sadak haadse men maut, 'death in a road accident') and casual talk, whereas families speaking about their own loved one usually soften it with गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa) or स्वर्गवास (svargvaas). Knowing when to reach for the gentler option is part of showing respect in Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मृत्यु (mrityu) in everyday speech; मौत (maut) is the word actually used in conversation, news headlines, and casual remarks, while मृत्यु (mrityu) and देहांत (dehaant) sound more formal or written. 2) When talking about someone's death respectfully (e.g., an elder relative), देहांत हो गया (dehaant ho gayaa) or गुज़र गए (guzar gae) are preferred over the blunter मौत हो गई (maut ho gaii).
Sample Sentences:
सड़क हादसे में उसकी मौत हो गई। He died in a road accident.
(sadak haadse men uskii maut ho gaii।)

Sense 2 : a brush with death; extreme danger or a narrow escape (figurative)
noun, feminine Common in the set phrase मौत के मुँह से बचना (maut ke munh se bachnaa) / मौत को क़रीब से देखना (maut ko qariib se dekhnaa), literally 'to escape from death's mouth' or 'to see death up close.'
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi loves personifying death as having a मुँह (munh, mouth) that can swallow you -- मौत के मुँह से निकल आना (maut ke munh se nikal aanaa, 'to come out of death's mouth') is the vivid, everyday way to describe surviving a serious accident, illness, or disaster, much more dramatic-sounding than a flat English 'I almost died.'

Sense 3 : a hopeless, unbearable, or dreaded situation (figurative intensifier)
noun, feminine Often used predicatively as मौत आना (maut aanaa, 'to feel like dying [from boredom/dread/embarrassment]') or किसी को मौत लगना (kisii ko maut lagnaa) with a dative subject.
Synonyms: आफ़त (aafat, colloquial), मुसीबत (musiibat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English speakers say 'I could just die' out of exasperation, Hindi speakers reach for मौत (maut) the same way -- an exam that drags on, a boring meeting, or an awkward family function can all make someone say यहाँ तो मौत आ रही है (yahaan to maut aa rahii hai, 'this is pure torture here'). It is casual hyperbole, not literal despair.
Common Mistakes: 1) उसे मौत आ रही थी (use maut aa rahii thii) does not mean the person was literally dying -- it means they were dying of boredom, dread, or embarrassment; learners sometimes take it literally. 2) This figurative use takes a को (ko)-marked experiencer, like लगना (lagnaa) constructions the learner already knows -- e.g., बोर होकर मौत आ रही है (bor hokar maut aa rahii hai, 'I'm dying of boredom').
Sample Sentences:
यार, इस मीटिंग में तो मौत आ रही है। Dude, this meeting is pure torture.
