नाचना (naachnaa)

verb A single core meaning, 'to dance', with a common figurative extension for being made to jump through hoops or act at someone else's whim.
Sense 1 : to dance
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: थिरकना (thiraknaa, to sway/dance lightly), नृत्य करना (nrity karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a Delhi wedding baraat (baaraat) with the dhol (dhol) playing -- नाचना (naachnaa) is the word for exactly that unrestrained, joyful dancing in the street, not a technical or choreographed performance. For trained classical or stage dance, especially in a more formal context, नृत्य (nrity) and नृत्य करना (nrity karnaa) are preferred.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for नृत्य करना (nrity karnaa) as the default, but नाचना (naachnaa) is the everyday verb used for dancing at weddings, festivals, or in films; नृत्य करना (nrity karnaa) sounds more formal, like describing classical dance in a program note. 2) गाना (gaanaa, to sing) and नाचना (naachnaa) are paired so often in speech (गाना-नाचना, gaanaa-naachnaa, 'singing and dancing') that learners should get used to hearing them as a set phrase for festive celebration in general.

Sense 2 : to be made to dance to someone's tune, to be jerked around
verb, intransitive Almost always appears in the causative नचाना (nachaanaa, 'to make someone dance/jerk around') rather than in this base form; the base verb itself is used figuratively when someone describes their own state, e.g. उसके इशारों पर नाचना (uske ishaaron par naachnaa, 'to dance to his/her tune').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a vivid body-as-puppet image very alive in everyday complaint -- कोई किसी के इशारों पर नाचता है (koii kisii ke ishaaron par naachtaa hai, 'someone dances to another's cues') captures being under someone's thumb, much like the English 'jump when someone snaps their fingers.' It is closely tied to the causative नचाना (nachaanaa), which describes the person pulling the strings.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense almost never stands alone; it typically appears with इशारों पर (ishaaron par, 'on the signals/cues of') or पैसों के लिए (paison ke lie) to specify who is pulling the strings. A learner who says केवल नाचना (keval naachnaa) meaning 'being controlled' without this frame will just be understood as talking about literal dancing.
