छाया (chhaayaa)

noun Primarily a noun meaning 'shade/shadow' -- both the literal shade cast by a tree or building and the figurative sense of a lingering influence or protective cover. Also functions as a feminine given name.
Sense 1 : shade, shadow (literal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: साया (saayaa), परछाई (parchhaaii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Sitting छाया में (chhaayaa men, 'in the shade') under a tree is a classic scene of respite from the North Indian summer sun -- you will hear people say पेड़ की छाया (ped kii chhaayaa, 'the tree's shade') as a small oasis on a hot afternoon. The word also shows up in the phrase छाया देना (chhaayaa denaa, 'to give shade'), often used affectionately of a tree, an elder, or even a protective institution.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for परछाई (parchhaaii) for 'shadow' and छाया (chhaayaa) for 'shade' as if they were strictly separate words -- in practice both overlap heavily, with छाया (chhaayaa) leaning slightly more literary/written and परछाई (parchhaaii) slightly more everyday-spoken for a cast shadow. 2) It takes में (men) for 'in the shade' (छाया में बैठना (chhaayaa men baithnaa, 'to sit in the shade')), not पर (par) or से (se).

Sense 2 : lingering influence, trace, or protective presence (figurative)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रभाव (prabhaav, influence, broader meaning), साया (saayaa, protective presence)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this in phrases like किसी की छाया में पलना (kisii kii chhaayaa men palnaa, 'to grow up under someone's protective shadow'), said of a child raised under a strong parent or mentor's care. It also appears in slightly formal or reflective speech about how one event 'casts a shadow' over another, e.g. an accident's छाया (chhaayaa) lingering over a family's mood.
Common Mistakes: 1) In the figurative sense छाया (chhaayaa) often pairs with पड़ना (padnaa) -- छाया पड़ना (chhaayaa padnaa, 'for a shadow/influence to fall') -- rather than होना (honaa), which sounds flat here.
