इकट्ठा (ikatthaa)

adjective/adverb Covers the adjective sense 'gathered/collected/together' and the adverbial use meaning 'together' (as in doing something jointly), plus the common compound verb इकट्ठा करना (ikatthaa karnaa, 'to collect/gather') and इकट्ठा होना (ikatthaa honaa, 'to gather/assemble').
Sense 1 : gathered, collected, accumulated
adjective
Synonyms: जमा (jamaa), संचित (sanchit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around money and paperwork -- पैसे इकट्ठा करना (paise ikatthaa karnaa, 'to save up money') is the everyday way to talk about saving, more concrete and physical-feeling than संचय करना (sanchay karnaa), which sounds bookish by comparison.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect इकट्ठा (ikatthaa) like a regular आ-ending adjective (इकट्ठी, इकट्ठे) -- it stays invariant regardless of the noun's gender or number, e.g. इकट्ठा पैसा (ikatthaa paisaa, 'accumulated money') and इकट्ठा रकम (ikatthaa rakam, 'accumulated amount') both use the same form.
Sample Sentences:
मैंने कई सालों में इतना पैसा इकट्ठा कर लिया है। I have saved up this much money over many years.
(mainne kaii saalon men itnaa paisaa ikatthaa kar liyaa hai।)

Sense 2 : together, at once, in one go
adverb Used adverbially to mean 'all together/in one lump' -- e.g. पैसे इकट्ठा दे दो (paise ikatthaa de do, 'give the money all at once') -- distinct from साथ (saath) which emphasizes companionship rather than combining into one batch.
Synonyms: साथ (saath), एक साथ (ek saath)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A shopkeeper might say सामान इकट्ठा ले लो (saamaan ikatthaa le lo, 'take all the goods together/in one go') -- the word carries a sense of consolidating into a single unit, useful when talking about payments, deliveries, or errands done in one trip.
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this with साथ (saath): इकट्ठा (ikatthaa) means 'combined into one lot/batch,' while साथ (saath) means 'in each other's company' -- हम साथ गए (ham saath gae, 'we went together') is about companionship, not combining things.
