पाँव (paanv)

noun The everyday word for 'foot' or 'leg', used in countless idioms about status, respect, and travel. It largely overlaps with पैर (pair) in literal meaning but shows up in a distinct set of fixed expressions.
Sense 1 : foot / leg (body part)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पैर (pair)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, पैर (pair) is slightly more common in casual conversation, while पाँव (paanv) has a warmer, slightly more traditional or affectionate flavor -- you'll hear it often in songs, blessings, and elders' speech, like touching someone's पाँव (paanv) to seek their blessing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume पाँव (paanv) and पैर (pair) are perfectly interchangeable in all fixed phrases -- they overlap heavily for the literal body part, but many idioms fix on one word and don't accept the other, so it's safer to learn idioms as whole chunks rather than swapping freely.

Sense 2 : foot/feet as a symbol of respect, touch, or humility (in fixed idioms)
noun, masculine Appears mainly inside set phrases like पाँव पड़ना (paanv padnaa, 'to touch someone's feet in respect/pleading') and पाँव छूना (paanv chhoonaa, 'to touch someone's feet as a gesture of respect').
Synonyms: पैर (pair)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Touching an elder's or teacher's पाँव (paanv) is a living custom across North India, especially at festivals, weddings, or before a journey -- it's a small physical ritual that carries real emotional and social weight, not just a figure of speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) This idiomatic 'feet' sense doesn't translate literally -- touching someone's पाँव (paanv) in Hindi culture signals deep respect or a humble request, which can confuse learners expecting a literal description of a physical action rather than a social gesture.
