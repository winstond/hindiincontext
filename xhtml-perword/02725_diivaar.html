दीवार (diivaar)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'wall' -- the physical structure of a room, house, or compound. It also extends naturally into a common figurative sense of 'barrier' between people.
Sense 1 : wall (physical structure)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भीत (bhiit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR you will hear दीवार पर लिखना (diivaar par likhnaa, 'to write on the wall') used both literally, for old election-slogan graffiti still fading on brick walls, and figuratively for something being obviously predictable or foretold, much like the English idiom.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse दीवार (diivaar) with दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa, 'door') because both are common household nouns learned around the same time -- keep the meanings distinct even though they sound faintly similar in fast speech.

Sense 2 : barrier, divide (figurative)
noun, feminine Used with बीच (biich, 'between') to describe an emotional or social barrier: दो लोगों के बीच दीवार (do logon ke biich diivaar, 'a wall between two people').
Synonyms: बाधा (baadhaa, obstacle, not identical but often overlaps), दूरी (doorii, distance/emotional gap)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use is everywhere in Hindi film dialogue and songs about broken relationships -- a दीवार (diivaar) standing 'between' (के बीच, ke biich) two hearts is a very familiar poetic image, parallel to how English speakers talk about someone 'building walls.'
