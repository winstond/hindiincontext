बताना (bataanaa)

verb The everyday verb for 'to tell/say/inform' -- it covers giving information, explaining, and pointing something out. Causative-flavored in origin (related to बतलाना (batlaanaa)) but functions as a plain transitive verb in modern speech.
Sense 1 : to tell, inform, say (convey information)
verb, transitive Takes an indirect object marked with को (ko) for the person being told: उसे बताना (use bataanaa), and the thing told as a direct object or clause.
Synonyms: बतलाना (batlaanaa, variant form), कहना (kahnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first 'useful' verbs a learner reaches for -- रास्ता बताना (raastaa bataanaa, 'to tell/show the way') is the classic phrase you'd use asking a stranger for directions in any Delhi market or metro station.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कहना (kahnaa) when they mean बताना (bataanaa) -- कहना (kahnaa) is 'to say' something (often reporting speech), while बताना (bataanaa) is 'to inform/tell' someone a fact; ask 'मुझे बताओ' (mujhe bataao, 'tell me'), not 'मुझसे कहो' (mujhse kaho) in that context. 2) The person being told takes को (ko), not से (se) -- मुझे बताओ (mujhe bataao) is correct, not मुझसे बताओ.

क्या आप मुझे रास्ता बता सकते हैं? Can you tell me the way?
(kyaa aap mujhe raastaa bataa sakte hain?)

Sense 2 : to explain, describe, point out
verb, transitive Used when the content is more involved than a simple fact -- explaining how something works or describing something in detail, still with the को-marked (ko) listener.
Synonyms: समझाना (samjhaanaa, to make understand -- broader than बताना (bataanaa))
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A teacher or elder saying ज़रा बताओ तो (zaraa bataao to, 'go on, explain/tell me') has a gently prompting tone, inviting someone to walk through something rather than just state a fact.
