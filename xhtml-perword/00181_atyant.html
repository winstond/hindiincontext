अत्यंत (atyant)

adverb A formal-register intensifier meaning 'extremely/very' -- one sense, used before adjectives and adverbs. Also spelled अत्यन्त (atyant) with the full anusvaar-as-nasal-consonant form.
Sense 1 : extremely, very (intensifier)
adverb
Synonyms: बहुत (bahut), बेहद (behad), अत्याधिक (atyaadhik, less common variant), बहुत ज़्यादा (bahut zyaadaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet अत्यंत (atyant) constantly in newspapers, official announcements, and formal speeches -- it is the 'extremely' of written and spoken-formal Hindi, while बहुत (bahut) and बेहद (behad) carry the same job in everyday conversation. A news anchor might say अत्यंत दुखद (atyant dukhad, 'extremely sad') about an event, but a friend telling you about their day would just say बहुत बुरा हुआ (bahut buraa huaa, 'it was really bad').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use अत्यंत (atyant) in casual speech where बहुत (bahut) would sound far more natural; अत्यंत (atyant) belongs mainly to writing, speeches, and news -- using it in everyday chat can sound stiff or bookish. 2) It intensifies adjectives and adverbs, not verbs directly -- you cannot say something like 'अत्यंत खाया' to mean 'ate a lot'; a verb needs a different intensifying structure.

यह निर्णय अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण है। This decision is extremely important.
(yah nirnay atyant mahatvpoorn hai।)
