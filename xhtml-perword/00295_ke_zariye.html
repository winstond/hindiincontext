के ज़रिये (ke zariye)

postposition A postposition phrase meaning 'through, by means of, via' -- used after an oblique noun to mark the medium, channel, or agent through which something happens. Closely related to the standalone noun ज़रिया (zariyaa, 'means, medium'), of which this is the oblique-plus-postposition form.
Sense 1 : through, by means of (a medium, channel, or method)
postposition Takes an oblique noun before it, like most compound postpositions: दोस्त के ज़रिये (dost ke zariye, 'through a friend'). Commonly used with means of communication or transmission -- फ़ोन, ईमेल, अख़बार, आदमी, दोस्त, आदि.
Synonyms: के द्वारा (ke dvaaraa, formal), की मदद से (kii madad se, 'with the help of'), से (se, simpler/plainer)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in news and formal contexts -- 'सरकार के ज़रिये' (sarkaar ke zariye, 'through the government'), 'सोशल मीडिया के ज़रिये' (soshal miidiyaa ke zariye, 'through social media') -- it has a slightly more written/deliberate feel than से, similar to how 'via' feels a touch more formal than 'through' in English. Its close cousin के द्वारा (ke dvaaraa) is even more formal and common in passive/official writing, so के ज़रिये sits in a comfortable middle register between casual से and stiff के द्वारा.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for से (se) in every 'through/by' context, but के ज़रिये (ke zariye) specifically highlights a channel or intermediary (a person, medium, or method) rather than a simple instrument or cause -- 'मैंने चाकू से काटा' (mainne chaakoo se kaataa, 'I cut with a knife') uses से, not के ज़रिये, because a knife is a tool, not a channel of transmission. 2) Do not drop के: ज़रिये alone attached directly to a noun is a common error -- the phrase always needs the oblique marker के before it (except after pronouns already in oblique form, e.g. मेरे ज़रिये (mere zariye)).

मैंने उससे ईमेल के ज़रिये संपर्क किया। I contacted him through email.
(mainne usse iimel ke zariye sampark kiyaa।)
