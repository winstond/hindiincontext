नासमझ (naasmajh)

adjective/noun A negated form of समझ (samajh, 'understanding'), built with the Persian-origin prefix ना- (naa, 'not/un-'). It works as both an adjective ('foolish, uncomprehending') and a noun for the person themselves ('a fool, an unwise person').
Sense 1 : lacking sense or judgment; foolish, naive
adjective
Synonyms: बेवकूफ (bevkooph), मूर्ख (moorkh, formal), नादान (naadaan)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot from parents and elders about children: बच्चे नासमझ होते हैं (bachche naasmajh hote hain, 'children are naive/don't understand better') is said almost as an excuse or explanation for a child's mistake, not as an insult. It carries real tenderness compared to a word like बेवकूफ (bevkooph).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बेवकूफ (bevkooph) as a blunt insult, but नासमझ (naasmajh) is softer -- it points to a lack of understanding or maturity (often used affectionately about children) rather than outright stupidity. 2) It is invariant -- it does not change form for gender or number, unlike many -आ adjectives learners have already met.
Sample Sentences:
बच्चे नासमझ होते हैं, इसलिए उन्हें डांटने से पहले समझाना चाहिए। Children are naive, so before scolding them one should explain things to them.
(bachche naasmajh hote hain, islie unhen daantne se pahle samjhaanaa chaahie।)

Sense 2 : a foolish or naive person
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun by referring directly to the person, often with a possessive or in direct address, e.g. तुम कैसे नासमझ हो (tum kaise naasmajh ho, 'what a naive person you are').
Synonyms: बेवकूफ (bevkooph), नादान (naadaan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This noun use overlaps closely with the adjective sense -- Hindi often lets a describing word double as the person it describes, so नासमझ (naasmajh) can mean both 'naive' and 'a naive one' depending on the sentence, much like 'a fool' versus 'foolish' collapsing into one word.
