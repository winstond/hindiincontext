सज़ा देना (sazaa denaa)

verb A compound expression built from सज़ा (sazaa, 'punishment') plus देना (denaa, 'to give'), meaning 'to punish' or 'to inflict punishment on someone.' It patterns like other noun+देना constructions (e.g. सज़ा देना sazaa denaa alongside सज़ा मिलना sazaa milnaa for the receiving end).
Sense 1 : to punish, to give punishment
verb, transitive The person being punished is marked with को (ko): उसे सज़ा दी (use sazaa dii, 'punished him/her'). The kind of punishment (जेल की सज़ा jel kii sazaa, 'jail punishment', मौत की सज़ा maut kii sazaa, 'death penalty') attaches with की (kii).
Synonyms: दंड देना (dand denaa, formal), सजा देना (sajaa denaa, variant spelling without nuqta)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase shows up constantly in courtroom dramas, school discipline talk, and even parenting -- a teacher might say बच्चे को सज़ा दी (bachche ko sazaa dii, 'punished the child') for something as minor as not doing homework. It pairs naturally with सज़ा मिलना (sazaa milnaa, 'to receive punishment'), which frames the same event from the receiver's side using a dative-subject-like construction familiar from लगना (lagnaa) patterns.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say सज़ा करना (sazaa karnaa) by analogy with other noun+करना pairs, but the fixed idiom is सज़ा देना (sazaa denaa) -- देना (denaa) is the only natural partner here. 2) Since सज़ा (sazaa) is feminine, past-tense agreement goes with सज़ा (sazaa), not with the subject or the person punished: उसने सज़ा दी (usne sazaa dii, 'he/she gave punishment'), not दिया (diyaa).
Sample Sentences:

(jaj ne mujrim ko kadii sazaa dii।)
