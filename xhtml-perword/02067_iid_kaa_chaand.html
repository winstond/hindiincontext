ईद का चाँद (iid kaa chaand)

noun Literally 'the Eid moon' -- the crescent moon that signals the end of Ramzan; used idiomatically for someone or something rarely seen. This is a fixed idiomatic phrase, not a compositional noun phrase.
Sense 1 : someone/something seen very rarely (idiom)
noun, masculine Almost always used with होना (honaa) or दिखना (dikhnaa) in constructions like तुम तो ईद का चाँद हो गए हो (tum to iid kaa chaand ho gae ho, 'you've become such a rare sight').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This idiom comes straight from the practice of moon-sighting that determines the date of Eid (iid) -- families and neighborhoods used to (and often still do) scan the sky for that first sliver of crescent, so a person you rarely see gets teasingly compared to it. It is warm, affectionate teasing between friends or relatives, not an insult -- something an aunt might say to a nephew who only visits once a year: अरे, तुम तो ईद का चाँद हो गए हो! (are, tum to iid kaa chaand ho gae ho, 'oh, you've become such a rare sight!').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes take this literally as 'the moon of Eid' in a sentence about the festival itself, missing that in everyday speech it is almost always the idiom about a person who has been absent or hard to reach. 2) The phrase is invariant as a set expression -- do not modify it to ईद के चाँद (iid ke chaand) or otherwise inflect चाँद (chaand) within it; it functions as a fixed noun phrase, typically the complement of होना (honaa).
Sample Sentences:
अरे, तुम तो ईद का चाँद हो गए हो, कभी मिलते ही नहीं! Oh, you've become such a rare sight, you never meet up anymore!
(are, tum to iid kaa chaand ho gae ho, kabhii milte hii nahiin!)
