धन्य (dhany)

adjective/interjection An adjective meaning 'blessed' or 'fortunate,' used mainly in devotional and exclamatory contexts rather than everyday small talk.
Sense 1 : blessed, fortunate (of a person, honored by association)
adjective Commonly used in the fixed phrase धन्य हो (dhany ho, 'blessed be you') or धन्य हैं आप (dhany hain aap, 'you are blessed/wonderful'), often addressed to elders, saints, or someone praised for a generous or noble act.
Synonyms: भाग्यशाली (bhaagyshaalii), कृतार्थ (kritaarth, literary), सौभाग्यशाली (saubhaagyshaalii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word at temples and in stories about saints -- धन्य हैं वे (dhany hain ve, 'blessed are they') -- echoing a devotional register close to English 'blessed art thou.' In everyday Delhi speech, it also gets flipped sarcastically, so tone and context matter a lot with this one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use धन्य (dhany) as a casual compliment like 'great job,' but it carries a devotional or reverential weight -- closer to calling someone's deed saintly or their presence a blessing, not just 'nice.' 2) It is often said with a touch of irony or sarcasm in modern colloquial speech -- धन्य हो तुम (dhany ho tum, 'oh, aren't you something') can mock someone's foolish or self-important behavior, which surprises learners expecting only reverence.

Sense 2 : expression of gratitude or awe (thank god, how wonderful)
interjection Used alone as an exclamation, e.g. धन्य! (dhany, 'blessed!'), typically in devotional or highly formal/literary speech rather than casual conversation.
Synonyms: धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad, distinct word for 'thank you')
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This standalone exclamatory use is rarer in daily Delhi speech and leans literary or devotional -- you are more likely to meet it in a bhajan or a story than at the dinner table.
