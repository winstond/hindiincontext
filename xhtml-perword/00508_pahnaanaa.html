पहनाना (pahnaanaa)

verb The causative of पहनना (pahannaa, 'to wear') -- means 'to dress someone / put clothing or an ornament on someone.' One core sense, used constantly around weddings, festivals, and everyday dressing of children or others.
Sense 1 : to dress someone / put on (clothes, jewelry, garland) for someone else
verb, transitive Takes two objects: the person (often marked with को (ko)) and the item worn, e.g. बच्चे को कपड़े पहनाना (bachche ko kapde pahnaanaa, 'to dress the child in clothes').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb shows up everywhere in ritual life: mothers पहनाना (pahnaanaa) uniforms on kids for school, brides and grooms are पहनाना (pahnaanaa)'d garlands (माला पहनाना, maalaa pahnaanaa, 'to garland someone') at weddings, and idols are dressed in fresh clothes on festival days. It is the natural causative partner of पहनना (pahannaa), and thinking of the two together helps the pairing stick.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use पहनना (pahannaa) when talking about dressing someone else -- पहनना (pahannaa) is what you do to yourself; पहनाना (pahnaanaa) is what you do to another person. 2) The person being dressed is usually marked with को (ko), which learners sometimes drop by analogy with English 'dress him.'

(maan roz bachche ko skool kii yooniform pahnaatii hai।)
