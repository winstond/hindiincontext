भी x भी (bhii x bhii)

pattern This is not a fixed word but a correlative frame: भी (bhii) repeated across two elements to mean 'both X and Y'. It relies entirely on the ordinary meaning of भी (bhii, 'also/too') placed twice.
Sense 1 : both...and... (correlative use of also)
pattern Frame: [X] भी [Y] भी (X bhii Y bhii) placed directly after each of the two coordinated elements, e.g. वह भी आया, मैं भी आया (vah bhii aayaa, main bhii aayaa, 'both he and I came'). Unlike English 'both...and', Hindi does not need a separate word for 'both' -- the repeated भी (bhii) alone carries that meaning.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is less a 'word' than a habit of Hindi speech: repeating भी (bhii) is the everyday way to say 'both...and...', and it feels much more natural in conversation than reaching for दोनों (donon) plus 'and'. Listen for it in casual complaints or lists, like खाना भी ठीक था, कमरा भी अच्छा था (khaanaa bhii thiik thaa, kamraa bhii achchhaa thaa, 'both the food was fine and the room was nice').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to translate 'both' with a separate word like दोनों (donon) in addition to repeating भी (bhii), producing an unnatural double marking; native speakers usually let the repeated भी (bhii) alone do the job. 2) Placement matters: भी (bhii) goes right after each coordinated item, not at the end of the whole sentence -- राम भी श्याम भी आए (raam bhii shyaam bhii aae, 'both Ram and Shyam came') not राम श्याम आए भी भी.
Sample Sentences:
वो चाय भी पीता है, कॉफ़ी भी पीता है। He drinks both tea and coffee.
(vo chaay bhii piitaa hai, kofii bhii piitaa hai।)
