मोल लेना (mol lenaa)

verb Literally 'to take/buy at a price'; used mainly in the idiom मुसीबत मोल लेना (musiibat mol lenaa) and similar phrases meaning to knowingly bring trouble on oneself.
Sense 1 : to knowingly invite trouble/hardship on oneself
verb, transitive Almost always paired with an abstract noun like मुसीबत (musiibat), झगड़ा (jhagdaa), or दुश्मनी (dushmnii) as its object -- the frame is [trouble-word] + मोल लेना (mol lenaa).
Synonyms: बुलाना (bulaanaa, invite/summon, used figuratively of trouble), निमंत्रण देना (nimantran denaa, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a shopkeeper haggling over मोल (mol, price) -- the idiom borrows that image of deliberately agreeing to pay for something, and applies it to bad outcomes: कहीं मुसीबत मोल ले ली (kahiin musiibat mol le lii, 'you've gone and bought yourself trouble') is the classic scolding tone a parent or friend uses when someone acts recklessly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes take मोल (mol, price/value) too literally here and expect a real transaction; the sense is fully figurative -- no money changes hands. 2) The verb is transitive and takes ने (ne) marking in the perfective, matching लेना (lenaa) -- easy to forget since the whole phrase feels idiomatic rather than like an ordinary verb.

(tumne khaamkhaah yah musiibat mol le lii।)

Sense 2 : to purchase, buy at a price (literal)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: खरीदना (khariidnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You may still meet this literal sense in older stories or proverbs about bargaining in the bazaar, but a Delhi shopkeeper today will almost always say खरीदना (khariidnaa) rather than मोल लेना (mol lenaa) for a straightforward purchase.
Common Mistakes: 1) This literal sense is quite rare in modern speech -- खरीदना (khariidnaa) is the everyday word for 'to buy'; मोल लेना (mol lenaa) in this literal meaning sounds slightly old-fashioned or bookish.
