भर (bhar)

particle भर (bhar) is mainly a suffix-like particle attached to a noun meaning 'the whole of' or 'entire' (as in दिनभर (dinbhar, 'all day long')), and by extension it also means 'only, just' (limiting sense). Both uses attach directly after a noun without space in most modern writing, though older texts may show a space.
Sense 1 : the whole/entire duration or extent of
particle Attaches directly to a time or place noun (no space) to mean 'throughout/the entire span of': दिनभर (dinbhar, 'all day'), रातभर (raatbhar, 'all night'), उम्रभर (umrbhar, 'one's whole life').
Synonyms: पूरा (pooraa, used as a separate word before the noun rather than attached after it), समस्त (samast, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday complaints and small talk: दिनभर काम (dinbhar kaam, 'work all day long'), रातभर जागना (raatbhar jaagnaa, 'staying up all night'). It's a very economical way to say 'the whole' without reaching for पूरा (pooraa) every time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often insert a space or treat भर (bhar) as a standalone adjective like पूरा (pooraa); in this sense it is a bound suffix and is written attached to the noun it follows. 2) It comes after the noun, unlike पूरा (pooraa) which comes before: दिनभर (dinbhar) not भर दिन (bhar din).

Sense 2 : only, just, merely (limiting emphasis)
particle Follows the word it limits, often a quantity or a single item: एक भर (ek bhar, 'just one'), इतना भर (itnaa bhar, 'just this much') -- gives a sense of 'that's all, nothing more.'
Synonyms: सिर्फ़ (sirf), केवल (keval, slightly more formal), मात्र (maatr, formal/written)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in the common phrase नाम भर का (naam bhar kaa, 'in name only'), used to describe something that exists only nominally, like a dost naam bhar ka -- a friend only in name, not in deed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners reach for सिर्फ़ (sirf) automatically and forget that भर (bhar) placed after the word gives the same limiting sense with a slightly softer, more conversational feel, as in नाम भर के लिए (naam bhar ke lie, 'just for the sake of name/formality').
