छोड़कर (chhodkar)

postposition/conjunction The conjunctive participle form of छोड़ना (chhodnaa, 'to leave/let go'), used mainly to mean 'except/apart from' and to describe leaving a place or doing one thing before another.
Sense 1 : except, apart from
postposition Follows the noun it excludes, often in the frame X को छोड़कर (X ko chhodkar) meaning 'except for X'.
Synonyms: सिवाय (sivaay), के अलावा (ke alaavaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is probably the most common way to say 'except' in everyday Hindi -- you will hear it constantly in sentences like सबको छोड़कर (sabko chhodkar, 'except everyone') or इसे छोड़कर (ise chhodkar, 'except this'). It grew naturally out of the verb छोड़ना (chhodnaa) -- think of it as 'setting aside' something before talking about the rest.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the को (ko) before छोड़कर (chhodkar) when excluding a noun -- राम को छोड़कर (raam ko chhodkar, 'except for Ram') is the natural pattern, not just राम छोड़कर (raam chhodkar). 2) Confusing this exclusive sense with the literal 'having left' sense of the same word can lead to ambiguity, but context (a list of items vs. a place) usually clears it up.

Sense 2 : having left, after leaving (sequential action)
conjunction Functions as a conjunctive participle: 'X छोड़कर, Y' means 'having left/quit X, (subject did) Y' -- links two actions by the same subject in sequence.
Synonyms: छोड़के (chhodke, colloquial variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in real-life stories all the time -- घर छोड़कर (ghar chhodkar, 'having left home') शहर आ गया (shahar aa gayaa, 'came to the city') is the classic shape of a migration story in Hindi conversation. It is the same verb root as sense 1, just used in its literal, physical meaning of departure rather than the abstract 'except' meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'leaving' in every context, but with abstract nouns like नौकरी (naukrii, 'job') or आदत (aadat, 'habit') it means 'having given up/quit', not physically walking away.
