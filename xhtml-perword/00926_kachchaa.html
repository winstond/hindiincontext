कच्चा (kachchaa)

adjective The core idea is 'unfinished, raw, or unstable' -- it covers literal rawness (food, materials), physical fragility (roads, structures), and figurative incompleteness (relationships, plans, knowledge). One adjective, many domains where 'not yet ready/solid' applies.
Sense 1 : raw, uncooked
adjective
Synonyms: अधपका (adhapkaa, undercooked, narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around food: कच्चा प्याज़ (kachchaa pyaaz, 'raw onion') on the side of a plate, or a mother scolding that the सब्ज़ी (sabzii) is still कच्ची (kachchii, 'raw/undercooked') and needs more time on the flame. Its opposite पक्का (pakkaa) means 'cooked/ripe' in this same food frame, so the pair is worth learning together.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अनकुक्ड (ankukd) or a borrowed word -- कच्चा (kachchaa) is the natural, native choice for 'raw/uncooked' in everyday kitchen talk.

Sense 2 : unpaved, temporary, or structurally weak (roads, houses, materials)
adjective
Synonyms: अस्थायी (asthaayii, temporary, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In villages and smaller towns you will hear कच्चा मकान (kachchaa makaan, 'a mud/unbaked-brick house') contrasted with पक्का मकान (pakkaa makaan, 'a proper concrete house') -- the पक्का (pakkaa house is a real marker of a family's rising economic status. Similarly कच्ची सड़क (kachchii sadak, 'unpaved road') is the dirt track before the government lays a पक्की सड़क (pakkii sadak).

Sense 3 : unripe (fruit)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: कच्चा आम (kachchaa aam, 'raw/unripe mango') is a specific and beloved category in Indian cooking -- it is what goes into अचार (achaar, pickle) and खट्टी चटनी (khattii chatnii, tangy chutney), quite distinct from the sweet ripe mango eaten as fruit.

Sense 4 : incomplete, unreliable, or immature (plans, relationships, skills, information)
adjective
Synonyms: अधूरा (adhooraa, incomplete, closer to 'unfinished' than 'shaky')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is where कच्चा (kachchaa) becomes genuinely useful outside the kitchen: a teacher might call a student's answer कच्चा (kachchaa) meaning under-prepared or not solid, and elders describe a young couple's रिश्ता (rishtaa, relationship/bond) as कच्चा (kachchaa) if it feels untested or fragile. The underlying image is always the same: something that hasn't 'cooked' or 'set' enough yet.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only know the food sense and miss this very common figurative one -- कच्चा इरादा (kachchaa iraadaa, 'a shaky resolve/intention') or कच्ची जानकारी (kachchii jaankaarii, 'sketchy/unreliable information') are natural, frequent phrases. 2) कच्चा माल (kachchaa maal) is a fixed business/economics term for 'raw material,' not a literal food phrase -- worth learning as a set expression.
Sample Sentences:
उसका जवाब अभी भी कच्चा है, थोड़ी और तैयारी करो। His answer is still half-baked, prepare a bit more.
