गला बैठना (galaa baithnaa)

verb A body-idiom pairing गला (galaa, throat) with बैठना (baithnaa, to sit) to mean the voice going hoarse or croaky, usually from a cold, shouting, or overuse.
Sense 1 : for the voice to become hoarse
verb, intransitive गला (galaa) is the grammatical subject and takes intransitive agreement; the person affected is marked with का (kaa) or की/के as needed to show possession, e.g. मेरा गला बैठ गया है (meraa galaa baith gayaa hai, 'my voice has gone hoarse') -- not with को (ko) as one might expect from other body-state idioms.
Synonyms: आवाज़ बैठना (aavaaz baithnaa, same idea, using voice instead of throat), गला ख़राब होना (galaa kharaab honaa, broader: sore/unwell throat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in winter, when Delhi's dry cold and pollution make गला बैठना (galaa baithnaa) a near-daily complaint -- often followed by home remedies like गर्म पानी (garm paanii, 'warm water') with नमक (namak, 'salt') or हल्दी वाला दूध (haldii vaalaa doodh, 'turmeric milk'). The image is of the throat 'sitting down,' i.e. losing its usual lift or resonance, much as legs 'sit' when tired.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for को (ko) as with लगना-type constructions (e.g. मुझे गला बैठ गया, incorrect) -- this idiom instead uses a possessive: मेरा गला बैठ गया (meraa galaa baith gayaa). 2) Confusing this with गला ख़राब होना (galaa kharaab honaa), which covers general throat trouble (soreness, infection) rather than specifically the voice becoming hoarse/croaky. 3) The compound verb बैठ जाना (baith jaanaa) is far more common in speech than the bare बैठना (baithnaa) for this sense -- learners who only use बैठना alone sound slightly incomplete here.
