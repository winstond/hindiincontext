डालना (daalnaa)

verb A workhorse verb of putting/dropping/pouring something in (daalnaa), which extends to throwing things into a receptacle, draping one thing over another, and -- most importantly for fluent speech -- attaching to another verb's stem as a vector verb marking abrupt or decisive completion.
Sense 1 : to put in / add (an ingredient)
verb, transitive Common frame: [ingredient] + में/directly + डालना (daalnaa), as when adding something to a pot or dish already on the stove.
Synonyms: मिलाना (milaanaa, to mix in)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb of the Indian kitchen: every recipe narration is full of it -- नमक डालना (namak daalnaa, 'add salt'), चीनी डालना (chiinii daalnaa, 'add sugar'), तेल डालना (tel daalnaa, 'add oil'). If you've watched a Hindi cooking video, you've heard this word a dozen times before the दाल (daal) is even done.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for रखना (rakhnaa) when they mean 'add an ingredient' -- रखना (rakhnaa) is for placing something down carefully, while डालना (daalnaa) is specifically about pouring or dropping something in. 2) With liquids, डालना (daalnaa) means 'pour in/add', not 'pour out' -- for emptying something out, other verbs are used.

Sense 2 : to throw/cast/drop (into a receptacle)
verb, transitive Frame: [object] + [receptacle/box/slot] + में + डालना (daalnaa) -- the sense of committing something into a defined space (a bin, a ballot box, a mailbox).
Synonyms: फेंकना (phenknaa, to throw/discard, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Election season in Delhi is full of इस बार वोट ज़रूर डालना (is baar vot zaroor daalnaa, 'be sure to cast your vote this time') on posters and in family WhatsApp forwards -- a nice window into how डालना (daalnaa) frames the ballot box like any other slot you drop something into.
Common Mistakes: 1) वोट डालना (vot daalnaa, 'to cast a vote') is a fixed civic expression -- learners sometimes substitute देना (denaa) here, but डालना (daalnaa) is the standard verb because you are physically dropping a ballot in. 2) कूड़ा डालना (koodaa daalnaa, 'to dump trash') and चिट्ठी डालना (chitthii daalnaa, 'to post a letter') both keep this same 'commit into a slot/receptacle' logic.

Sense 3 : to drape/spread over, cover
verb, transitive Frame: [surface/person] + पर + [covering] + डालना (daalnaa) -- one object is spread so it covers another, e.g. मेज़ पर कपड़ा डालना (mez par kapdaa daalnaa, 'to lay a cloth over the table').
Synonyms: ओढ़ाना (odhaanaa, to cover someone with a wrap)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a winter evening in Delhi: someone pulls a रज़ाई (razaaii, quilt) up and says कंबल डाल लो (kambal daal lo, 'pull the blanket over yourself') -- the verb captures that simple gesture of one layer settling over another, whether it's a sheet over a sleeping face or a cloth over a table before guests arrive.
Common Mistakes: 1) When covering *oneself* with a shawl or blanket, ओढ़ना (odhnaa) is more natural for the self-directed action, while डालना (daalnaa) tends to describe draping something over a surface or over someone else (or casually over one's own shoulders), e.g. कंबल डालना (kambal daalnaa, 'to throw a blanket over oneself').

Sense 4 : vector verb marking completion, abruptness, or decisiveness
verb, transitive Attaches to a verb stem (V + डालना, e.g. कह डालना (kah daalnaa, 'to blurt out'), मार डालना (maar daalnaa, 'to kill'), खा डालना (khaa daalnaa, 'to polish off eating')) as a vector/light verb: it doesn't add its own literal 'put/throw' meaning here, but colors the main verb with a sense of finality, suddenness, or full commitment.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same grammatical move you'll see with other light verbs like ले लेना (le lenaa) or कर देना (kar denaa) -- Hindi loves stacking a second verb onto the main one to sharpen its flavor. With डालना (daalnaa) specifically, the flavor is always about decisiveness or abruptness: something done suddenly, completely, or with a bit of force, as in रो डालना (ro daalnaa, 'to break down crying') or पी डालना (pii daalnaa, 'to down a drink in one go').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to translate डालना (daalnaa) literally in these compounds and get confused -- in कह डालना (kah daalnaa) there's no 'putting' happening at all; the डालना (daalnaa) is just intensifying कहना (kahnaa, 'to say') into 'blurt out/say it and be done with it'. 2) मार डालना (maar daalnaa) is noticeably stronger than plain मारना (maarnaa) -- मारना (maarnaa) can mean 'to hit', but मार डालना (maar daalnaa) pushes it all the way to 'kill'.
