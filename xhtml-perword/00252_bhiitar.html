भीतर (bhiitar)

postposition/adverb/adjective One core meaning: 'inside/within,' functioning both as a postposition (governing the oblique with के) and as an adverb of place. Closely related to अंदर (andar), which is far more common in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : inside (something), within
postposition Takes the oblique noun with के: घर के भीतर (ghar ke bhiitar, 'inside the house').
Synonyms: अंदर (andar), भीतर (bhiitar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: भीतर (bhiitar) and अंदर (andar) are near-perfect synonyms for physical 'inside,' but भीतर (bhiitar) leans a touch more formal or reflective -- you'll hear it more in writing, speeches, or when someone is being thoughtful, while अंदर (andar) dominates casual chatter about rooms, bags, and boxes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to अंदर (andar) and forget भीतर (bhiitar) exists as an equally correct, slightly more literary-sounding alternative -- both take के before them, so there's no structural pitfall, just a word-choice one.
Sample Sentences:
कमरे के भीतर एक पुरानी अलमारी रखी थी। An old cupboard was kept inside the room.
(kamre ke bhiitar ek puraanii almaarii rakhii thii।)

Sense 2 : inside, within (place, no object stated)
adverb Used alone, without a following noun, to mean 'inside' as a location: भीतर आओ (bhiitar aao, 'come inside').
Synonyms: अंदर (andar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: When used on its own -- भीतर आइए (bhiitar aaie, 'please come in') -- it carries a slightly warmer, more inviting or formal tone than the blunter अंदर आओ (andar aao), so it shows up often in polite hosting language.
Sample Sentences:
भीतर आइए, बाहर बहुत ठंड है। Please come inside, it's very cold outside.

Sense 3 : inner, internal (figurative, of mind/heart/self)
adjective Used in reflective or introspective contexts, often paired with मन (man) or हृदय (hriday) to point at inner feeling rather than physical space: मेरे भीतर (mere bhiitar, 'within me, inside my heart').
Synonyms: अंतर्मन (antarman, of the inner self), मन (man)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense -- मेरे भीतर एक डर है (mere bhiitar ek dar hai, 'there is a fear within me') -- is common in reflective speech, poetry, and self-help or spiritual talk, treating emotions as things residing inside a person rather than things one simply 'has,' a very Hindi way of framing inner experience.

(mere bhiitar ek dar hai jo mujhe kabhii chain se sone nahiin detaa।)
