नहाना (nahaanaa)

verb A simple everyday verb meaning 'to bathe/take a bath'; one core sense, used both literally and in a few figurative expressions of being drenched or thoroughly covered in something.
Sense 1 : to bathe, take a bath
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: स्नान करना (snaan karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Indian households नहाना (nahaanaa) is a fixed part of the morning routine, often done before the first meal or before entering the kitchen or puja room, so you will hear questions like नहा लिया? (nahaa liyaa?, 'Did you bathe already?') as a normal check-in, especially from parents to kids in the morning rush. It is very commonly paired with the completive verb लेना (lenaa) as नहा लेना (nahaa lenaa) to suggest finishing the bath, e.g. before leaving the house.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to add an object like पानी (paanii, 'water') as if 'bathing water' were required grammatically -- नहाना (nahaanaa) is intransitive and complete on its own: मैं नहाया (main nahaayaa, 'I bathed') needs nothing else. 2) The common daily routine phrase is नहाना-धोना (nahaanaa-dhonaa, 'to bathe and wash up'), often used as a set pair rather than नहाना (nahaanaa) alone when talking about the whole morning routine.

Sense 2 : to be drenched or covered all over (figurative)
verb, intransitive Used with an instrumental से (se) phrase to say what one is covered/drenched in, e.g. खून से नहाना (khoon se nahaanaa, 'to be bathed in blood').
Synonyms: भीगना (bhiignaa, 'to get wet', related but not identical)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative extension shows up mainly in dramatic or poetic contexts -- खून से नहाना (khoon se nahaanaa, 'to be bathed in blood') or पसीने से नहाना (pasiine se nahaanaa, 'to be drenched in sweat') -- rather than in casual daily conversation, so treat it as a vivid but occasional usage rather than something you will reach for every day.
