चाहे x चाहे (chaahe x chaahe)

pattern This is a correlative frame using चाहे (chaahe) twice around alternatives to mean 'whether X or Y' -- it presents two (or more) options as equally irrelevant to the outcome that follows. It is not a single word but a fixed pattern built on चाहे (chaahe), the concessive form of चाहना (chaahnaa).
Sense 1 : whether...or (presenting alternatives as making no difference)
pattern The frame is चाहे + [option 1] + चाहे + [option 2] + [main clause], and the main clause typically states a result that holds regardless of which option is true -- often reinforced with फिर भी (phir bhii, 'even so') or तो भी (to bhii, 'even then'). Each slot after चाहे (chaahe) can be a noun phrase, adjective, or full clause: चाहे कोई कुछ भी कहे, चाहे कुछ भी हो (chaahe koii kuchh bhii kahe, chaahe kuchh bhii ho, 'whatever anyone says, whatever happens').
Synonyms: चाहे...या (chaahe...yaa, one-word-position variant), या तो...या (yaa to...yaa, not a true synonym -- marks a strict choice rather than indifference)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this pattern constantly in everyday Delhi speech when someone is being firm or fatalistic: चाहे कुछ भी हो जाए, मैं नहीं जाऊँगा (chaahe kuchh bhii ho jaae, main nahiin jaaoongaa, 'whatever happens, I won't go'). It has the flavor of throwing up your hands at fate or at other people's opinions -- a very Hindi way of saying 'I don't care which, the answer is the same.' The pattern pairs naturally with चाहे...न चाहे (chaahe...n chaahe, 'whether one wants to or not'), which circles back to its root meaning in चाहना (chaahnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to translate this word-for-word as 'want' because of its root in चाहना (chaahnaa) -- but in this frame चाहे (chaahe) has nothing to do with wanting; it purely marks 'no matter which.' 2) The verb in each चाहे-clause is usually in the subjunctive (चाहे वह जाए, चाहे न जाए (chaahe vah jaae, chaahe n jaae, 'whether he goes or doesn't go')), not the simple present, which trips up learners still shaky on the subjunctive. 3) Dropping the second चाहे (chaahe) and just using one, expecting the pattern to still read as a contrast rather than a plain conditional.
