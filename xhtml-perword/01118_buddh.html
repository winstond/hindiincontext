बुद्ध (buddh)

noun/adjective Primarily a proper noun referring to Gautama Buddha (buddh); also used as a common adjective/noun meaning 'enlightened, awakened one'.
Sense 1 : Gautama Buddha (proper noun)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गौतम बुद्ध (gautam buddh), तथागत (tathaagat, literary/religious epithet), भगवान बुद्ध (bhagvaan buddh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the name every Indian schoolchild learns alongside महात्मा गांधी (mahaatmaa gaandhii) and अशोक (ashok) -- बुद्ध (buddh) founded बौद्ध धर्म (bauddh dharm, Buddhism) and is revered across India, especially in places like सारनाथ (saarnaath) and बोधगया (bodhagyaa). Statues of him sitting in meditation are a common sight in homes and gardens across Delhi-NCR, often just for their peaceful aesthetic rather than strict religious observance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat बुद्ध (buddh) and बुध (budh, the planet Mercury / the day Wednesday) as the same word because of the similar spelling and single vs double द; they are unrelated. 2) As a proper name it does not take gender agreement changes the way the common-noun/adjective sense might suggest.

Sense 2 : enlightened, awakened (literal/spiritual sense)
adjective
Synonyms: ज्ञानी (gyaanii), प्रबुद्ध (prabuddh, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: बुद्ध (buddh) literally comes from the Sanskrit root बुध (budh, 'to know, awaken'), so calling someone बुद्ध (buddh) in this sense casts them as having attained deep spiritual awareness -- it is a weighty, respectful word, not used lightly the way 'wise' might be in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) This adjectival sense is rare in everyday speech outside religious/philosophical discussion -- learners are more likely to need the proper-noun sense than this one.
