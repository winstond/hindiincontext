सही (sahii)

adjective/interjection/adverb Mainly an adjective meaning 'correct/right,' but also doubles as an everyday interjection of agreement ('right!', 'okay!') and appears in a common idiomatic phrase meaning 'let's just say/granted.'
Sense 1 : correct, right, accurate
adjective
Synonyms: ठीक (thiik), दुरुस्त (durust), उचित (uchit, somewhat formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear सही (sahii) constantly in classrooms and exam contexts -- सही जवाब (sahii javaab, 'correct answer') is the phrase every Hindi-medium student grows up with, much like checking a homework sheet for right and wrong marks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ठीक (thiik) in every context; सही (sahii) leans more toward factual/logical correctness (a correct answer, a correct decision) while ठीक (thiik) also covers 'fine/okay' as a state of being. 2) सही (sahii) is invariant -- it does not change form for gender or number, unlike many other -ई adjectives that look similar.

Sense 2 : right!, correct!, okay! (as an exclamation of agreement)
interjection Used standalone as a one-word response to confirm or agree with something someone just said, much like English 'right!' or 'exactly!'.
Synonyms: ठीक है (thiik hai), बिल्कुल (bilkul)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear friends toss out just सही! (sahii!, 'right on!') in casual chat -- it has a slightly youthful, upbeat energy, almost like saying 'nice!' or 'spot on!' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes overuse this as a full sentence replacement for ठीक है (thiik hai); सही (sahii) alone as an interjection feels punchier and more casual, closer to snapping 'right!' than the fuller, more neutral 'okay.'

Sense 3 : granted, let's say, so be it (in the fixed phrase भला सही / यही सही / न सही)
adverb Appears after a clause to concede a point, often in patterns like ऐसा सही (aisaa sahii, 'fine, let it be so'), न सही तो (n sahii to, 'if not, then...'), or यही सही (yahii sahii, 'this is fine too'). It marks resigned acceptance or concession, not literal 'correctness.'
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is a lovely bit of understated Hindi resignation -- चलो, यही सही (chalo, yahii sahii, 'fine, let's go with this then') is the kind of thing you say with a small shrug when you're settling for a decent-but-not-ideal option.
Common Mistakes: 1) This concessive use is easy to miss because it looks identical to the adjective; the giveaway is that it trails a clause rather than modifying a noun, e.g. न आए तो न सही (n aae to n sahii, 'if he doesn't come, so be it') -- there's no noun being called 'correct' here.
