समर्थ (samarth)

adjective An adjective meaning capable or able-bodied enough to do something, often used with a following infinitive or with होना (honaa) to describe having the capacity or resources for a task.
Sense 1 : capable, able (to do something)
adjective Often followed by an infinitive plus होना (honaa), as in ऐसा करने में समर्थ है (aisaa karne men samarth hai, 'is capable of doing this').
Synonyms: सक्षम (saksham), योग्य (yogy, can also mean 'worthy'), काबिल (kaabil)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet समर्थ (samarth) most often in formal writing, news reports, or official contexts talking about a person's or a country's capability -- for instance, discussions of a state being 'self-reliant' or 'capable' economically. In everyday chat about whether someone can lift a box or finish a task, most Delhi speakers reach for सक्षम (saksham) or काबिल (kaabil) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the में (men) that usually links समर्थ (samarth) to the preceding infinitive phrase, saying something like यह करने समर्थ (yah karne samarth) instead of यह करने में समर्थ (yah karne men samarth). 2) समर्थ (samarth) is more formal/written than सक्षम (saksham) or काबिल (kaabil), which are more common in everyday speech; using it in casual conversation can sound a bit bookish.

Sense 2 : financially well-off, strong/resourceful
adjective
Synonyms: संपन्न (sampann, often specifically financial), सशक्त (sashakt, more about strength/power)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: When applied to a person's overall standing rather than a specific task, समर्थ (samarth) shades toward 'well-off' or 'resourceful' -- someone who has the means to manage life comfortably, not just the physical ability to do one thing. This sense overlaps with संपन्न (sampann) but keeps a flavor of inner strength or capability rather than pure wealth.
