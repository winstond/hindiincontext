समय (samay)

noun The standard, everyday word for 'time' in both the abstract sense and the concrete sense of 'a particular moment/occasion'; also appears in fixed phrases about timeliness.
Sense 1 : time (as a general concept)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वक़्त (vaqt), काल (kaal, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in daily life -- from समय पर आइए (samay par aaie, 'please come on time') at a family function to news anchors saying अब समय हो गया है (ab samay ho gayaa hai, 'now it is time'). It pairs naturally with पर (par) to mean 'on time', a value Indian families invoke often even as punctuality itself is treated flexibly in practice.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for घंटा (ghantaa) or देर (der) when they mean 'time' in general; घंटा (ghantaa) is specifically 'hour' and देर (der) is specifically 'delay/lateness', not time as a concept. 2) समय (samay) and वक़्त (vaqt) are freely interchangeable in most everyday speech -- there is no strong register split here despite वक़्त (vaqt) being of Persian origin.
Sample Sentences:

(samay bahut kiimtii hotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : a particular point/period, occasion
noun, masculine Used in phrases like उस समय (us samay, 'at that time') and इस समय (is samay, 'at this time/currently') to anchor an event to a specific moment or era.
Synonyms: मौका (maukaa, occasion/opportunity), दौर (daur, era/phase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in storytelling and history-telling alike: a grandparent might begin a story with उस समय (us samay, 'in those days') the way an English speaker says 'back then'. It also appears in formal contexts like exam papers asking about a ऐतिहासिक समय (aitihaasik samay, 'historical period').
Common Mistakes: 1) उस समय (us samay) and तब (tab) both mean 'then/at that time', but उस समय (us samay) is more explicit and slightly more formal -- learners can use them somewhat interchangeably in speech.
