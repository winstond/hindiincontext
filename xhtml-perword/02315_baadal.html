बादल (baadal)

noun Means 'cloud' -- a simple, high-frequency noun with one main everyday sense, plus a mild figurative extension used for gloom or a spreading darkness.
Sense 1 : cloud
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मेघ (megh, literary), घन (ghan, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बादल (baadal) shows up constantly in monsoon small talk in Delhi -- आज बादल छाए हैं (aaj baadal chhaae hain, 'clouds have gathered today') is the classic way to comment on a heavy, humid sky before rain. The verb छाना (chhaanaa, 'to spread/cover') pairs naturally with it, since clouds are pictured as spreading over the sky rather than just appearing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat बादल (baadal) as feminine by analogy with other -अल words, but it is masculine: काला बादल (kaalaa baadal, 'black cloud'), not काली बादल (kaalii baadal).

Sense 2 : figurative: a gloom, shadow, or spreading darkness (of sorrow, danger, etc.)
noun, masculine Used mainly in set phrases like गम के बादल (gam ke baadal, 'clouds of sorrow') or खतरे के बादल (khatre ke baadal, 'clouds of danger'), where बादल (baadal) stands for something ominous or sorrowful gathering over a person or situation.
Synonyms: छाया (chhaayaa, shadow/gloom sense), साया (saayaa, shadow/gloom sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same 'gathering over the sky' image extended to emotion -- Hindi film dialogue and poetry love lines like ग़म के बादल छा गए (gham ke baadal chhaa gae, 'clouds of sorrow have gathered') to describe sudden sadness settling over someone, much like English 'a cloud hangs over him' but rooted in the visual of an actual monsoon sky darkening.
