धूमधाम (dhoomdhaam)

noun A single-sense noun meaning pomp, fanfare, or grand festivity -- the noise, color, and show of a big celebration.
Sense 1 : pomp, grandeur, festivity
noun, feminine Almost always used with से (se) as an adverbial phrase -- धूमधाम से (dhoomdhaam se, 'with great pomp/fanfare') -- to describe how an event like a wedding or festival was celebrated.
Synonyms: तड़क-भड़क (tadak-bhadak, more about showy display than festivity), शानो-शौकत (shaano-shaukat, formal), जश्न (jashn, focuses on celebration itself rather than the grandeur)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word captures the sensory overload of a big Indian wedding or festival -- the lights, the band, the crowd, the noise -- all at once. You'll hear it constantly around wedding season: शादी बहुत धूमधाम से हुई (shaadii bahut dhoomdhaam se huii, 'the wedding happened with a lot of pomp'). It's an echo-word (a reduplicated pair built on धूम, dhuum, meaning noise/fame), which is common in Hindi for words describing sensory abundance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use धूमधाम (dhoomdhaam) as an adjective directly before a noun (like 'a grand wedding'); instead, Hindi typically turns it into the adverbial धूमधाम से (dhoomdhaam se) and pairs it with a verb like मनाना (manaanaa, 'to celebrate'), e.g. शादी धूमधाम से मनाई गई (shaadii dhoomdhaam se manaaii gaii, 'the wedding was celebrated with great pomp'). 2) It describes external grandeur and celebration, not inner joy or happiness -- don't confuse it with खुशी (khushii).

शादी बहुत धूमधाम से हुई। The wedding happened with a lot of pomp and show.
(shaadii bahut dhoomdhaam se huii।)
