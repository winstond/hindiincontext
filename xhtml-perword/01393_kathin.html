कठिन (kathin)

adjective A common adjective meaning 'difficult, hard, tough' -- used for tasks, situations, exams, and questions. Close in meaning to मुश्किल (mushkil) but slightly more formal/bookish in feel.
Sense 1 : difficult, hard (of a task, situation, question)
adjective
Synonyms: मुश्किल (mushkil), दुष्कर (dushkar, literary), कष्टसाध्य (kashtsaadhy, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet कठिन (kathin) constantly in the school and exam world -- teachers and textbooks love saying यह प्रश्न कठिन है (yah prashn kathin hai, 'this question is difficult'). In daily bazaar chatter, though, people often reach for मुश्किल (mushkil) instead, so knowing both gives you the right register for the right room.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मुश्किल (mushkil) as the default word and forget कठिन (kathin) exists -- both are correct and interchangeable in most everyday contexts, so don't overthink the choice. 2) कठिन (kathin) sounds a touch more formal or written than मुश्किल (mushkil), so in casual chit-chat about a rough day, मुश्किल (mushkil) or मुश्किल-सा (mushkil-saa) may feel more natural, while कठिन (kathin) fits school, exams, and writing.

Sense 2 : harsh, severe, stern (of a person's nature or a strict rule)
adjective
Synonyms: सख़्त (sakht), कठोर (kathor)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with कठोर (kathor), which shares the same root idea of 'hardness' extended to character or discipline -- a कठिन अनुशासन (kathin anushaasan, 'strict discipline') describes a regime as unyielding, not just difficult to complete.
