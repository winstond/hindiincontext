रवैया (ravaiyaa)

noun A single-sense everyday word meaning 'attitude' or 'manner of behaving' -- usually used to comment on how someone is treating a person or situation.
Sense 1 : attitude, manner of behaving toward someone/something
noun, masculine Commonly used with के साथ (ke saath) to name who the attitude is directed at, e.g. किसी के साथ रवैया (kisii ke saath ravaiyaa, 'attitude toward someone'), and paired with verbs like अपनाना (apnaanaa, to adopt) or बदलना (badalnaa, to change).
Synonyms: रुख (rukh), बर्ताव (bartaav), नज़रिया (nazriyaa, viewpoint/attitude), व्यवहार (vyavahaar, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word shows up constantly in Indian news and political commentary -- सरकार का रवैया (sarkaar kaa ravaiyaa, 'the government's attitude') is a stock phrase you'll hear in debates about policy or bureaucracy. It's also a very common word for venting about a shopkeeper, boss, or relative who is being difficult: उसका रवैया अच्छा नहीं है (uskaa ravaiyaa achchhaa nahiin hai, 'his attitude isn't good') is something you'll hear in everyday complaints.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for व्यवहार (vyavahaar) in every context, but रवैया (ravaiyaa) specifically carries a judgmental undertone -- it is usually used when commenting critically on someone's attitude (उसका रवैया ठीक नहीं है, uskaa ravaiyaa thiik nahiin hai, 'his attitude isn't right'), whereas व्यवहार (vyavahaar) is more neutral and can describe behavior in general, positive or negative. 2) The word is almost always used with a possessive or genitive (मेरा रवैया, meraa ravaiyaa; सरकार का रवैया, sarkaar kaa ravaiyaa) -- it rarely stands alone without specifying whose attitude is being discussed.
