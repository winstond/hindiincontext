भ्रांति (bhraanti)

noun A formal/literary word meaning 'illusion' or 'misconception' -- a mistaken belief that feels true. Mostly found in writing, speeches, and thoughtful conversation rather than casual chat.
Sense 1 : misconception, false belief
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गलतफहमी (galatphahmii), भ्रम (bhram), ग़लतफ़हमी (ghalatfahmii, variant form)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will meet this word most often in newspaper editorials, political speeches, or moral/philosophical writing -- for instance correcting a popular but false idea: 'यह एक भ्रांति है' (yah ek bhraanti hai, 'this is a misconception'). It shares its root with भ्रम (bhram) and भ्रमित (bhramit, 'confused/deluded'), all built on the Sanskrit idea of 'wandering' or 'going astray' -- so a भ्रांति (bhraanti) is literally a mind that has wandered off the correct path.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गलतफहमी (galatphahmii) in everyday speech instead -- भ्रांति (bhraanti) sounds noticeably more formal/written and is better suited to essays, speeches, or serious discussion than to casual conversation. 2) Don't confuse with भ्रम (bhram), which is more common in speech and covers similar ground; भ्रांति (bhraanti) leans slightly more toward a specific mistaken belief or error of judgment rather than a general state of confusion.

यह एक भ्रांति है कि सफलता के लिए किस्मत ही सब कुछ है। It is a misconception that luck is everything for success.
(yah ek bhraanti hai ki saphaltaa ke lie kismat hii sab kuchh hai।)

Sense 2 : illusion, optical/perceptual illusion
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भ्रम (bhram), मायाजाल (maayaajaal, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: In more technical or philosophical contexts -- discussing perception, dreams, or Vedantic ideas about maya -- भ्रांति (bhraanti) can mean a perceptual illusion, like mistaking a rope for a snake in the dark, a classic example used in Indian philosophy classes.
