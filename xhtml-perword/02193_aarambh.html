आरंभ (aarambh)

noun/verb A formal/literary word for 'beginning' or 'start', used as both a noun and (less commonly) a verb; in everyday speech, शुरू (shuroo) is far more common.
Sense 1 : beginning, start, commencement
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शुरुआत (shuruaat), शुरू (shuroo), प्रारंभ (praarambh, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear आरंभ (aarambh) at the start of formal events -- a school assembly, a puja, a speech -- where the tone is deliberately elevated, much like 'commencement' versus 'start' in English. In daily chatter about starting your commute or a movie, शुरू (shuroo) is what people actually say.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for आरंभ (aarambh) in casual speech where a native speaker would simply say शुरुआत (shuruaat) or शुरू (shuroo) -- आरंभ (aarambh) sounds noticeably formal or written, like opening a ceremony or a book chapter, not starting your day.
Sample Sentences:
सत्र का आरंभ सरस्वती वंदना से हुआ। The session began with a prayer to Saraswati.
(satr kaa aarambh sarasvtii vandnaa se huaa।)

Sense 2 : to begin, to commence (something)
verb, transitive Used as आरंभ करना (aarambh karnaa) 'to begin (something)' -- आरंभ (aarambh) itself does not conjugate as a standalone verb but combines with करना (karnaa) to form this compound.
Synonyms: शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa), प्रारंभ करना (praarambh karnaa, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the formal counterpart to the everyday शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa); you will meet आरंभ करना (aarambh karnaa) in inaugurations, textbooks, and official announcements rather than in kitchen or market talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) This word almost never appears alone as a verb -- learners should expect it paired with करना (karnaa) or होना (honaa), and using it without either sounds incomplete to a native ear.

(samaaroh adhyaksh ke bhaashan se aarambh hogaa।)
