आम (aam)

noun/adjective Two distinct senses: the fruit mango (a noun) and the common adjective meaning 'ordinary, general, common' as in आम आदमी (aam aadmii, 'common man').
Sense 1 : mango (the fruit)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आम (aam) is called the 'king of fruits' (फलों का राजा, phalon kaa raajaa) in India, and summer conversation revolves heavily around which variety -- दशहरी (dashahrii), लंगड़ा (langdaa), अल्फांसो (alphaanso) -- is best. Raw green mango (कच्चा आम, kachchaa aam) is used for tangy pickles and chutneys, quite distinct from the ripe fruit.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget आम (aam) is masculine, leading to wrong adjective/verb agreement, e.g. आम मीठा है (aam miithaa hai, 'the mango is sweet') not आम मीठी है (aam miithii hai).

Sense 2 : common, ordinary, general
adjective
Synonyms: सामान्य (saamaany), साधारण (saadhaaran)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आम आदमी (aam aadmii, 'the common man') is a phrase you'll hear constantly in Indian political and social discourse -- it even became a party name (आम आदमी पार्टी, aam aadmii paartii). You'll also often meet आम तौर पर (aam taur par, 'generally, usually') as a very frequent discourse adverbial.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may confuse this आम (aam, 'common') with the unrelated noun आम (aam, 'mango') since they are spelled identically; context (usually preceding a noun like आदमी aadmii or तौर taur) makes the intended sense clear.
