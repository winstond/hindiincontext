खूँटी (khoontii)

noun A small peg, hook, or pin used to hang things or stake something down; also used figuratively for a fixed anchor point, as in the idiom about tying an animal to its peg.
Sense 1 : wall peg / hook
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कील (kiil, nail-like peg), हुक (huk, loanword, for a metal hook)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In most Indian homes you will spot a row of these on the back of a door or inside an almirah -- coats, bags, keys, even the odd towel end up on a खूँटी (khoontii). It is a humble, everyday object, the kind of word that shows up in household instructions like 'hang it on the peg' (use khoontii par taang do).
Common Mistakes: 1) खूँटी (khoontii) is a small peg or hook fixed to a wall or post, distinct from कील (kiil) which leans toward a metal nail driven into wood or wall -- the two overlap but खूँटी (khoontii) is what you actually hang a coat or a bag on.

Sense 2 : stake / picket (for tethering an animal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मेख (mekh, driven-in stake)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the peg driven into the ground to which a goat, cow, or buffalo is tied with a rope -- a very common rural and semi-rural image. It feeds directly into the well-known idiom अपनी खूँटी से बंधे रहना (apnii khoontii se bandhe rahnaa, 'to stay tied to one's own peg'), used for someone who sticks to their own limited world or habits and does not venture beyond it.
