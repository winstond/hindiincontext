गिनना (ginnaa)

verb A transitive verb meaning 'to count' -- covers literal counting of items as well as the figurative sense of 'considering' or 'reckoning' someone/something as a certain type.
Sense 1 : to count (numbers, items)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a shopkeeper in a Delhi market counting out change -- पैसे गिनना (paise ginnaa) -- this is the everyday image behind the word. It is the ordinary verb used whenever numbers, coins, days, or objects are being tallied one by one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse गिनना (ginnaa, 'to count') with गिरना (girnaa, 'to fall') -- the spelling and sound are close but the meanings are unrelated. 2) As a transitive verb, it takes ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने पैसे गिने (usne paise gine, 'he counted the money').

Sense 2 : to consider, regard as, count someone/something as
verb, transitive Used with एक/समान/जैसा-type complements, e.g. उसे अपना दोस्त गिनना (use apnaa dost ginnaa, 'to consider him a friend') -- structurally similar to how English uses 'to count someone as.'
Synonyms: समझना (samajhnaa), मानना (maannaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the figurative cousin of the counting sense: just as you count coins one by one, you mentally 'count' a person into a category -- as a friend, an enemy, or among the great. You'll hear this in phrases like उसे बड़े आदमियों में गिना जाता है (use bade aadmiyon men ginaa jaataa hai, 'he is counted among great men'), often in the passive.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for समझना (samajhnaa) by default in this sense and don't realize गिनना (ginnaa) works the same way -- both are natural, but गिनना (ginnaa) carries a slight flavor of 'tallying' someone into a category or group.
