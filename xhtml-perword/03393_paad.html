पाद (paad)

noun Primarily a crude/casual word for breaking wind, though it also has a literary sense meaning 'foot' or 'verse-quarter' (as in a poem's structure), which is deprioritized here.
Sense 1 : fart, flatulence
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गैस (gais, euphemistic, medical-ish)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is playground and family-joke vocabulary in Hindi -- the kind of word that gets kids (and honestly, adults too) giggling. It is not vulgar in the sense of being taboo like a swear word, but it is definitely not something you would say in a boardroom or to your boss.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a blunt, somewhat crude word -- not used in polite or formal company. In front of elders or in mixed formal settings, people say something vaguer like गैस बनना (gais bannaa) or पेट में गड़बड़ (pet men gadbad) instead. 2) The common verb phrase is पाद मारना (paad maarnaa, 'to fart') or पाद निकलना (paad nikalnaa, 'a fart to escape/slip out') -- learners sometimes try to use पाद (paad) as a verb directly, but it needs one of these support verbs.

Sense 2 : foot; quarter of a verse (literary/prosody)
noun, masculine This sense survives mainly in compound/literary words (e.g. चतुष्पाद (chatushpaad, 'four-footed')) and in classical poetry, where a verse is divided into पाद (paad, 'quarters/feet').
Synonyms: पैर (pair, everyday word for 'foot', not used for the verse sense), चरण (charan, literary, closer overlap for the verse sense)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense less in daily conversation and more if you ever study Sanskritic compounds or classical Hindi/Sanskrit poetry, where a doha or shloka is described as having four पाद (paad).
