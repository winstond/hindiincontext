परोसना (parosnaa)

verb A single-sense verb: to serve food (onto a plate or to a person). Extends easily to a figurative sense of 'serving up' something (like news or information) in a packaged way.
Sense 1 : to serve food
verb, transitive Takes the food as direct object and the person served marked with को (ko): खाना परोसना (khaanaa parosnaa, 'to serve the food'), मुझे खाना परोसा (mujhe khaanaa parosaa, 'served me the food').
Synonyms: देना (denaa, plain 'give/serve'), बाँटना (baantnaa, to distribute/serve out to several people)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for the hostly act of dishing out food onto someone's plate -- at an Indian meal, especially at home or at a wedding, it's common and polite for the host or an elder to insist on परोसना (parosnaa) again and again, refilling your plate whether you asked or not. Saying 'बस, बस, मत परोसो' (bas, bas, mat paroso, 'enough, enough, don't serve more') is a very familiar dinner-table phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use देना (denaa) for every 'give' context, but परोसना (parosnaa) is specifically about plating/serving food at a meal, not handing over objects in general -- कोई चीज़ देना (koii chiiz denaa) is fine for objects, but food at the table calls for परोसना (parosnaa). 2) The person receiving the food takes को (ko), not से (se) or पर (par): मेहमानों को खाना परोसा (mehmaanon ko khaanaa parosaa, 'served the food to the guests'), not मेहमानों पर (mehmaanon par).

Sense 2 : to present/package information in a certain way (figurative)
verb, transitive Used mainly with abstract objects like खबर (khabar, 'news'), बातें (baaten, 'talk'), or झूठ (jhooth, 'lies'): झूठ को सच की तरह परोसना (jhooth ko sach kii tarah parosnaa, 'to serve up a lie as if it were truth').
Synonyms: पेश करना (pesh karnaa, to present)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use is common in media criticism -- people say news channels परोसते हैं (paroste hain) a particular version of events, as if handing you a plate already arranged the way they want you to see it. It carries a faint edge of 'spoon-feeding' or manipulation.
