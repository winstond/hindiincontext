अंग (ang)

noun Primarily a body part / limb (अंग (ang)); by extension it means a component or part of any whole, and shows up compounded in many common words.
Sense 1 : body part, limb
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अवयव (avyav, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in compounds rather than standing alone -- अंगदान (angdaan, 'organ donation') and अंगरक्षक (angrakshak, 'bodyguard', literally 'body-protector') are both built on it. In yoga and Ayurveda contexts अंग (ang) is the standard word for a limb or body part.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अंग (ang) where हाथ-पैर (haath-pair, 'hands and feet') or शरीर (shariir, 'body') would sound more natural in casual speech -- अंग (ang) alone tends to sound a bit clinical or literary when used for a specific limb in everyday conversation.
Sample Sentences:
उसके शरीर का एक अंग बुरी तरह घायल हो गया है। One of the limbs of his body has been badly injured.
(uske shariir kaa ek ang burii tarah ghaayal ho gayaa hai।)

Sense 2 : part, component, section (of a whole)
noun, masculine Used for a functional part of an organization, document, or system -- e.g. a branch/wing of a movement or a section of a text.
Synonyms: भाग (bhaag), हिस्सा (hissaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind अंग्रेज़ी (angrezii)-adjacent bureaucratic and institutional language: a 'wing' of a party or a 'section' of a law gets called its अंग (ang). You'll also hear सर्वांग (sarvaang, 'all limbs/parts') and अभिन्न अंग (abhinn ang, 'inseparable/integral part') -- the latter is a very common set phrase for saying something is an essential part of a bigger whole.
