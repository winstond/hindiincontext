अंधा (andhaa)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'blind', with a related noun sense meaning 'a blind person'; it also shows up figuratively for being 'blind' with rage, love, greed, etc.
Sense 1 : unable to see; blind
adjective
Synonyms: नेत्रहीन (netrahiin, formal), दृष्टिहीन (drishtihiin, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Like most basic Hindi adjectives, अंधा (andhaa) agrees in gender and number with the noun it describes -- अंधी लड़की (andhii ladkii, 'blind girl') versus अंधा लड़का (andhaa ladkaa, 'blind boy'). The word is blunt and direct, so many Hindi speakers now reach for softer alternatives in formal or polite speech, much like the shift from 'blind' to 'visually impaired' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Calling someone अंधा (andhaa) to their face or about them in casual conversation can come across as rude; कहीं देख नहीं सकता (kahiin dekh nahiin saktaa, 'cannot see at all') or दृष्टिबाधित (drishtibaadhit, 'visually impaired') are gentler in polite or formal contexts.

Sense 2 : a blind person
noun, masculine Functions as a noun when the adjective is used on its own to stand for the person, e.g. अंधा रास्ता पार कर रहा था (andhaa raastaa paar kar rahaa thaa, 'the blind man was crossing the road').
Synonyms: नेत्रहीन (netrahiin, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the adjective simply used without a noun to lean on -- very common in Hindi, where adjectives often double as nouns for the person they describe.

Sense 3 : figuratively blind (with rage, love, greed, power, etc.), i.e. lacking judgment
adjective Common in fixed phrases like प्यार में अंधा (pyaar men andhaa, 'blind with love') or पैसे के पीछे अंधा (paise ke piichhe andhaa, 'blind in the pursuit of money'), where अंधा (andhaa) follows a म (men) or के पीछे (ke piichhe) phrase naming the cause of the blindness.
Synonyms: बावला (baavlaa, 'frenzied', not an exact synonym but similar force)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense parallels English very closely -- 'blind with love,' 'blind with rage' -- so it is an easy, natural extension for learners once they know the literal meaning. You will hear it often in everyday moralizing, like अंधा प्यार (andhaa pyaar, 'blind love'), often used with a hint of warning or disapproval.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'blinded by rage' too literally; the natural Hindi frame is गुस्से में अंधा होना (gusse men andhaa honaa, 'to become blind with anger'), using में (men) rather than से (se) to mark the cause.
