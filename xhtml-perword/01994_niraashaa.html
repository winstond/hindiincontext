निराशा (niraashaa)

noun A single-sense abstract noun meaning 'disappointment' or 'despair' -- the feeling of hope having been dashed. Related to आशा (aashaa, 'hope'), with the negative prefix निर् (nir) attached.
Sense 1 : disappointment, despair, hopelessness
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मायूसी (maayoosii), हताशा (hataashaa, slightly more literary), विफलता (viphaltaa, failure, narrower scope)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly around exam results, cricket matches, and elections -- any moment where collective hope was riding high. Its opposite आशा (aashaa, hope) is just as common, and Hindi speakers like to play the two off each other, as in आशा और निराशा (aashaa aur niraashaa, 'hope and despair') describing an emotional rollercoaster.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उदास (udaas, sad) or दुख (dukh, sorrow) when they mean this specific flavor of disappointment tied to unmet expectations -- निराशा (niraashaa) is the right word when hope existed first and then failed, not just general sadness. 2) The common verb pairing is निराशा होना (niraashaa honaa, 'to feel disappointment') with a को-marked experiencer, e.g. मुझे निराशा हुई (mujhe niraashaa huii, 'I felt disappointed') -- learners sometimes try to make निराशा (niraashaa) itself the subject of a verb like लगना (lagnaa), which sounds off.

परीक्षा का परिणाम देखकर उसे बहुत निराशा हुई। Seeing the exam result, he felt great disappointment.
(pariikshaa kaa parinaam dekhkar use bahut niraashaa huii।)
