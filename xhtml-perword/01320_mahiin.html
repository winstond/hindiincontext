महीन (mahiin)

adjective Mainly describes something fine, thin, or delicate in texture -- as of fabric, powder, thread, or sound. A secondary figurative sense extends this to subtle, refined distinctions of meaning or perception.
Sense 1 : fine, thin (in texture)
adjective
Synonyms: पतला (patlaa, thin in shape rather than texture), बारीक (baariik)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around food and cloth in Delhi markets -- a shopkeeper selling suji or besan will describe the grind as महीन (mahiin) versus मोटा (motaa, coarse), and a सारी (saarii) seller will praise महीन बुनाई (mahiin bunaaii, fine weave) as a mark of quality.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पतला (patlaa) when describing powder, flour, or fabric weave -- पतला (patlaa) describes thinness of shape or diameter, while महीन (mahiin) describes fineness of grain or weave, so बारीक पीसा हुआ आटा (baariik piisaa huaa aataa, finely ground flour) or महीन आटा (mahiin aataa, fine flour) is natural, but पतला आटा (patlaa aataa) sounds odd.

Sense 2 : subtle, delicate (of distinction, sound, or perception)
adjective
Synonyms: सूक्ष्म (sookshm, more formal/literary), नाज़ुक (naazuk, leans toward fragile rather than subtle)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word stretched from touch to thought -- a महीन फ़र्क़ (mahiin farq, a fine/subtle difference) or महीन आवाज़ (mahiin aavaaz, a thin, delicate voice) uses the fabric-and-powder sense metaphorically, much as English speakers say 'fine distinction.' Recognizing this link helps you feel why one word covers both a sieve and a nuance.
