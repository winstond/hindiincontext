पैदल (paidal)

adverb/adjective One core meaning: on foot / walking, used mainly as an adverb but also as an adjective describing a walker or foot-based mode.
Sense 1 : on foot, by walking
adverb Functions as an adverb modifying verbs of motion, e.g. पैदल जाना (paidal jaanaa, 'to go on foot').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi-NCR conversation, पैदल (paidal) comes up constantly when people are deciding how to get somewhere short -- ऑटो ले लें या पैदल चलें? (oto le len yaa paidal chalen?, 'should we take an auto or just walk?') is a very ordinary line. It carries a slight practical undertone of saving money or the distance being manageable, not really a fitness connotation the way 'walking' can in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes add a postposition like से (se) after पैदल (paidal), saying पैदल से (paidal se) -- native speakers just say पैदल जाना (paidal jaanaa) or पैदल आना (paidal aanaa) without से (se). 2) Don't confuse with चलना (chalnaa) alone -- पैदल (paidal) specifically contrasts with going by vehicle, so it's often used precisely to make that contrast clear, as in पैदल जाओ या गाड़ी से? (paidal jaao yaa gaadii se?, 'will you go on foot or by car?').

Sense 2 : pedestrian, on-foot (describing a person or mode)
adjective Used before a noun as an invariant modifier, e.g. पैदल यात्री (paidal yaatrii, 'pedestrian traveler') or पैदल सेना (paidal senaa, 'infantry').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this sense on road signs and in formal contexts -- पैदल यात्रियों के लिए (paidal yaatriyon ke lie, 'for pedestrians') is common signage near crossings in Delhi. पैदल सेना (paidal senaa, 'infantry') is the standard military term, built on the same root idea of moving on foot rather than by horse or vehicle.
