परेशानी (pareshaanii)

noun A single core sense: 'trouble/worry/difficulty' -- covers both the emotional state (worry, distress) and the practical situation (a problem, hassle, inconvenience) that causes it. Related to the adjective परेशान (pareshaan, 'worried, troubled').
Sense 1 : trouble, worry, difficulty, hassle
noun, feminine Very commonly used with होना (honaa) in the dative-subject pattern -- मुझे परेशानी है (mujhe pareshaanii hai, 'I have a problem/I'm troubled') -- and with देना (denaa), उठाना (uthaanaa), or में डालना (men daalnaa) to mean causing or landing someone in trouble.
Synonyms: मुसीबत (musiibat), दिक्कत (dikkat), तकलीफ़ (takliif), चिंता (chintaa, leans toward mental worry rather than the situation itself), झंझट (jhanjhat, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear constantly in shops, offices, and family life in Delhi -- a rickshaw-wallah asking for extra fare might say थोड़ी परेशानी हो गई (thodii pareshaanii ho gaii, 'a bit of trouble came up'), understating the actual scale of the problem, which is a very Hindi conversational habit. It sits right next to परेशान (pareshaan) -- if परेशान describes how a person feels, परेशानी is the noun for the trouble itself, and the two are almost always learned as a pair.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चिंता (chintaa) when they mean a concrete problem or hassle rather than free-floating anxiety -- परेशानी (pareshaanii) covers both the feeling and the situation causing it, so it is the safer default word in most everyday complaints. 2) The set phrase कोई परेशानी नहीं (koii pareshaanii nahiin, 'no problem') is extremely common as a reassurance and shouldn't be replaced with a literal 'no problem' translation like कोई समस्या नहीं (koii samasyaa nahiin), which sounds slightly more formal/bureaucratic.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे इस काम में बहुत परेशानी हो रही है। I'm having a lot of trouble with this work.
(mujhe is kaam men bahut pareshaanii ho rahii hai।)
