घंटी (ghantii)

noun Mainly means a small bell or a buzzer/ringer -- the thing that makes a ringing sound, as on a door, bicycle, or phone. Related to the larger word घंटा (ghantaa, 'bell' or 'hour').
Sense 1 : bell (small, e.g. door, bicycle, temple)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a Delhi household: a guest arrives and presses the दरवाज़े की घंटी (darvaaze kii ghantii, 'the doorbell'), or a cyclist rings उसकी घंटी (uskii ghantii, 'his/her bicycle bell') to warn pedestrians. In schools, घंटी (ghantii) also informally means the school bell that marks period changes -- so a student might say अगली घंटी (aglii ghantii, 'the next bell/period') to mean the next class.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse घंटी (ghantii) with घंटा (ghantaa) -- घंटी is the small bell/buzzer sound-object, while घंटा is both the large temple bell and the word for 'hour'. 2) घंटी बजाना (ghantii bajaanaa, 'to ring the bell') is the standard collocation; बजाना (bajaanaa) is the verb used for making any instrument or bell sound, not मारना (maarnaa) or कोई और verb learners might guess.

Sense 2 : telephone ring / call (informal)
noun, feminine Used in the colloquial phrase घंटी मारना/करना (ghantii maarnaa / ghantii karnaa, 'to give a ring/missed call'), especially among friends to signal without actually talking.
Synonyms: मिस्ड कॉल (misd kol, missed call, related but not identical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Very common among Delhi youth: कॉलेज में पैसे नहीं थे तो सिर्फ़ घंटी मारते थे (kolej men paise nahiin the to sirf ghantii maarte the, 'we didn't have money in college so we'd just give a missed call') -- a whole generation communicated 'call me back' this way before unlimited call plans became common.
Common Mistakes: 1) घंटी मारना (ghantii maarnaa) sounds odd translated literally ('to hit a bell') but is completely normal slang for giving someone a quick ring or missed call to get their attention -- don't overthink the verb choice here.

Sense 3 : class period (school, informal)
noun, feminine By metonymy from the bell that signals period changes -- घंटी (ghantii) itself comes to mean the class period.
Synonyms: पीरियड (piiriyad, loanword, common in schools)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A student complaining about a long day might say आज छह घंटी हैं (aaj chhah ghantii hain, 'there are six periods today') -- here घंटी stands in for the class itself, not the sound.
