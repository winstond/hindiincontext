मूली (moolii)

noun A common root vegetable -- the white radish familiar from Indian kitchens, pickles, and the famous stuffed paratha (moolii).
Sense 1 : radish (the vegetable)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मूली (moolii) is best known to learners through मूली का पराठा (moolii kaa paraathaa, 'radish-stuffed flatbread'), a North Indian winter breakfast staple, and through मूली के पत्ते (moolii ke patte, 'radish greens'), which are cooked as a saag. Winters in Delhi-NCR markets bring stacks of long white मूली (moolii) alongside carrots and turnips.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat मूली (moolii) as masculine by default -- it is consistently feminine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. मूली अच्छी है (moolii achchhii hai, 'the radish is good'), not अच्छा (achchhaa).

Sense 2 : used idiomatically for something worthless or given away too easily
noun, feminine Appears in comparative idioms rather than as a standalone label for an object -- always in a phrase comparing something to मूली (moolii) to stress how cheap, plentiful, or easily obtained it is.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Because मूली (moolii) is cheap and sold in heavy bunches in Indian vegetable markets, it became shorthand in idiom for things that are plentiful and low-value -- similar to how English might say something is 'a dime a dozen.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only surfaces inside a fixed comparison (like मूली की तरह बिकना (moolii kii tarah biknaa, 'to sell like radishes' -- i.e. dirt cheap or in huge quantity)); learners shouldn't use मूली (moolii) alone to mean 'worthless thing' outside such a comparison.
