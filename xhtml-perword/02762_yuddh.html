युद्ध (yuddh)

noun A formal, Sanskritic word for 'war/battle' -- one core sense, but it appears constantly in news, history, and set phrases about conflict.
Sense 1 : war, battle, armed conflict
noun, masculine
Synonyms: लड़ाई (ladaaii), जंग (jang), संग्राम (sangraam, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet युद्ध (yuddh) constantly in the news (महायुद्ध (mahaayuddh, world war), युद्धविराम (yuddhviraam, ceasefire)) and in references to the Mahabharata's great battle, which is itself often just called युद्ध (yuddh) in context. Its register sits a notch above लड़ाई (ladaaii), the word you would actually use for a scuffle in the street or a fight between siblings.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for लड़ाई (ladaaii) in casual speech and युद्ध (yuddh) in writing/news, but treat them as interchangeable; native speakers keep युद्ध (yuddh) for formal, large-scale, or historical conflict and use लड़ाई (ladaaii) for everyday fights (including a household quarrel), so calling a couple's spat a युद्ध (yuddh) sounds comically grand. 2) The verb pairing is युद्ध करना (yuddh karnaa, to wage war) or युद्ध लड़ना (yuddh ladnaa, to fight a war) -- not युद्ध होना (yuddh honaa) alone when you mean 'to fight a war' actively, though युद्ध होना (yuddh honaa) works fine for 'a war occurred/broke out.'

दोनों देशों के बीच युद्ध छिड़ गया। War broke out between the two countries.
(donon deshon ke biich yuddh chhid gayaa।)
