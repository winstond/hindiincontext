हथियार (hathiyaar)

noun Mainly a concrete noun for a weapon, with a very common figurative extension to any 'tool' or 'leverage' used against someone in an argument or contest.
Sense 1 : weapon, arm
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शस्त्र (shastr, formal/literary), अस्त्र (astr, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news reports about crime and conflict -- हथियार डालना (hathiyaar daalnaa, 'to lay down weapons') is the standard phrase for surrendering, used both literally (an army) and figuratively (giving up in an argument or fight).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for शस्त्र (shastr) in everyday speech because it looks like the 'proper' word; हथियार (hathiyaar) is what people actually say for guns, knives, swords, etc. in normal conversation and on the news.

(pulis ne apraadhii se ek hathiyaar baraamad kiyaa।)

Sense 2 : figurative weapon, leverage, tool used against someone
noun, masculine Common in the phrase X को हथियार बनाना (X ko hathiyaar banaanaa, 'to make X into a weapon'), for turning some fact, statement, or issue into a means of attack.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you hear constantly in Indian political and office talk -- someone's own words, an old promise, or a rule gets described as a हथियार (hathiyaar) that the other side picks up and uses against them; it maps closely to English 'ammunition' in an argument.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often miss this figurative sense entirely and only recognize हथियार (hathiyaar) as a physical weapon, so they get confused when a politician is said to 'wield' a scandal or a rule as a हथियार (hathiyaar).
