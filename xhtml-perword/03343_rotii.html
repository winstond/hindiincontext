रोटी (rotii)

noun The everyday flatbread that anchors most North Indian meals, plus its extended sense of 'food/livelihood' in common idioms.
Sense 1 : flatbread (roti/chapati)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चपाती (chapaatii), फुल्का (phulkaa, thin soft roti)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian homes, रोटी (rotii) is made fresh at every meal, not stored -- asking 'क्या खाना बना है?' (kyaa khaanaa banaa hai, 'what's for the meal?') will very often get the answer 'रोटी-सब्ज़ी' (rotii-sabzii, 'bread and vegetables'), the default home dinner. The word so thoroughly stands for 'a proper meal' that a mother calling her kids in from playing will shout रोटी खा लो (rotii khaa lo, 'come eat!') regardless of what's actually on the table.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate 'bread' as रोटी (rotii) in all contexts, but sliced/loaf bread is ब्रेड (bred) or डबल रोटी (dabal rotii) -- रोटी (rotii) specifically means the round flatbread. 2) रोटी (rotii) is used as a generic word for 'a meal' in phrases like रोटी खाना (rotii khaanaa, 'to eat a meal'), even when the meal has no actual roti in it.
Sample Sentences:

(maan roz subah garam rotii banaatii hai।)

Sense 2 : livelihood, one's daily bread/earnings
noun, feminine Almost always appears in the fixed pair रोटी-रोज़ी (rotii-rozii) or रोज़ी-रोटी (rozii-rotii), meaning 'livelihood/bread and butter.'
Synonyms: रोज़ी (rozii, as in रोज़ी-रोटी)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like English 'bread and butter,' रोटी (rotii) stretches from the physical food to what puts food on the table at all -- अपनी रोटी चलाना (apnii rotii chalaanaa, 'to keep one's livelihood running') is a very common way to talk about making ends meet.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss that रोटी कमाना (rotii kamaanaa, 'to earn one's bread') is about earning a living in general, not literally earning bread -- it works even for someone in a completely unrelated profession.
