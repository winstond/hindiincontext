ढाबा (dhaabaa)

noun A roadside or highway food stall/eatery, usually simple and inexpensive; one primary sense, but rich in cultural connotation.
Sense 1 : roadside eatery / highway food stall
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रेस्तरां (restraan, more formal/urban sit-down restaurant), खाने की दुकान (khaane kii dukaan, descriptive, not a fixed term)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian highway trip runs on ढाबे (dhaabe) -- truckers, families on road trips, and college students on a budget all stop at these open-air eateries for tandoori rotis, dal makhani, and strong sweet chai served in small glasses. Punjabi-style dhabas along the Delhi-Chandigarh highway are especially famous, and the culture around them (loud dhaba names, charpai seating, tadka simmering in giant pots) is as much a part of the experience as the food itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use ढाबा (dhaabaa) interchangeably with रेस्तरां (restraan), but a ढाबा (dhaabaa) specifically implies a no-frills, often outdoor or truck-stop style setup with simple bench seating and a limited, hearty menu -- calling a proper city restaurant a ढाबा (dhaabaa) sounds off unless you are deliberately going for a rustic vibe. 2) The phrase ढाबे पर (dhaabe par, 'at the dhaba') is the natural way to say 'at the dhaba' -- learners sometimes default to में (men) out of habit, but पर (par) is what you will hear used with ढाबा (dhaabaa) in practice.
