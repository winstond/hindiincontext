पेंच (pench)

noun Literally a 'screw,' this word branches into a couple of vivid figurative senses -- a 'twist' or complication in a situation, and a wrestling/rhetorical 'move' or trick used against an opponent. Inline Hindi as Devanagari (pench).
Sense 1 : screw (the hardware fastener)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a loanword (from Persian, via long use in Hindi-Urdu) and shows up constantly in everyday repair talk -- a loose पेंच (pench) is the classic complaint about wobbly furniture, ceiling fans, or door hinges. Ask any local carpenter or electrician and you will hear this word within the first minute.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पेंच (pench) with पेच (pech) as if they were unrelated -- they are simply variant spellings of the same word, though पेंच (pench) with the chandrabindu is the more common form today.

Sense 2 : a twist, complication, or hidden catch in a situation
noun, masculine Commonly used in the phrase पेंच फँसना (pench phansnaa, 'for a snag/complication to arise') or पेंच डालना (pench daalnaa, 'to throw in a complication').
Synonyms: उलझन (uljhan), पेचीदगी (pechiidgii, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a screw that will not go in straight -- that image of something not sitting right is exactly the feel of this sense. It is a favorite word in negotiations, contracts, and gossip: कहीं कोई पेंच तो नहीं? (kahiin koii pench to nahiin, 'surely there's no catch somewhere?') is the kind of wary question a shrewd shopkeeper or relative might ask before agreeing to a deal.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is about a situation getting tangled or tricky, not about a physical screw -- learners fresh off the hardware meaning sometimes miss the metaphorical leap. 2) इसमें कोई पेंच है (ismen koii pench hai, 'there's some catch in this') is a very natural way to voice suspicion; a flat 'there is a problem' translation loses the flavor of hidden trickiness.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : a wrestling hold or clever tactical move (against a rival)
noun, masculine Often appears as पेंच लड़ाना (pench ladaanaa, 'to pit moves against each other, to spar tactically').
Synonyms: दाँव (daanv), चाल (chaal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This comes straight out of the akhaara (akhaadaa, wrestling ring) tradition, where a पेंच (pench) is a specific grappling hold. From there it jumped into everyday language for any cunning tactical maneuver, political or otherwise -- you'll hear it used about scheming politicians or rival business owners just as easily as wrestlers.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense leans competitive/strategic -- it's the move itself, not the general cleverness of a person, so it pairs naturally with verbs like लड़ाना (ladaanaa) or चलना (chalnaa) rather than standing alone as a character trait.
