पत्थर (patthar)

noun The everyday word for a physical stone or rock, with a well-worn figurative extension to something emotionally hard or unfeeling.
Sense 1 : stone, rock
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शिला (shilaa, literary), चट्टान (chattaan, boulder/rock face)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear at a construction site, in a garden, or when someone trips on the road and grumbles about a पत्थर (patthar) in their path. It's completely concrete and everyday, the base meaning from which the emotional sense (below) grows.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पत्थर (patthar) with चट्टान (chattaan) -- पत्थर (patthar) is a general or loose stone, while चट्टान (chattaan) is a large rock face or boulder, like something you'd see on a cliff.

Sense 2 : emotionally hard, unfeeling (of a person or heart)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or in set phrases like दिल पत्थर का हो जाना (dil patthar kaa ho jaanaa, 'the heart turns to stone') and पत्थर दिल (patthar dil, 'stone-hearted') rather than as a plain noun on its own.
Synonyms: संगदिल (sangdil, literary/poetic), निर्दयी (nirdyii, cruel, broader sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi loves this body-as-container image: the heart itself doesn't feel, so it 'becomes stone' -- दिल पत्थर का हो गया (dil patthar kaa ho gayaa, 'the heart became stone') -- rather than the person simply 'being' unfeeling. You'll hear this a lot in film dialogue and songs about heartbreak.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense almost always appears bundled with दिल (dil, heart) -- learners sometimes try to use पत्थर (patthar) alone to mean 'cruel person,' which sounds incomplete; you need the heart-frame to make the metaphor land.
