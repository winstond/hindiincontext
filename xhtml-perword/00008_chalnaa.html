चलना (chalnaa)

verb चलना (chalnaa) is Hindi's basic verb of motion, stretching from literal walking to a whole family of extended senses: machines and businesses 'running,' people 'setting off,' events 'being in progress,' things 'being acceptable,' and trends or currency 'staying in circulation.' Its causative partner is चलाना (chalaanaa, 'to drive/operate/run something'), processed as a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to walk, to move
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: टहलना (tahalnaa, to stroll), घूमना (ghoomnaa, to wander/move about)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you learn first: टांगों से चलना (taangon se chalnaa, 'to walk on one's legs') for babies learning to walk, or बूढ़ी दादी धीरे-धीरे चलती हैं (boodhii daadii dhiire-dhiire chaltii hain, 'the old grandmother walks slowly') for describing gait and pace.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चलना (chalnaa) when जाना (jaanaa) is meant -- चलना (chalnaa) describes the act or manner of moving (walking, being in motion), while जाना (jaanaa) is about reaching a destination. 2) पैदल चलना (paidal chalnaa, 'to go on foot') is the natural phrase for 'to walk' rather than a literal word-for-word translation.

Sense 2 : to function, to run or operate (machines, vehicles, businesses)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: काम करना (kaam karnaa, to work/function)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask about a shop and a Delhi shopkeeper might say दुकान अच्छी चल रही है (dukaan achchhii chal rahii hai, 'business is running well') -- the same word that describes a person's legs now describes an engine, a business, even the internet connection.

Sense 3 : to leave, to set off, to get going
verb, intransitive The imperative चलो (chalo) is used almost as a discourse marker meaning 'come on' or 'alright, let's go,' independent of literal walking.
Synonyms: निकलना (nikalnaa, to head out)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चलो, फिर मिलते हैं (chalo, phir milte hain, 'alright, see you later') is a classic way to wrap up a visit or phone call in Delhi-NCR -- the softness of चलो (chalo) makes goodbyes feel casual rather than abrupt.
Common Mistakes: 1) चलो (chalo) doesn't always mean 'walk!' -- as a stand-alone utterance it often just means 'come on' or signals the end of a conversation, closer to English 'well, I should get going.' 2) मैं चलता हूँ (main chaltaa hoon, 'I'll get going') is a common, slightly formal way to take leave, not a statement about literally walking somewhere.

Sense 4 : to be going on, to be in progress
verb, intransitive Almost always appears in the progressive: चल रहा है (chal rahaa hai, 'is going on').
Synonyms: होना (honaa, to happen), जारी रहना (jaarii rahnaa, to continue)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi likes to frame ongoing life and events as motion: ज़िंदगी चल रही है (zindgii chal rahii hai, 'life is going on') treats time itself as something that moves forward, a subtly different picture from the more static English 'is happening.'
Common Mistakes: 1) क्या चल रहा है? (kyaa chal rahaa hai?, 'what's going on?') is a very common casual greeting/check-in, not a literal question about movement.

Sense 5 : to be acceptable, to work out, to pass muster
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: ठीक होना (thiik honaa, to be fine)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly while bargaining in a Delhi market: सौ रुपये चलेगा? (sau rupye chalegaa?, 'will a hundred rupees do?') -- चलना (chalnaa) here carries a sense of mutual give-and-take, not a fixed yes/no.
Common Mistakes: 1) यह चलेगा (yah chalegaa, 'this will do') is a very handy, situational phrase for accepting something as good enough -- learners often reach for ठीक है (thiik hai) instead, which is fine but misses this more negotiable, 'it'll pass' flavor.

Sense 6 : to circulate, to be current or in vogue
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: प्रचलित होना (prachlit honaa, to be prevalent)
Frequency: medium
