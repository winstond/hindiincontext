मुमकिन (mumkin)

adjective/noun A single-sense adjective meaning 'possible' -- an everyday Urdu-origin word used constantly alongside संभव (sambhav).
Sense 1 : possible, feasible
adjective Very commonly paired with है/था/होगा in impersonal constructions: यह मुमकिन है (yah mumkin hai, 'this is possible') -- there is usually no grammatical subject doing the 'being possible,' the situation itself is possible.
Synonyms: संभव (sambhav), मुमकिन-सा (mumkin-saa, colloquial hedge, 'sort of possible')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear जितना मुमकिन हो (jitnaa mumkin ho, 'as much as possible') all the time in everyday requests and instructions, like जितना मुमकिन हो जल्दी आना (jitnaa mumkin ho jaldii aanaa, 'come as soon as possible'). It sits comfortably in both casual chat and slightly formal speech, unlike its Sanskrit cousin संभव (sambhav), which leans a touch more formal/written.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make मुमकिन (mumkin) agree in gender/number like a variable adjective (मुमकिना, मुमकिने) -- it never changes form, regardless of what noun it describes. 2) मुमकिन है कि (mumkin hai ki, 'it's possible that...') is often followed by a subjunctive verb, which learners forget to switch to: मुमकिन है वह आए (mumkin hai vah aae, 'it's possible he might come'), not the plain present.

Sense 2 : the possible, that which is possible (abstract)
noun, masculine Used as an invariant masculine noun mainly in the fixed phrase हद तक मुमकिन (had tak mumkin, 'to the extent possible') or जो मुमकिन हो (jo mumkin ho, 'whatever is possible'), where it functions like a nominalized adjective rather than a true independent noun.
Synonyms: संभव (sambhav)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This use is fairly rare on its own -- most of the time मुमकिन (mumkin) is doing adjective work, and this nominal flavor only shows up folded into set phrases rather than standing alone as a countable noun.
