कृपा (kripaa)

noun A formal/devotional noun meaning 'grace' or 'kindness' -- most commonly met in the polite request phrase कृपया (kripyaa) and in devotional or literary contexts referring to divine grace.
Sense 1 : kindness, favor shown by a person
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दया (dayaa), मेहरबानी (meharbaanii), अनुग्रह (anugrah, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in formal writing and announcements as कृपया (kripyaa) -- 'kindly do such-and-such' -- on signs, forms, and train announcements, even though the plain noun कृपा (kripaa) itself is rarer in casual conversation than everyday words like मेहरबानी (meharbaanii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners overuse कृपा (kripaa) where मेहरबानी (meharbaanii) or simple politeness markers like प्लीज़ (pliiz) would sound more natural in casual speech -- कृपा (kripaa) itself carries a formal, almost old-fashioned weight, closer to 'grace' than 'please be nice.' 2) The common polite-request word कृपया (kripyaa) looks like an inflected form of this noun but is actually a frozen adverb ('kindly') -- it does not decline or combine with postpositions the way कृपा (kripaa) does.
Sample Sentences:
आपकी कृपा के लिए धन्यवाद। Thank you for your kindness.
(aapkii kripaa ke lie dhanyvaad।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Voice: Active
कृपया अपना फॉर्म यहाँ जमा करें। Kindly submit your form here.

Sense 2 : divine grace, blessing
noun, feminine Frequently used in the fixed phrase भगवान की कृपा से (bhagvaan kii kripaa se, 'by God's grace') to attribute good fortune to divine will rather than one's own effort.
Synonyms: आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad), अनुग्रह (anugrah, literary), प्रसाद (prasaad, meaning scope: also means blessed food)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: This sense sits at the heart of how many Hindi speakers talk about good fortune -- rather than saying 'I got lucky,' the natural framing is often गुरु/भगवान की कृपा से हुआ (guru/bhagvaan kii kripaa se huaa, 'it happened by the guru's/God's grace'), reflecting a cultural instinct to credit a higher power or a respected elder for one's success.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for भाग्य (bhaagy, 'luck/fate') where a Hindi speaker would instead say भगवान की कृपा (bhagvaan kii kripaa), which frames the outcome as a gift from God rather than impersonal chance.
Sample Sentences:
यह सब गुरु की कृपा से हुआ है। All this has happened by the guru's grace.
(yah sab guru kii kripaa se huaa hai।)
Register: Devotional -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
