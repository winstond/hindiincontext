ज़ख़म (zakham)

noun A noun meaning 'wound' or 'injury', used both for physical injuries and, very commonly, in figurative/emotional contexts like heartbreak. Borrowed from Persian and extremely common in everyday speech, poetry, and film dialogue.
Sense 1 : physical wound, injury
noun, masculine
Synonyms: घाव (ghaav), चोट (chot)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Doctors and everyday speakers both use ज़ख़म (zakham) for anything from a kitchen knife cut to a battlefield injury; it sits between the clinical घाव (ghaav) and the very casual चोट (chot) in feel. The nuqta under the ज is standard in careful pronunciation and writing, reflecting its Persian origin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चोट (chot) in all contexts, but ज़ख़म (zakham) suggests a deeper or more serious wound (a cut, gash, or injury that breaks the skin) rather than a minor bump or bruise, for which चोट (chot) is more natural. 2) The verb often paired with this noun is लगना (lagnaa) -- ज़ख़म लगना (zakham lagnaa, 'to sustain a wound') -- rather than a direct transitive verb.

Sense 2 : emotional hurt, heartbreak
noun, masculine
Synonyms: घाव (ghaav), चोट (chot, often used for emotional hurt too), दर्द (dard, broader term for pain/hurt)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense that powers countless Hindi film songs and ghazals -- दिल के ज़ख़म (dil ke zakham, 'wounds of the heart') is a stock phrase for lost love or betrayal. Hindi, like Urdu poetry it draws from, treats emotional pain as a physical injury on the heart, so verbs like भरना (bharnaa, 'to heal/fill') and हरा होना (haraa honaa, 'to become fresh/raw again') are applied to this figurative wound just as to a real one.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers may underuse this word for emotional pain, defaulting to दर्द (dard); but ज़ख़म (zakham) is the standard poetic and everyday metaphor for heartbreak, e.g. दिल पर ज़ख़म (dil par zakham, 'a wound on the heart').
