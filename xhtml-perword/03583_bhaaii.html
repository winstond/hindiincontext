भाई (bhaaii)

noun/interjection Primarily means 'brother,' both literal and as a warm term for male friends or acquaintances; also works as a casual address term and interjection of exasperation or emphasis.
Sense 1 : sibling relation
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भ्राता (bhraataa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Hindi-speaking families, cousins are routinely called भाई (bhaaii) too -- the English distinction between 'brother' and 'cousin' is much less sharply drawn in everyday speech, and people often need to add सगा भाई (sagaa bhaaii) if they want to specify a real, blood brother.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget भाई (bhaaii) itself never inflects for number/gender in the singular/plural distinction the way some nouns do -- 'brothers' needs the full plural marking covered in the morphology table, not a separate word.

(meraa bhaaii dillii men rahtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : friendly term of address for a man
noun, masculine Used vocatively to address or refer to a man one is not related to, signaling warmth or casual familiarity.
Synonyms: यार (yaar, colloquial), दोस्त (dost)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Call out भाई साहब (bhaaii saahab) to a shopkeeper or rickshaw driver in Delhi and you'll get instant, easy attention -- it's respectful without being stiff, striking a register between the too-familiar यार (yaar) and the more distant जी (jii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often don't realize भाई (bhaaii) is used to address total strangers too -- an auto-rickshaw driver, a shopkeeper, a man on the street -- not just people you know; it is a default polite-but-casual way to get a man's attention.
Sample Sentences:
भाई, ज़रा सुनिए, ये रिक्शा कहाँ जाएगा? Excuse me, buddy, where does this rickshaw go?
(bhaaii, zaraa sunie, ye rikshaa kahaan jaaegaa?)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Future -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : exclamation of exasperation, emphasis, or plea
interjection Stands alone at the start or end of a sentence to add emotional color -- frustration, pleading, or strong emphasis -- rather than referring to any actual brother.
Synonyms: अरे (are)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi traffic or family arguments -- भाई, क्या कर रहे हो? (bhaaii, kyaa kar rahe ho?, 'man, what are you doing?') -- said with real irritation, no brother in sight.
Common Mistakes: 1) This use has nothing to do with siblinghood, so learners shouldn't try to map it to 'brother' literally in translation -- it functions closer to English 'man' or 'dude' used as a filler of emotion, e.g. भाई, ये मत करो (bhaaii, ye mat karo, 'come on, don't do this').
