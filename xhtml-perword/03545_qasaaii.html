क़साई (qasaaii)

noun A single-sense word meaning 'butcher' -- the person who slaughters animals and sells meat, with a strong extended/figurative use for a cruel or merciless person.
Sense 1 : butcher (person who slaughters animals and sells meat)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Indian towns and neighborhoods, the क़साई (qasaaii) is a familiar local figure -- often Muslim by community tradition -- whose shop is a recognizable fixture near a बाज़ार (baazaar, market) or मोहल्ला (mohallaa, neighborhood). Because the trade involves killing animals, the word has picked up strong metaphorical weight for cruelty, discussed in the next sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write कसाई (kasaaii) -- both spellings are heard, but क़साई (qasaaii) with the nuqta reflects the word's Persian/Arabic-derived pronunciation more precisely. 2) This word names an occupation, not a generic 'meat seller' -- a shop that only sells cut/packaged meat without slaughtering is still often called by this word colloquially, but the core image is of someone who does the cutting.

Sense 2 : a cruel, merciless person (figurative)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as an insult, e.g. calling a harsh doctor, ruthless boss, or violent person 'क़साई' (qasaaii) -- the English idiom 'butcher' works almost identically here.
Synonyms: जल्लाद (jallaad, executioner, similar figurative force), हत्यारा (hatyaaraa, murderer)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as in English a bad surgeon might be called 'a butcher,' Hindi speakers reach for क़साई (qasaaii) to condemn someone's brutality or incompetence with life-and-death stakes -- you'll hear it hurled at doctors, dictators, or anyone seen as inflicting suffering without conscience.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is used only for humans acting with cruelty, not for describing violent events or objects -- you would not call a weapon or a war क़साई (qasaaii), you'd call the *person* responsible one.
