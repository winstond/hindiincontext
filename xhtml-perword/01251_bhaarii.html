भारी (bhaarii)

adjective Primarily 'heavy' in weight, but extends naturally to intensity/severity (heavy rain, heavy loss, heavy voice) and to 'important/influential' in a figurative sense.
Sense 1 : heavy (in weight)
adjective
Synonyms: वज़नी (vaznii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a mother testing a sabzi bag at the market and saying यह बहुत भारी है (yah bahut bhaarii hai, 'this is very heavy') -- भारी (bhaarii) is the everyday word your hands reach for before you'd ever think of वज़नी (vaznii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for वज़नी (vaznii) as the default 'heavy' word, but भारी (bhaarii) is the far more common everyday choice for physical weight -- वज़नी (vaznii) leans slightly more descriptive/technical (e.g. discussing exact weight).

Sense 2 : intense, severe, heavy (of rain, loss, voice, traffic, etc.)
adjective Commonly pairs with बारिश (baarish), नुकसान (nuksaan), आवाज़ (aavaaz), and ट्रैफ़िक (traifik) to mean 'heavy/severe' rather than literal weight.
Synonyms: तेज़ (tez, for rain/intensity), गंभीर (gambhiir, for loss/damage)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi monsoon news reports love the phrase भारी बारिश की चेतावनी (bhaarii baarish kii chetaavnii, 'warning of heavy rain') -- this extended, non-literal sense of भारी (bhaarii) is exactly how you'll meet it on the news or in a weather app.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'heavy rain' word-for-word with भारी बारिश (bhaarii baarish) which is fine, but often miss that तेज़ बारिश (tez baarish) is at least as common in speech -- both are correct, not one right and one wrong.

Sense 3 : important, influential, weighty (of a person, voice, or presence)
adjective Used of people or institutions to mean 'a heavyweight, someone with clout' -- e.g. भारी नेता (bhaarii netaa, 'a heavyweight/influential leader').
Synonyms: प्रभावशाली (prabhaavshaalii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English says 'a heavyweight politician,' Hindi reaches for the same body-weight metaphor: भारी (bhaarii) applied to a person signals clout and influence, not literal size.

Sense 4 : difficult, burdensome (used with पड़ना or on/for someone)
adjective Frequently appears in the frame X पर भारी पड़ना (X par bhaarii padnaa, 'to prove too much for X / to get the upper hand over X') and in दिल पर भारी (dil par bhaarii, 'weighing on the heart').
Synonyms: मुश्किल (mushkil)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear commentators say टीम इंडिया पाकिस्तान पर भारी पड़ी (tiim indiyaa paakistaan par bhaarii padii, 'Team India got the better of Pakistan') -- this idiomatic 'outweighing' sense is alive and well in cricket commentary.
Common Mistakes: 1) The idiom पर भारी पड़ना (par bhaarii padnaa) means 'to outdo/overpower' someone, not literally 'to be heavy on' them -- learners sometimes read it too literally in sports or competition contexts.
