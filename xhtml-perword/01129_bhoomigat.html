भूमिगत (bhoomigat)

adjective A compound of भूमि (bhoomi, 'earth/ground') and गत (gat, 'gone into'), giving the literal sense 'gone into the ground.' Used both literally (underground, as in structures) and figuratively (living in hiding, in political contexts).
Sense 1 : underground (physically beneath the surface)
adjective
Synonyms: जमीन के नीचे (jamiin ke niiche, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word most often in news and civic contexts: भूमिगत जल (bhoomigat jal, 'groundwater'), भूमिगत बिजली की तार (bhoomigat bijlii kii taar, 'underground electric wires'), or भूमिगत मार्ग (bhoomigat maarg, 'underpass/subway'). It's a common word in Delhi civic-planning talk given how often roads and metro lines come up in conversation about the city.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use this as a noun meaning 'the underground' (a place); it functions as an adjective modifying a noun, e.g. भूमिगत जल (bhoomigat jal, 'groundwater'), not standing alone as a place-noun.

Sense 2 : living or operating in hiding, especially for political reasons
adjective
Synonyms: छिपा हुआ (chhipaa huaa), फरार (pharaar, fleeing/absconding sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word used for activists, revolutionaries, or criminals who vanish from public life to evade arrest -- भूमिगत हो जाना (bhoomigat ho jaanaa, 'to go underground'). It comes up often in discussions of the freedom struggle and Emergency-era politics, where many leaders went भूमिगत (bhoomigat) to avoid detention.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is about a person's status (hiding from authorities), not their physical location underground -- learners sometimes read भूमिगत होना (bhoomigat honaa, 'to go underground') too literally.
