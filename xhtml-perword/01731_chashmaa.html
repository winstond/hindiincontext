चश्मा (chashmaa)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'spectacles/glasses'; it also has a secondary sense referring to a natural spring of water. Both senses come from the same Persian-origin word, whose core image is an 'eye' or 'aperture' through which something is seen or flows.
Sense 1 : eyeglasses, spectacles
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ऐनक (ainak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear चश्मा लगाना (chashmaa lagaanaa, 'to put on glasses') constantly in Delhi households, especially from parents nagging kids about eye strain from too much screen time. Sunglasses get their own compound, धूप का चश्मा (dhoop kaa chashmaa, 'sun glasses' -- literally 'glasses of sunlight'), rather than a separate root word.
Common Mistakes: 1) चश्मा (chashmaa) is grammatically singular in Hindi even though English 'glasses' is plural -- learners often mistakenly pluralize the verb or adjective agreement around it. 2) The verb used for 'wearing' glasses is लगाना (lagaanaa) or पहनना (pahannaa), not डालना (daalnaa), which would sound odd here.

Sense 2 : natural spring (of water)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्रोत (srot, more general term for 'source')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up mostly in descriptions of hill stations and pilgrimage spots -- for instance a गरम पानी का चश्मा (garam paanii kaa chashmaa, 'hot water spring') near a Himalayan town. The link to the 'glasses' sense is poetic: both are an 'eye' -- one through which you see, one through which water emerges from the earth.
