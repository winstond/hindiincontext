पुरज़ा (purzaa)

noun A mechanical/technical noun meaning a part, piece, or component -- most at home talking about machines, engines, and gadgets; also spelled पुर्ज़ा (purzaa), which is actually the more common spelling in print.
Sense 1 : a part or component of a machine
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भाग (bhaag), अंश (ansh, more formal/literary), हिस्सा (hissaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear mechanics at a local garage (gairaaj) say things like गाड़ी का पुर्ज़ा (gaadii kaa purzaa, 'a part of the car') खराब हो गया है when something has broken down -- it is everyday workshop vocabulary, not technical jargon reserved for engineers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this for a generic 'piece' of anything (like a piece of cake or paper) -- it is specifically for mechanical/engineered parts, not food, paper, or abstract pieces. 2) The more standard spelling in dictionaries and print is पुर्ज़ा (purzaa) with a half-र; पुरज़ा (purzaa) is a commonly heard/written variant.
Sample Sentences:
गाड़ी का एक पुर्ज़ा खराब हो गया है। One part of the car has broken down.
(gaadii kaa ek purzaa kharaab ho gayaa hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative: a small, replaceable cog or unit within a larger system or organization
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मोहरा (mohraa, 'pawn', figurative for someone used or expendable), हिस्सा (hissaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just like English 'a cog in the machine,' Hindi speakers extend पुर्ज़ा (purzaa) to talk about a person or small part within a big system -- e.g. a low-level worker calling himself सिर्फ़ एक पुर्ज़ा (sirf ek purzaa, 'just a cog') in a large office or bureaucracy.
Sample Sentences:
मैं तो इस दफ़्तर में सिर्फ़ एक पुर्ज़ा हूँ। I'm just a cog in this office, that's all.
(main to is daftar men sirf ek purzaa hoon।)

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
