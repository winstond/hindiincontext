होशियार (hoshiyaar)

adjective/interjection Primarily an adjective meaning 'clever, smart, skillful' or 'alert, careful'; also used as a standalone interjection meaning 'watch out!'.
Sense 1 : clever, intelligent, skillful
adjective
Synonyms: समझदार (samajhdaar), चालाक (chaalaak, can imply cunning), तेज़ (tez, quick-witted), बुद्धिमान (buddhimaan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian parent, teacher, or relative uses to praise a sharp kid -- 'बहुत होशियार है!' (bahut hoshiyaar hai, 'very clever/sharp!') is practically a national compliment for children who do well in school. It carries warmth and approval, unlike चालाक (chaalaak), which can carry a whiff of suspicion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चालाक (chaalaak) as a direct synonym, but चालाक (chaalaak) often leans toward 'cunning, sly' -- होशियार (hoshiyaar) is the safer, more positive word for praising someone's intelligence or skill. 2) होशियार (hoshiyaar) is commonly used for being good at something practical (studies, work, a game), not just abstract intelligence -- होशियार छात्र (hoshiyaar chhaatr, 'a smart/sharp student') is a very natural combination.

Sense 2 : alert, careful, on guard
adjective Often paired with रहना (rahnaa) in the frame होशियार रहना (hoshiyaar rahnaa, 'to stay alert/careful').
Synonyms: सावधान (saavdhaan), चौकस (chaukas, formal/watchful)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this shouted by a street vendor or rickshaw driver as a quick heads-up -- 'होशियार!' (hoshiyaar) -- functioning almost like 'watch out!' in the moment, which connects directly to its use as an interjection below.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with सावधान (saavdhaan) but होशियार (hoshiyaar) feels slightly more colloquial and is often used as a sharp, direct warning in the moment, while सावधान (saavdhaan) can sound a touch more formal or instructional (like on signs).

Sense 3 : watch out! be careful! (as a warning shout)
interjection
Synonyms: सावधान (saavdhaan, slightly more formal/instructional), ख़बरदार (khabardaar, sharper, can sound like a threat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic shout you hear from someone carrying a heavy load through a crowded market or a cyclist weaving through traffic -- a quick, functional 'watch it!' that keeps the flow of chaotic Indian streets moving safely.
Common Mistakes: 1) ख़बरदार (khabardaar) is a stronger, more threatening version -- होशियार (hoshiyaar) as a shouted warning is more neutral, like alerting someone to a physical danger (a step, a vehicle) rather than issuing a threat.
