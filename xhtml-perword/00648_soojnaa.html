सूजना (soojnaa)

verb A single-sense intransitive verb meaning 'to swell up' -- used for body parts like a foot, cheek, or eye reacting to injury, insect bite, or illness.
Sense 1 : to swell, become swollen
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: फूलना (phoolnaa, can also mean 'to swell/puff up', less specific to injury)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around minor domestic injuries -- a mother pressing ice on a child's सूजा हुआ घुटना (soojaa huaa ghutnaa, swollen knee) after a fall, or someone saying 'रात भर रोने से आँखें सूज गई (raat bhar rone se aankhen sooj gaii, the eyes got swollen from crying all night)'. It almost always pairs with जाना (jaanaa) as सूज जाना (sooj jaanaa) to mark the completed change of state, so learning it as that compound verb from the start will match how it's actually used.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for फूलना (phoolnaa) for any swelling, but सूजना (soojnaa) is the default word specifically for a body part swelling from injury, infection, or a bite -- 'पैर सूज गया (pair sooj gayaa, the foot swelled up)' is the natural way to describe a twisted ankle, not फूलना (phoolnaa). 2) Being intransitive, it takes no ने (ne) in the past tense -- the body part itself is the subject: 'उसका हाथ सूज गया (uskaa haath sooj gayaa, his hand swelled up)', not a ने-marked construction.
Sample Sentences:
गिरने के बाद उसका घुटना सूज गया। After falling, his knee swelled up.
(girne ke baad uskaa ghutnaa sooj gayaa।)
