शुक्रिया (shukriyaa)

interjection/noun A common, warm way to say 'thank you' in Hindi -- used as an interjection on its own or as a noun meaning 'thanks/gratitude' in set phrases.
Sense 1 : thank you
interjection
Synonyms: धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad), आभार (aabhaar, formal, used more in writing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word came into Hindi from Urdu/Persian and is used constantly in daily life across Delhi, from thanking an auto-wallah to thanking a friend for a favor -- it carries no religious or regional weight and works everywhere. You will often hear it paired with बहुत (bahut) as बहुत शुक्रिया (bahut shukriyaa, 'thanks a lot') for extra warmth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add ने (ne) or try to conjugate it like a verb -- शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) is said flat, with no verb needed, just like 'thanks' in English. 2) Some learners overuse धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad) thinking it is more polite; in everyday spoken Delhi-NCR Hindi, शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) is actually the warmer, more natural everyday choice, while धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad) can feel a touch stiff or ceremonial in casual talk.
Sample Sentences:
शुक्रिया! Thank you!
(shukriyaa!)

Sense 2 : thanks, gratitude
noun, masculine Used mainly in the fixed frame शुक्रिया अदा करना (shukriyaa adaa karnaa, 'to express thanks'), where शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) functions as a noun object of the verb अदा करना (adaa karnaa).
Synonyms: धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad), आभार (aabhaar, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: As a noun, it shows up in slightly more formal or written contexts, like a thank-you note or speech: उसने अपने शिक्षक का शुक्रिया अदा किया (usne apne shikshak kaa shukriyaa adaa kiyaa, 'He expressed his thanks to his teacher'). This is the same root as the interjection, just doing noun duty in a set phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) as needing a verb like 'to say' -- but the natural collocation is अदा करना (adaa karnaa, 'to render/pay'), not कहना (kahnaa, 'to say'), when using it as a noun.
