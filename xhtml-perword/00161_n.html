न (n)

particle/conjunction A small but very active negation/tag word: it means 'no'/'not' in negation, shows up as the 'isn't it?' tag at the end of sentences, and links two negative alternatives in the sense of 'neither... nor.' (English; inline Hindi as Devanagari (n)
Sense 1 : tag question ('isn't it?', 'right?')
particle Placed at the very end of a statement to turn it into a request for agreement or confirmation, much like English 'isn't it?' or 'right?'.
Synonyms: है ना (hai naa, colloquial variant), क्या (kyaa, different construction, seeking confirmation)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the Hindi equivalent of tacking 'na?' onto a sentence in casual English-Hindi code-switching -- Delhi speakers use 'है ना' (hai naa) constantly to seek a nod of agreement, from haggling in a market to confirming plans with friends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this tag-question न (n) with the negation sense; in speech it is almost always heard as ना (naa) in this role, e.g. 'सही है ना' (sahii hai naa, 'that's right, isn't it?'). 2) Overusing it in writing where it sounds distinctly conversational.
Sample Sentences:
यह अच्छा है ना? This is good, isn't it?
(yah achchhaa hai naa?)

Sense 2 : negation ('not')
particle Used mainly in fixed or formal negative constructions (e.g. with चाहे, कोई, कुछ, or before certain verb forms) rather than as the everyday standalone negator, which is नहीं (nahiin).
Synonyms: नहीं (nahiin), मत (mat, used for prohibitions/commands)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of न (n) as the more compact, slightly literary cousin of नहीं (nahiin) -- you will hear it a lot in songs, proverbs, and set phrases (जो न हो सके, jo n ho sake, 'what cannot happen') where नहीं (nahiin) would feel heavier.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for न (n) where नहीं (nahiin) is the natural everyday choice; न (n) alone sounds clipped or literary in plain statements like 'main nahin jaata' rather than 'main na jaata'. 2) In prohibitions ('don't go'), मत (mat) is the normal choice, not न (n), though न (n) does appear in gentler or more formal prohibitions.
Sample Sentences:
जो न हो सके, वह मत सोचो। Don't think about what cannot happen.
(jo n ho sake, vah mat socho।)

Sense 3 : correlative negative conjunction ('neither... nor')
conjunction Appears paired as न...न (n ... n) linking two negated alternatives, e.g. 'न वह आया, न उसने फोन किया' (n vah aayaa, n usne phon kiyaa, 'he neither came nor called').
Synonyms: ना...ना (naa ... naa, colloquial variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This paired न...न (n ... n) construction is a clean, elegant way to dismiss two things at once, and you'll hear it often in complaints or excuses, like 'न पैसा है, न समय' (n paisaa hai, n samay, 'there's neither money nor time').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget to repeat न (n) before each item in the pair -- both halves need their own न (n), unlike English where 'nor' alone can carry the second clause.
Sample Sentences:
न पैसा है, न समय। There's neither money nor time.
(n paisaa hai, n samay।)
