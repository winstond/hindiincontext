गंगा (gangaa)

noun The river Ganga -- primarily a proper noun naming India's most sacred river, with a well-known secondary use as a common feminine given name.
Sense 1 : the Ganges river
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गंगा मैया (gangaa maiyaa, devotional, 'Mother Ganga'), गंगा जी (gangaa jii, respectful address form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: For most Hindus, गंगा (gangaa) is not just a river but a goddess and mother figure -- you'll hear people say गंगा मैया की जय (gangaa maiyaa kii jay, 'victory to Mother Ganga') at ghats in Varanasi or Haridwar. Bringing home a bottle of गंगा जल (gangaa jal, 'Ganga water') for rituals is common practice in many North Indian households, so the word carries a devotional weight well beyond simple geography.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate this simply as "the Ganges," missing that in everyday Hindi speech गंगा (gangaa) is rarely referred to as a mere geographic feature -- it is almost always spoken of with a respectful or affectionate tone, often with जी (jii) or मैया (maiyaa) attached. 2) As a proper noun naming a specific river, it does not take a determiner the way common nouns do -- you say गंगा में (gangaa men, 'in the Ganga'), not एक गंगा में (ek gangaa men).

Sense 2 : female given name
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: गंगा (gangaa) is also a common first name for women across North India, directly borrowed from the river's sacred status -- naming a daughter गंगा (gangaa) is a way of invoking purity and blessing, much like naming a child Grace or Faith in English.
