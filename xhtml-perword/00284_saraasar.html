सरासर (saraasar)

adverb An intensifying adverb used to brand something as complete, total, or through-and-through -- almost always with a negative-leaning noun like झूठ (jhooth), गलत (galat), or बेईमानी (beiimaanii).
Sense 1 : utterly, completely, through and through
adverb Placed directly before the noun or adjective it intensifies, e.g. सरासर झूठ (saraasar jhooth, 'a complete lie'), sarcastically underscoring how blatant or unmistakable the quality is.
Synonyms: बिल्कुल (bilkul), पूरी तरह (poorii tarah), नरा (naraa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word has a courtroom or accusation flavor to it -- you'll hear it when someone is calling out a lie or an injustice in a raised, indignant voice: यह सरासर झूठ है (yah saraasar jhooth hai, 'this is an outright lie'). It carries a built-in tone of moral outrage that plain बिल्कुल (bilkul) lacks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use सरासर (saraasar) as a general-purpose intensifier like 'very,' but it almost always carries a negative or accusatory shade -- you'd say सरासर झूठ (saraasar jhooth, 'a complete lie') or सरासर अन्याय (saraasar anyaay, 'sheer injustice'), not सरासर खुशी (saraasar khushii) for 'complete happiness.' 2) It modifies nouns/adjectives, not verbs -- you can't use it to mean 'completely' in the sense of finishing an action.

यह सरासर झूठ है। This is an outright lie.
(yah saraasar jhooth hai।)
