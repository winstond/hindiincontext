दोपहर (dopahar)

noun A single everyday time-of-day noun meaning 'noon/afternoon'; used constantly for scheduling and greetings.
Sense 1 : noon, midday, afternoon
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मध्याह्न (madhyaahn, literary/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian households, दोपहर (dopahar) is strongly associated with the midday meal and the customary afternoon rest -- many families eat lunch and then take a short nap during these hours, especially in summer when the sun is harshest. You'll often hear दोपहर का खाना (dopahar kaa khaanaa, 'afternoon/midday meal') as the standard way to refer to lunch, paired with शाम (shaam) for evening and रात (raat) for night.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume दोपहर (dopahar) means the whole afternoon in the English sense, but it centers more on midday and the early afternoon hours (roughly noon to 3-4pm); evening activities shift to शाम (shaam). 2) In greetings, the fixed phrase is दोपहर की नमस्ते (dopahar kii namaste) is not used the way English says 'good afternoon' -- Hindi speakers typically just say नमस्ते (namaste) regardless of time, so don't force a time-specific greeting.
