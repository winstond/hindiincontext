मिश्रित (mishrit)

adjective A learned, Sanskrit-derived adjective meaning 'mixed' or 'blended' -- used mostly in formal, written, or technical contexts rather than everyday kitchen talk.
Sense 1 : mixed, blended (of substances, elements, or feelings)
adjective
Synonyms: मिला हुआ (milaa huaa), मिश्र (mishr, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word on packaged food labels (मिश्रित सब्ज़ियाँ, mishrit sabziyaan, 'mixed vegetables') and in news Hindi (मिश्रित प्रतिक्रिया, mishrit pratikriyaa, 'mixed reaction'), which is exactly the register it belongs to -- formal, written, a notch above the spoken मिला-जुला (milaa-julaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मिश्रित (mishrit) in casual speech where मिला हुआ (milaa huaa) or मिक्स (miks) would sound far more natural -- मिश्रित (mishrit) belongs on a menu, in a chemistry class, or in a news report, not at the dinner table. 2) It describes the resulting mixed state, not the act of mixing -- 'मिश्रित करना' (mishrit karnaa) sounds stiff; native speakers say मिलाना (milaanaa) for the verb 'to mix'.

Sense 2 : mixed, ambivalent (of feelings, results, or reviews)
adjective
Synonyms: मिला-जुला (milaa-julaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the figurative extension you meet in phrases like मिश्रित भावनाएँ (mishrit bhaavnaaen, 'mixed feelings') -- the same idea as English 'mixed', where a single emotional or evaluative word carries two opposite pulls at once.
Sample Sentences:
फिल्म देखकर उसके मन में मिश्रित भावनाएँ थीं। After watching the film, he had mixed feelings.
(philm dekhkar uske man men mishrit bhaavnaaen thiin।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
नई नीति पर लोगों की प्रतिक्रिया मिश्रित रही है। People's reaction to the new policy has been mixed.

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
