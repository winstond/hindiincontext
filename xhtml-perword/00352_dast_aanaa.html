दस्त आना (dast aanaa)

verb A set phrase meaning 'to have diarrhea' -- literally 'loose motions to come,' following the common Hindi pattern where bodily afflictions arrive rather than being 'had' or 'done.'
Sense 1 : to have diarrhea
verb, intransitive Takes a को (ko)-marked experiencer, as with other involuntary-affliction verbs: मुझे दस्त आ रहे हैं (mujhe dast aa rahe hain, 'I have diarrhea').
Synonyms: पेट खराब होना (pet kharaab honaa, 'stomach to go bad', broader term for stomach upset), लूज़ मोशन होना (looz moshan honaa, colloquial, English loanword)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sits in the same family as other body-as-recipient expressions like बुखार आना (bukhaar aanaa, 'fever to come') and खांसी आना (khaansii aanaa, 'cough to come') -- Hindi consistently frames minor ailments as things that visit you rather than things you actively have. It's a common, slightly informal phrase you'd use with a doctor or at home, not clinical vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) दस्त (dast) is grammatically plural/treated as such in agreement even when referring to a single ongoing bout -- learners often default to singular agreement instead of matching the plural pattern seen in दस्त आ रहे हैं (dast aa rahe hain). 2) Learners sometimes try दस्त होना (dast honaa) by analogy with बुखार होना (bukhaar honaa, 'to have a fever') -- दस्त आना (dast aanaa) is the natural collocation, though दस्त लगना (dast lagnaa) is also heard and equally acceptable.
