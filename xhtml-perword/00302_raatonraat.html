रातोंरात (raatonraat)

adverb An adverb meaning 'overnight' -- both literally (during the course of one night) and, more commonly, figuratively (very suddenly, with startling speed). Built by doubling रात (raat, 'night') with the oblique plural marker, a common pattern in Hindi time-adverbs.
Sense 1 : suddenly, overnight (figurative -- rapid change)
adverb
Synonyms: अचानक (achaanak), एकदम (ekdam)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Hindi speakers reach for when talking about someone who became a star, went bankrupt, or had their life turned upside down 'रातोंरात' (raatonraat) -- think of gossip about a Bollywood newcomer's rise or a businessman's sudden ruin. It carries a slight flavor of disbelief, as if to say 'can you imagine, that fast?'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as literally about nighttime only; in most everyday use it describes the speed of a change (fame, fortune, decisions) rather than the actual passage of a single night. 2) It is invariant -- it does not take postpositions or inflect like a noun, even though it is built from रात (raat).

Sense 2 : during the course of a single night (literal)
adverb
Synonyms: एक रात में (ek raat men, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this literal sense in practical contexts too -- a tailor promising a suit 'रातोंरात' (raatonraat) tailored by morning, or dough left to ferment overnight. It is the same word as the figurative sense, just anchored to real clock-time rather than metaphorical speed.
