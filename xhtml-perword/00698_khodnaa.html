खोदना (khodnaa)

verb A single core sense: to dig, physically excavating earth or a surface. Extends naturally into a couple of common figurative/idiomatic uses built on that same digging image.
Sense 1 : to dig (earth, a hole, a well, etc.)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a well being dug in a village, या (yaa) a construction site with a JCB machine -- कुआँ खोदना (kuaan khodnaa, 'to dig a well') is a classic phrase in stories and proverbs about hard, patient labor. The related passive form खुदना (khudnaa) is worth learning alongside this word since the two are constantly paired in real speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) खोदना (khodnaa) is transitive and takes ने (ne) in the perfective -- मज़दूर ने गड्ढा खोदा (mazdoor ne gaddhaa khodaa, 'the laborer dug a pit') -- do not drop ने (ne) the way you might for a related-sounding intransitive verb. 2) Don't confuse with खुदना (khudnaa), the intransitive/passive counterpart meaning 'to be dug' -- गड्ढा खुद गया (gaddhaa khud gayaa, 'the pit got dug') has no agent and no ने (ne).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to pry, dig for (information), rake up (the past)
verb, transitive Commonly appears as the intensified form खोद-खोद कर पूछना (khod-khod kar poochhnaa) -- 'to dig and dig and ask,' i.e. to grill someone with questions.
Synonyms: खोद-खोद कर पूछना (khod-khod kar poochhnaa, to interrogate persistently)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like in English 'digging for dirt,' Hindi uses the same earthy image: पुरानी बातें खोदना (puraanii baaten khodnaa, 'to dig up old matters') is what a nosy relative does at a family gathering, dredging up things everyone else wanted to forget.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost always shows up with पूछना (poochhnaa) or ऐसी बातें (aisii baaten) attached -- learners sometimes try to use खोदना (khodnaa) alone to mean 'to interrogate,' which sounds incomplete without that frame.

(vah hameshaa puraanii baaten khodtaa rahtaa hai।)
