नटखट (natkhat)

adjective A single-sense adjective describing playful, mischievous behavior, most often used affectionately about children.
Sense 1 : mischievous, naughty (in a cute, playful sense)
adjective
Synonyms: शरारती (sharaartii), चंचल (chanchal, more about restlessness/fickleness than mischief)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: नटखट (natkhat) is the word every Indian grandmother reaches for when a toddler steals sweets or hides a shoe -- it's warm, not scolding. It's also permanently attached in the popular imagination to baby Krishna, whose butter-stealing antics are described as नटखट (natkhat) in countless bhajans and calendar-art captions, so the word carries a gentle devotional-cultural echo even in everyday use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for this word to describe genuinely bad or harmful behavior -- नटखट (natkhat) carries an affectionate, indulgent tone and is almost always about children or cute mischief, not real misconduct. For that, शरारती (sharaartii) or बदतमीज़ (badatmiiz) fit better depending on severity. 2) It's rarely used for adults except in a teasing, romantic, or joking register.
