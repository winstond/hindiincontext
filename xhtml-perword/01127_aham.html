अहम (aham)

adjective/noun Two related but distinct uses: an adjective meaning 'important', and a noun meaning 'ego, self' (borrowed from Sanskrit 'aham', I).
Sense 1 : important, significant
adjective
Synonyms: महत्वपूर्ण (mahatvpoorn), ज़रूरी (zaroorii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear अहम भूमिका (aham bhoomikaa, 'important role') constantly in news broadcasts and formal speeches -- it is the go-to word for 'important' in a slightly elevated, official register, sitting a notch above the everyday ज़रूरी (zaroorii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse अहम (aham, 'important') with अहंकार (ahankaar, 'ego/arrogance') or with the noun sense of अहम (aham, 'ego') itself -- context (adjective before a noun vs. standalone noun) usually disambiguates. 2) अहम (aham) is invariant and does not change form for gender or number, unlike many adjectives ending in a vowel sound.

(is maamle men saboot bahut aham hain।)

Sense 2 : ego, self
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अहंकार (ahankaar, often stronger: 'arrogance/pride'), अहं (ahan, variant form, more literary/psychological)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense comes straight from Sanskrit अहम् (aham, 'I'), the same root that gives us the philosophical idea of the self as the seat of pride. You will hear it in phrases like उसका अहम आड़े आ गया (uskaa aham aade aa gayaa, 'his ego got in the way'), often in family or workplace conflict stories.
Common Mistakes: 1) अहम (aham) as 'ego' is more commonly seen as अहं (ahan) in psychological or philosophical discussion (e.g. Freud's 'ego'); the two are the same word but अहं (ahan) is the more usual spelling in that specific noun sense.
