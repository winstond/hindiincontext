ख़रोंच (kharonch)

noun/verb A concrete, everyday word for a scratch or scrape on skin or a surface, used as a noun; it also appears as a verb meaning 'to scratch/scrape.'
Sense 1 : a scratch, scrape (minor injury or mark on a surface)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: खरोंच (kharonch, variant form without nuqta), निशान (nishaan, general mark or scar, broader meaning)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this a lot from worried Indian mothers and grandmothers: a kid falls off a bicycle and someone immediately checks -- 'कहीं ख़रोंच तो नहीं आई?' (kahiin kharonch to nahiin aaii?, 'did you get a scratch anywhere?'). It's the word for the small stuff, the kind of injury that gets a dab of Dettol and a lot of fuss rather than a doctor's visit.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चोट (chot) for any injury, but ख़रोंच (kharonch) is specifically a surface scratch or scrape, not a deeper wound or bruise -- using चोट (chot) for a light scratch sounds like you're overstating the injury. 2) The nuqta on ख़ is often dropped in casual writing (खरोंच), which is an accepted variant, not an error.

(girne se uske ghutne par kharonch aa gaii।)

Sense 2 : to scratch, scrape (a surface or skin)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: खुरचना (khurachnaa, scraping a surface, e.g. a pan), नोचना (nochnaa, more forceful, to claw/tear at)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a cat leaving marks on furniture, or a kid dragging a stick along a wall -- ख़रोंचना (kharonchnaa) captures that light, surface-level scraping action, related to the noun ख़रोंच (kharonch) above.
Common Mistakes: 1) This verb describes the action of scratching/scraping something, and is distinct from the noun sense above -- learners sometimes try to use the noun form as if it were the verb itself.
