फुफेरा (phupheraa)

adjective An adjective describing cousins related through one's father's sister (फूफा phoophaa/फूफी phoophii); almost always seen in the fixed phrase फुफेरा भाई (phupheraa bhaaii) or फुफेरी बहन (phupherii bahan).
Sense 1 : paternal-aunt's-side cousin (relational modifier)
adjective Used almost exclusively in the compound phrases फुफेरा भाई (phupheraa bhaaii, 'cousin brother, father's sister's son') and फुफेरी बहन (phupherii bahan, 'cousin sister, father's sister's daughter'); it does not stand alone as a noun.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi has a whole family of these four parallel terms (फुफेरा phupheraa, ममेरा mameraa, मौसेरा mauseraa, चचेरा chacheraa) that pinpoint exactly which aunt or uncle connects you to a cousin -- a level of precision English's blanket word 'cousin' just doesn't have. Growing up in a Delhi household, you'd naturally say 'my फुफेरा भाई (phupheraa bhaaii)' at a family gathering so everyone instantly knows you mean your father's sister's son, not just any cousin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often just say भाई (bhaaii) or बहन (bahan) alone for 'cousin,' losing the specific family branch that फुफेरा (phupheraa) signals -- Hindi speakers usually specify which side of the family a cousin is from. 2) Don't confuse this with ममेरा (mameraa, mother's brother's side), मौसेरा (mauseraa, mother's sister's side), or चचेरा (chacheraa, father's brother's side) -- all four sound similar but map to different relatives.
