निचोड़ना (nichodnaa)

verb A physical verb meaning to squeeze or wring something (a cloth, a lemon), with a common figurative extension to squeezing/extracting the essence or last effort out of something or someone.
Sense 1 : to wring or squeeze (a physical object)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: दबाना (dabaanaa, to press)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a mother wringing out a wet towel or squeezing a lemon over dal -- यही निचोड़ना (nichodnaa) है। It's a very kitchen-and-laundry-room verb, learned early by anyone who has watched Hindi cooking shows.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दबाना (dabaanaa) when they mean the twisting, wringing motion used on wet cloth or on a lemon -- निचोड़ना (nichodnaa) specifically implies extracting liquid out through squeezing, not just pressing down. 2) कपड़ा निचोड़ना (kapdaa nichodnaa, 'to wring out a cloth') is the classic collocation; learners often forget the object needs to be something that holds liquid for the verb to sound natural.
Sample Sentences:
गीले कपड़े को निचोड़ो। Wring out the wet cloth.
(giile kapde ko nichodo।)

Sense 2 : to extract the essence, effort, or last bit from someone/something (figurative)
verb, transitive Often used with a person as the object to mean overworking or exhausting them, as if wringing all their energy out like a wet cloth.
Synonyms: खींचना (khiinchnaa, to extract/pull), भींचना (bhiinchnaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense shows up naturally in complaints about bosses or exams: निचोड़ना (nichodnaa) frames a person as a lemon or a wet rag that's been squeezed of every last drop of energy -- a very physical, embodied way Hindi talks about exhaustion.
Common Mistakes: 1) When used about a person -- जैसे मालिक ने उसे निचोड़ लिया (maalik ne use nichod liyaa, 'the boss wrung him dry') -- learners may not realize this is a normal, vivid way to describe being overworked, not an unusually harsh or unusual metaphor.
