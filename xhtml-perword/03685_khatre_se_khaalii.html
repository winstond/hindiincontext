ख़तरे से ख़ाली (khatre se khaalii)

phrase A fixed idiomatic phrase literally meaning 'empty of danger,' used sarcastically to say someone or something is far from safe -- almost always in negation or as a rhetorical jab. Built from ख़तरा (khatraa, 'danger') and ख़ाली (khaalii, 'empty').
Sense 1 : not without risk (usually ironic)
phrase Almost always used with a negative sense implied or stated, e.g. 'यह काम ख़तरे से ख़ाली नहीं है' (yah kaam khatre se khaalii nahiin hai, 'this work is not without danger') -- the phrase itself carries an ironic 'safe' meaning that is usually negated to state the obvious: that something IS risky.
Synonyms: बेख़तर (bekhtar, rare), निडर (nidar, not a true synonym, describes a person not a situation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a classic bit of Hindi wordplay beloved by journalists and elders alike -- you will hear it in news commentary about a political move, or from a parent warning a child about a plan, always with a wry undertone. It pairs nicely with the straight adjective ख़तरनाक (khatarnaak, 'dangerous') as its blunt, non-ironic cousin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use it as a plain, sincere statement of safety ('यह जगह ख़तरे से ख़ाली है' meaning 'this place is truly safe') -- in practice it is almost always deployed with नहीं (nahiin) or in a sarcastic tone to underline that something is risky, not to reassure. 2) Because it looks like a simple adjectival phrase, learners may try to inflect ख़ाली (khaalii) for gender/number agreement with the subject when the whole expression is better treated as a fixed unit.
