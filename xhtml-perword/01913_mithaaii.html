मिठाई (mithaaii)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'sweet(s)' -- the general term for Indian confections, especially the kind exchanged at festivals and celebrations.
Sense 1 : Indian sweets/confectionery
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मीठा (miithaa, can mean 'sweets' informally but literally 'sweet thing')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: No trip to a Hindu or Sikh household during Diwali, a wedding, or after exam results is complete without a box of मिठाई (mithaaii) -- from काजू कतली (kaajoo katlii) to गुलाब जामुन (gulaab jaamun). Giving and receiving मिठाई (mithaaii) is such a strong social ritual that मिठाई (mithaaii, 'sweets') often stands in for the good news itself, as in मुँह मीठा करो (munh miithaa karo, 'sweeten your mouth') said when sharing celebratory sweets.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मीठा (miithaa) and मिठाई (mithaaii) interchangeably, but मीठा (miithaa) is the adjective/noun for 'sweet' in general (a sweet dish, a sweet taste), while मिठाई (mithaaii) specifically means the shop-bought or festival confections like लड्डू (laddoo) or बर्फ़ी (barfii). 2) मिठाई (mithaaii) is typically treated as a mass noun in everyday speech (like 'sweets' collectively), so learners overuse the plural form when the singular/collective मिठाई (mithaaii) already covers it.
