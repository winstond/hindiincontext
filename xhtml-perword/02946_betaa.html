बेटा (betaa)

noun Literally means 'son', but is used constantly as a warm term of address for any younger person, male or female, regardless of actual parentage.
Sense 1 : son (one's male child)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पुत्र (putr, formal/literary), लड़का (ladkaa, boy, not quite the same as 'son' but overlaps in casual speech)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear a hundred times a day in any Indian household with a son -- from berating him for coming home late to boasting about his exam results to the neighbors. It pairs naturally with बेटी (betii), 'daughter'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पुत्र (putr) as the default word for 'son' because it looks like a dictionary match, but बेटा (betaa) is what people actually say at home; पुत्र (putr) sounds formal or literary, more suited to a wedding invitation or a legal document than a family conversation.

(meraa betaa dillii men padhtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : affectionate term of address for a younger person (male)
noun, masculine Used vocatively, often as बेटे (bete) when directly addressing someone, even if the speaker is not the person's parent.
Synonyms: बच्चा (bachchaa, child, broader), बेटे (bete, oblique/vocative form used in direct address)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: An auto-rickshaw driver's mother, a doctor, or an elderly stranger might call a young man बेटा (betaa) purely out of warmth or worry -- it is one of the clearest examples of how Hindi kinship terms spill over into everyday address between strangers, especially across a generation gap.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners are often surprised that a shopkeeper, teacher, doctor, or stranger on the street can call a young man बेटा (betaa) with no family relationship implied -- it is simply a marker of affectionate seniority, not literal kinship. 2) Do not assume this address is condescending; it usually signals warmth or concern, not superiority.

Sense 3 : young offspring of an animal (male)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बच्चा (bachchaa, general term for young animal or child)

Cultural Note: You might hear a farmer refer to a calf or a pet's male offspring as बेटा (betaa) with real affection -- the kinship framing extends naturally to animals in a family-like household.
