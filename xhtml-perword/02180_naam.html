नाम (naam)

noun/postposition Mainly 'name' as a noun, with a common extended sense meaning 'fame/reputation'; also appears in a few frequent idioms and postposition-like phrases such as 'in the name of' (ke naam par).
Sense 1 : name (of a person, place, or thing)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the very first words every learner meets, usually in the exchange आपका नाम क्या है? (‘what is your name?’) followed by मेरा नाम ... है (‘my name is ...’). Note that नाम itself is invariant in gender agreement (always treated as masculine: मेरा नाम), regardless of the gender of the person it names.
Common Mistakes: 1) Asking someone's name uses the dative-subject-style question आपका नाम क्या है (aapkaa naam kyaa hai, 'what is your name'), not a literal 'you are called' construction like in some other languages -- learners sometimes overthink this and try to translate 'to be named' with a verb instead of this simple possessive frame. 2) मेरा नाम X है (meraa naam raaj hai, 'my name is Raj') uses है, not हूँ, since the sentence is about नाम (grammatically masculine), not about 'I'.

Sense 2 : fame, reputation, renown
noun, masculine Common in set phrases like नाम कमाना (naam kamaanaa, 'to earn a name/fame') and नाम होना (naam honaa, 'to become famous, to have a reputation').
Synonyms: शोहरत (shohrat), यश (yash, literary), इज़्ज़त (izzat, reputation/honor, close but broader)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in praise or gossip: किसी का नाम है (kisii kaa naam hai, 'so-and-so has a name/reputation') is how Hindi speakers talk about someone being well-known in their field, whether a chef, a doctor, or a wrestler in the local akhara.
Common Mistakes: 1) नाम कमाना (naam kamaanaa) literally 'to earn a name' means 'to become famous/well-regarded,' not literally to acquire a name -- learners sometimes miss the idiomatic reading and take it too literally. 2) In this sense नाम is often used without an article-like word in English but still needs का/की/के possessives in Hindi, e.g. उसके नाम की चर्चा (uske naam kii charchaa, 'talk of his/her fame').
Sample Sentences:
उस डॉक्टर का बहुत नाम है। That doctor has a great reputation.

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : in the name of, on behalf of, under the pretext of
postposition Occurs in the fixed frame [X] के नाम पर (ke naam par, 'in the name of X'), where X is in the oblique case before के.
Synonyms: के नाम पर (ke naam par, full standard form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a favorite construction in Hindi news and political commentary: विकास के नाम पर (vikaas ke naam par, 'in the name of development') is a very common critical phrase you will hear when someone suspects an official excuse is covering up something else.
Common Mistakes: 1) This phrase often carries a skeptical or critical edge in real speech -- धर्म के नाम पर (dharm ke naam par, 'in the name of religion') usually implies the speaker doubts the sincerity of the claim, which learners can miss if they read it as neutral. 2) Do not confuse with के नाम से (ke naam se, 'by/under the name of,' used for aliases or addressing), which is a different frame with a different sense.

(vikaas ke naam par kaii gaanv ujaad die gae।)
