दरार (daraar)

noun Primarily a physical noun -- a crack or fissure -- with a very common figurative extension to a rift or split in a relationship, group, or organization.
Sense 1 : rift, split (figurative, in relationships/groups)
noun, feminine Commonly used with पड़ना (padnaa) or आना (aanaa): रिश्ते में दरार आना (rishte men daraar aanaa, 'a rift to appear in a relationship').
Synonyms: दरार (daraar), फूट (phoot, 'discord, split among people'), दूरी (doorii, 'distance, more general')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Hindi news anchors reach for when describing cracks in a political alliance or a family feud -- पार्टी में दरार (paartii men daraar, 'a rift in the party') is a stock headline phrase. It extends the physical image of a crack to something that starts small and can widen.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is often paired with में (men) marking the relationship or group affected -- दोस्ती में दरार (dostii men daraar, 'a rift in the friendship') -- learners sometimes try को (ko) instead. 2) Do not confuse with तलाक़ (talaaq, 'divorce') -- दरार implies growing distance or discord, not a final legal break.

(donon bhaaiyon ke biich daraar badhtii jaa rahii hai।)

Sense 2 : crack, fissure (physical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दरार (daraar), चिराड़ (chiraad, rare), दरज़ (daraz, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word used for a crack in a wall (दीवार में दरार, diivaar men daraar, 'a crack in the wall') after monsoon dampness or an earthquake tremor -- a very ordinary household observation in North India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for टूटना (tootnaa, 'to break') when they mean a crack has appeared -- the natural collocation is दरार पड़ना (daraar padnaa, 'a crack to appear') or दरार आना (daraar aanaa), not दरार टूटना (daraar tootnaa).
