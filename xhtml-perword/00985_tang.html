तंग (tang)

adjective/verb Primarily an adjective meaning 'narrow, tight' (of space or clothing) or, figuratively, 'troubled/distressed'; also lives in the common idiom तंग करना (tang karnaa) meaning 'to harass, pester.'
Sense 1 : narrow, tight (physical space or fit)
adjective
Synonyms: सँकरा (sankraa), छोटा (chhotaa, when describing tight-fitting clothes)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the winding lanes of Old Delhi (puraanii dillii) -- गलियाँ तंग हैं (galiyaan tang hain, 'the lanes are narrow') is exactly the kind of line a rickshaw driver might mutter as he squeezes through. The same word describes a shirt that has shrunk in the wash, so तंग (tang) covers both 'narrow space' and 'tight fit' without needing two different words the way English does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for छोटा (chhotaa) to describe a tight-fitting garment, but तंग (tang) is the natural word for 'tight' when talking about clothes or shoes, not just 'small.' 2) For describing a narrow lane or street, तंग (tang) is the default choice over सँकरा (sankraa) in everyday speech, even though both are correct.

Sense 2 : distressed, troubled, in financial or emotional difficulty
adjective Often appears in the set phrase तंग हाल (tang haal, 'in a bad/difficult state,' especially financially) and तंग आना (tang aanaa, 'to become fed up/exasperated').
Synonyms: परेशान (pareshaan), मुश्किल में (mushkil men, phrase: 'in difficulty')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मैं तंग आ गया हूँ (main tang aa gayaa hoon, 'I've become fed up') is something you'll hear from an exasperated parent or a shopkeeper dealing with a difficult customer -- it carries a sense of having reached one's limit, similar to 'I've had it' in English. तंग हाल (tang haal) meanwhile leans toward financial hardship, as in a family तंग हाल में गुज़ारा कर रहे हैं (tang haal men guzaaraa kar rahe hain, 'getting by in hard circumstances').
Common Mistakes: 1) तंग आना (tang aanaa) means 'to become fed up or exasperated,' not 'to arrive in a narrow place' -- learners sometimes misparse it literally. 2) In this sense तंग (tang) pairs with होना (honaa) or आना (aanaa), not करना (karnaa) -- तंग करना (tang karnaa) is a different, transitive idiom (see other sense) where तंग (tang) describes what is done *to* someone, not how someone feels.

Sense 3 : to harass, pester, trouble (someone)
verb, transitive This sense exists only as the compound तंग करना (tang karnaa) -- तंग (tang) does not function as a standalone verb; the transitivity given here belongs to the whole compound.
Synonyms: परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa), सताना (sataanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बच्चे को तंग मत करो (bachche ko tang mat karo, 'don't pester the child') is a classic line an elder sibling hears from a parent. The neat pairing of तंग करना (tang karnaa, 'to pester someone') and तंग आना (tang aanaa, 'to become fed up') shows how Hindi builds a small family of idioms around one adjective, one active and one experiential.
Common Mistakes: 1) The object of harassment takes को (ko): वह मुझे तंग करता है (vah mujhe tang kartaa hai, 'he pesters me') -- learners sometimes drop को (ko) or use से (se) by analogy with English 'bother with.' 2) Do not confuse with तंग आना (tang aanaa) -- करना (karnaa) means someone is doing the harassing, while आना (aanaa) means someone is the one becoming exasperated.
Sample Sentences:
