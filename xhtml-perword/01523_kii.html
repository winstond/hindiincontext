की (kii)

postposition/verb Most commonly the feminine possessive postposition ('of'), the counterpart to का (kaa) and के (ke); it is also the perfective feminine form of the verb करना (karnaa, 'to do').
Sense 1 : possessive marker (feminine)
postposition Agrees with the feminine noun that follows it, e.g. राम की किताब (raam kii kitaab, 'Ram's book') because किताब (kitaab) is feminine.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first grammar hurdles for English speakers, since English 'of'/'-'s' has no gender at all -- Hindi possession is really a hidden adjective agreeing with the possessed noun. Think of राम की बहन (raam kii bahan, 'Ram's sister') and राम का भाई (raam kaa bhaaii, 'Ram's brother') as your anchor pair for hearing the contrast.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often pick का (kaa)/की (kii)/के (ke) based on the gender of the preceding noun (the possessor) instead of the following noun (the thing possessed) -- the agreement is entirely with what comes after. 2) When the possessed noun is plural or oblique, की (kii) itself never changes since it already ends in ी -- only का (kaa) alternates to के (ke) in those environments, so learners sometimes hunt for a nonexistent plural form of की (kii).
Sample Sentences:
यह मेरी किताब है। This is my book.
(yah merii kitaab hai।)

Sense 2 : did (feminine perfective of 'to do')
verb, transitive This is the feminine singular/plural perfective form of करना (karnaa); it appears with a feminine subject marked by ने (ne), e.g. उसने चाय बनाई (usne chaay banaaii) versus उसने काम किया (usne kaam kiyaa) -- compare उसने बात की (usne baat kii, 'she talked') where की (kii) agrees with the feminine object बात (baat).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This form shows up constantly in set phrases like बात की (baat kii, 'talked'), मेहनत की (mehnat kii, 'worked hard'), and मदद की (madad kii, 'helped') -- all built on feminine nouns (बात, मेहनत, मदद) paired with करना (karnaa) as a light verb, so listening for these collocations is the fastest way to internalize the agreement pattern.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because करना (karnaa) is transitive, this feminine form appears not for a feminine subject but to agree with a feminine direct object -- learners who have just learned ने-agreement sometimes expect agreement with the (often masculine) subject instead. 2) Easy to visually confuse with the possessive postposition की (kii) in a sentence like उसने मेहनत की (usne mehnat kii, 'he/she worked hard') where it looks like it could be marking possession but is actually the verb itself.
Sample Sentences:
उसने बहुत मेहनत की। She worked very hard.
(usne bahut mehnat kii।)
