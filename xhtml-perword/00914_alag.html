अलग (alag)

adjective/adverb अलग (alag) is a common invariant adjective meaning 'separate' or 'different'; it also functions as an adverb meaning 'separately/apart' and appears in several everyday compound-verb-like phrases such as अलग करना (alag karnaa) and अलग होना (alag honaa).
Sense 1 : different, distinct
adjective
Synonyms: भिन्न (bhinn, formal), विभिन्न (vibhinn, formal, used before plural/collective nouns)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi markets and homes: यह उससे अलग है (yah usse alag hai, 'this is different from that') is the everyday way to distinguish two options, whether it's two kurtas or two opinions in a family argument.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect अलग (alag) like कड़ा (kadaa), giving wrong forms like अलगा (algaa) or अलगी (algii) -- it never changes form regardless of the noun's gender or number. 2) When comparing two things, the pattern is X से अलग (X se alag, 'different from X'), not X से अलग है की (X se alag hai kii) style constructions learners sometimes attempt by analogy with English 'different that'.

Sense 3 : separately, apart, aside
adverb Functions adverbially with verbs like रखना (rakhnaa) and करना (karnaa), e.g. इसे अलग रख दो (ise alag rakh do, 'keep this aside').
Synonyms: अलग से (alag se, common variant with से)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: At a roadside dhaba you'll often hear रोटी अलग से लगेगी (rotii alag se lagegii, 'the roti will be charged separately/extra') -- a very practical, real-life use of this word every visitor to India should know.
Common Mistakes: 1) अलग से (alag se) often means 'additionally, on top of that' rather than just 'separately' -- e.g. ordering something अलग से (alag se) at a dhaba can mean 'as an extra item', which surprises learners expecting only a spatial meaning.
