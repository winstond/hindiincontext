आँखें चार होना (aankhen chaar honaa)

verb A vivid idiom literally meaning 'for eyes to become four' -- used when two people's eyes meet, especially in a romantic first-glance sense, and less commonly for a tense face-off. Both senses share the same 'four eyes' image.
Sense 1 : eyes to meet (especially at first sight, romantically)
verb, intransitive Subject is usually the pair of people involved, expressed with की (kii) or के (ke) rather than as a simple grammatical subject: उसकी और उसकी आँखें चार हुईं (uskii aur uskii aankhen chaar huiin, 'his and her eyes met'). होना (honaa) is the finite verb and agrees with आँखें (aankhen), which is grammatically feminine plural.
Synonyms: नज़रें मिलना (nazren milnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase is a Bollywood-movie staple for the classic love-at-first-sight moment -- hero and heroine lock eyes across a crowded room or market, and 'आँखें चार हो गईं' (aankhen chaar ho gaiin, 'their eyes met') captures that instant spark. It leans romantic far more often than confrontational, so think 'meet-cute,' not 'stare-down.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to insert a normal subject-verb order as in English ('he and she eyes four became'), but the idiom keeps आँखें चार (aankhen chaar) fixed as a unit with होना (honaa) simply supplying tense/aspect. 2) This is not used for ordinary eye contact between strangers or colleagues -- it implies a charged or romantic spark, so using it for a mundane glance sounds odd to a native speaker.

Sense 2 : to have a tense face-off, eyes locking in confrontation
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: आमने-सामने होना (aamne-saamne honaa, less idiomatic, more literal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This confrontational reading exists but is much rarer in everyday speech than the romantic one -- if you say आँखें चार हुईं (aankhen chaar huiin) without other context, most Delhi listeners will assume you mean the romantic spark, not a showdown.
