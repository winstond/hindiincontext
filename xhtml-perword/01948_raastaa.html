रास्ता (raastaa)

noun Mainly a concrete noun for 'road/path/way,' with a natural extension into figurative 'way/means/solution' -- much like English 'way' does the same double duty.
Sense 1 : road, path, route
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सड़क (sadak), मार्ग (maarg, formal), राह (raah, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask for directions in Delhi and you will hear रास्ता (raastaa) constantly: यह रास्ता कहाँ जाता है? (yah raastaa kahaan jaataa hai?, 'where does this road go?'). It is the default word an auto-rickshaw driver or a shopkeeper uses when pointing you somewhere, far more than सड़क (sadak) or मार्ग (maarg).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सड़क (sadak) when they mean a specific paved road and रास्ता (raastaa) when they mean 'the way to get somewhere' -- रास्ता (raastaa) is broader and covers dirt paths, routes, and directions, not just paved streets. 2) रास्ता भूलना (raastaa bhoolnaa, 'to lose one's way') and रास्ता भटकना (raastaa bhataknaa, 'to go astray/wander off the path') are both common and learners often mix up which verb pairs naturally with रास्ता (raastaa) in speech.

Sense 2 : way, method, means (figurative)
noun, masculine Used in frames like कोई रास्ता निकालना (koii raastaa nikaalnaa, 'to find a way out') and रास्ता दिखाना (raastaa dikhaanaa, 'to show the way/guide') for abstract problem-solving, not literal travel.
Synonyms: उपाय (upaay), तरीका (tariikaa), हल (hal, solution)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: When someone is stuck -- money trouble, a family dispute, a stalled plan -- you will hear कोई रास्ता निकालते हैं (koii raastaa nikaalte hain, 'let's find some way out'). It carries a hopeful, problem-solving tone, the same way English speakers say 'we'll figure something out.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners tend to default to तरीका (tariikaa) for 'method' and forget रास्ता (raastaa) works just as naturally for 'a way out of a problem,' especially with निकालना (nikaalnaa) as in कोई रास्ता निकालना (koii raastaa nikaalnaa, 'to work something out').
