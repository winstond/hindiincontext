टोपी (topii)

noun Basically a single-sense word meaning 'cap/hat', but it lives inside a very common idiom for cheating or humiliating someone that every learner should pick up.
Sense 1 : cap, hat
noun, feminine
Synonyms: टोप (top, usually a larger helmet-like cap)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the little white Gandhi cap (gaandhii topii) worn at political rallies, or the skullcaps (topii) worn by Muslim men for prayer -- टोपी (topii) covers everything from a baby's woolen cap to a chef's tall white hat. It's a very concrete, everyday word you'll hear in markets and at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use टोपी (topii) for any headwear, but a formal turban is पगड़ी (pagdii), not टोपी (topii).

Sense 2 : being cheated/duped (idiomatic)
noun, feminine Almost always appears inside the fixed expression टोपी पहनाना (topii pahnaanaa, 'to make someone wear a cap' = to dupe/cheat someone), with the cheated person marked with को (ko).
Synonyms: धोखा (dhokhaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: किसी को टोपी पहनाना (kisii ko topii pahnaanaa, 'to make someone wear a cap') is a classic Delhi bazaar expression for pulling a fast one on someone, especially in a shady deal -- you'll hear shopkeepers and street-smart friends use it about being overcharged or tricked.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense barely occurs as a standalone noun outside the idiom टोपी पहनाना (topii pahnaanaa) -- learners shouldn't try to use टोपी (topii) alone to mean 'a cheat.' 2) The verb here is पहनाना (pahnaanaa, the causative of पहनना (pahannaa)), not पहनना (pahannaa) itself -- you 'make someone wear' the cap, you don't just 'wear' it in this idiom.
