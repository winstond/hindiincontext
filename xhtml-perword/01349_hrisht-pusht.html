हृष्ट-पुष्ट (hrisht-pusht)

adjective A single descriptive sense: someone (or an animal) who is healthy, well-fed, and physically robust -- the word people reach for when praising a chubby, thriving baby or a sturdy, well-nourished animal.
Sense 1 : healthy and well-built, robust/plump in a good way
adjective
Synonyms: तंदुरुस्त (tandurust), मोटा-ताज़ा (motaa-taazaa, informal, used of people/animals), सेहतमंद (sehatmand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic compliment for a baby at a family gathering or a buffalo at a village fair -- दादी कहती हैं, 'बच्चा तो हृष्ट-पुष्ट है' (bachchaa to hrisht-pusht hai, 'the child is so healthy and sturdy') with real pride, since a plump, well-fed look is traditionally read as a sign of good care and prosperity rather than a figure flaw. The two halves, हृष्ट (hrisht, 'delighted/thriving') and पुष्ट (pusht, 'nourished/strong'), are a fused Sanskritic pair that Hindi speakers no longer split apart -- you always hear them together.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this for a sick person who has just 'recovered' -- हृष्ट-पुष्ट (hrisht-pusht) is about robust build and nourishment, not recovery from illness; ठीक हो गया (thiik ho gayaa) is the word for that. 2) It is almost always positive and slightly admiring in tone -- it doesn't carry the negative 'overweight' connotation that 'fat' can have in English.

दादी कहती हैं कि बच्चा तो हृष्ट-पुष्ट है। Grandma says the child is so hale and hearty.
(daadii kahtii hain ki bachchaa to hrisht-pusht hai।)
