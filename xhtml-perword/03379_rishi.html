ऋषि (rishi)

noun A single core meaning: a sage or seer in the Hindu/Vedic tradition. Learners meet it mostly in religious, mythological, or literary contexts rather than everyday chit-chat.
Sense 1 : sage, seer
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मुनि (muni), साधु (saadhu, broader term, also covers ascetics generally), संत (sant, more devotional/saint-like connotation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the ऋषि (rishi) as the archetypal figure meditating in a forest hermitage (aashram), composing hymns or imparting wisdom to kings and disciples -- names like वाल्मीकि (vaalmiiki) or वशिष्ठ (vashishth) come up constantly in stories every Indian kid grows up with. The word carries real reverence, so it is not used casually or jokingly the way baabaa sometimes is.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use ऋषि (rishi) loosely for any holy man; it specifically evokes the Vedic-era tradition of wisdom-through-penance/knowledge, distinct from a temple priest (pandit) or a modern-day godman (baabaa).
