बाक़ायदा (baaqaaydaa)

adverb/adjective One core meaning that stretches across two related uses: as an adjective it means 'proper, formal, systematic,' and as an adverb it means 'properly, formally, in due order' -- both senses point to something done the 'right' way, following rule or procedure. Also commonly spelled बाकायदा (baakaaydaa) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : properly, formally, according to due procedure
adverb
Synonyms: विधिवत (vidhivat, formal), ठीक तरीके से (thiik tariike se)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the far more common use in everyday Delhi speech -- you'll hear it in sentences like बाक़ायदा (baaqaaydaa) निमंत्रण भेजा ('sent a proper/formal invitation'), where it signals that something was done with full seriousness and not half-heartedly. It often adds a flavor of 'and mind you, this was no small or casual thing.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use it loosely to mean simply 'really' or 'actually' (as in casual speech: बाक़ायदा (baaqaaydaa) उसने मुझे बुलाया, 'he actually/formally invited me') -- in colloquial use it can carry a mild sense of surprised emphasis ('he went so far as to...') beyond its dictionary meaning of 'properly.' Don't assume it always means strict procedural correctness.

(usne baaqaaydaa nimantran bhejaa thaa।)

Sense 2 : proper, formal, systematic, official
adjective
Synonyms: विधिवत (vidhivat, formal), औपचारिक (aupchaarik)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used to describe something -- a document, a ceremony, an arrangement -- that has been done through the recognized, correct channel rather than informally: बाक़ायदा शादी (baaqaaydaa shaadii, 'a proper/formal wedding') versus a quiet or informal arrangement. It carries the flavor of 'no shortcuts taken here.'

यह एक बाक़ायदा शादी थी, कोई भागकर की गई शादी नहीं। This was a proper wedding, not some elopement.
(yah ek baaqaaydaa shaadii thii, koii bhaagkar kii gaii shaadii nahiin।)
