बसंत (basant)

noun Primarily a noun naming the spring season, and also a common Hindi male given name; also spelled वसंत (vasant) with a व instead of ब, especially in more formal or literary writing.
Sense 1 : the spring season
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वसंत (vasant, variant spelling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: बसंत (basant) is one of the six traditional ऋतुएँ (rituen, 'seasons') of the Indian calendar, and it carries a festive, romantic charge in poetry and song, evoking mustard fields in bloom and new beginnings. It is closely tied to the festival बसंत पंचमी (basant panchmii), which marks the onset of spring and is dedicated to the goddess Saraswati.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume बसंत (basant) maps neatly onto the Western calendar's March-April spring, but in the Indian seasonal cycle it refers to a distinct, shorter season around February-March, associated festivals, and a particular quality of weather -- not simply 'not-winter, not-summer.' 2) The word is often used adjectivally in compounds like बसंत ऋतु (basant ritu, 'the spring season'), where a learner might expect a separate adjective form; बसंत (basant) itself does the job as a noun-modifier.

Sense 2 : a male given name
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: बसंत (basant) is a fairly common Hindi male first name, chosen precisely because of its association with spring's freshness and renewal -- much like 'Spring' or 'April' can occasionally appear as an English name, but far more commonplace here.
