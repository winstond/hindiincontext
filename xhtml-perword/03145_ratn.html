रत्न (ratn)

noun Means 'jewel' or 'gem' in a literal sense, and by extension a 'gem of a person' -- someone outstanding in their field. Also appears as the second element in honorific titles.
Sense 1 : jewel, precious stone
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जवाहर (javaahar), मणि (mani, literary), नगीना (nagiinaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a jeweler's shop window in Chandni Chowk: the loose, uncut or polished stones on display are रत्न (ratn), while the finished necklaces and earrings are गहने (gahne). This word also shows up in the Navratna (navratn, 'nine gems') tradition, a set of nine gemstones linked to the nine planets in Vedic astrology, still worn as rings by many people today.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this interchangeably with गहना (gahnaa), but गहना (gahnaa) means a piece of jewelry (a finished ornament like a necklace or bangle), while रत्न (ratn) is the raw precious stone itself, often set into such jewelry.

Sense 2 : an outstanding or exceptional person, a 'gem' of a person
noun, masculine Used appositively after a field or category noun to praise someone as its finest example, e.g. कला-रत्न (kalaa-ratn, 'gem of the arts'). Very common as the final element of honorific titles.
Synonyms: हीरा (hiiraa, 'diamond', used the same figurative way)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is most alive in national honors: भारत रत्न (bhaarat ratn, 'Jewel of India') is India's highest civilian award, given to people considered the 'gems' of the nation in their field. You'll also hear it in phrases like कुल का रत्न (kul kaa ratn, 'the pride/gem of the family'), praising a child who brings honor to their household.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense rarely stands alone in casual speech the way हीरा (hiiraa) does (as in वह तो हीरा है (vah to hiiraa hai, 'he's a real gem')); रत्न (ratn) in this sense is mostly seen in compounds and titles rather than as a loose predicate.
