बोलना (bolnaa)

verb The everyday verb for speaking/talking, and by extension, saying something specific. Also appears in idioms about speaking up or being outspoken.
Sense 1 : to speak, to talk
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: बात करना (baat karnaa), बतियाना (batiyaanaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first verbs learners meet, and for good reason: हिंदी बोलिए (hindii bolie, 'please speak Hindi') is a phrase you will hear constantly if you are learning in India, often good-naturedly demanded by shopkeepers and auto drivers once they realize you know some.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बात करना (baat karnaa) for 'to talk' in every context, but बोलना (bolnaa) is the more basic verb for the physical act of speaking (making sounds/words come out), while बात करना (baat karnaa) frames it as having a conversation. 2) हिंदी बोलना (hindii bolnaa, 'to speak Hindi') takes the language as a direct object with no postposition -- learners sometimes insert में (men) by analogy with English 'speak in Hindi'.
Sample Sentences:
क्या तुम हिंदी बोलते हो? Do you speak Hindi?
(kyaa tum hindii bolte ho?)

Sense 2 : to say, to utter (something specific)
verb, transitive Takes a direct object (what is said) or reported speech, unlike the intransitive 'to speak' sense.
Synonyms: कहना (kahnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, kids and adults alike often use बोलना (bolnaa) interchangeably with कहना (kahnaa) for quoting someone -- क्या बोला उसने? (kyaa bolaa usne?, 'what did he say?') is extremely common and sounds more natural in casual conversation than the more bookish क्या कहा उसने?.
Common Mistakes: 1) कहना (kahnaa) and बोलना (bolnaa) overlap heavily in casual speech for 'to say', but कहना (kahnaa) is the more neutral reporting verb in writing, while बोलना (bolnaa) in this sense often carries a slightly blunter or more colloquial flavor, as in वो ऐसा बोला (vo aisaa bolaa, 'he said it like that'). 2) In this transitive sense with a direct quote, ने-marking applies normally in the perfective -- उसने बोला (usne bolaa, 'he said') -- which learners sometimes forget because they mentally file बोलना (bolnaa) as purely intransitive from its first sense.
Sample Sentences:
उसने क्या बोला? What did he say?
(usne kyaa bolaa?)

Sense 3 : to be outspoken, to speak one's mind (idiomatic/figurative)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: मुँह खोलना (munh kholnaa, 'to open one's mouth', idiom)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear जो बोलना है बोल दो (jo bolnaa hai bol do, 'say whatever you have to say') when someone is being pressed to stop hesitating and just speak their mind -- it captures a very Indian social dynamic of hinting rather than confronting directly, and this phrase is the nudge to drop the hints.
Sample Sentences:
