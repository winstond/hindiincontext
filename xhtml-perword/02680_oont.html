ऊँट (oont)

noun A single, simple sense: the animal 'camel'. Also carries a well-known idiomatic use referring to a person's unpredictable temperament, tied to the camel's famously erratic behavior.
Sense 1 : camel (the animal)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Rajasthan and the Delhi-NCR fringe you still see ऊँट (oont) pulling carts or decked out for tourists at fairs like Pushkar. It's a common, everyday noun, not a bookish one, even though many city kids only meet it in zoos or on trips out west.

Sense 2 : an unpredictable or fickle person (idiomatic)
noun, masculine Almost always used inside the fixed idiom ऊँट किस करवट बैठेगा (oont kis karvat baithegaa, 'which way will the camel sit down'), not as a free-standing noun meaning 'fickle person.'
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This idiom plays on the real-life image of a camel lowering itself to sit -- an oddly lurching, hard-to-predict motion -- and Hindi speakers reach for it when discussing an unpredictable boss, politician, or situation whose next move nobody can call.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use ऊँट (oont) alone to mean 'an unpredictable person' -- in practice this sense almost never appears outside the set phrase ऊँट किस करवट बैठेगा (oont kis karvat baithegaa), so using the bare noun this way sounds odd to a native speaker.

(pataa nahiin oont kis karvat baithe, bos kaa mood har din badal jaataa hai।)
