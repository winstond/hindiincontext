चढ़ाव (chadhaav)

noun A noun built from the verb चढ़ना (chadhnaa, 'to climb/rise') referring to an upward slope or a rise/increase, most often in physical terrain or in levels like prices and tides.
Sense 1 : a rise or increase in level, price, or intensity (figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बढ़ोतरी (badhotrii), उछाल (uchhaal, sharper/sudden rise, e.g. in prices or markets)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news about the stock market or petrol prices: बाज़ार में उतार-चढ़ाव (baazaar men utaar-chadhaav, 'ups and downs in the market'). The tide-like imagery (उतार (utaar, 'ebb') paired with चढ़ाव (chadhaav, 'rise')) reflects how Hindi naturally reaches for a river or sea metaphor to describe volatility.
Common Mistakes: 1) In market or price contexts, उतार-चढ़ाव (utaar-chadhaav, 'ups and downs, fluctuations') is the far more frequent form than चढ़ाव (chadhaav) alone -- learners should recognize this compound as a fixed expression for fluctuation rather than assembling it themselves.
Sample Sentences:
शेयर बाज़ार में आजकल बहुत उतार-चढ़ाव है। There is a lot of ups and downs in the stock market these days.
(sheyar baazaar men aajkal bahut utaar-chadhaav hai।)

Sense 2 : an upward slope, ascent, incline
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ढलान (dhalaan, usually means downward slope, contrast), चढ़ाई (chadhaaii, more common word for a climb/ascent)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a hilly road in the hills around Shimla or Mussoorie: locals describe the route in terms of चढ़ाव (chadhaav) and ढलान (dhalaan) -- where it climbs and where it drops. This pairing extends naturally into the figurative sense below, where life itself is described as having such rises and falls.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चढ़ाई (chadhaaii) by default for 'climb/ascent' since it is far more common in speech; चढ़ाव (chadhaav) is used but skews toward describing terrain or a route's gradient rather than the act of climbing itself. 2) Do not confuse with ढलान (dhalaan), which is the downward slope -- the two are often paired as चढ़ाव और ढलान (chadhaav aur dhalaan, 'ups and downs / rises and falls') when describing a road's terrain.
