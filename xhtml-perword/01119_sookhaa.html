सूखा (sookhaa)

adjective/noun/verb Covers three related uses: an adjective meaning 'dry', a noun meaning 'drought', and the masculine past-tense form of the verb सूखना (sookhnaa, 'to dry/wither'), which itself gets a verb sense here since it is so commonly met in this form.
Sense 1 : dry (not wet)
adjective
Synonyms: रूखा (rookhaa, also means 'harsh/curt' for people)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around laundry and weather: कपड़े सूखे नहीं हैं (kapde sookhe nahiin hain, 'the clothes aren't dry yet') is a very common household line, since many homes air-dry clothes on a line or rooftop and everyone tracks whether they're ready to bring in.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse with रूखा (rookhaa) -- सूखा (sookhaa) describes something that has lost moisture (कपड़े (kapde, 'clothes'), मौसम (mausam, 'weather')), while रूखा (rookhaa) more often describes texture or a person's curt manner. 2) As an adjective it agrees in gender/number with the noun -- सूखी रोटी (sookhii rotii, 'dry bread/roti') not सूखा रोटी (sookhaa rotii).

Sense 2 : drought
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अकाल (akaal, famine/drought, often more severe connotation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word shows up often in news about farming regions -- सूखा पड़ना (sookhaa padnaa, 'for a drought to occur') is the standard collocation you'll hear in reports about monsoon failure and its effect on farmers.
Common Mistakes: 1) सूखा (sookhaa) as a noun is masculine and refers specifically to a dry spell/drought affecting crops or a region, distinct from अकाल (akaal) which leans toward famine (the human crisis that can follow).

Sense 3 : to dry, to wither (past tense form)
verb, intransitive This is the masculine singular perfective form of the intransitive verb सूखना (sookhnaa); it appears constantly on its own as a predicate, e.g. तालाब सूखा (taalaab sookhaa, 'the pond dried up'), which is why it earns its own sense here even though the infinitive is the true lemma.
Synonyms: मुरझाना (murjhaanaa, specifically for plants/flowers wilting)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The extended, figurative use is very much alive: मुँह सूख गया (munh sookh gayaa, 'my mouth went dry', i.e. from fear or nervousness) is a classic body-as-agent expression, matching how English says 'my mouth went dry' but treating it as something that happens to you rather than something you do.
Common Mistakes: 1) सूखना (sookhnaa) is intransitive, so there is no ने (ne) marking even though it looks like a completed action -- पत्ता सूखा (pattaa sookhaa, 'the leaf dried up'), not पत्ते ने सूखा (patte ne sookhaa).
Sample Sentences:

(baarish n hone se taalaab sookh gayaa।)
