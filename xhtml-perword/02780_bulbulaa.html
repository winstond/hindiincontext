बुलबुला (bulbulaa)

noun A concrete noun for a bubble (soap, water, air), with a common figurative sense for something fleeting or illusory -- like a financial bubble or a short-lived situation.
Sense 1 : bubble (literal, physical)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kids blowing साबुन के बुलबुले (saabun ke bulbule, 'soap bubbles') is a common sight in Indian parks and streets, often from little plastic wands sold by roadside vendors -- so the word has a warm, everyday, childhood association before it ever gets used figuratively.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat बुलबुला (bulbulaa) as invariant like many loanwords, but it is a native masculine noun that inflects regularly in the oblique/plural -- this behavior is covered in the morphology table, not repeated here.

Sense 2 : something fleeting, illusory, or bound to burst (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the set phrase बुलबुला फूटना (bulbulaa phootnaa, 'for the bubble to burst') describing an illusion or inflated situation collapsing.
Synonyms: छलावा (chhalaavaa, illusion/deception, narrower sense), भ्रम (bhram, illusion, broader sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English speakers talk about a 'bubble economy' or living in a 'bubble,' Hindi speakers use बुलबुला (bulbulaa) the same way -- for a relationship, a boom, or a false sense of security that is beautiful but temporary, and everyone already expects the pop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may reach for this word to mean any 'illusion' in the abstract sense (like a hallucination), but बुलबुला (bulbulaa) specifically carries the image of something inflated and fragile that is destined to pop -- closer to English 'bubble' in 'housing bubble' or 'bubble of happiness' than to 'mirage' or 'delusion'.
