जैसा (jaisaa)

adjective A correlative/relative adjective meaning 'like, such as, the kind that' -- it inflects for gender/number and usually partners with वैसा (vaisaa) in जैसा...वैसा (jaisaa...vaisaa, 'as...so') constructions, or stands alone meaning 'as you like' or 'according to.'
Sense 1 : like, similar to, of the kind that (relative/correlative)
adjective Functions as the relative half of the जैसा...वैसा (jaisaa...vaisaa) correlative pair; agrees in gender/number with the noun it describes.
Synonyms: जिस तरह का (jis tarah kaa), जैसे (jaise, adverbial variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic Hindi correlative pattern: जैसा करोगे, वैसा पाओगे (jaisaa karoge, vaisaa paaoge, 'as you sow, so you shall reap') -- Hindi loves pairing a 'which-kind' clause with a 'that-kind' clause rather than using a single relative pronoun like English 'as.' You'll hear elders use this proverb constantly to talk about karma and consequences.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the agreeing form and default to जैसा (jaisaa) even when the noun is feminine or plural, where जैसी (jaisii) or जैसे (jaise) is required. 2) Confusing this with the adverb जैसे (jaise, 'for example, as if') which looks similar but functions differently in a sentence.

Sense 2 : as you wish, according to (in fixed phrases)
adjective Common in the fixed polite phrase जैसी आपकी मर्ज़ी (jaisii aapkii marzii, 'as you wish') and जैसा आप चाहें (jaisaa aap chaahen, 'as you prefer'), used to defer a decision politely.
Synonyms: जैसी आपकी इच्छा (jaisii aapkii ichchhaa, 'as you wish', fixed phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear जैसी आपकी मर्ज़ी (jaisii aapkii marzii) all the time in shops and homes in Delhi -- it's a graceful way to hand a decision back to someone else without seeming indifferent, a small everyday act of politeness.
