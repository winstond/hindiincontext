संपर्क (sampark)

noun A formal-leaning noun meaning 'contact' or 'touch,' used both for physical contact and for staying in touch with people. Also spelled सम्पर्क (sampark) with the anusvara written as म.
Sense 1 : contact, touch (with a person, staying in touch)
noun, masculine Used with संपर्क में रहना (sampark men rahnaa, 'to stay in contact') and संपर्क करना (sampark karnaa, 'to contact/get in touch'), typically taking से (se) for the person contacted: उससे संपर्क करना (usse sampark karnaa, 'to contact him/her').
Synonyms: संबंध (sambandh, relationship/connection), रिश्ता (rishtaa, relationship), मेल-जोल (mel-jol, social contact/mingling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll see on business cards, forms, and office doors as संपर्क करें (sampark karen, 'contact us/please contact'). It sits a notch above everyday बात करना (baat karnaa, 'to talk') in formality, so it feels natural in written or official contexts, government notices, school circulars, job ads, but slightly stiff if used casually about calling a friend.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say संपर्क मिलना (sampark milnaa) when they mean 'to get in touch' -- the natural verb is संपर्क करना (sampark karnaa) or संपर्क में आना (sampark men aanaa). 2) The person you contact is marked with से (se), not को (ko) -- मुझसे संपर्क करें (mujhse sampark karen, 'contact me'), not मुझे संपर्क करें.

Sense 2 : physical contact, touching
noun, masculine Common in cautionary or technical registers, e.g. त्वचा के संपर्क में आना (tvachaa ke sampark men aanaa, 'to come into contact with skin'), used on medicine labels, safety instructions, and in scientific or medical description.
Synonyms: स्पर्श (sparsh, more literary/tactile), छूना (chhoonaa, verb form, 'to touch')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense on medicine strips and product labels: आँखों के संपर्क में आने पर (aankhon ke sampark men aane par, 'if it comes into contact with the eyes'). It reflects the same underlying idea as the social sense, things or people coming into a shared zone, whether that's a phone call or a chemical spill.
