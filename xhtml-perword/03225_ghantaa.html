घंटा (ghantaa)

noun Primarily means 'hour' as a unit of time, and also 'bell' (the large hanging kind, like at a temple); a related sense uses it colloquially to mean 'a whole lot of nothing' or express dismissive sarcasm.
Sense 1 : hour
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily complaints about traffic or waiting: एक घंटा हो गया (ek ghantaa ho gayaa, 'it's been an hour already') is a classic Delhi-NCR grumble, often said while stuck at a signal or waiting for chai to arrive.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that घंटा (ghantaa) needs a number or quantifier before it to mean a specific duration, e.g. दो घंटे (do ghante, 'two hours') -- used alone it defaults to 'an hour' or 'the hour' depending on context. 2) Don't confuse with घड़ी (ghadii), which means 'clock/watch' or a shorter moment in time, not a duration of sixty minutes.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे दफ़्तर पहुँचने में एक घंटा लगता है। It takes me an hour to reach the office.
(mujhe daftar pahunchne men ek ghantaa lagtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : bell (large, hanging)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: घड़ियाल (ghadiyaal, a large gong-like bell)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ringing the घंटा (ghantaa) at a temple entrance before entering is a common devotional gesture across North India -- the sound is believed to announce your arrival to the deity and clear the mind of distractions.
Common Mistakes: 1) घंटा (ghantaa) refers to the big bell you'd see at a temple entrance or on a bell-tower, whereas घंटी (ghantii) is its diminutive -- a small bell, like a doorbell or cycle bell -- learners sometimes use these interchangeably, but native speakers keep them distinct by size and context.

Sense 3 : nothing at all (sarcastic/dismissive)
noun, masculine Used colloquially, often standalone or in a mock-rhetorical question, to dismiss what someone claims or expects: क्या घंटा मिलेगा? (kyaa ghantaa milegaa?, 'what the heck will you get [i.e. nothing]?').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi street-register sass -- घंटा लगा है (ghantaa lagaa hai, roughly 'yeah right, as if') or घंटा मिलेगा (ghantaa milegaa) are the kinds of things you'd hear from friends teasing each other, similar in spirit to saying 'yeah, nothing' with heavy sarcasm in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is distinctly informal/slangy and can sound rude in polite or formal company -- it's the kind of word you'd use with close friends, not with an elder or in a professional setting. 2) Learners sometimes miss that it's used as a stand-in for 'nothing' with a sarcastic edge, not literally about bells or hours.
Sample Sentences:
