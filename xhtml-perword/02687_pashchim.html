पश्चिम (pashchim)

noun/adjective One core meaning: the cardinal direction 'west', used both as a noun and as an adjective modifying direction/location.
Sense 1 : west (the direction)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indians often orient themselves and describe geography using the four cardinal directions -- उत्तर (uttar, north), दक्षिण (dakshin, south), पूर्व (poorv, east), and पश्चिम (pashchim, west) -- more habitually than English speakers do, since traditional house layouts, temple orientations, and even daily weather reports lean heavily on these four terms.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पश्चिम (pashchim) with पश्चिमी (pashchimii) -- the plain noun names the direction itself ('the west'), while the derived adjective describes something as 'western' (e.g. पश्चिमी संस्कृति (pashchimii sanskriti, 'western culture')). 2) Direction words like पश्चिम (pashchim) typically combine with की तरफ़ (kii taraf) or में (men) rather than a simple postposition alone -- पश्चिम की तरफ़ (pashchim kii taraf, 'towards the west') is the natural phrase, not पश्चिम को (pashchim ko).

Sense 2 : western, of/relating to the west
adjective In everyday speech पश्चिमी (pashchimii) is the usual adjective for 'western'; पश्चिम (pashchim) itself appears adjectivally mainly in compound geographic names like पश्चिम बंगाल (pashchim bangaal, 'West Bengal').
Synonyms: पश्चिमी (pashchimii, more common in this adjectival role)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this bare form frozen into official names -- पश्चिम बंगाल (pashchim bangaal, 'West Bengal']) is the standard example -- which is why the pattern feels familiar even though पश्चिमी (pashchimii) does most of the adjectival work elsewhere.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners overuse पश्चिम (pashchim) as a general adjective ('western food', 'western clothes') where native speakers would say पश्चिमी (pashchimii) instead -- पश्चिम (pashchim) as an adjective is largely restricted to proper names and fixed compounds.
