मैदा (maidaa)

noun A single, concrete sense: refined white wheat flour, the base of Indian breads like naan and pastries like samosas.
Sense 1 : refined wheat flour (all-purpose flour)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every North Indian kitchen keeps both आटा (aataa) and मैदा (maidaa) on hand but treats them very differently: आटा (aataa) is the everyday, wholesome flour for home-cooked रोटी (rotii), while मैदा (maidaa) carries a slight 'unhealthy indulgence' connotation, since it shows up in fried and bakery items like कचौरी (kachaurii) and पेस्ट्री (pestrii). You will often hear a mother or dietician warn मैदा मत खाओ (maidaa mat khaao, 'don't eat maida') the way one might warn against too much refined sugar in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often conflate मैदा (maidaa) with आटा (aataa); they are not interchangeable -- आटा (aataa) is the whole-wheat flour used daily for रोटी (rotii), while मैदा (maidaa) is the finely milled, bran-removed flour reserved for breads like नान (naan) and भटूरा (bhatooraa), and for pastries and fried snacks like समोसा (samosaa).
