फ़ेल होना (fel honaa)

verb An English loan-plus-Hindi-verb combination meaning 'to fail' (an exam, a plan, a device, etc.); built from the borrowed adjective/noun फ़ेल (fel, 'fail') plus होना (honaa, 'to become'), following the productive N/Adj + होना (honaa) pattern for intransitive change-of-state.
Sense 1 : to fail (an exam or test)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: अनुत्तीर्ण होना (anuttiirn honaa, formal), असफल होना (asphal honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is classic school-and-college slang in Delhi-NCR -- every student has heard a parent or teacher say मत फ़ेल होना! (mat fel honaa!, 'don't fail!') or बोर्ड में फ़ेल हो गया (bord men fel ho gayaa, 'failed the board exam'), the last phrase carrying real social weight in Indian families where exam results are a big deal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say पास नहीं होना (paas nahiin honaa) when they mean फ़ेल होना (fel honaa); both are heard, but फ़ेल होना (fel honaa) is the more direct, commonly used phrase among students. 2) The subject who fails takes no postposition and is not ने (ne)-marked, since होना (honaa) is intransitive: राम फ़ेल हो गया (raam fel ho gayaa, 'Ram failed'), not राम ने (raam ne).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to fail (a plan, device, system, relationship, etc.)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: नाकाम होना (naakaam honaa), असफल होना (asphal honaa), ख़राब होना (kharaab honaa, for machines specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in casual talk about gadgets and schemes alike -- एसी फ़ेल हो गया (esii fel ho gayaa, 'the AC broke down') in the middle of a Delhi summer is a genuine domestic crisis, and मेरा प्लान फ़ेल हो गया (meraa plaan fel ho gayaa, 'my plan fell through') is equally common when a scheme doesn't pan out.
Common Mistakes: 1) With machines or plans, फ़ेल होना (fel honaa) is used more loosely than in English 'fail' -- इंजन फ़ेल हो गया (injan fel ho gayaa, 'the engine failed') and प्लान फ़ेल हो गया (plaan fel ho gayaa, 'the plan failed') both sound completely natural, whereas an English learner might reach for a more technical word.
