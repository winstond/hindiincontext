साबित करना (saabit karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the Persian/Urdu-derived adjective साबित (saabit, 'proven/established') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'), meaning 'to prove.' It has a related intransitive partner, साबित होना (saabit honaa, 'to be proven/turn out to be'), which is not covered here since it is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to prove, to demonstrate as true
verb, transitive Takes a clause or noun phrase as the thing being proven, often with कि (ki, 'that') introducing a clause: उसने साबित कर दिया कि (usne saabit kar diyaa ki, 'he proved that...').
Synonyms: प्रमाणित करना (pramaanit karnaa, formal), सिद्ध करना (siddh karnaa, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb of courtroom dramas and heated family arguments alike -- तुम कुछ भी साबित नहीं कर सकते (tum kuchh bhii saabit nahiin kar sakte, 'you can't prove anything') is the kind of line that gets thrown around in both contexts. Pair it mentally with its intransitive twin साबित होना (saabit honaa) -- वह चोर साबित हुआ (vah chor saabit huaa, 'he turned out to be a thief') -- since Hindi often prefers letting the fact 'become proven' on its own rather than always naming who proved it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the completive light verb that almost always rides along in real speech: साबित कर देना (saabit kar denaa) or साबित कर दिखाना (saabit kar dikhaanaa) is far more natural in a finished, punchy claim than the bare साबित करना (saabit karnaa) alone. 2) The object of proof is frequently a full कि-clause rather than a simple noun, which trips up learners used to simpler verb-object patterns.
Sample Sentences:
पुलिस को यह साबित करना होगा कि वह वहाँ मौजूद था। The police will have to prove that he was present there.
(pulis ko yah saabit karnaa hogaa ki vah vahaan maujood thaa।)
