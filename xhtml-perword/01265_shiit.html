शीत (shiit)

noun/adjective शीत (shiit) is a somewhat literary/formal word for 'cold', used mostly as a noun for coldness/winter chill and as an adjective meaning 'cold' in compounds and formal registers; everyday speech leans on ठंड (thand) and ठंडा (thandaa) instead.
Sense 1 : coldness, cold (as a condition/season)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ठंड (thand), सर्दी (sardii), जाड़ा (jaadaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will meet शीत (shiit) most often in the news phrase शीत लहर (shiit lahar, 'cold wave') during North Indian winters, or in the compound शीत युद्ध (shiit yuddh, 'Cold War'). In casual chat about feeling cold, though, people say मुझे सर्दी लग रही है (mujhe sardii lag rahii hai, 'I'm feeling cold') rather than reaching for शीत (shiit).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शीत (shiit) in ordinary conversation about weather, but native speakers almost always say ठंड (thand) or सर्दी (sardii) day-to-day; शीत (shiit) shows up more in written, formal, or compound contexts like शीत लहर (shiit lahar, 'cold wave').

Sense 2 : cold (in temperament or temperature, formal/literary)
adjective
Synonyms: ठंडा (thandaa), शीतल (shiital, softer/literary), सर्द (sard, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: As a plain adjective for 'cold water' or 'cold weather' in speech, ठंडा (thandaa) is what you will actually hear; शीत (shiit) as an adjective survives mainly inside fixed compounds like शीत ऋतु (shiit ritu, 'winter season') rather than as a freely used descriptive word.
