लदना (ladnaa)

verb A physical verb meaning 'to be loaded' -- describing goods, luggage, or weight being piled onto a vehicle, animal, or person; also used figuratively for being 'laden' with something (work, debt, guilt). Related to the transitive लादना (laadnaa, 'to load'), which is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to be loaded, be piled on (goods, luggage)
verb, intransitive Takes पर (par) for the vehicle/surface: सामान ट्रक पर लदा (saamaan trak par ladaa, 'the goods were loaded onto the truck').
Synonyms: भरना (bharnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around Delhi's mandis and on the highway: trucks piled absurdly high with सब्ज़ी (sabzii, 'vegetables') or गन्ना (gannaa, 'sugarcane') are described as लदा हुआ (ladaa huaa, 'all loaded up'), often with a tone of amazement at how much weight was stacked on.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners reach for the transitive लादना (laadnaa) when the subject is the goods themselves rather than the person doing the loading -- सामान लदा (saamaan ladaa, 'the goods got loaded') is the natural phrasing, not a passive construction with लादना (laadnaa).

Sense 2 : to be laden, burdened (figurative, with weight/debt/emotion)
verb, intransitive Common in the participial form लदा हुआ (ladaa huaa) used adjectivally: कर्ज़ में लदा हुआ (karz men ladaa huaa, 'laden with debt').
Synonyms: दबना (dabnaa, burdened, of debt/pressure)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just as a truck gets लदा हुआ (ladaa huaa) with sacks of grain, a person can be described the same way when weighed down by कर्ज़ (karz, 'debt') or ज़िम्मेदारियाँ (zimmedaariyaan, 'responsibilities') -- the image of physical overloading carries straight over into emotional and financial burden.
