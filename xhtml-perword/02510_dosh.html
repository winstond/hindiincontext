दोष (dosh)

noun A noun covering fault, flaw, or blame -- used both for a physical/character defect and for moral or legal culpability.
Sense 1 : fault, blame (moral/legal culpability)
noun, masculine Commonly used in the pattern X पर दोष लगाना/देना (X par dosh lagaanaa/denaa, 'to blame X') or दोष लगना (dosh lagnaa, intransitive, 'blame to fall on someone').
Synonyms: गलती (galtii), कमी (kamii, sometimes overlaps), कसूर (kasoor)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in everyday moral reasoning -- a mother might say किसी और को दोष मत दो (kisii aur ko dosh mat do, 'don't blame someone else') -- and also in more classical or philosophical registers, where दोष (dosh) can mean a deeper character flaw, as in the phrase दोष निकालना (dosh nikaalnaa, 'to find fault').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गलती (galtii) in every context, but दोष (dosh) carries a slightly more formal, moral-weight tone -- closer to 'blame/culpability' than a simple 'mistake.' 2) The verb pairing matters: दोष देना (dosh denaa, 'to blame someone,' transitive) versus दोष लगना (dosh lagnaa, 'blame to attach/fall on,' intransitive) -- mixing these up is a common slip.

Sense 2 : defect, flaw (physical or inherent)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खराबी (kharaabii), नुक्स (nuks, colloquial), ऐब (aib)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in traditional contexts too -- in Ayurveda, वात-पित्त-कफ दोष (vaat-pitt-kaph dosh) refers to the three bodily humors, and older relatives may still talk about health in these terms.
