डंक (dank)

noun Primarily the physical sting or stinger of an insect/scorpion (dank); also used figuratively for a sharp, hurtful remark or the 'sting' of an accusation.
Sense 1 : sting/stinger (insect, scorpion)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Bichhoo ka dank (bichchhoo kaa dank, 'scorpion's sting') is a common phrase invoked whenever someone describes a sudden, sharp pain -- monsoon season in Delhi brings out plenty of scorpion and wasp stories from grandmothers warning kids to check their shoes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse डंक (dank) with काटना (kaatnaa, 'to bite'); a bee or scorpion is usually said to 'strike' its sting -- डंक मारना (dank maarnaa) -- rather than 'bite,' even though English speakers default to 'sting/bite' interchangeably.

बिच्छू का डंक बहुत दर्दनाक होता है। A scorpion's sting is very painful.
(bichchhoo kaa dank bahut dardnaak hotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative sting (of words, criticism, betrayal)
noun, masculine Almost always appears with मारना (maarnaa) or लगना (lagnaa) in this figurative sense, e.g. उसकी बात का डंक (uskii baat kaa dank, 'the sting of his remark').
Synonyms: चोट (chot, 'hurt/wound', broader sense), कटाक्ष (kataaksh, 'sarcastic barb')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension is very much alive in everyday speech -- a sharp taunt from a relative at a family gathering might be described as उसकी बात का डंक अभी तक चुभ रहा है (uskii baat kaa dank abhii tak chubh rahaa hai, 'the sting of his words is still smarting'), linking the emotional pain directly back to the physical image of an insect's sting.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate 'that comment stung me' too literally with काटना (kaatnaa) instead of reaching for the idiomatic डंक मारना (dank maarnaa) or डंक लगना (dank lagnaa), which is what a native speaker would actually say.
