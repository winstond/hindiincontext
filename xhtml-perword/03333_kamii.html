कमी (kamii)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'shortage, lack, deficiency' -- covers both quantity (not enough of something) and quality (a flaw or shortcoming in someone/something).
Sense 1 : shortage, lack, deficiency (of something needed)
noun, feminine Commonly used with होना (honaa) in the pattern X की कमी होना (X kii kamii honaa), 'there to be a lack of X', and with पूरी करना (poorii karnaa) meaning 'to make up/fulfill' a shortfall.
Synonyms: अभाव (abhaav), कमज़ोरी (kamzorii, leans toward 'weakness' rather than 'shortage')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday complaints: पानी की कमी (paanii kii kamii, 'water shortage'), समय की कमी (samay kii kamii, 'lack of time'), पैसों की कमी (paison kii kamii, 'shortage of money'). It's the go-to word for 'not enough' in a way that feels less abstract than अभाव (abhaav), which leans slightly more formal/written.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say पानी नहीं है (paanii nahiin hai) when they mean there's a shortage rather than a total absence -- पानी की कमी है (paanii kii kamii hai, 'there's a water shortage') is the more natural way to express a partial or ongoing lack. 2) The thing lacking is marked with की/का/के (kii/kaa/ke) before कमी (kamii), not with को (ko) -- पैसों की कमी (paison kii kamii, 'shortage of money'), not पैसों को कमी.

Sense 2 : a flaw, shortcoming, or defect (in a person or thing)
noun, feminine Used with निकालना (nikaalnaa) in the common phrase कमी निकालना (kamii nikaalnaa, 'to find fault/pick out flaws'), and with होना (honaa) to say someone/something has a shortcoming.
Synonyms: खामी (khaamii), दोष (dosh, leans more formal/moral)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense turns up in self-reflection and relationship talk: मुझमें क्या कमी है? (mujhmen kyaa kamii hai?, 'what flaw do I have?') is a classic line of hurt or self-doubt, often heard in Hindi film dialogue and everyday arguments alike -- it's the natural way to ask 'what's wrong with me' when comparing yourself to someone else.
Common Mistakes: 1) कमी निकालना (kamii nikaalnaa) specifically means 'to nitpick/find fault,' often with a critical or nagging tone -- it's stronger than neutrally 'noticing' something is wrong. 2) Don't confuse this sense with the 'shortage' sense: मुझमें कमी है (mujhmen kamii hai) means 'I have a flaw/shortcoming,' not 'I am in short supply.'
