पकाना (pakaanaa)

verb The main sense is 'to cook' (to prepare food by applying heat), transitive and causative in feel since it is the causative of पकना (paknaa, 'to ripen/cook [on its own]'). A secondary, informal figurative sense means 'to bore someone senseless' by talking on and on.
Sense 1 : to cook food
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa, also means 'to make/prepare food')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पकाना (pakaanaa) is the causative partner of पकना (paknaa) ('to ripen, to cook by itself') -- the same root pattern you see in पीना/पिलाना (piinaa/pilaanaa) or सोना/सुलाना (sonaa/sulaanaa): someone causes the base action to happen. In a Delhi kitchen you will hear खाना पका लो (khaanaa pakaa lo, 'go cook some food') constantly -- it is the default verb for the whole act of cooking a meal, not just one technique.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use पकाना (pakaanaa) and बनाना (banaanaa) as if fully interchangeable; पकाना (pakaanaa) specifically evokes the heat/cooking process itself (boiling, simmering, roasting), while बनाना (banaanaa) is the more general 'to prepare/make' a dish, used even for things that involve no fire, like a sandwich or salad. 2) This is a transitive verb taking ने (ne) in the perfective (मैंने खाना पकाया, mainne khaanaa pakaayaa, 'I cooked food') -- don't drop the ने (ne) by analogy with intransitive पकना (paknaa).

Sense 2 : to bore someone with endless talk (figurative)
verb, transitive Used almost always as दिमाग पकाना (dimaagh pakaanaa, 'to cook someone's brain') or सिर पकाना (sir pakaanaa, 'to cook someone's head') -- the object is the listener's mind/head, marked with को (ko), e.g. मेरा दिमाग़ मत पकाओ (meraa dimaagh mat pakaao, 'don't fry my brain / don't go on at me').
Synonyms: बोर करना (bor karnaa, English loan, informal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is one of the most common bits of Delhi youth slang: दिमाग़ मत पका (dimaagh mat pakaa, 'don't fry my brain') is what you say to a friend who won't stop nagging or over-explaining something. It plays on the same 'cooking' image as the literal sense -- someone's endless talking is metaphorically 'cooking' your brain the way heat slowly cooks food.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is colloquial and almost always appears fixed with दिमाग़ (dimaagh) or सिर (sir) as the object -- पकाना (pakaanaa) alone, without one of these, will usually just be understood as literal cooking, not as 'annoying.' 2) It is mildly irritated/informal in tone -- not appropriate in formal writing or with strangers/elders in a respectful register.
