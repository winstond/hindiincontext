सूर्ख़ी (soorkhii)

noun Covers two related senses: a newspaper headline, and (less commonly in learner conversation) redness/rouge on the cheeks or lips.
Sense 1 : newspaper headline
noun, feminine
Synonyms: शीर्षक (shiirshak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly on Hindi news channels and in papers like Dainik Jagran or Hindustan -- सुर्खियां (surkhiyaan, 'headlines') is the go-to word for 'the top stories of the day.' The idiom सुर्खियों में रहना (surkhiyon men rahnaa, 'to stay in the headlines') is very commonly used for celebrities and politicians.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse सूर्ख़ी (soorkhii) with शीर्षक (shiirshak); शीर्षक (shiirshak) is the broader word for 'title/heading' of any text, while सूर्ख़ी (soorkhii) is used specifically for news headlines, especially in the phrase सुर्खियों में आना (surkhiyon men aanaa, 'to come into the headlines').

Sense 2 : redness, rouge (on cheeks or lips)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: लाली (laalii)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older, more poetic sense -- the natural red glow of cheeks or lips -- shows up in Urdu-influenced Hindi poetry and songs, describing a blush or the redness of paan-stained lips. It shares its root with the Persian-derived सुर्ख़ (surkh, 'red'), the same root behind the headline sense (a headline is, in a way, the 'red' or bold-highlighted part of a page).
