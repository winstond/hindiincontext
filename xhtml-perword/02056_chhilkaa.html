छिलका (chhilkaa)

noun छिलका (chhilkaa) is the outer skin or peel of a fruit, vegetable, or grain -- the everyday word you reach for when you're peeling an aloo (aaloo) or a kelaa (kelaa).
Sense 1 : peel, skin, husk (of fruit/vegetable/grain)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खोल (khol, shell/husk of nuts or grains), त्वचा (tvachaa, skin, more anatomical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Any Indian kitchen conversation about cooking sabzi (sabzii) involves this word constantly -- mothers telling children आलू का छिलका उतारो (aaloo kaa chhilkaa utaaro, 'peel the potato') is a classic kitchen instruction. Note the related verb छीलना (chhiilnaa, 'to peel'), which is the action that produces a छिलका (chhilkaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for त्वचा (tvachaa) when talking about fruit skin, but त्वचा (tvachaa) is mainly used for human/animal skin -- छिलका (chhilkaa) is the natural word for a fruit or vegetable's outer covering. 2) The common verb pairing is छिलका उतारना (chhilkaa utaarnaa, to peel off the skin) or छिलका निकालना (chhilkaa nikaalnaa) -- not a literal translation of "to peel" using छीलना (chhiilnaa) alone with छिलका (chhilkaa) as object in every context; both patterns exist but idiomatic speech favors उतारना (utaarnaa) here.

Sense 2 : shell (of egg, nut)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खोल (khol)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The same word extends naturally from soft fruit peel to harder coverings like an egg's shell -- अंडे का छिलका (ande kaa chhilkaa, 'eggshell') is the standard phrase; Hindi doesn't reach for a separate word the way English distinguishes 'peel' from 'shell'.
