गले लगाना (gale lagaanaa)

verb A compound expression built on गला (galaa, 'throat/neck') plus लगाना (lagaanaa, 'to attach'), literally 'to attach to the neck' -- used both for the physical act of embracing and, figuratively, for accepting or welcoming an idea, change, or person.
Sense 1 : to hug, to embrace (physically)
verb, transitive The person embraced is marked with को (ko): उसे गले लगाया (use gale lagaayaa, 'hugged him/her').
Synonyms: आलिंगन करना (aalingan karnaa, formal/literary), भेंटना (bhentnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the standard, warm way to describe hugging in Hindi -- at airports, weddings, and family reunions across Delhi-NCR, you will hear relatives say आओ, गले लगो (aao, gale lago, 'come, hug me') rather than any word borrowed from English. It carries real emotional weight, especially between family members meeting after a long time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use से (se) for the person being hugged; the correct marking is को (ko), e.g. दोस्त को गले लगाया (dost ko gale lagaayaa, 'hugged the friend'). 2) The verb is गले लगाना (gale lagaanaa) as a unit -- learners sometimes drop लगाना (lagaanaa) and just say गला (galaa) plus another verb, which does not work.

(jab vah eyarport par aaii, to mainne use gale lagaa liyaa।)

Sense 2 : to embrace, accept, or welcome (an idea, change, responsibility, or person figuratively)
verb, transitive Used with abstract objects marked by को (ko) or implied by context: बदलाव को गले लगाना (badlaav ko gale lagaanaa, 'to embrace change').
Synonyms: अपनाना (apnaanaa), स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will often hear this in motivational or inspirational Hindi speeches -- अपनी गलतियों को गले लगाओ (apnii galtiyon ko gale lagaao, 'embrace your mistakes') -- borrowing the tenderness of a physical hug to describe accepting something difficult.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to अपनाना (apnaanaa) for 'embrace change' and don't realize गले लगाना (gale lagaanaa) works just as naturally and sounds more heartfelt in speeches or personal reflection. 2) This figurative sense is less used in casual daily chat than in speeches, self-help talk, or motivational writing -- overusing it in ordinary conversation can sound slightly dramatic.
