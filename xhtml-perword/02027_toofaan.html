तूफ़ान (toofaan)

noun Primarily a weather-word meaning 'storm,' with a very common figurative extension to any overwhelming uproar, chaos, or intense emotional outburst.
Sense 1 : storm, cyclone
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आँधी (aandhii, windstorm/dust storm), चक्रवात (chakrvaat, formal/technical term for cyclone)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, summer often brings a sudden आँधी (aandhii) with dust and wind, while तूफ़ान (toofaan) is the word you hear on the news for named cyclones hitting the coasts. It's a good example of how Hindi weather vocabulary maps onto real seasonal experience rather than being purely abstract.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use तूफ़ान (toofaan) for any rain, but it specifically implies violent wind and destructive force, not just rainfall -- for ordinary rain, बारिश (baarish) is the word. 2) आँधी (aandhii) is the more common word for the dust/windstorms typical of North Indian summers, while तूफ़ान (toofaan) leans toward larger-scale storms and cyclones, especially in news contexts.

Sense 2 : figurative: uproar, upheaval, intense commotion
noun, masculine Frequently appears as तूफ़ान आना (toofaan aanaa, 'a storm to come/arise') or तूफ़ान मचाना (toofaan machaanaa, 'to create an uproar'), treating emotional or social chaos as a weather event that arrives or is stirred up.
Synonyms: हंगामा (hangaamaa, commotion/ruckus), उथल-पुथल (uthal-puthal, upheaval, turmoil)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in everyday drama: a mother might say ज़रा सी बात पर तूफ़ान मत खड़ा करो (zaraa sii baat par toofaan mat khadaa karo, 'don't kick up a storm over such a small thing'). It's the same instinct as English 'storm in a teacup,' extended to family arguments, office politics, or heartbreak alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) मचाना (machaanaa) pairs specifically with तूफ़ान (toofaan) in this figurative sense ('to kick up a storm'); learners sometimes reach for करना (karnaa) instead, which sounds off here. 2) This figurative sense can describe a person's inner turmoil (दिल में तूफ़ान, dil men toofaan, 'a storm in the heart') as easily as external chaos -- learners often only associate it with external events.
