हो जाना (ho jaanaa)

verb A compound verb built from होना (honaa, 'to be/become') plus जाना (jaanaa) as a completive helper; it marks a change of state as having happened, often on its own. The core sense is 'to become/happen/turn out', with common idiomatic extensions like getting done, getting late, or falling in love.
Sense 1 : to become, to turn into a new state
verb, intransitive Takes the new state as a predicate complement (adjective or noun), not a direct object: वह बड़ा हो गया (vah badaa ho gayaa, 'he became/grew up big').
Synonyms: बन जाना (ban jaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb Hindi reaches for when something 'happens to' you rather than something you actively do -- ठंड हो गई (thand ho gaii, 'it got cold') or वह बीमार हो गया (vah biimaar ho gayaa, 'he got sick') both frame the change as arriving on its own, which fits the language's general comfort with events happening to a subject rather than being driven by it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बनना (bannaa) here, but हो जाना (ho jaanaa) is the default choice for a change of state that happens to someone/something, while बनना (bannaa) leans toward deliberate transformation or profession (जैसे डॉक्टर बनना (doktar bannaa, 'to become a doctor')). 2) The subject of this sense is never ने-marked, since जाना (jaanaa) here is intransitive and the whole compound behaves intransitively.

Sense 2 : to happen, to occur, to take place

Sense 3 : to get finished, to be completed/done
verb, intransitive Common with काम (kaam, 'work'), खाना (khaanaa, 'food/meal'), and similar nouns: काम हो गया (kaam ho gayaa, 'the work got done').
Synonyms: पूरा होना (pooraa honaa), खत्म होना (khatm honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Asking बन गया? (ban gayaa?) or हो गया? (ho gayaa?) about food or a task is an everyday check-in, roughly 'is it ready/done yet?' -- heard constantly in kitchens and offices alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use खत्म होना (khatm honaa) and हो जाना (ho jaanaa) interchangeably, but हो जाना (ho jaanaa) more often implies a task reaching successful completion, while खत्म होना (khatm honaa) can carry a neutral or even negative 'used up, over' sense.

Sense 4 : to fall in love (with someone), idiomatic with प्यार
verb, intransitive Fixed frame: X को Y से प्यार हो गया (X ko Y se pyaar ho gayaa, 'X fell in love with Y') -- the person falling in love is को-marked, not the subject of हो जाना (ho jaanaa) itself.
Synonyms: प्यार में पड़ना (pyaar men padnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a favorite Bollywood-song construction because it captures the Hindi idea that love is not chosen but happens involuntarily -- मुझे तुमसे प्यार हो गया (mujhe tumse pyaar ho gayaa, 'I fell in love with you') literally says 'to me, love happened from you.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person who falls in love the grammatical subject (ने-marked or plain nominative), but Hindi frames love as something that happens *to* the दिल (dil, 'heart') or the person, so को-marking on the experiencer is essential: उसे मुझसे प्यार हो गया (use mujhse pyaar ho gayaa, 'she fell in love with me'), not वह ... (vah).
Sample Sentences:
मुझे तुमसे प्यार हो गया। I fell in love with you.

Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Sense 5 : to become late, said of time
verb, intransitive Frame: (किसी को) देर हो गई (der ho gaii, '(someone) became late'); the person affected, if named, is को-marked.
Synonyms: देर होना (der honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मुझे देर हो गई, माफ़ करना! (mujhe der ho gaii, maaf karnaa!, 'I'm late, sorry!') is one of the most-heard apologetic openers in daily Delhi life, from auto-rickshaw delays to college lectures.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget the को-marked experiencer and just say मैं देर हो गया (main der ho gayaa), but the correct pattern is मुझे देर हो गई (mujhe der ho gaii, 'I got/became late') -- देर (der) is feminine and is what 'becomes', not the person.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे देर हो गई, माफ़ करना! I'm late, sorry!
(mujhe der ho gaii, maaf karnaa!)
