मरीज़ (mariiz)

noun A single core meaning: 'patient' (the sick person, not the character trait). Borrowed from Persian/Arabic and extremely common in everyday and hospital contexts.
Sense 1 : patient (a person under medical care)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रोगी (rogii, more formal/clinical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly outside any clinic or hospital in Delhi -- डॉक्टर साहब, मरीज़ बहुत तकलीफ़ में है (doktar saahab, mariiz bahut takliif men hai, 'Doctor sahib, the patient is in a lot of pain') is a classic anxious-family-member line. It also gets used jokingly for someone who is obsessively into something, like दिल का मरीज़ (dil kaa mariiz, 'heart patient') for someone always falling in love.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse मरीज़ (mariiz) with बीमार (biimaar) -- मरीज़ (mariiz) is the noun 'patient,' while बीमार (biimaar) is the adjective 'sick/ill'; you can say कोई बीमार आदमी (koii biimaar aadmii, 'some sick man') but the person as a hospital case is मरीज़ (mariiz). 2) Despite ending in a consonant with no obvious feminine marker, मरीज़ (mariiz) is grammatically masculine even when referring to a woman in casual speech, though some speakers do treat it as m/f depending on agreement with the referent.
Sample Sentences:

(doktar saahab, mariiz bahut takliif men hai।)

Sense 2 : someone obsessed/afflicted with something (figurative)
noun, masculine Used in the pattern X का मरीज़ (X kaa mariiz) where X is the thing someone is obsessed with or afflicted by, e.g. इश्क़ का मरीज़ (ishq kaa mariiz, 'a patient of love,' i.e. lovesick).
Synonyms: आशिक़ (aashiq, narrower, romantic sense only)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension is very alive in Bollywood-flavored banter -- calling a friend इश्क़ का मरीज़ (ishq kaa mariiz) or मोहब्बत का मरीज़ (mohabbat kaa mariiz) teasingly means they're hopelessly in love, sick with it the way one is sick with a fever.
