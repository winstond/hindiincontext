मजबूर (majboor)

adjective An adjective meaning 'compelled' or 'helpless to do otherwise,' almost always used with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa) to describe being forced into a situation. Also spelled मज़बूर (mazboor) with the nuqta.
Sense 1 : compelled, forced, having no choice
adjective Typically appears in the frame X मजबूर है (X majboor hai) or with a following infinitive plus होना, as in ...करने के लिए मजबूर होना (...karne ke lie majboor honaa, 'to be compelled to do...').
Synonyms: विवश (vivash, more formal/literary), लाचार (laachaar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries real emotional weight in Hindi -- it's the language of Bollywood ballads and heartfelt confessions, describing being trapped by circumstance rather than simply 'having to' do something. It pairs naturally with लाचार (laachaar), and together the two words paint a picture of helplessness that's a favorite theme in film dialogue and poetry.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मजबूर करना (majboor karnaa) when they mean 'to force someone,' forgetting it needs the person as object plus a following infinitive with के लिए (ke lie): उसने मुझे जाने के लिए मजबूर किया (usne mujhe jaane ke lie majboor kiyaa, 'he forced me to go'). 2) Confusing मजबूर (majboor) with मशहूर (mashahoor, 'famous') due to similar sound.
