दंड (dand)

noun Mainly means 'punishment/penalty' in formal, legal, and moral contexts; also used for a 'stick/rod' and, extended from that, the traditional Indian exercise of push-ups (dands). Also spelled दण्ड (dand) with the conjunct nasal.
Sense 1 : punishment, penalty
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सज़ा (sazaa), जुर्माना (jurmaanaa, specifically a fine), शासन (shaasan, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word on official signage like अपराध और दंड (apraadh aur dand, 'crime and punishment') or in a teacher's lecture about karma and consequence -- it carries a slightly weightier, almost moralistic tone compared to the everyday सज़ा (sazaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सज़ा (sazaa) by default; दंड (dand) is the more formal/legal register word, common in written notices, court contexts, and moral or religious discourse (e.g. describing consequences of sin) rather than everyday speech about scoldings.

(apraadhii ko uske apraadh ke lie kadaa dand diyaa gayaa।)

Sense 2 : a push-up (traditional Indian exercise)
noun, masculine Usually appears in the plural as दंड लगाना (dand lagaanaa, 'to do push-ups'), paired with बैठक (baithak, squats) in akhara/gym culture.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This comes straight from traditional Indian wrestling (kushti) and akhara culture, where दंड-बैठक (dand-baithak, 'push-ups and squats') was the classic daily fitness routine long before gyms became common in Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not confuse this with the punishment sense just because both come from the same word -- in old-school akharas (wrestling gyms), doing दंड (dand) was itself sometimes assigned as a form of दंड (dand) in the punishment sense, which is a genuine wordplay native speakers enjoy pointing out.

Sense 3 : staff, rod, stick (esp. of authority or ritual)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: छड़ी (chhadii), लाठी (laathii)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This older sense survives in compound words and titles -- for instance दंडवत (dandvat) describes prostrating flat like a rod/staff before someone, a gesture of deep respect still seen at temples and touching elders' feet in a very formal way.
