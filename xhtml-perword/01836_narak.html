नरक (narak)

noun Means 'hell' both in the literal mythological/religious sense and in the common figurative sense of a miserable, unbearable place or situation.
Sense 1 : hell (as a place of misery, figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जहन्नुम (jahannum, colloquial/Urdu-flavored)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Delhi folks reach for after a brutal commute or a chaotic wedding season: दिल्ली की सड़कें नरक हैं (dillii kii sadken narak hain, 'Delhi's roads are hell'). It carries real emotional punch, so it is used more sparingly than the English 'hell' is in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this word only in jokes about traffic or offices, but it works equally well for describing any genuinely miserable stretch of life, e.g. a bad relationship or a tough exam season. 2) Used as a predicate, it usually needs है/था etc., or appears in set exclamations like नरक हो गया (narak ho gayaa, 'it became hell') rather than as a standalone noun.

(dillii kii sadken narak hain।)

Sense 2 : hell (in Hindu/religious cosmology)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: यमलोक (yamlok, devotional)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: In Hindu cosmology नरक (narak) is where souls face the consequences of bad karma before eventually being reborn, contrasted with स्वर्ग (svarg, 'heaven'). This sense shows up in mythology, storytelling by grandparents, and religious discourse rather than everyday chatter.
