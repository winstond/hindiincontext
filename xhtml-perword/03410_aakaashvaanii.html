आकाशवाणी (aakaashvaanii)

noun A compound of आकाश (aakaash, 'sky') and वाणी (vaanii, 'voice/speech'), meaning literally 'voice from the sky.' It has two everyday senses: the mythological/divine 'voice from heaven' and, more commonly today, the name of India's state-run radio broadcaster.
Sense 1 : All India Radio (the state broadcaster)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ऑल इंडिया रेडियो (ol indiyaa rediyo, common abbreviation-style usage), प्रसार भारती (prasaar bhaartii, the current corporate name, not fully interchangeable)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: For decades this was the only radio service in India, so for older generations the name itself is synonymous with the crackly voice of the morning news, cricket commentary, and film songs on the family radio set. The name was chosen deliberately for its poetic, almost divine resonance, tying the modern broadcaster back to the older mythological sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume this is a generic word for 'radio' -- it isn't; रेडियो (rediyo) is the everyday word for the device or medium, while आकाशवाणी (aakaashvaanii) specifically names the government broadcasting service. 2) It is often heard in the fixed announcer's phrase यह आकाशवाणी है (yah aakaashvaanii hai, 'This is All India Radio'), which learners may not recognize as a set formula rather than an ordinary sentence.

Sense 2 : a voice from the heavens (divine/prophetic utterance)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दिव्य वाणी (divy vaanii, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This is the older, mythological sense: in stories and scripture, an आकाशवाणी (aakaashvaanii) is an unseen, bodiless voice that speaks from the sky to announce fate or divine will, as happens at Krishna's birth in the Mahabharata tradition. A learner will mostly meet this sense in religious storytelling or references to mythology rather than daily conversation.
