ज्वाला (jvaalaa)

noun A literary/formal word for a blazing flame or fire, used mostly in elevated speech, poetry, and set phrases rather than everyday kitchen talk.
Sense 1 : flame, blaze
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आग (aag), लपट (lapat), अग्नि (agni, literary), शोला (sholaa)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: ज्वाला (jvaalaa) is the word behind ज्वालामुखी (jvaalaamukhii, 'volcano', literally 'flame-mouth'), and it shows up in the name of the goddess Jwala Devi, worshipped at a famous shrine in Himachal Pradesh where natural gas flames burn continuously -- a nice image to anchor the word's intensity and sacredness.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ज्वाला (jvaalaa) in ordinary sentences like 'light the stove,' where आग (aag) is what everyone actually says; save ज्वाला (jvaalaa) for dramatic, written, or figurative contexts. 2) It is feminine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. ज्वाला भड़क उठी (jvaalaa bhadak uthii, 'the flame flared up').
Sample Sentences:
पहाड़ी से ज्वाला की लपटें ऊपर उठ रही थीं। Flames were rising up from the hillside.
(pahaadii se jvaalaa kii lapten oopar uth rahii thiin।)

Sense 2 : intense passion, fury (figurative)
noun, feminine Used in phrases like क्रोध की ज्वाला (krodh kii jvaalaa, 'the flame of anger') to describe an emotion as a fire burning inside someone.
Synonyms: आग (aag, figurative), आवेश (aavesh), जोश (josh)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: Hindi loves describing strong feelings as fire -- क्रोध की ज्वाला (krodh kii jvaalaa) or बदले की ज्वाला (badle kii jvaalaa, 'the flame of revenge') are common in speeches, film dialogue, and devotional poetry, painting emotion as something that consumes a person from within.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost always appears with a genitive noun before it (X की ज्वाला (kii jvaalaa)) -- using it bare to mean 'anger' or 'passion' sounds incomplete to a native speaker.
