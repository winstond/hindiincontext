कब (kab)

adverb An interrogative adverb meaning 'when', used to ask about time; also appears in a few fixed idiomatic turns of phrase expressing surprise or rhetorical emphasis.
Sense 1 : when (question word)
adverb
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first question words learners meet, right alongside क्या (kyaa, 'what') and कहाँ (kahaan, 'where'). Notice the family resemblance: कब (kab, 'when'), कहाँ (kahaan, 'where'), कैसे (kaise, 'how'), कौन (kaun, 'who') -- most Hindi question words share that initial क- (k) sound, which is a handy memory hook.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place कब (kab) at the end of the sentence as in English word order ('You are coming when?'); in Hindi it goes right before the verb phrase, e.g. तुम कब आओगे (tum kab aaoge, 'when will you come'). 2) Do not confuse with कभी (kabhii, 'ever/sometime'), which is not a question word -- कब (kab) always asks or marks a direct/indirect question, while कभी (kabhii) refers to an indefinite time.

Sense 2 : rhetorical 'never' (implying the opposite of when/ever)
adverb Used in a rhetorical question to assert the opposite -- मैंने कब कहा? (mainne kab kahaa?, 'when did I say that?') implies 'I never said that.'
Synonyms: कभी नहीं (kabhii nahiin, 'never')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Delhi speakers love this rhetorical jab -- it lets you deny something with a bit of attitude instead of a flat नहीं (nahiin, 'no'). You will hear it a lot in arguments between friends or siblings, often with a raised eyebrow and a stretched-out tone on कब (kab).
Common Mistakes: 1) This use can trip up learners because it looks like a genuine question but is actually a strong denial -- मैं कब मानता हूँ! (main kab maantaa hoon!, 'when do I ever agree!') means 'I never agree', not a real question expecting a time as an answer.
