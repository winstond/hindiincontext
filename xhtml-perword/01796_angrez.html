अँग्रेज़ (angrez)

noun/adjective A person of English or British nationality/ethnicity; also used adjectivally to mean 'English/British' in a nationality sense. Often seen spelled without the chandrabindu as अंग्रेज़ (angrez).
Sense 1 : an English or British person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: गोरा (goraa, colloquial, refers to white foreigners generally)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries real historical weight in Delhi and North India generally, since it's the standard term for the British colonizers in phrases like अँग्रेज़ों का ज़माना (angrezon kaa zamaanaa, 'the era of the British/colonial times'). Older relatives will often use it with a mix of resentment and grudging respect when talking about colonial-era discipline, trains, or bureaucracy.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use अँग्रेज़ (angrez) loosely for any white foreigner, but native speakers reserve it specifically for English/British people (or, historically, colonial-era British rulers); other Europeans and Americans are usually called by their own nationality or the informal umbrella term गोरा (goraa). 2) The word is variable for gender in the plural/oblique but the singular masculine form is used generically; don't assume it needs feminine marking for a woman -- अँग्रेज़ औरत (angrez aurat, 'English woman') is the natural phrasing, not a separate feminine noun form.

Sense 2 : English/British (nationality), or by extension 'Western' in manner
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. अँग्रेज़ी हुकूमत (angrezii hukoomat, 'British rule') -- note that in this adjectival function the related form अँग्रेज़ी (angrezii) is actually far more common than अँग्रेज़ (angrez) itself; अँग्रेज़ used adjectivally tends to modify people directly, as in अँग्रेज़ लोग (angrez log, 'English people').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear अँग्रेज़ी ढंग (angrezii dhang, 'the English/Western way') used to describe anything seen as foreign-influenced, from eating habits to clothing -- a mild cultural shorthand still common among older Delhi families distinguishing 'desi' from 'Western' ways of doing things.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse this with अँग्रेज़ी (angrezii), which is the far more frequent word for 'English' as applied to the language, culture, or abstract nouns (अँग्रेज़ी भाषा (angrezii bhaashaa, 'the English language')); अँग्रेज़ in the adjectival sense is mostly limited to describing people or rule directly.
