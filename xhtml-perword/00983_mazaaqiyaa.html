मज़ाक़िया (mazaaqiyaa)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'funny' or 'humorous', describing a person's nature or a remark. Related to मज़ाक़ (mazaaq, 'joke'), the noun from which it is derived.
Sense 1 : funny, humorous, joking (of a person or their manner)
adjective
Synonyms: हंसोड़ (hansod, of a person), विनोदी (vinodii, formal), मस्तमौला (mastmaulaa, carefree and jovial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is built right on मज़ाक़ (mazaaq, 'joke, fun'), so if you know that noun, you already have this adjective for free -- just add the -इया (iyaa) suffix that turns nouns into 'characterized by' adjectives, similar to how नुक़सान (nuqsaan) becomes नुक़सानदायक (nuqsaandaayak). In everyday Delhi speech, calling someone मज़ाक़िया (mazaaqiyaa) is a genuine compliment -- it marks them as fun to be around, the friend who keeps the group laughing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मज़ाक़िया (mazaaqiyaa) to describe a situation or event as 'funny' the way English does ('a funny movie'), but it more naturally describes a person's temperament or way of talking -- वह बहुत मज़ाक़िया है (vah bahut mazaaqiyaa hai, 'he is very funny/jokey'). For a funny film or joke itself, हंसाने वाला (hansaane vaalaa) or मज़ेदार (mazedaar) fit better. 2) Don't confuse with मज़ाकिया tone in English 'sarcastic' -- मज़ाक़िया (mazaaqiyaa) is warmer and implies playfulness, not mockery.
