कोठी (kothii)

noun Primarily means a large house or bungalow, especially one belonging to someone wealthy; also has an older commercial sense referring to a trading firm or bank/exchange house.
Sense 1 : large house, mansion, bungalow
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बंगला (banglaa), हवेली (havelii, older, grander connotation), मकान (makaan, plain house, less grand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR you will hear people describe someone as रहते हैं बड़ी कोठी में (rahte hain badii kothii men, 'they live in a big mansion') as a shorthand for wealth and status -- think of the sprawling farmhouse-style homes in South Delhi or parts of Punjab. It carries a slightly old-money or landowning flavor, distinct from the more modern-sounding बंगला (banglaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use कोठी (kothii) for any house, but it specifically implies size and wealth -- an ordinary flat or small house is मकान (makaan) or घर (ghar), not कोठी (kothii). 2) Do not confuse with कोठा (kothaa), which has a different (and sometimes indelicate) set of connotations in some contexts.

वे लोग बहुत बड़ी कोठी में रहते हैं। Those people live in a very big mansion.
(ve log bahut badii kothii men rahte hain।)

Sense 2 : trading firm, commercial house, exchange house (older usage)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: फर्म (pharm), दुकान (dukaan, much narrower, everyday shop)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This older, mercantile sense of कोठी (kothii) survives mainly in historical or business-history contexts -- old trading families were said to run their कोठी (kothii) much like a bank or trading post. A modern learner will meet the mansion sense far more often in daily conversation.
