ज़ुकाम (zukaam)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'a cold' (the common respiratory ailment) -- almost always paired with the verb होना (honaa) in a dative-subject construction.
Sense 1 : common cold (illness)
noun, masculine Almost always used with को + होना: मुझे ज़ुकाम है (mujhe zukaam hai, 'I have a cold') -- the person is the dative subject, not the grammatical subject.
Synonyms: सर्दी (sardii, also means 'coldness/winter', context decides sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Hindi-speaking household has a go-to home remedy for ज़ुकाम (zukaam) -- adrak (adrak, ginger) tea, haldii doodh (haldii doodh, turmeric milk), or steam inhalation -- before anyone reaches for medicine. You will often hear people describe the whole cluster of cold symptoms together as ज़ुकाम-खांसी (zukaam-khaansii, 'cold and cough') as if it is one combined ailment.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं ज़ुकाम हूँ (main zukaam hoon) by analogy with English 'I am having a cold' -- correct is मुझे ज़ुकाम है (mujhe zukaam hai). 2) सर्दी (sardii) is often used interchangeably in casual speech for 'a cold', but it also means 'winter/coldness' -- ज़ुकाम (zukaam) is unambiguous for the illness itself. 3) The nuqta in ज़ुकाम (zukaam) is often dropped in casual writing as जुकाम (jukaam); both are understood, but ज़ुकाम (zukaam) is the fuller, more careful spelling reflecting its Persian-via-Urdu origin.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे ज़ुकाम हो गया है। I've caught a cold.
(mujhe zukaam ho gayaa hai।)
