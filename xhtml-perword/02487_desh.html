देश (desh)

noun देश (desh) primarily means 'country/nation' but also carries an extended sense of 'region, land, place' in more literary or compound usage.
Sense 1 : country, nation
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राष्ट्र (raashtr, formal), मुल्क (mulk)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: देश (desh) is the word at the heart of everyday patriotic vocabulary -- देशभक्ति (deshbhakti, 'patriotism'), देशवासी (deshvaasii, 'countryman'), and स्वदेश (svadesh, 'one's own homeland') all build on it. In casual conversation, Indians abroad often say अपने देश में (apne desh men, 'back in our country') with real warmth, marking a kind of belonging that goes beyond simple geography.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use राज्य (raajy) when they mean देश (desh) -- राज्य (raajy) is 'state/province' (like Punjab or Bihar), not the whole nation. 2) विदेश (videsh) is a separate word meaning 'foreign country,' not a phrase built with देश (desh) at the moment of speaking -- it's worth learning as its own item.
Sample Sentences:
भारत एक बहुत बड़ा देश है। India is a very large country.
(bhaarat ek bahut badaa desh hai।)

Sense 2 : region, land, place (extended/literary use)
noun, masculine This sense survives mainly inside compounds and set phrases rather than as a freestanding word for 'region' in casual speech.
Synonyms: प्रदेश (pradesh, region), भू-भाग (bhoo-bhaag, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This older, broader sense of 'land, terrain' lives on in words like मध्य प्रदेश (madhy pradesh, 'Madhya Pradesh,' literally 'middle region') and परदेस (pardes, 'foreign land'), showing देश (desh)'s roots as a general word for 'territory' before it narrowed to mean 'nation' in modern usage.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners rarely produce this sense actively -- it's more useful for recognizing words like प्रदेश (pradesh, 'province/state') and परदेस (pardes, 'foreign land') than for building new sentences.
