व्यंग्य (vyangy)

noun A literary/rhetorical noun meaning sarcasm, satire, or an ironic taunt -- used both for the literary genre (satire writing) and for a barbed remark in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : sarcasm / taunt in speech
noun, masculine Often used with कसना (kasnaa, 'to aim/direct') as व्यंग्य कसना (vyangy kasnaa, 'to make a sarcastic remark') or with मारना (maarnaa) in casual speech.
Synonyms: ताना (taanaa), फब्ती (phabtii, less common), चुटकी (chutkii, lighter, playful jab)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi families and friend circles love a good व्यंग्य (vyangy) -- someone says something dripping with irony at the dinner table, and everyone appreciates the wit even as they wince a little. It carries a flavor of 'I'm too clever/polite to say this directly, so I'll say it sideways.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सारकैज़्म (saarkaizm) borrowed from English, but व्यंग्य (vyangy) is the natural word educated Hindi speakers use -- it doesn't feel bookish in speech the way it might look on paper. 2) व्यंग्य (vyangy) implies a somewhat clever, pointed barb, not casual teasing -- for light-hearted banter, मज़ाक (mazaak) fits better.

Sense 2 : satire (literary genre)
noun, masculine Used for the genre in writing/journalism, as in व्यंग्य लेखक (vyangy lekhak, 'satirist/satire writer') or व्यंग्य लिखना (vyangy likhnaa, 'to write satire').
Synonyms: व्यंग्य-रचना (vyangy-rachnaa, specifically 'a satirical piece')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi has a strong tradition of व्यंग्य (vyangy) as a newspaper column genre -- witty, socially critical short pieces that comment on politics and daily life, a bit like an op-ed with a smirk. Writers like Harishankar Parsai are closely associated with this form.
