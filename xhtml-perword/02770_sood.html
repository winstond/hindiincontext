सूद (sood)

noun A financial term meaning interest (on a loan or investment); appears constantly in the fixed phrase सूद पर (sood par, 'on interest') and in the idiom मूल से सूद प्यारा (mool se sood pyaaraa).
Sense 1 : interest (on money lent or borrowed)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ब्याज (byaaj)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: सूद (sood) is the everyday word you'll hear from moneylenders, shopkeepers running informal credit, or relatives discussing a loan -- मूल (mool) is the principal, and सूद (sood) is what grows on top of it. The old saying मूल से सूद प्यारा (mool se sood pyaaraa, 'interest is dearer than the principal') captures a very Indian bazaar-wisdom observation: people often care more about the extra earnings than the original sum.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ब्याज (byaaj) exclusively and don't recognize सूद (sood) as the same concept -- both are common and interchangeable in everyday and financial speech. 2) The set phrase is सूद पर देना/लेना (sood par denaa/lenaa, 'to lend/borrow at interest') -- learners sometimes try को (ko) or से (se) instead of पर (par) here.
