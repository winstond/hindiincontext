इधर (idhar)

adverb Mainly a place adverb meaning 'here/this way', paired naturally with उधर (udhar, 'there/that way'); also has a common time-related idiom sense meaning 'recently/lately'.
Sense 1 : here, this way, in this direction
adverb
Synonyms: यहाँ (yahaan), इस तरफ़ (is taraf, toward this side)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear इधर आओ (idhar aao, 'come here') constantly in Delhi households, from mothers calling children to shopkeepers waving over a customer. It naturally pairs with उधर (udhar, 'there') in phrases like इधर-उधर (idhar-udhar, 'here and there, all over the place'), which describes anything scattered or someone wandering aimlessly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use यहाँ (yahaan) and इधर (idhar) interchangeably, but इधर (idhar) carries more of a directional 'this way' feel (as when beckoning someone), while यहाँ (yahaan) is more purely locational ('at this place'). 2) इधर आओ (idhar aao, 'come here') is the natural everyday command; a learner might overuse यहाँ आओ (yahaan aao) instead, which sounds slightly flatter.

इधर आओ, मुझे तुमसे कुछ कहना है। Come here, I need to tell you something.
(idhar aao, mujhe tumse kuchh kahnaa hai।)

Sense 2 : recently, lately, these days
adverb Used with a time reference to mean 'in this recent period', often in the frame इधर कुछ दिनों से (idhar kuchh dinon se, 'for the past few days').
Synonyms: आजकल (aajkal), हाल में (haal men)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in slightly more reflective or narrative speech -- someone catching up with an old friend might say इधर तबीयत ठीक नहीं रही (idhar tabiiyat thiik nahiin rahii, 'I haven't been feeling well lately'). It is the same word as the locational 'here', extended metaphorically from physical nearness to temporal nearness ('this' stretch of time, just as 'this' side of space).
Common Mistakes: 1) This time-related sense surprises learners who only know इधर (idhar) as 'here' -- in sentences like इधर वह बहुत व्यस्त है (idhar vah bahut vyast hai, 'he has been very busy lately'), it has nothing to do with location.
