के सामने (ke saamne)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'in front of' or 'facing' -- both physically and, in extended use, socially/emotionally, as in facing a person or a challenge. Built from सामने (saamne, 'in front') governed by के (ke), the oblique-taking form of का (kaa).
Sense 1 : physically in front of, facing
postposition Governs the oblique case of the preceding noun, like other compound postpositions (e.g. घर के सामने (ghar ke saamne, 'in front of the house')).
Synonyms: के आगे (ke aage)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture standing के सामने (ke saamne) of a shopkeeper's counter or a teacher's desk -- this phrase is your everyday word for 'facing' someone or something directly, and it shows up constantly in giving directions, like दुकान के सामने (dukaan ke saamne, 'in front of the shop').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use के आगे (ke aage) and के सामने (ke saamne) interchangeably, but के सामने (ke saamne) specifically implies facing something directly (like across a table), while के आगे (ke aage) can mean 'ahead of' in a path or sequence as well. 2) Forgetting that सामने (saamne) alone (without के (ke)) can also stand as an adverb meaning 'in front, opposite' when no noun object is specified -- के सामने (ke saamne) is used only when a specific noun is being related to.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे घर के सामने एक बड़ा पार्क है। There is a big park in front of my house.
(mere ghar ke saamne ek badaa paark hai।)

Sense 2 : in the presence of; when confronted with (a person, challenge, or comparison)
postposition Common in constructions expressing being outmatched, intimidated, or overshadowed, e.g. उसके सामने मेरी कोई हैसियत नहीं (uske saamne merii koii haisiyat nahiin, 'I am nothing compared to him/in front of him').
Synonyms: की मौजूदगी में (kii maujoodgii men, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense carries real social weight in Hindi -- बड़ों के सामने (badon ke saamne, 'in front of elders') isn't just about physical location, it evokes the whole idea of deference and decorum expected in front of respected people, which is a very Indian family/social dynamic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often miss the figurative extension -- के सामने (ke saamne) is used not just for physical facing but for social confrontation, like standing up to someone or being compared unfavorably to them, e.g. बड़ों के सामने (badon ke saamne, 'in front of elders/in the presence of elders').
