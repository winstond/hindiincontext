निराश (niraash)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'disappointed' or 'hopeless/despondent' -- describing a person's emotional state, almost always with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa).
Sense 1 : disappointed, discouraged, hopeless
adjective Most commonly paired with होना (honaa) to describe the person feeling this way (subject-agreeing), or with करना (karnaa) when someone else causes the disappointment: उसने मुझे निराश किया (usne mujhe niraash kiyaa, 'he disappointed me').
Synonyms: हताश (hataash), उदास (udaas, sad rather than disappointed), निराशावादी (niraashaavaadii, pessimistic person, not the same feeling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around exam results and job outcomes -- a parent might sigh मैं तुमसे निराश हूँ (main tumse niraash hoon, 'I am disappointed in you'), a line every Indian kid dreads more than outright anger. Note the से (se) marking the source of disappointment, mirroring 'disappointed in/with' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सैड (said) or उदास (udaas) when they mean 'disappointed' rather than plain 'sad' -- निराश (niraash) specifically carries the sense of hopes not being met, not general sadness. 2) The causative pattern निराश करना (niraash karnaa, 'to disappoint someone') is the natural way to say 'X disappointed me' -- a direct word-for-word translation of the English sentence structure often sounds off.
Sample Sentences:
मैं तुमसे निराश हूँ। I am disappointed in you.
(main tumse niraash hoon।)
