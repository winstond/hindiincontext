परस्पर (paraspar)

adjective/adverb A formal/literary word meaning 'mutual' or 'mutually' -- used as an adjective (mutual, reciprocal) or adverb (mutually, with each other), often in written or somewhat elevated speech.
Sense 1 : mutual, reciprocal
adjective
Synonyms: आपसी (aapsii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word shows up constantly in formal writing -- newspaper editorials, diplomatic statements, school essays on 'unity' -- talking about परस्पर सहयोग (paraspar sahyog, 'mutual cooperation') or परस्पर सम्मान (paraspar sammaan, 'mutual respect'). In everyday chat, most Delhi speakers would just say आपसी (aapsii) instead, which means the same thing but sounds far more casual.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect this like a regular आ-ending adjective (paraspraa, paraspre) -- it never changes form, regardless of the gender or number of the noun it modifies. 2) It almost always precedes an abstract noun like सहयोग (sahyog, cooperation), समझ (samajh, understanding), or प्रेम (prem, love) -- it doesn't modify concrete physical objects.

Sense 2 : mutually, with/among each other
adverb Functions adverbially with verbs of interaction, often glossable as 'with each other' -- e.g. परस्पर बात करना (paraspar baat karnaa, 'to talk with each other').
Synonyms: आपस में (aapas men)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in slightly bookish or formal contexts -- a teacher telling students to परस्पर सहयोग करें (paraspar sahyog karen, 'cooperate with one another'), or a company memo urging departments to काम परस्पर तालमेल से करें (kaam paraspar taalmel se karen, 'work in mutual coordination'). Its everyday twin आपस में (aapas men) is what you'd actually use chatting with friends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because it looks like it should take a postposition (like आपस में aapas men does with में), learners sometimes add one after परस्पर (paraspar) -- but it stands alone as the adverb itself, no में needed.
