धुँधला (dhundhlaa)

adjective/verb An adjective meaning hazy, blurry, or dim -- used for foggy weather, blurred vision or images, and faded memories alike; it also has a related intransitive verb sense meaning 'to become blurred/dim.' Also spelled धुंधला (dhundhlaa) with the anusvaar instead of the chandrabindu -- both are common.
Sense 1 : hazy, blurry, dim, faded (visual or mental)
adjective
Synonyms: अस्पष्ट (aspasht), अंधियारा (andhiyaaraa, dim/dark, less common), फीका (phiikaa, faded, meaning scope: color/vividness rather than clarity)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of looking through fogged-up glasses on a winter morning in Delhi -- that's the classic धुँधला (dhundhlaa) image, and indeed it's the go-to word for Delhi's famous winter smog and fog (कोहरा, kohraa) making everything hazy. It extends naturally to memory too: धुँधली याद (dhundhlii yaad, 'a hazy/faded memory') is a very common phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अस्पष्ट (aspasht) for everything meaning 'unclear,' but धुँधला (dhundhlaa) specifically evokes a visual haze or fog-like quality -- unclear speech or writing is better called अस्पष्ट (aspasht), not धुँधला (dhundhlaa). 2) Don't confuse with अंधेरा (andheraa, 'dark') -- धुँधला (dhundhlaa) is about blur/haze, not absence of light, though a dim room can be described either way depending on what you mean.

Sense 2 : to become blurred, hazy, or dim
verb, intransitive Functions as an intransitive verb describing eyes, glass, vision, or memory growing hazy over time; often heard in the compound-verb form धुँधला जाना (dhundhlaa jaanaa, 'to become blurred/faded').
Synonyms: धुँधलाना (dhundhlaanaa, the more standard verb form for this action)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear आँखें धुँधला जाना (aankhen dhundhlaa jaanaa, 'the eyes to grow hazy/teary') used for eyes welling up with emotion, not just poor eyesight -- a lovely example of how Hindi ties blurred vision to feeling something deeply.
