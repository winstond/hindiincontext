पूर्व (poorv)

adjective/noun/postposition पूर्व (poorv) covers two related ideas: as a noun/direction word it means 'east,' and as an adjective/postposition-like word it means 'former, prior, previous' -- the sense of coming before something in time or order.
Sense 1 : former, previous, prior
adjective Commonly used before a noun as in पूर्व प्रधानमंत्री (poorv pradhaanmantrii, 'former prime minister') -- functions much like the English prefix 'ex-' or 'former' attached to a title or role.
Synonyms: पहला (pahlaa), पिछला (pichhlaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will see constantly in newspapers and news channels: पूर्व मुख्यमंत्री (poorv mukhymantrii, 'former chief minister'), पूर्व राष्ट्रपति (poorv raashtrapti, 'former president'). It has a formal, official flavor -- in casual speech about someone's 'ex' (a former partner, for instance), people are more likely to say कभी का (kabhii kaa) or just describe the situation rather than reach for पूर्व (poorv).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect पूर्व (poorv) like a regular -आ adjective; it stays invariant no matter the gender or number of the noun it describes, e.g. पूर्व मंत्री (poorv mantrii) and पूर्व सरकार (poorv sarkaar) both use the same unchanged form. 2) Don't confuse this with पहले (pahle, 'before/earlier') as an adverb of time -- पूर्व (poorv) in this sense is used attributively right before a noun, not as a standalone time adverb in a sentence.

Sense 2 : east (the cardinal direction)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पूरब (poorab, everyday variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In school geography and news weather reports you will always hear पूर्व (poorv), उत्तर (uttar), दक्षिण (dakshin), पश्चिम (pashchim) as the formal set of the four directions, while in casual bazaar-style directions people often say पूरब (poorab) instead -- much like 'east' versus a more colloquial way of pointing and saying 'that way, sunrise side.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to पूरब (poorab) in casual speech and पूर्व (poorv) in writing/formal contexts -- both are correct, but पूर्व (poorv) sounds slightly more formal or textbook-ish when talking about directions in everyday conversation.

Sense 3 : before, prior to (in time)
postposition Used in the frame [oblique noun] + के पूर्व (ke poorv) meaning 'before X,' parallel to and more formal than [oblique noun] + से पहले (se pahle).
Synonyms: पहले (pahle)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet यह (yah) + के पूर्व (ke poorv) constructions in formal invitations, official notices, and news reports -- for instance स्वतंत्रता के पूर्व (svatantrtaa ke poorv, 'before independence') is a very common phrase in history textbooks and documentaries.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for से पहले (se pahle) by default; के पूर्व (ke poorv) means the same thing but sounds noticeably more formal or written, so using it in casual chat can sound a bit stiff, like saying 'prior to' instead of 'before' in English.
