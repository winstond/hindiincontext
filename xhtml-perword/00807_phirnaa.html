फिरना (phirnaa)

verb The core idea is aimless or repeated movement -- 'to roam, wander, turn' -- which extends into a change-of-state sense ('to turn back on, go back on'), and shows up constantly as a light/compound-verb helper attached to other verbs (e.g. घूमना (ghoomnaa) फिरना (phirnaa)).
Sense 1 : to wander, roam, stroll about
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: घूमना (ghoomnaa), टहलना (tahalnaa, focuses on strolling/walking for leisure), भटकना (bhataknaa, aimless/lost wandering)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi families use घूमना (ghoomnaa) फिरना (phirnaa) all the time for a casual outing -- 'बच्चे बाहर घूमने-फिरने गए हैं' (bachche baahar ghoomne-phirne gae hain, 'the kids have gone out to roam around') -- it is warmer and less purposeful-sounding than 'जाना' (jaanaa) alone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for घूमना (ghoomnaa) alone and forget that घूमना (ghoomnaa)-फिरना (phirnaa) as a pair is extremely common in speech to mean 'to roam about, to go out and about' -- using फिरना (phirnaa) solo is fine but sounds slightly more literary/bare than the paired form. 2) फिरना (phirnaa) is intransitive, so it never takes ने (ne) in the past: मैं बाज़ार फिरा (main baazaar phiraa, 'I wandered around the market'), not मैंने फिरा।
Sample Sentences:
वह पूरे दिन बाज़ार में घूमता-फिरता रहा। He kept wandering around the market all day.
(vah poore din baazaar men ghoomtaa-phirtaa rahaa।)

Sense 2 : to turn, turn around, reverse direction
verb, intransitive Often used with a direction word or postposition: पीछे फिरना (piichhe phirnaa, 'to turn back/around').
Synonyms: मुड़ना (mudnaa, more common for physically turning a corner/direction), पलटना (palatnaa, turning back/reversing)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps with the 'go back on' sense below -- both share the root idea of reversing direction, one physical and one about loyalty or a promise.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this with मुड़ना (mudnaa) -- मुड़ना (mudnaa) is the default verb for 'turning' (a corner, one's head), while फिरना (phirnaa) in this sense feels a little more literary or is reserved for turning back/reversing rather than a simple physical turn.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to go back on, renounce, turn against (a promise, word, person)
verb, intransitive Common frame: अपनी बात से फिरना (apnii baat se phirnaa, 'to go back on one's word') -- takes से (se) marking what is being abandoned.
Synonyms: मुकरना (mukarnaa, specifically 'to go back on one's word/promise'), बदल जाना (badal jaanaa, to change/turn, broader)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the common scolding 'तुम अपनी बात से फिर गए' (tum apnii baat se phir gae, 'you went back on your word') -- a serious accusation in Hindi, since going back on one's बात (baat) touches on honor and trustworthiness in everyday social dealings.
Common Mistakes: 1) The से (se) marking here is easy to miss -- learners try को (ko) by analogy with other verbs, but this sense specifically wants से (se): वह अपने वादे से फिर गया (vah apne vaade se phir gayaa, 'he went back on his promise'), not वादे को।
