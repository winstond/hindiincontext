गिरना (girnaa)

verb The basic verb of physical falling (girnaa), with natural extensions into things like prices, health, and reputation 'falling' -- much as in English.
Sense 1 : to fall, to fall down (physically)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: गिर पड़ना (gir padnaa, compound form emphasizing suddenness), ढहना (dhahnaa, for structures collapsing), लुढ़कना (ludhaknaa, to tumble/roll down)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first 'body in trouble' verbs learners meet -- बच्चा गिर गया (bachchaa gir gayaa, 'the child fell') is something every Indian parent has said, often followed by concerned checking for चोट (chot, injury). Note the common compound-verb pairing गिर जाना (gir jaanaa), which adds a sense of completeness or suddenness to the fall.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for गिराना (giraanaa) when they mean 'to fall' -- but that is the causative, 'to drop/knock down (something)'; गिरना (girnaa) itself is intransitive and describes the falling happening to the subject, not something done to an object. 2) Since गिरना (girnaa) is intransitive, it never takes ने (ne) in the perfective: वह गिरा (vah giraa, 'he fell'), not वह ने गिरा.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to fall/drop (of prices, levels, temperature, etc.)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: घटना (ghatnaa, to decrease), नीचे जाना (niiche jaanaa, to go down)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday talk about money and weather: भाव गिर गए (bhaav gir gae, 'prices fell') or तापमान गिर रहा है (taapmaan gir rahaa hai, 'the temperature is falling'). It mirrors English 'fall' for prices almost exactly, which makes it an easy sense for learners to pick up quickly.

Sense 3 : to decline (health, reputation, morale, standards)
verb, intransitive Often used with abstract subjects like इज़्ज़त (izzat, honor/reputation), सेहत (sehat, health), or स्तर (star, standard/level).
Synonyms: बिगड़ना (bigadnaa, to worsen/go bad), गिरावट आना (giraavat aanaa, noun+verb phrase, 'a decline to occur')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension is very much alive in everyday speech -- उसकी सेहत गिर रही है (uskii sehat gir rahii hai, 'his health is declining') or उसकी नज़रों में गिर गया (uskii nazron men gir gayaa, 'he fell in her estimation/eyes') both use the same falling logic as English, so it tends to feel intuitive rather than foreign.
