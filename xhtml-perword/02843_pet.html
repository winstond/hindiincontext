पेट (pet)

noun The basic word for 'stomach/belly', both as a body part and as the seat of hunger, appetite, and (figuratively) livelihood.
Sense 1 : stomach, belly (body part)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: उदर (udar, formal/literary), शिकम (shikam, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Pain, growth, and physical sensations in the पेट (pet) are usually described with dative-subject or possessive constructions -- मेरा पेट दर्द कर रहा है (meraa pet dard kar rahaa hai, 'my stomach is aching') -- reflecting the Hindi habit of treating the body as the site of an experience rather than an active agent.
Common Mistakes: 1) पेट (pet) refers to the whole belly/abdomen area, not narrowly the internal organ -- so पेट दर्द है (pet dard hai, 'there's a stomachache') covers general abdominal pain, not just gastric issues. 2) Learners sometimes say पेट में दर्द है (pet men dard hai, 'there's pain in the stomach') where a native speaker might just as naturally drop the में (men) and say पेट दर्द है (pet dard hai) in casual speech.
Sample Sentences:
मेरा पेट दर्द कर रहा है। My stomach is aching.
(meraa pet dard kar rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : hunger, appetite (as a felt need)
noun, masculine Common in the pattern पेट भरना (pet bharnaa, 'to fill the stomach/be full') and पेट भरा होना (pet bharaa honaa, 'to be full/satisfied').
Synonyms: भूख (bhookh, more common for pure 'hunger')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पेट भरना (pet bharnaa) is the everyday way to talk about being fed or full, and पेट भर खाना (pet bhar khaanaa, 'to eat one's fill') is a common phrase heard at family meals when someone urges you to eat more.
Common Mistakes: 1) पेट भरना (pet bharnaa) means 'to satisfy hunger, be full' -- learners sometimes reach for भूख भरना (bhookh bharnaa) by analogy, but that is not how native speakers phrase it; भूख (bhookh) is 'satisfied' (मिटाना/शांत करना (mitaanaa/shaant karnaa)), while पेट (pet) is what gets 'filled'.
Sample Sentences:
पहले पेट भर लो, फिर बात करेंगे। First fill your stomach, then we'll talk.
(pahle pet bhar lo, phir baat karenge।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

(daadii hameshaa kahtii hain, pet bhar ke khaao, sharmaao mat।)

Sense 3 : livelihood, one's living/sustenance (figurative)
noun, masculine Occurs mainly in the fixed idiom पेट भरना (pet bharnaa, 'to earn one's living') and पेट पालना (pet paalnaa, 'to support oneself/one's family').
Synonyms: रोज़ी (rozii), गुज़ारा (guzaaraa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear people say मेरा पेट इससे चलता है (meraa pet isse chaltaa hai, 'this is how I make my living') when talking about their job or trade -- the belly stands in for one's whole economic survival, a very common metaphor in Hindi about work and money.
Common Mistakes: 1) पेट पालना (pet paalnaa) literally 'to nurture the stomach' means 'to make a living, support a family' -- a learner unfamiliar with the idiom might misread it as literally about feeding oneself rather than about earning income to survive.
Sample Sentences:
