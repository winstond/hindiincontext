सच्चाई (sachchaaii)

noun A noun built from सच्चा (sachchaa, 'true') that covers both 'truth' as an abstract quality and 'the truth' as a specific fact or reality of a situation.
Sense 1 : truth, truthfulness (as a quality/virtue)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सत्य (saty, formal), ईमानदारी (iimaandaarii, honesty, meaning scope narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian parents and teachers love invoking सच्चाई (sachchaaii) as a core virtue, right alongside ईमानदारी (iimaandaarii, honesty) -- think of the moral lessons in old Doordarshan shows or school essays titled 'सच्चाई का महत्व' (sachchaaii kaa mahatv, 'the importance of truthfulness').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सच (sach) in every context since it also means 'truth,' but सच्चाई (sachchaaii) is the abstract noun form, more natural when talking about someone's character or a general virtue rather than a single true statement. 2) Don't confuse with सच्चा (sachchaa), the adjective 'true/genuine' -- सच्चाई (sachchaaii) is only the noun.

Sense 2 : the truth, the actual facts/reality of a matter
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सच (sach), असलियत (asliyat, reality/the real story), हकीकत (hakiikat, reality)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense constantly in news and drama contexts -- सच्चाई का पता चलना (sachchaaii kaa pataa chalnaa, 'for the truth to come to light') is a very common phrase in Hindi television reporting and family arguments alike, where someone is always trying to uncover what really happened.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense सच्चाई (sachchaaii) behaves like 'the truth' about a specific situation -- e.g. सच्चाई सामने आ गई (sachchaaii saamne aa gaii, 'the truth came out') -- rather than a quality someone possesses; mixing up the two senses is the main trap.
