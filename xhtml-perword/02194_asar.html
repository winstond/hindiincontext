असर (asar)

noun A single core meaning -- 'effect/influence' -- that shows up constantly in everyday speech about medicine, advice, weather, and persuasion, plus a fixed idiom for being influenced by someone.
Sense 1 : effect, impact, influence
noun, masculine Commonly used with होना (honaa) 'to have an effect' and करना (karnaa) 'to have an effect on (something)', as in दवा का असर होना (davaa kaa asar honaa, 'for the medicine to take effect').
Synonyms: प्रभाव (prabhaav), नतीजा (natiijaa, effect/result, meaning scope)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly around medicine and home remedies -- दवा असर नहीं कर रही (davaa asar nahiin kar rahii, 'the medicine isn't working') is something every Indian household has said at some point. It also shows up in advice-giving: उसकी बात का कोई असर नहीं हुआ (uskii baat kaa koii asar nahiin huaa, 'his words had no effect') captures a very common feeling of talking to someone who just won't listen.
Common Mistakes: 1) असर पड़ना (asar padnaa) is used with पर (par) marking the thing affected -- e.g. उस पर असर पड़ा (us par asar padaa, 'it affected him') -- learners sometimes reach for को (ko) instead. 2) असर करना (asar karnaa) takes पर (par) too, not a direct object, even though 'affect' in English feels transitive.
Sample Sentences:
यह दवा बहुत असर करती है। This medicine works very well.
(yah davaa bahut asar kartii hai।)

Sense 2 : being under someone's influence (idiomatic)
noun, masculine Chiefly appears in the fixed phrase किसी के असर में होना/आना (kisii ke asar men honaa/aanaa, 'to be/come under someone's influence'), often with a mildly negative shade -- picking up someone's habits or attitudes.
Synonyms: प्रभाव (prabhaav), रंग (rang, figurative use, meaning scope)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Parents often use this about children and friend groups: वह गलत लोगों के असर में आ गया है (vah galat logon ke asar men aa gayaa hai, 'he has fallen under the influence of bad company') is a classic worried-parent line in Delhi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) The phrase uses में (men) -- असर में आना (asar men aanaa) -- not पर (par), unlike the plain 'effect' sense of this word which pairs with पर (par).
