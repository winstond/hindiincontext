बाज़ (baaz)

verb/noun A Persian-origin word meaning 'hawk/falcon'; it also appears bound in the common idiomatic phrase बाज़ आना (baaz aanaa), 'to give up/desist from', which is treated as its own sense here.
Sense 1 : to give up, desist, refrain (from a habit or action)
verb, intransitive Occurs only as the fixed compound बाज़ आना (baaz aanaa), with the thing given up marked by से (se): बुरी आदतों से बाज़ आना (burii aadton se baaz aanaa, 'to give up bad habits'). बाज़ (baaz) does not function as an independent verb outside this frame.
Synonyms: छोड़ना (chhodnaa, covers a broader range of meanings), रुकना (ruknaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: बाज़ आना (baaz aanaa) has a scolding, exasperated flavor -- a mother telling a child नादानी से बाज़ आ जाओ (naadaanii se baaz aa jaao, 'quit this foolishness already') captures the tone perfectly. It's stronger and more emphatic than a plain छोड़ना (chhodnaa), often carrying a sense of 'enough is enough.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use बाज़ (baaz) alone as a verb root -- it only exists bound to आना (aanaa) in this sense. 2) The thing being given up takes से (se), not को (ko) -- शरारत से बाज़ आओ (sharaarat se baaz aao, 'stop the mischief'), not शरारत को.

Sense 2 : hawk, falcon
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शिकरा (shikraa, a smaller hawk species), श्येन (shyen, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Falconry has a long history in North Indian and Mughal courtly culture, so बाज़ (baaz) carries a slightly regal or poetic flavor when used literally -- you're more likely to meet it in a nature documentary, a poem, or a phrase like बाज़ की नज़र (baaz kii nazar, 'hawk's eye', meaning sharp-eyed) than in casual chat about birds.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse बाज़ (baaz) with बाज़ी (baazii, 'a round/turn/bet') because of the similar spelling -- the two are unrelated in meaning. 2) The nuqta under ज़ is often dropped in casual typing, but the pronunciation with the 'z' sound is standard for this word.
