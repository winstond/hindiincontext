चीता (chiitaa)

noun Refers to the big cat, the cheetah or (colloquially, by loose overlap) the leopard; also survives as a proper name and in the funeral-pyre sense in some registers.
Sense 1 : the animal: cheetah
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तेंदुआ (tenduaa, technically 'leopard,' but often confused with चीता in everyday speech)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Cheetahs went extinct in India by the mid-20th century, so most Indians only know चीता (chiitaa) from zoos, wildlife documentaries, or news; there was major national excitement a few years back when cheetahs were reintroduced from Africa into Kuno National Park, so the word has recently become more present in everyday news chatter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Many Hindi speakers use चीता (chiitaa) loosely for both cheetah and leopard, since the animals are visually similar to a non-specialist; a learner should know that in casual conversation, precise zoological distinction is often not intended. 2) Do not confuse with चिड़िया (chidiyaa, 'small bird'), which sounds similar to beginners but is unrelated.

Sense 2 : figurative: someone very fast
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as an exclamation about a person's speed, e.g. praising a fast runner or driver.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just like English speakers say 'fast as a cheetah,' Hindi speakers casually call a quick runner or a rash driver चीता (chiitaa) -- for instance, a motorcyclist weaving through Delhi traffic might get called चीता (chiitaa) by an admiring or exasperated friend.
