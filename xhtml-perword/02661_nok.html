नोक (nok)

noun Mainly a physical noun meaning 'tip/point' of something sharp, with a common figurative extension in the idiom नोक-झोंक (nok-jhonk) for playful bickering.
Sense 1 : tip, point (of something sharp or pointed)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सिरा (siraa), अगला भाग (aglaa bhaag, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the tip of a पेंसिल (pensil, pencil) or सुई (suii, needle) -- नोक (nok) is the word Hindi speakers reach for whenever something comes to a fine point. It's a very everyday, concrete noun.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse नोक (nok) with सिरा (siraa): नोक (nok) specifically suggests a sharp or narrowing point (like a pencil, needle, or knife), while सिरा (siraa) is more neutral for 'end' of something (like a rope or road) without implying sharpness.

Sense 2 : playful bickering, teasing banter (in the idiom नोक-झोंक)
noun, feminine Almost always appears as the compound नोक-झोंक (nok-jhonk) rather than नोक (nok) alone in this sense.
Synonyms: छेड़छाड़ (chhedchhaad), नोक-झोंक (nok-jhonk, fixed idiom form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: नोक-झोंक (nok-jhonk) is the light, affectionate bickering you hear between siblings or a newly married couple -- teasing with an edge but no real anger, much like how the 'point' (नोक, nok) of a jab is felt but not meant to wound.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only exists inside the fixed pair नोक-झोंक (nok-jhonk) -- using नोक (nok) alone to mean 'banter' will not be understood; the pairing is obligatory.
