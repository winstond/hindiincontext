दीयासलाई (diiyaaslaaii)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'matchstick' or 'matchbox' -- the little wooden stick (or the box holding them) used to strike a flame.
Sense 1 : matchstick / matchbox (for lighting fire)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: माचिस (maachis)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This word literally paints a picture: दीया (diiyaa) is the little clay oil lamp lit every evening in Hindu households, and सलाई (salaaii) is just a slender stick -- so दीयासलाई (diiyaaslaaii) was originally 'the stick you use to light the lamp.' In modern Delhi-NCR speech, माचिस (maachis) has largely taken over for the everyday matchbox you buy at a corner shop, so दीयासलाई (diiyaaslaaii) now has a slightly quaint, almost grandmotherly charm -- lovely to know, but don't be surprised if it's माचिस (maachis) you hear at the paan shop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to माचिस (maachis) since it is far more common in spoken Delhi-NCR Hindi; दीयासलाई (diiyaaslaaii) means exactly the same thing but sounds a touch more old-fashioned or literary in everyday conversation. 2) The compound is दीया (diiyaa, 'oil lamp') plus सलाई (salaaii, 'thin stick'), so learners sometimes mis-hear or mis-spell it as दीयासिलाई -- the second element is सलाई (salaaii), not सिलाई (silaaii, 'sewing').
