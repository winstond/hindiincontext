मशहूर (mashahoor)

adjective A single, straightforward sense: 'famous, well-known' -- used for people, places, foods, and things alike.
Sense 1 : famous, well-known
adjective
Synonyms: प्रसिद्ध (prasiddh), विख्यात (vikhyaat, literary), नामी (naamii, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in India when people talk up a city, a sweet shop, or a dhaba: पुराणी दिल्ली अपने खाने के लिए मशहूर है (puraanii dillii apne khaane ke lie mashahoor hai, 'Old Delhi is famous for its food'). It is a Persian-origin loanword that has fully settled into everyday Hindi, sitting comfortably alongside its more literary cousin विख्यात (vikhyaat).
Common Mistakes: 1) मशहूर (mashahoor) is invariant -- learners sometimes try to add gender/number endings to it as if it were a native adjective like कड़ा (kadaa); it stays मशहूर (mashahoor) regardless of the noun's gender or number. 2) Often paired with के लिए (ke lie) to say what someone/something is famous for, e.g. अपने खाने के लिए मशहूर (apne khaane ke lie mashahoor, 'famous for its food') -- learners sometimes reach for से (se) instead.
