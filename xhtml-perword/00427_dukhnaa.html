दुखना (dukhnaa)

verb One core meaning: to hurt or ache, used for physical pain in a body part. Closely related to the noun/adjective दुख (dukh, sorrow/pain) but दुखना (dukhnaa) itself is only used for bodily ache.
Sense 1 : to ache, to hurt (of a body part)
verb, intransitive The body part that hurts is the grammatical subject, not the person: सिर दुखना (sir dukhnaa, 'for the head to hurt') -- the person experiencing it, if mentioned, takes को (ko), e.g. मुझे (mujhe).
Synonyms: पीड़ा होना (piidaa honaa, more formal/literary phrasing), टीसना (tiisnaa, a sharper throbbing ache)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic 'body as agent' verb: Hindi says पैर दुखता है (pair dukhtaa hai, 'the foot aches') rather than 'my foot hurts,' putting the aching part in charge of the sentence. You'll hear this constantly in daily complaints -- सिर दुखना (sir dukhnaa) for a headache, कमर दुखना (kamar dukhnaa) for backache after a long day of chores or travel.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make the person the subject with ने (ne), as if it were a transitive action -- but दुखना (dukhnaa) is intransitive and the body part itself is the subject; the person appears only with को (ko). 2) Confusing दुखना (dukhnaa) with दुःख देना (duhkh denaa, 'to give sorrow') or दुखी होना (dukhii honaa, 'to feel sad') -- दुखना (dukhnaa) is strictly physical pain, never emotional sadness.
Sample Sentences:
आज सुबह से मेरा सिर दुख रहा है। My head has been aching since this morning.
(aaj subah se meraa sir dukh rahaa hai।)
