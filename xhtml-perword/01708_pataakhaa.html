पटाखा (pataakhaa)

noun Primarily 'firecracker' -- the thing you light during Diwali -- with a lively figurative extension to a spirited or feisty person, mostly used for a woman.
Sense 1 : firecracker
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आतिशबाज़ी (aatishbaazii, usually the display/fireworks show rather than a single piece)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पटाखा (pataakhaa) is one of the first words every kid learns before Diwali -- पटाखे चलाना (pataakhe chalaanaa, 'to set off firecrackers') is the classic phrase you will hear all over a Delhi mohalla in late October. In recent years it also comes loaded with the pollution debate, so you will hear ग्रीन पटाखे (griin pataakhe, 'green firecrackers') as a common compromise term on the news.
Common Mistakes: 1) पटाखा (pataakhaa) is the individual firecracker; for the whole fireworks display or the general activity of setting off firecrackers, आतिशबाज़ी (aatishbaazii) is more natural, e.g. आतिशबाज़ी देखना (aatishbaazii dekhnaa, 'to watch the fireworks') rather than पटाखे देखना (pataakhe dekhnaa).

Sense 2 : a spirited, feisty, or bold person (figurative)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as a description, almost always affectionately or admiringly: वह लड़की तो पटाखा है (vah ladkii to pataakhaa hai, 'that girl is a real firecracker').
Synonyms: तेज़ (tez, adjective use, sharp/spirited)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear in Bollywood-adjacent chatter and Hindi film songs to praise a bold, vivacious woman -- it carries the same punch as English 'firecracker' or 'spitfire' used the same way, so the metaphor actually translates quite directly.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is almost always applied to a woman in everyday speech, describing boldness, sharpness, or striking looks/personality; using it for a man sounds odd to most speakers.
