बकबक (bakbak)

noun/verb An onomatopoeic word for pointless, nonstop chatter. It appears as a noun (the chatter/nagging itself) and as part of the compound verb बकबक करना (bakbak karnaa, 'to yak on/nag'); both senses share the same flavor of mild irritation.
Sense 1 : nonstop, pointless chatter or nagging
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बड़बड़ (badbad, mumbling/grumbling), बकवास (bakvaas, nonsense talk)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every mother or elder brother uses when someone won't stop yapping: 'बस भी करो, कितनी बकबक करते हो!' (bas bhii karo, kitnii bakbak karte ho, 'Enough already, how much you go on!'). It's reduplicated onomatopoeia, like the sound of a mouth just running, similar in spirit to English 'yakety-yak.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat बकबक (bakbak) as a verb on its own; it needs करना (karnaa) or a similar light verb to actually 'do' the chattering -- बकबक (bakbak) by itself is just the noun naming the noise. 2) It is mildly negative/annoying in tone, not neutral like 'conversation' -- don't use it to describe a pleasant chat.
Sample Sentences:
बस भी करो, कितनी बकबक करते हो! Enough already, how much you go on!
(bas bhii karo, kitnii bakbak karte ho!)

Sense 2 : to chatter/nag endlessly (compound verb with करना)
verb, transitive Functions as a compound verb: बकबक (bakbak) + करना (karnaa). ने-marking follows करना (karnaa) normally in past perfective, since करना (karnaa) is the transitive carrier here.
Synonyms: बड़बड़ करना (badbad karnaa, to grumble/mutter), बकवास करना (bakvaas karnaa, to talk nonsense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kids hear this constantly from parents: 'इतनी बकबक मत करो' (itnii bakbak mat karo, 'Don't chatter so much') is a classic scolding line in Delhi households, said with equal parts exasperation and affection.
