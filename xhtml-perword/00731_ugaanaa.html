उगाना (ugaanaa)

verb A causative verb meaning 'to grow/cultivate (something)' -- one core sense, used for plants, crops, and by extension hair, teeth, etc.
Sense 1 : to grow, cultivate (plants, crops)
verb, transitive This is the causative of उगना (ugnaa, 'to sprout/grow' -- intransitive): उगाना (ugaanaa) means to make something grow, i.e. to cultivate it. The grower takes ने (ne) in the perfective, and the thing grown is the direct object: किसान ने गेहूं उगाया (kisaan ne gehoon ugaayaa, 'the farmer grew wheat').
Synonyms: उपजाना (upjaanaa, crops/produce specifically), पैदा करना (paidaa karnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb comes up constantly in conversations about kitchen gardens and farming -- a very common thing for a Delhi-NCR family to say proudly is हम गमले में सब्ज़ियां उगाते हैं (ham gamle men sabziyaan ugaate hain, 'we grow vegetables in pots'), reflecting how many urban households keep a small balcony garden. It pairs naturally with उगना (ugnaa) as the intransitive counterpart -- think of उगना (ugnaa) as what the sun or a seed does on its own, and उगाना (ugaanaa) as what a person does to help it happen.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use उगाना (ugaanaa) when they mean उगना (ugnaa) -- e.g. saying 'the plant grew' with उगाना (ugaanaa) instead of उगना (ugnaa); remember उगाना (ugaanaa) needs an agent who does the growing (a farmer, a gardener), while उगना (ugnaa) is what the plant itself does on its own. 2) By extension, उगाना (ugaanaa) is also used for hair, nails, a mustache, etc. -- दाढ़ी उगाना (daadhii ugaanaa, 'to grow a beard') -- which can feel like an odd extension to English speakers used to 'grow' working the same way for plants and body hair.
Sample Sentences:
हम गमले में सब्ज़ियां उगाते हैं। We grow vegetables in pots.
(ham gamle men sabziyaan ugaate hain।)
