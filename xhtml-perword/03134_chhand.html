छंद (chhand)

noun Primarily a literary/prosodic term for a metrical verse form or poetic meter; also used more loosely to mean a stanza or a fixed rhythmic pattern in speech/poetry.
Sense 1 : poetic meter / verse form
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पद्य (pady, verse in general), काव्य (kaavy, poetry, broader scope)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: छंद (chhand) is central to classical Hindi and Sanskrit poetics -- terms like दोहा (dohaa) and चौपाई (chaupaaii) are actually specific types of छंद (chhand). If you have read the Ramcharitmanas or Tulsidas, you have encountered these named meters constantly; a Hindi teacher will often introduce छंद (chhand) as the 'rulebook' behind traditional verse.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse छंद (chhand) with कविता (kavitaa) -- कविता (kavitaa) is the poem itself, while छंद (chhand) refers specifically to its metrical structure or the rules governing rhythm and syllable count. 2) The word is often mistaken as always meaning 'stanza,' but it more precisely denotes the metrical pattern a stanza follows, not the stanza as a block of text.

(tulsiidaas ne raamachritmaanas men kaii chhandon kaa prayog kiyaa।)

Sense 2 : rhythmic pattern / cadence (extended sense)
noun, masculine Used when describing the rhythmic quality of speech, chanting, or even ordinary phrasing that has a sing-song cadence, without referring to a formally named classical meter.
Synonyms: लय (lay, rhythm/flow, broader musical sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will hear this extended sense when someone praises how a speech or bhajan flows -- इसमें एक अलग छंद है (ismen ek alag chhand hai, 'it has a certain rhythm to it') -- even if no one could name the actual meter being used.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी बातचीत में एक अलग छंद है। There's a certain rhythm to the way he talks.
(uskii baatchiit men ek alag chhand hai।)
