ढाँचा (dhaanchaa)

noun Primarily a physical/structural noun -- the frame, skeleton, or built structure of something -- extended figuratively to abstract frameworks like a plan, system, or organizational setup.
Sense 1 : physical frame or skeleton (of a building, vehicle, etc.)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कंकाल (kankaal, skeleton, of a body/building), संरचना (sanrachnaa, formal), खाका (khaakaa, outline/sketch of a structure)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Drive past any construction site in Delhi-NCR and you will hear workers and passersby refer to the exposed iron-and-concrete frame of a new building as its ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) -- it is the everyday word for 'the bones are up, but it's not a building yet.' The same word covers a car's chassis or a cot's wooden frame.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for इमारत (imaarat) when they mean the frame/skeleton of a building under construction -- इमारत (imaarat) is the finished building itself, while ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) is the bare structure or frame before or beneath the finished form. 2) संरचना (sanrachnaa) is a good synonym but sounds more technical/written; in everyday speech about a half-built house or a vehicle's frame, ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) is the natural word.

Sense 2 : abstract framework, system, or setup (organizational, political, conceptual)
noun, masculine Often appears as ढाँचा तैयार करना (dhaanchaa taiyaar karnaa, 'to draw up a framework') or किसी चीज़ का ढाँचा (kisii chiiz kaa dhaanchaa, 'the framework of something') when talking about plans, policies, or institutions.
Synonyms: व्यवस्था (vyavasthaa, system/arrangement), रूपरेखा (rooprekhaa, outline/blueprint), तंत्र (tantr, formal, system/apparatus)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear in news discussions about a policy's ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) or an exam's syllabus ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) -- Hindi extends the 'building frame' image naturally onto any system that needs to be planned, load-bearing, and filled in later, much like English 'framework' does with 'frame.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to व्यवस्था (vyavasthaa) for any 'system,' but when the sense is specifically 'the underlying skeleton/blueprint that something is built on' (a curriculum's ढाँचा (dhaanchaa), a novel's ढाँचा (dhaanchaa)), ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) is the more natural and vivid choice, carrying over the literal 'frame' imagery. 2) रूपरेखा (rooprekhaa) leans toward a rough sketch/outline still being drafted, while ढाँचा (dhaanchaa) can describe something already fairly solid and load-bearing, even if abstract.
