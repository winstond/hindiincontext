जेठ (jeth)

noun Two unrelated senses: a family-relations noun (husband's elder brother) and the name of a hot summer month in the traditional Hindu calendar.
Sense 1 : husband's elder brother
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In a joint Indian family, in-law relations get very specific names rather than descriptive phrases, and जेठ (jeth) is one of the most commonly used -- a woman will refer to and address her husband's elder brother by this term, often with real social weight attached since he is traditionally treated with particular respect and a bit of formal distance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use भाई (bhaaii) with an adjective for 'elder' to express this relation, but Hindi has a dedicated kinship term -- जेठ (jeth) is the only natural way to say 'husband's elder brother,' just as देवर (devar) is used for the younger one. 2) Do not confuse with जेठा (jethaa), an adjective meaning 'firstborn/eldest,' which is a related but distinct word.

(mere jeth bahut bhale insaan hain।)

Sense 2 : name of a summer month (May-June) in the Hindu calendar
noun, masculine
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: जेठ (jeth) is proverbially the hottest month of the North Indian year, and you will hear people say things like जेठ की दुपहरी (jeth kii dupahrii, 'the midday heat of Jeth') to evoke scorching, oppressive heat -- much like English speakers might invoke 'the dog days of summer.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume Hindi month names map neatly onto a single Gregorian month, but जेठ (jeth) spans roughly mid-May to mid-June, so it is best learned as 'peak summer' rather than pinned to one English month.
