शुरू (shuroo)

verb/noun A single core meaning -- 'beginning/start' -- that shows up as a noun, an adjective-like predicate, and inside the very common compound verb शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa, to start something) and शुरू होना (shuroo honaa, for something to start).
Sense 1 : to start, begin (something)
verb, transitive Compound verb शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa) -- शुरू (shuroo) itself stays invariant here while करना (karnaa) carries all the agreement and ने (ne)-marking.
Synonyms: आरंभ करना (aarambh karnaa, formal), शुरुआत करना (shuruaat karnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa) is the everyday verb Indians use for kicking off anything from a business to a movie to a fight -- चलो शुरू करते हैं (chalo shuroo karte hain, 'let's get started') is a classic way to rally a group into action.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to conjugate शुरू (shuroo) itself (adding gender endings) -- it never changes form; only करना (karnaa) inflects, e.g. मैंने काम शुरू किया (mainne kaam shuroo kiyaa, 'I started the work').
Sample Sentences:
चलो शुरू करते हैं। Come on, let's get started.
(chalo shuroo karte hain।)

Sense 2 : the beginning, start
noun, masculine Often appears in postposition phrases like शुरू से (shuroo se, 'from the beginning') and शुरू में (shuroo men, 'in the beginning').
Synonyms: आरंभ (aarambh, formal), आगाज़ (aagaaz, literary), शुरुआत (shuruaat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear शुरू से लेकर आख़िर तक (shuroo se lekar aakhir tak, 'from beginning to end') a lot when someone is recounting a story or explaining a process step by step -- Hindi speakers love bookending a narrative this way.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शुरुआत (shuruaat) as the only noun for 'beginning' -- शुरू (shuroo) itself works just as well as a noun in phrases like शुरू से (shuroo se) and शुरू में (shuroo men), so both are worth knowing side by side.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to start, begin (of something happening on its own)
verb, intransitive Compound verb शुरू होना (shuroo honaa); the subject is whatever starts (a film, rain, a class), not a person performing the starting.
Synonyms: आरंभ होना (aarambh honaa, formal), शुरुआत होना (shuruaat honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around schedules -- क्लास कब शुरू होगी? (klaas kab shuroo hogii?, 'when will the class start?') -- since Hindi treats events as starting on their own rather than being started by an unnamed agent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this with शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa): फ़िल्म शुरू हुई (film shuroo huii, 'the film started') describes the film starting by itself, while कहीं someone actively starting it needs करना (karnaa) instead.
