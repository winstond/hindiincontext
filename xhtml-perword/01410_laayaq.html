लायक़ (laayaq)

adjective An adjective meaning 'worthy, deserving, capable' -- used both to praise someone's abilities and, in the negative, to dismiss someone as incompetent. Also spelled लायक (laayak) without the nuqta, which is just as common in everyday writing.
Sense 1 : worthy, deserving (of something)
adjective Typically follows a noun or infinitive it governs: तारीफ़ के लायक़ (taariif ke laayaq, 'worthy of praise'), देखने लायक़ (dekhne laayaq, 'worth seeing'). The governed word takes के (ke) before लायक़ (laayaq), or the oblique infinitive directly.
Synonyms: योग्य (yogy), उपयुक्त (upyukt, formal), काबिल (kaabil)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in praise: क्या बात है, आप तो तारीफ़ के लायक़ हैं (kyaa baat hai, aap to taariif ke laayaq hain, 'well done, you deserve praise'). It pairs naturally with काबिल (kaabil), and the two are largely interchangeable in casual speech, though काबिल (kaabil) leans slightly more toward 'capable/skilled' while लायक़ (laayaq) leans toward 'deserving/fit for.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the के (ke) before लायक़ (laayaq) when the following word is a noun, saying तारीफ़ लायक़ (taariif laayaq) instead of तारीफ़ के लायक़ (taariif ke laayaq). 2) With an infinitive, no के (ke) is needed -- देखने लायक़ (dekhne laayaq), not देखने के लायक़ (dekhne ke laayaq) -- learners sometimes insert it by analogy with the noun case.
Sample Sentences:

(aap to taariif ke laayaq hain।)

Sense 2 : capable, competent (of a person, often their general worth)
adjective Used standalone (without a governed noun) to describe a person's overall competence or worth, often in judgmental contexts: वह किसी लायक़ नहीं है (vah kisii laayaq nahiin hai, 'he isn't good for anything').
Synonyms: काबिल (kaabil), समर्थ (samarth, formal), नाकाबिल (naakaabil, antonym: incapable)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind a common scolding line from elders: तुम किसी लायक़ नहीं हो (tum kisii laayaq nahiin ho, 'you're good for nothing') -- a phrase many Hindi speakers will recognize from being told off as children or teenagers, whether in jest or in earnest.
Common Mistakes: 1) In the dismissive negative sense, किसी लायक़ नहीं (kisii laayaq nahiin) is a fixed put-down meaning 'good for nothing' -- learners sometimes try to translate 'good for nothing' more literally and miss this natural idiom.
