कोई न कोई (koii n koii)

pronoun/adjective An indefinite pronoun/adjective meaning 'someone or other' / 'some or other' -- it stresses that the identity is unknown or unimportant, while existence is certain. Built from कोई (koii, 'someone/some') repeated around न (n, 'not'), the same x-न-x skeleton used in चीज़ न चीज़-type expressions.
Sense 1 : someone or other (unspecified person)
pronoun Functions as the subject or object of a clause on its own, standing in for a person whose exact identity is unknown or irrelevant: कोई न कोई तो आएगा (koii n koii to aaegaa, 'someone or other will surely come').
Synonyms: कोई (koii, less emphatic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic Hindi way of saying 'somebody, don't ask me who' -- very common when a mother says कोई न कोई तो घर पर होगा (koii n koii to ghar par hogaa, 'someone or other will be at home'), reassuring without naming names. The same doubling pattern shows up with things (कुछ न कुछ, kuchh n kuchh, 'something or other') and places (कहीं न कहीं, kahiin n kahiin, 'somewhere or other') -- once you know this frame, you can generate all of them.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use plain कोई (koii) when they mean to emphasize 'no matter who, someone will definitely be there' -- कोई न कोई (koii n koii) adds that certainty-of-existence flavor that कोई alone lacks. 2) Don't mistake the न (n) here for a negation of the sentence -- the whole phrase is affirmative; the न is purely structural, not a 'not'.

Sense 2 : some ... or other (unspecified, modifying a noun)
adjective Precedes a noun the way कोई (koii) normally does, but insists that some instance definitely exists even though which one is unspecified: कोई न कोई बहाना (koii n koii bahaanaa, 'some excuse or other').
Synonyms: कोई (koii, less emphatic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Perfect for the classic parental complaint तुम्हें कोई न कोई बहाना मिल जाता है (tumhen koii n koii bahaanaa mil jaataa hai, 'you always find some excuse or other') -- a very Delhi-household way of expressing mild exasperation at a pattern rather than one specific instance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert the noun between कोई and न कोई (e.g. wrongly splitting the phrase) -- the noun always comes after the full कोई न कोई (koii n koii) unit, not inside it.
