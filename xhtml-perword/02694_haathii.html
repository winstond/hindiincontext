हाथी (haathii)

noun A single, straightforward sense: 'elephant' -- both the literal animal and a small set of common figurative uses built on the image of an elephant's size and grandeur.
Sense 1 : elephant (the animal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गज (gaj, literary), हस्ती (hastii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Elephants are woven into everyday Indian life, not just zoos and forests -- from temple processions in the south to wedding baraats in the north, and of course Ganesh ji's (ganesh jii) head. In Delhi you're most likely to meet हाथी (haathii) in idioms, kids' rhymes, or the chess piece (the 'elephant' is actually the bishop in Indian chaupar/chess terminology).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget हाथी (haathii) is masculine even when referring to a female elephant -- gender here tracks the word, not the animal's biological sex, unlike some animal names in English usage.

Sense 2 : something huge, unwieldy, or hard to maintain (figurative)
noun, masculine Used attributively or in comparison to describe something oversized or costly to upkeep, often in the set phrase सफ़ेद हाथी (safed haathii, 'white elephant' -- a costly, useless burden), which is a direct calque of the English idiom.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear सफ़ेद हाथी (safed haathii) used in news and everyday griping about government projects -- a big stadium or scheme that costs a fortune to maintain and does nobody much good.
Common Mistakes: 1) सफ़ेद हाथी (safed haathii) is borrowed from English idiom, so learners sometimes assume it is a native Hindi image -- worth knowing it isn't a coincidence if you already know the English phrase.
