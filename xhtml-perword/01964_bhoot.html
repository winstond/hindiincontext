भूत (bhoot)

noun/adjective Mainly known as the everyday word for 'ghost' or 'spirit,' but it also carries an older sense of 'past' (as in grammar or philosophy) and shows up in a few common idioms about being obsessed or possessed.
Sense 1 : ghost, spirit
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रेत (pret), रूह (rooh), साया (saayaa, shadow-presence sense), चुड़ैल (chudail, female ghost specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ghost stories are a staple of Indian childhoods -- grandmothers warning kids not to go near the peepal tree (piipal) after dark because भूत (bhoot) supposedly live there. The word is also used affectionately to scold or tease: बच्चों को भूत का डर दिखाना (bachchon ko bhoot kaa dar dikhaanaa, 'scaring kids with talk of ghosts') is a classic disciplining tactic in many households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat भूत (bhoot) as gender-neutral for all spirits, but चुड़ैल (chudail) is the specific word for a female ghost/witch -- भूत (bhoot) alone defaults to a generic or male-coded spirit. 2) The common phrase भूत लगना (bhoot lagnaa, 'to be possessed/haunted by a ghost') uses लगना (lagnaa) with a को-marked person -- मुझे भूत लगा है (mujhe bhoot lagaa hai, 'a ghost has possessed me') -- not a verb like 'to have.'

Sense 2 : obsessed, possessed (by an idea or habit)
adjective Used in the fixed phrase भूत सवार होना (bhoot savaar honaa, 'for an obsession to take over someone,' literally 'for a ghost to mount someone') -- functions almost like a compound idiom rather than a plain adjective.
Synonyms: पागल (paagal, crazy about, broader sense), दीवाना (diivaanaa, more romantic/passionate connotation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear उस पर पैसे कमाने का भूत सवार है (us par paise kamaane kaa bhoot savaar hai, 'he's obsessed with making money') -- the imagery of a ghost 'riding' someone captures how an obsession feels involuntary, almost like possession, which fits Hindi's broader habit of framing overwhelming states as things that happen to you rather than things you choose.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only really appears inside the idiom भूत सवार होना (bhoot savaar honaa) or similar fixed phrasing -- भूत (bhoot) is not freely used alone as a standalone adjective meaning 'obsessed' the way पागल (paagal) is.
Sample Sentences:
उस पर पैसे कमाने का भूत सवार है। He's obsessed with making money.

Sense 3 : the past (grammatical or philosophical)
noun, masculine Common in the grammar term भूतकाल (bhootkaal, 'past tense') and in more formal or philosophical speech about time.
Synonyms: अतीत (atiit)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: If you've studied Hindi verb tenses, you've already met this sense: भूतकाल (bhootkaal) is literally 'ghost-time,' a poetic way of naming the past tense, since what is past has become like a ghost -- present in influence but gone in substance.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday conversation about 'the past' (like reminiscing), speakers reach for अतीत (atiit) or पुराना ज़माना (puraanaa zamaanaa) far more often than भूत (bhoot) alone -- this sense of भूत (bhoot) is mostly confined to grammar terminology (भूतकाल, bhootkaal) or formal/philosophical registers.
Sample Sentences:
व्याकरण में भूतकाल का अर्थ है बीता हुआ समय। In grammar, 'bhutkal' means past tense.
(vyaakran men bhootkaal kaa arth hai biitaa huaa samay।)
