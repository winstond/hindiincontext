बकरा (bakraa)

noun Literally a male goat; also a very common colloquial metaphor for someone who gets exploited, cheated, or made a scapegoat/victim in a scheme.
Sense 1 : male goat
noun, masculine
Synonyms: छेला (chhelaa, regional/less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बकरा (bakraa) is the animal families across North India buy and fatten before Bakr-Eid (bakriid) for sacrifice, which is exactly why the word also carries the 'sacrificial victim' sense below -- the metaphor comes straight from the ritual.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use बकरा (bakraa) for any goat regardless of sex; बकरी (bakrii) is the female goat and is the more general word used in idioms about goats/milk, so don't default to बकरा (bakraa) when गender is unspecified or female is meant.

Sense 2 : dupe, scapegoat, easy target (figurative)
noun, masculine Most often seen in the fixed expression बकरा बनाना (bakraa banaanaa, 'to make someone a scapegoat/fool, to dupe someone') and बकरा बनना (bakraa bannaa, 'to become the dupe/fall for it').
Synonyms: शिकार (shikaar, broader: 'prey/victim' in general)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi bazaar and office-banter language -- if a friend gets conned into paying the whole bill or believing an obvious lie, someone will laugh and say उसे बकरा बना दिया (use bakraa banaa diyaa, 'they made him the goat'), painting the person as led to slaughter just like the animal at Eid.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is almost always used with बनाना (banaanaa) or बनना (bannaa) -- saying बकरा (bakraa) alone to mean 'victim' sounds incomplete to native ears without that verb frame. 2) It's informal; avoid it in formal writing or polite/professional contexts where शिकार (shikaar) or धोखे का शिकार (dhokhe kaa shikaar, 'victim of deceit') fits better.
