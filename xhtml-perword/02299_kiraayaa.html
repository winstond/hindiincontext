किराया (kiraayaa)

noun A single core meaning: money paid for the temporary use of something -- rent, fare, or hire charge -- with the exact English word depending on what is being rented (a house, an auto, a bicycle).
Sense 1 : rent (for housing or property)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भाड़ा (bhaadaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi-NCR conversation about flats and PGs, किराया (kiraayaa) is almost always paired with एडवांस (edvaans, 'security deposit') -- landlords and tenants routinely discuss both together, e.g. asking किराया कितना है? (kiraayaa kitnaa hai, 'what's the rent?') before even seeing the place.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say किराया देना (kiraayaa denaa) when they mean 'pay the rent' -- this is correct, but many then default to a literal 'pay' verb like पे करना (pe karnaa) instead, which sounds off in this context; किराया भरना (kiraayaa bharnaa, 'to pay up the rent') is also very common and worth learning alongside देना (denaa). 2) The verb बढ़ना (badhnaa) is used for rent 'going up' (किराया बढ़ गया (kiraayaa badh gayaa, 'the rent went up')) -- learners sometimes reach for होना (honaa) instead.
Sample Sentences:
इस फ्लैट का किराया कितना है? How much is the rent for this flat?
(is phlait kaa kiraayaa kitnaa hai?)

Sense 2 : fare (for transport)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भाड़ा (bhaadaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask an auto or e-rickshaw driver किराया कितना लगेगा? (kiraayaa kitnaa lagegaa, 'how much will the fare come to?') and you'll get a quick verbal quote -- meters are rare outside app-based cabs, so this phrase is a genuinely useful survival line in Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) With autos and rickshaws, bargaining over किराया (kiraayaa) is normal and learners often don't realize the quoted price is a starting point, not fixed -- किराया तय करना (kiraayaa tay karnaa, 'to settle/fix the fare') is the phrase used before getting in.
Sample Sentences:
ऑटो वाले से पूछो, किराया कितना लगेगा। Ask the auto driver how much the fare will come to.

Sense 3 : hire charge (for renting an object, e.g. a vehicle, equipment, costume)
noun, masculine किराए पर लेना (kiraae par lenaa, 'to take on rent/hire') and किराए पर देना (kiraae par denaa, 'to give out on rent/hire') are the standard frames for this sense, covering everything from bicycles to wedding décor.
Synonyms: भाड़ा (bhaadaa, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: किराए पर (kiraae par) is the everyday frame you'll see on shop signboards -- साड़ी किराए पर मिलती है (saadii kiraae par miltii hai, 'saris are available on hire') for wedding season, or गाड़ी किराए पर (gaadii kiraae par, 'car for hire') outside train stations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say पर किराया (par kiraayaa) instead of the fixed postposition-first order किराए पर (kiraae par) -- the oblique form किराए (kiraae) plus पर (par) is the fixed idiom, not a free word order choice.
