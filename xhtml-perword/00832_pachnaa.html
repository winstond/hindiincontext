पचना (pachnaa)

verb Primarily a physical verb meaning 'to be digested,' with a common figurative extension meaning 'to be absorbed/accepted' (an idea, insult, piece of news, or money) -- often used in negative constructions when something is hard to accept.
Sense 1 : to be digested (food)
verb, intransitive The food is the grammatical subject, not the person: खाना पचता है (khaanaa pachtaa hai, 'the food gets digested'), not the person 'digesting' it in an active sense.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Digestion is a favorite topic of everyday Hindi small talk, especially around heavy or oily food -- you'll hear things like तला हुआ खाना नहीं पचता (talaa huaa khaanaa nahiin pachtaa, 'fried food doesn't digest well') at family gatherings. This intransitive verb pairs with the causative पचाना (pachaanaa, 'to digest something, cause it to be digested'), which shifts agency onto a person or the stomach.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person the subject as in English ('I digested the food'), but Hindi puts the food itself as subject: यह खाना नहीं पचता (yah khaanaa nahiin pachtaa, 'this food doesn't digest well'), with the person optionally marked by से (se) or को (ko) if mentioned at all -- मुझसे यह नहीं पचता (mujhse yah nahiin pachtaa).
Sample Sentences:
तला हुआ खाना नहीं पचता। Fried food doesn't digest well.
(talaa huaa khaanaa nahiin pachtaa।)

Sense 2 : to be accepted/absorbed (figurative: an idea, insult, situation)
verb, intransitive Very commonly used in the negative to describe something a person cannot emotionally accept: उसकी तरक्की मुझसे नहीं पचती (uskii tarakkii mujhse nahiin pachtii, 'I can't stomach his promotion'), with the experiencer marked by से (se).
Synonyms: गले उतरना (gale utarnaa, 'to go down the throat,' idiomatic near-synonym for accepting an idea), बर्दाश्त होना (bardaasht honaa, to be tolerated)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb Hindi speakers reach for when someone's good fortune, promotion, or success is hard for others to swallow -- a very Delhi office/family scenario: पड़ोसी की नई गाड़ी किसी को नहीं पची (padosii kii naii gaadii kisii ko nahiin pachii, 'nobody could digest the neighbor's new car'). It captures envy and grudging non-acceptance in a wonderfully physical, food-rooted metaphor typical of Hindi's body-as-agent idiom style.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost always appears negated (नहीं पचना nahiin pachnaa) -- a positive statement like 'यह बात पचती है' sounds odd; native speakers reach for this verb specifically to express resentment or disbelief. 2) The experiencer takes से (se), not को (ko), which trips up learners used to दर्द होना (dard honaa)-style को-constructions.

(padosii kii naii gaadii kisii ko nahiin pachii।)
