सौ (sau)

numeral/adjective सौ (sau) is the number 'hundred' -- used as a numeral adjective and, idiomatically, to mean 'a great many' or 'countless.'
Sense 1 : the number 100
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Money and prices are where you will hear सौ (sau) constantly -- a सौ का नोट (sau kaa not, 'hundred-rupee note') is the everyday bill people reach for at the सब्ज़ी वाला (sabzii vaalaa, 'vegetable vendor') or auto-rickshaw stand, so this number becomes second nature very fast for anyone living in India.
Common Mistakes: 1) सौ (sau) itself never changes form regardless of the noun's gender or number -- unlike एक (ek) or दो (do), there is no agreement to worry about here. 2) Learners sometimes try to say 'one hundred' as एक सौ (ek sau) when just सौ (sau) alone already means 'a hundred' -- both are heard, but सौ (sau) alone is more natural for the round number itself, while एक सौ (ek sau) is normal when it is followed by more, e.g. एक सौ एक (ek sau ek, 'one hundred one').

Sense 2 : countless, a great many (idiomatic use)
adjective Used before a plural noun to mean 'so many/countless,' not a literal count, e.g. सौ बातें (sau baaten, 'a hundred things/complaints').
Synonyms: अनेक (anek, many), हज़ार (hazaar, used similarly for 'countless')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every language has its favorite number for exaggeration -- Hindi reaches for सौ (sau) the way English reaches for 'a million' or 'a thousand.' You will hear parents say सौ बार बोला है (sau baar bolaa hai, 'I have told you a hundred times') in nearly every Delhi household when a kid forgets to do something -- pure hyperbole, delivered with real feeling.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not meant literally -- सौ बार कहा (sau baar kahaa, 'told you a hundred times') means 'told you many, many times,' not exactly 100. 2) Learners sometimes hunt for a 'real' number nearby in context, but this sense is purely exaggeration for emphasis, much like English 'a hundred times' or 'a million reasons.'
