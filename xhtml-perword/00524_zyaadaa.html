ज़्यादा (zyaadaa)

adjective/adverb A high-frequency word for 'more' or 'too much,' used both as an adjective (quantity) and as an adverb (intensifier, and in comparative constructions with ज़्यादा...से).
Sense 1 : more, excessive (quantity)
adjective
Synonyms: अधिक (adhik, formal), बहुत (bahut, overlaps in 'a lot' sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear shopkeepers in any Delhi bazaar say यह ज़्यादा है (yah zyaadaa hai, 'this is too much') when haggling over price -- it's one of the first negotiation words every learner picks up.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add मात्रा (maatraa) or other quantity words redundantly when ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) alone already conveys 'too much.' 2) In comparisons, remember the structure X, Y से ज़्यादा है (X, Y se zyaadaa hai, 'X is more than Y') -- the standard of comparison takes से (se), not a preposition like 'than' translated literally.

Sense 2 : very, too, excessively (intensifier)
adverb Functions as an intensifier before adjectives or verbs, often signaling that something has crossed a comfortable threshold, e.g. ज़्यादा मत सोचो (zyaadaa mat socho, 'don't overthink').
Synonyms: बहुत (bahut), अत्यधिक (atydhik, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Parents and elders love this word for gentle scolding -- ज़्यादा बोलो मत (zyaadaa bolo mat, 'don't talk too much') is a classic line heard in Indian households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) where बहुत (bahut) would sound more natural for plain positive emphasis ('very good') -- ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) tends to carry a slight 'too much' or critical undertone, especially before adjectives.
