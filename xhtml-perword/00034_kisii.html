किसी (kisii)

pronoun/adjective This is the oblique form of कोई (koii, 'someone/any'), used whenever a postposition follows or the phrase requires oblique case -- for example before को (ko), से (se), or ने (ne), or before an oblique noun.
Sense 1 : someone, anyone (oblique form, used before postpositions)
pronoun Functions as the oblique-case form of कोई (koii); appears with a following postposition (किसी को kisii ko, किसी से kisii se) or as a ने-marked transitive subject (किसी ने kisii ne).
Synonyms: कोई (koii, direct-case counterpart)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of कोई (koii) and किसी (kisii) as one word wearing two hats: कोई (koii) stands alone as subject or object, but the moment a postposition shows up -- को, से, ने, का -- it must switch to किसी (kisii). This is the same shift you already know from मैं (main) becoming मुझे (mujhe) or मुझसे (mujhse).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use कोई (koii) where a postposition follows, saying कोई को (koii ko) instead of the correct किसी को (kisii ko) -- कोई (koii) is direct case only. 2) With transitive verbs in the past, the subject must be किसी ने (kisii ne), not कोई ने (koii ne), even though this looks like an exception until you remember किसी (kisii) is already the oblique form ने (ne) requires.

Sense 2 : some, any (indefinite, modifying an oblique noun)
adjective Precedes a noun that itself is in an oblique context, e.g. किसी दिन (kisii din, 'some day'), किसी वजह से (kisii vajah se, 'for some reason'), किसी किताब में (kisii kitaab men, 'in some book').
Synonyms: कोई (koii, direct-case counterpart, used before a direct-case noun)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear किसी तरह (kisii tarah) constantly in everyday complaining -- 'somehow I managed it' -- and किसी न किसी दिन (kisii n kisii din, 'some day or other') when someone is being vague about plans, a very Delhi way of not committing to a date.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes keep कोई (koii) before a noun that is followed by a postposition -- कोई वजह से (koii vajah se) is wrong; it must be किसी वजह से (kisii vajah se). 2) The fixed phrase किसी तरह (kisii tarah, 'somehow') and किसी न किसी तरह (kisii n kisii tarah, 'one way or another') are idiomatic and worth learning as chunks rather than building from scratch.
