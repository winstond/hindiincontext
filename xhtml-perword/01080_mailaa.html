मैला (mailaa)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning 'dirty, soiled, grimy'; also has a noun sense referring to filth/excrement, most familiar to learners through the term मैला ढोना (mailaa dhonaa, 'to carry night-soil'), tied to the caste-based practice of manual scavenging.
Sense 1 : dirty, soiled
adjective
Synonyms: गंदा (gandaa), मलिन (malin, literary), अशुद्ध (ashuddh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a shirt collar gone grey with sweat and dust, or hands smudged after fieldwork -- that visible, textured griminess is the heart of मैला (mailaa), distinct from the broader, more abstract गंदा (gandaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गंदा (gandaa) by default; मैला (mailaa) leans specifically toward clothes, cloth, or skin that look grimy or unwashed rather than general filth like garbage or a dirty room, where गंदा (gandaa) is more natural.

Sense 2 : filth, excrement
noun, masculine Almost always encountered inside the fixed phrase मैला ढोना (mailaa dhonaa, 'to carry night-soil').
Synonyms: गंदगी (gandgii)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: मैला ढोना (mailaa dhonaa, 'to carry night-soil') names the historical, caste-linked practice of manually removing human waste -- a grim reality now legally banned in India but still invoked in discussions of caste discrimination and Dalit rights, so the phrase carries real social weight, not just a literal cleaning task.
Common Mistakes: 1) This noun sense is narrow and mostly survives inside मैला ढोना (mailaa dhonaa); learners should not use it as a general word for 'trash' or 'dirt' -- that is कचरा (kachraa) or गंदगी (gandgii).
