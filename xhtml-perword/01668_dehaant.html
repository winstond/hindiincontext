देहांत (dehaant)

noun A polite, formal noun meaning 'death, demise' -- used to speak of a person's passing with respect.
Sense 1 : death, demise (of a person)
noun, masculine Used almost always with हो जाना (ho jaanaa) -- देहांत हो गया (dehaant ho gayaa, '[he/she] passed away') -- rather than with करना (karnaa) or a plain existential verb.
Synonyms: मृत्यु (mrityu), मौत (maut), निधन (nidhan, formal), स्वर्गवास (svargvaas, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear at a condolence visit or read in a newspaper obituary -- ऐसा सुनकर बहुत दुख हुआ, उनका देहांत हो गया (aisaa sunkar bahut dukh huaa, unkaa dehaant ho gayaa, 'I am so sorry to hear this, he/she passed away') is the kind of gentle, formal register this word belongs to. Hindi treats death itself as an event that 'happens' to the deceased (हो जाना) rather than something a person 'does,' which reflects a broader pattern of framing loss and misfortune as befalling someone rather than being enacted.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मौत (maut) in every context, but देहांत (dehaant) is the respectful word you use when speaking of someone's death directly to family or in formal announcements -- मौत (maut) can sound blunt or even callous in the same situation. 2) The subject takes का (kaa), not को (ko): उनका देहांत हो गया (unkaa dehaant ho gayaa, 'he passed away'), not उन्हें देहांत हो गया. 3) It is only used for people, never for animals, plants, or abstract things -- मृत्यु (mrityu) is more flexible in that sense.
Sample Sentences:
कल रात उनका देहांत हो गया। He passed away last night.
(kal raat unkaa dehaant ho gayaa।)
