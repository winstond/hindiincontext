प्रवेश (pravesh)

noun A formal/semi-formal noun meaning 'entry' or 'admission' -- into a place, an institution, or (in compound-verb use) as the base of प्रवेश करना (pravesh karnaa), 'to enter.'
Sense 1 : entry, admission (into a place)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रवेश (pravesh), आगमन (aagman, formal, arrival-flavored)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly on signs: प्रवेश (pravesh, entrance) marking a gate or door, and निषेध (nishedh) paired with it on 'no entry' boards -- प्रवेश निषेध (pravesh nishedh, 'entry prohibited'). It carries the same formal flavor as 'entry' does in English signage versus saying 'come in' out loud.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अंदर आना (andar aanaa) in speech and प्रवेश (pravesh) in writing/signage -- both are correct, but प्रवेश (pravesh) as a bare noun feels formal or official in casual conversation, more natural on a sign or in a notice than in a chat with a friend. 2) The verb form is प्रवेश करना (pravesh karnaa, to enter), not a direct verb of its own -- learners sometimes try to conjugate प्रवेश (pravesh) itself as if it were a verb root.

Sense 2 : admission (to a school, college, or program)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दाख़िला (daakhilaa, everyday word for school/college admission)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian student knows the phrase प्रवेश परीक्षा (pravesh pariikshaa, entrance exam) -- the high-stakes exams for engineering and medical colleges are always called this, never दाख़िला परीक्षा (daakhilaa pariikshaa). This is one place where प्रवेश (pravesh) is the dominant, expected word even in casual speech, because it is baked into the fixed term.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday talk about school or college admission, दाख़िला (daakhilaa) is usually what people actually say -- प्रवेश (pravesh) shows up more in official contexts, like प्रवेश परीक्षा (pravesh pariikshaa, entrance exam) or a university's formal प्रवेश प्रक्रिया (pravesh prakriyaa, admission process).
