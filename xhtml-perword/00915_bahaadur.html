बहादुर (bahaadur)

adjective/noun A loanword (via Persian) meaning 'brave, courageous'; used as an adjective describing courage and, historically, as a title/honorific suffix attached to names and ranks.
Sense 1 : brave, courageous
adjective
Synonyms: साहसी (saahsii), निडर (nidar), वीर (viir, literary), हिम्मती (himmtii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in stories told to kids -- a बहादुर बच्चा (bahaadur bachchaa, 'brave child') is a stock phrase in Hindi children's tales and moral lessons. It also survives as a formal title in some contexts (e.g. historical military ranks like राव बहादुर (raav bahaadur)), which is where the word's Persian-via-Mughal-administration history shows through most clearly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this like a regular -आ adjective and try to inflect it for gender/number (bahaadurii as a feminine form is wrong for the adjective use) -- as an adjective it stays invariant regardless of the noun's gender or number.

Sense 2 : a brave person, hero
noun, masculine Used as a noun of address or description, often affectionately or teasingly toward children or young men, e.g. calling someone बहादुर (bahaadur) to praise or gently mock a show of courage.
Synonyms: वीर (viir, literary), शूरवीर (shoorviir, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Older Delhi households sometimes still use बहादुर (bahaadur) as a generic, slightly old-fashioned way to address a domestic helper or watchman (historically linked to Gorkha/Nepali watchmen often being called by this name-like title) -- younger speakers today would find this usage dated and best avoided in casual conversation.
