करना (karnaa)

verb One of the most common verbs in Hindi: covers 'to do' a general action, 'to make/render' a state, the productive light-verb pattern that builds countless compound verbs (फ़ोन करना (fon karnaa)), and extended senses for holding a job/pursuing study and for behaving in a certain way.
Sense 1 : to do, to perform an action
verb, transitive
Synonyms: अंजाम देना (anjaam denaa, formal), निभाना (nibhaanaa, to fulfill a role)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: करना is the default 'do' verb you reach for constantly in daily conversation -- क्या कर रहे हो? (kyaa kar rahe ho, 'what are you doing?') is one of the first full sentences most learners produce.
Common Mistakes: 1) The perfective past agrees with the object, not the subject: मैंने पढ़ाई की (mainne padhaaii kii, 'I studied') agrees with the feminine पढ़ाई, not with मैंने. 2) For physical creation, बनाना (banaanaa) is usually the right word, not करना: चाय बनाना (chaay banaanaa, 'to make tea'), not चाय करना.
Sample Sentences:
तुम क्या कर रहे हो? What are you doing?
(tum kyaa kar rahe ho?)

Sense 2 : light verb pairing with a noun to form a specific verb (to love, wait, help, talk, marry, etc.)
verb, transitive Combines with an abstract noun to create one verbal meaning: प्यार करना (pyaar karnaa, 'to love'), इंतज़ार करना (intzaar karnaa, 'to wait'), मदद करना (madad karnaa, 'to help'), बात करना (baat karnaa, 'to talk'), शादी करना (shaadii karnaa, 'to marry').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the single most productive word-formation pattern in conversational Hindi -- new verbs for modern concepts get coined this way constantly, like फ़ोन करना (fon karnaa, 'to phone') or टेंशन करना (tenshan karnaa, 'to stress out').
Common Mistakes: 1) Each noun+करना pair takes its own postposition for the 'target': इंतज़ार करना takes का (kaa) -- उसका इंतज़ार करना (uskaa intzaar karnaa, 'to wait for him') -- while मदद करना takes की (kii) -- उसकी मदद करना (uskii madad karnaa, 'to help him'); these don't map directly from English 'wait for/help.'
Sample Sentences:
मैं तुमसे प्यार करता हूँ। I love you.
(main tumse pyaar kartaa hoon।)

Sense 3 : to make, to render (a state), typically adjective + करना
verb, transitive Pairs with an adjective to mean 'make X': साफ़ करना (saaf karnaa, 'to clean'), ठीक करना (thiik karnaa, 'to fix'), बड़ा करना (badaa karnaa, 'to enlarge').
Synonyms: बनाना (banaanaa, overlaps in some contexts)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This adjective-plus-करना pattern is Hindi's everyday causative -- ठीक करना (thiik karnaa) literally 'to make right' covers everything from fixing a leaky tap to patching up an argument.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बनाना (banaanaa) here out of habit: कमरा साफ़ करो (kamraa saaf karo, 'clean the room') is natural, not कमरा साफ़ बनाओ.

Sense 4 : to engage in, hold, or pursue (a job, course of study, or ongoing activity)
verb, transitive Used with nouns for professions, degrees, or sustained activities: नौकरी करना (naukrii karnaa, 'to hold a job'), MBA करना (MBA karnaa, 'to do an MBA'), UPSC की तैयारी करना (UPSC kii taiyaarii karnaa, 'to prepare for the UPSC exam').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: वह क्या करता है? (vah kyaa kartaa hai, 'what does he do for a living?') is the standard way to ask about someone's profession -- expect this question at every family gathering.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense describes an ongoing state rather than a one-off action, so it favors the habitual: मैं नौकरी करता हूँ (main naukrii kartaa hoon, 'I have a job') is far more natural than a completed-action framing.

Sense 5 : to behave, to act (in a specified manner)
verb, transitive Takes a manner word rather than a concrete object: बच्चों जैसा मत करो (bachchon jaisaa mat karo, 'don't act like a child'), अच्छा करना (achchhaa karnaa, 'to behave well/do the right thing').
Synonyms: पेश आना (pesh aanaa, to treat/behave -- with से), व्यवहार करना (vyavahaar karnaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A parent scolding ऐसा मत करो! (aisaa mat karo, 'don't act like that!') is leaning on this behavioral sense rather than the literal 'do' sense -- the presence of a manner word, not an object, is the giveaway.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes look for a separate 'behave' verb; Hindi just extends करना with जैसा (jaisaa) or a manner word instead of needing a distinct verb.
Sample Sentences:
ऐसा मत करो! Don't act like that!
