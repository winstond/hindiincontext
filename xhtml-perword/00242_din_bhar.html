दिन भर (din bhar)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'all day long, the whole day' -- built from दिन (din, 'day') plus भर (bhar, 'full/entire'), a marker that attaches after time-words to mean 'the whole of.'
Sense 1 : all day long, throughout the day
adverb Functions as an adverbial time expression, placed like other time adverbs before the verb phrase; no postposition is needed.
Synonyms: पूरा दिन (pooraa din), सारा दिन (saaraa din)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in complaints about work or weather: दिन भर काम किया (din bhar kaam kiyaa, 'worked all day long') or दिन भर गरमी रही (din bhar garmii rahii, 'it was hot all day'). The भर (bhar) pattern is productive -- you can build रात भर (raat bhar, 'all night'), साल भर (saal bhar, 'all year') the same way, so learning this one phrase unlocks a whole family of time expressions.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes add a postposition like में (men) after this phrase, saying दिन भर में (din bhar men) when they mean 'all day' -- but दिन भर में actually shifts the meaning to 'within a day/in the course of a day,' which is a different, also valid, but distinct sense. 2) Word order confusion: भर (bhar) always comes right after दिन (din), never before it.
