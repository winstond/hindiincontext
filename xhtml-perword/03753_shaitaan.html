शैतान (shaitaan)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning 'devil, Satan' (from Arabic via Persian/Urdu), with a very common extended adjective/noun use for a mischievous or naughty person, especially a child.
Sense 1 : a mischievous, naughty person (especially a child)
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun of address or description, often affectionately, most typically for children: वह बहुत शैतान है (vah bahut shaitaan hai, 'he/she is very naughty').
Synonyms: शरारती (sharaartii, adjective form, 'mischievous'), नटखट (natkhat, playfully naughty, used for children), बदमाश (badmaash, rascal, slightly stronger)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear constantly around kids in any Indian household -- a grandmother calling a running-around toddler छोटा शैतान (chhotaa shaitaan, 'little devil') is one of the warmest, most common domestic scenes in Hindi. It has completely lost its religious edge in this use.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is affectionate far more often than it is scolding -- a parent calling a toddler शैतान (shaitaan) is usually smiling, not angry, which surprises learners who only know the 'devil' sense. 2) Don't confuse with शरारती (sharaartii), which is the adjective describing behavior; शैतान (shaitaan) here names the person.

Sense 2 : the devil, Satan (as a religious/mythological figure)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: इबलीस (ibliis, literary/Islamic tradition), दानव (daanav, demon, not identical but overlapping), शैतान की औलाद (shaitaan kii aulaad, idiomatic, 'spawn of Satan')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word comes from Arabic shaytaan (shaitaan) via Persian and Urdu, and is shared across Hindi, Urdu, and Islamic religious vocabulary generally. In everyday Delhi speech you will hear it applied to naughty kids far more often than to any actual devil -- that extended sense is its own entry below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as identical to अंग्रेज़ी 'devil' in every context, but in casual speech it almost never carries heavy theological weight -- see the far more common extended sense below for a naughty person.

Sense 3 : mischievous, naughty, devilish (describing a person's nature)
adjective
Synonyms: शरारती (sharaartii), नटखट (natkhat, especially for children), चंचल (chanchal, restless/playful, softer)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: As an adjective it slides easily between describing a child's harmless pranks and, in a harder tone, an adult's genuinely troublesome or cunning streak -- context and tone of voice do the work of distinguishing the two.
Sample Sentences:
