साथी (saathii)

noun A companion or partner word, built on the same root as साथ (saath, 'together/company'); covers a general companion/friend sense as well as the specific 'life partner/spouse' sense.
Sense 1 : companion, friend, fellow traveler in some activity
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दोस्त (dost), मित्र (mitr, formal), संगी (sangii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in compounds like यात्रा-साथी (yaatraa-saathii, 'travel companion') or सह-साथी (sah-saathii, 'co-worker/colleague'), where it always carries the flavor of 'someone alongside you on the road,' echoing its root साथ (saath, 'together').
Common Mistakes: 1) साथी (saathii) implies shared activity or company, not just friendship in general -- for pure 'friend' without that shared-journey sense, दोस्त (dost) is more common. 2) Learners sometimes treat it as invariant like other -ी words, but it is a masculine noun that follows the regular ी/े pattern in oblique/plural.

Sense 2 : life partner, spouse
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जीवनसाथी (jiivansaathii, one-word variant), पति (pati, husband only), पत्नी (patnii, wife only)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: साथी (saathii) is the gender-neutral, modern way to say 'partner' in the marital sense, and it shows up constantly in matrimonial contexts and in the compound जीवनसाथी (jiivansaathii, 'life partner'), which many Delhi families use in everyday conversation about marriage.

Sense 3 : comrade, fellow member of a political or social cause
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कामरेड (kaamred, loanword), सहयोगी (sahyogii, colleague sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is common in political speeches and slogans, where leaders address supporters as साथियों (saathiyon, 'comrades/friends') -- a word you will hear opening many a public address in India, from union rallies to party conventions.
