नमस्कार (namaskaar)

interjection/noun A respectful greeting/salutation word, close cousin of नमस्ते (namaste) but slightly more formal; also used as a noun meaning 'greeting' or 'salutation.'
Sense 1 : formal greeting (hello/goodbye)
interjection
Synonyms: नमस्ते (namaste), प्रणाम (pranaam, used especially toward elders or in touching feet)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear नमस्कार (namaskaar) the moment a Hindi news anchor comes on air, or when a speaker opens a formal event -- it carries a slightly more ceremonial, public-address flavor than the warmer, more intimate नमस्ते (namaste) used between friends and family. Both are accompanied by the same folded-hands gesture (नमस्ते (namaste) mudra), which itself is the literal root of the word: a bow of respect.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat नमस्कार (namaskaar) and नमस्ते (namaste) as fully interchangeable in every context; नमस्कार (namaskaar) leans slightly more formal and is very common in public speeches, radio/TV announcements, and written correspondence, while नमस्ते (namaste) is the default for everyday face-to-face greeting. 2) It works for both 'hello' and 'goodbye' -- learners sometimes hunt for a separate goodbye word when this one already covers it.

Sense 2 : a greeting, salutation, or gesture of respect
noun, masculine Used with verbs like करना (karnaa) -- नमस्कार करना (namaskaar karnaa, 'to offer a greeting') -- to describe the act of greeting someone.
Synonyms: अभिवादन (abhivaadan, formal), प्रणाम (pranaam)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In its noun use you will often hear किसी को नमस्कार करना (kisii ko namaskaar karnaa, 'to greet someone'), for instance describing a student greeting a teacher formally at the start of class -- a small daily ritual of respect that is second nature in Indian schools and offices.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a noun this word takes करना (karnaa), not कहना (kahnaa) -- you 'do' a नमस्कार (namaskaar) rather than 'say' it, even though in English we'd say 'say hello.'
