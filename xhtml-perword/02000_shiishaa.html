शीशा (shiishaa)

noun Mainly means 'mirror' in everyday speech, and also 'glass' as a material or a glass pane (like a window or car window). Both senses are common and closely linked, since a mirror is literally a piece of glass.
Sense 1 : mirror
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आईना (aaiinaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian bathroom and bedroom has a शीशा (shiishaa), and asking 'शीशा कहाँ है? (shiishaa kahaan hai?, 'where is the mirror?')' before stepping out is a household routine. In auto-rickshaws and trucks you will also see the side mirror called शीशा (shiishaa) rather than any English loanword.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for आईना (aaiinaa) as the 'default' word from film songs and forget that शीशा (shiishaa) is at least as common, if not more so, in daily speech -- both are completely standard and interchangeable in most contexts. 2) शीशा देखना (shiishaa dekhnaa, 'to look in the mirror') is the natural phrase; learners sometimes try to add a postposition like में (men) when it is not idiomatically needed with this verb-object pairing, though it is not wrong to say शीशे में देखना (shiishe men dekhnaa) either.

Sense 2 : glass (material) or glass pane
noun, masculine
Synonyms: काँच (kaanch)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear शीशा नीचे करो (shiishaa niiche karo, 'roll the window down') in any auto or car in Delhi, since 'shisha' has become the default word for a car or window pane, not just for a mirror. This double life of the word (mirror and glass) reflects how the object itself, a flat piece of glass, is the common thread.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only know काँच (kaanch) for 'glass' and don't realize शीशा (shiishaa) is equally natural for a window or car glass pane, e.g. गाड़ी का शीशा (gaadii kaa shiishaa, 'the car's window/glass'). 2) शीशा तोड़ना (shiishaa todnaa, 'to break the glass/pane') is the standard idiom for smashing a window, not a literal 'mirror-breaking' phrase even though the same word is used.
