तीर्थ (tiirth)

noun A noun referring to a place of religious pilgrimage; it also extends figuratively to any place regarded as sacred or deeply significant.
Sense 1 : pilgrimage site, holy place
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तीर्थ स्थान (tiirth sthaan), पुण्य स्थल (puny sthal, literary), धाम (dhaam, often used for major pilgrimage sites like Char Dham)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Places like Haridwar (haridvaar), Varanasi (vaaraansii), and Rameswaram (raameshvram) are all called तीर्थ (tiirth) -- the word carries a sense of a whole sacred landscape, often centered on a river or confluence, rather than just a building. The common verb pairing तीर्थ करना (tiirth karnaa) or तीर्थ यात्रा करना (tiirth yaatraa karnaa) means 'to go on a pilgrimage.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse तीर्थ (tiirth) with मंदिर (mandir) -- a मंदिर (mandir) is a single temple building, while तीर्थ (tiirth) refers to a whole town or site of pilgrimage, often with many temples, a river, or other sacred geography. 2) तीर्थ (tiirth) is grammatically masculine even though it names a place, so learners should not default to feminine agreement out of habit with other place-related words.

Sense 2 : figurative: a revered or sanctified place/person/thing
noun, masculine Used metaphorically for something treated with the reverence of a pilgrimage site, e.g. calling one's mother or motherland a तीर्थ (tiirth).
Synonyms: पावन स्थल (paavan sthal, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll hear this extended use in devotional or patriotic speech, like referring to one's mother as मातृ तीर्थ (maatri tiirth, 'the pilgrimage that is one's mother') -- it elevates the ordinary to the sacred, a common poetic move in Hindi.
