सिलसिला (silsilaa)

noun A noun meaning a chain, series, or ongoing sequence of connected events -- literal or figurative -- and by extension a connection or ongoing affair between people.
Sense 1 : a series or sequence of connected events
noun, masculine
Synonyms: क्रम (kram), शृंखला (shrinkhlaa, literary), दौर (daur, round/phase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in news Hindi: बारिश का सिलसिला (baarish kaa silsilaa, 'the spell/run of rain') or हादसों का सिलसिला (haadson kaa silsilaa, 'a string of accidents'). It has a slightly narrative, almost cinematic feel -- like watching one thing lead to another -- which is part of why it turns up so often in song and film titles.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for क्रम (kram) when सिलसिला (silsilaa) is more natural and conversational for talking about a run of events like a string of bad luck or a series of meetings -- क्रम (kram) leans more formal/procedural (order, sequence). 2) The common phrase सिलसिला शुरू होना (silsilaa shuroo honaa, 'for a series/chain to begin') or सिलसिला चलना (silsilaa chalnaa, 'for a series to continue') use होना (honaa) and चलना (chalnaa) as the natural pairing verbs -- learners sometimes force करना (karnaa) here, which sounds odd.
Sample Sentences:
पिछले हफ्ते बारिश का सिलसिला रुक ही नहीं रहा था। Last week the spell of rain just wouldn't stop.
(pichhle haphte baarish kaa silsilaa ruk hii nahiin rahaa thaa।)

Sense 2 : an ongoing connection, affair, or relationship between people
noun, masculine Often used with रखना (rakhnaa) or होना (honaa): सिलसिला रखना (silsilaa rakhnaa, 'to keep up a connection/affair').
Synonyms: रिश्ता (rishtaa), ताल्लुक़ (taalluq, formal), संबंध (sambandh, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is famous partly because of the 1981 film 'Silsila,' about a tangled, ongoing romantic entanglement -- the title itself trades on this exact meaning of a relationship that keeps unfolding rather than being a single event.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense सिलसिला (silsilaa) usually implies something ongoing or repeated (a long-running affair or dealing), not a one-time meeting -- learners sometimes misuse it for a single encounter, where मुलाक़ात (mulaaqaat) or रिश्ता (rishtaa) fits better.
