बिलकुल (bilkul)

adverb/interjection An emphasis word meaning 'absolutely/completely' (adverb) or, in a short reply, 'exactly!/totally!' (interjection). Also commonly spelled बिल्कुल (bilkul) with a nukta-marked conjunct; both forms are used interchangeably by native speakers.
Sense 1 : completely, totally (intensifier)
adverb Functions as an intensifier before adjectives, adverbs, or negatives -- e.g. बिलकुल सही (bilkul sahii, 'completely right') or बिलकुल नहीं (bilkul nahiin, 'not at all').
Synonyms: पूरी तरह (poorii tarah), सर्वथा (sarvthaa, literary), एकदम (ekdam)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Delhi shopkeepers, friends, and family use constantly to add punch to a statement -- बिलकुल सही बात है (bilkul sahii baat hai, 'that's absolutely right') is something you'll hear in almost every animated conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often place बिलकुल (bilkul) after the word it intensifies, as in English word order; native speakers put it right before the adjective or negation, e.g. बिलकुल ठीक (bilkul thiik) not ठीक बिलकुल.

Sense 2 : exactly, absolutely (agreement response)
interjection Used as a standalone emphatic 'yes' in response to a question or statement, without needing a following adjective.
Synonyms: ज़रूर (zaroor), एकदम सही (ekdam sahii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Said on its own with a nod and a smile, बिलकुल! (bilkul) is the enthusiastic cousin of हाँ (haan) -- it signals wholehearted agreement, not just confirmation.
Sample Sentences:
बिलकुल! चलो चलते हैं। Absolutely! Let's go.
(bilkul! chalo chalte hain।)
