विक्रम संवत् (vikram sanvat)

noun A traditional Hindu calendar era (epoch), named for legendary king Vikramaditya, running about 57 years ahead of the Gregorian year; mostly encountered in cultural, religious, and calendar contexts rather than everyday speech.
Sense 1 : the Vikram Samvat era/calendar system
noun, masculine Used mainly to date a year, e.g. विक्रम संवत् 2081 (vikram sanvat 2081, 'Vikram Samvat year 2081'); the number precedes or follows depending on the phrase.
Synonyms: विक्रमी संवत् (vikrmii sanvat, variant form)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will see विक्रम संवत् (vikram sanvat) printed on Hindu calendars (पंचांग, panchaang) alongside the Gregorian date, and it is invoked especially at Hindu festivals like Diwali and for auspicious dates (मुहूर्त, muhoort) for weddings or ceremonies. Many North Indian almanacs and wedding invitations still cite the Vikram Samvat year as a mark of tradition, even though daily life runs on the Gregorian calendar.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume it lines up with the Gregorian year, but Vikram Samvat is roughly 57 years ahead (add 56 or 57 depending on the month) -- so 2081 VS corresponds to about 2024-2025 CE. 2) Don't confuse it with Shaka Samvat (shak sanvat), a different official era used in the Indian national calendar; the two numbers differ by about 135 years.
