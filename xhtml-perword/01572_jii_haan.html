जी हाँ (jii haan)

interjection An emphatic, polite way to say 'yes', combining the respectful particle जी (jii) with हाँ (haan); one sense only.
Sense 1 : yes (polite/emphatic affirmation)
interjection
Synonyms: हाँ (haan, plain version), जी (jii, softer standalone version)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear जी हाँ (jii haan) constantly from shopkeepers, receptionists, and students answering teachers. It signals respect the way 'yes' alone cannot, and pairing it with a slight head-nod or the classic Indian head-tilt is very natural. Compare it with जी नहीं (jii nahiin), the equally polite way to say 'no'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use जी हाँ (jii haan) in casual chats with friends where a plain हाँ (haan) would sound more natural; this phrase carries a note of formality or respect, so it fits better with elders, strangers, or in professional settings. 2) Some learners think जी (jii) here is optional filler; it's actually doing real work, softening and adding courtesy, similar to saying 'yes, sir/madam' in tone though not in literal meaning.
Sample Sentences:
जी हाँ, आपकी बात बिलकुल सही है। Yes indeed, what you're saying is absolutely right.
(jii haan, aapkii baat bilkul sahii hai।)
