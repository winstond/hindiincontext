चालाकी (chaalaakii)

noun A feminine noun meaning cleverness or cunning; the same word covers both admirable quick-wittedness and sly deceit, and context decides which one is meant.
Sense 1 : cunning, slyness, deceitful cleverness
noun, feminine
Synonyms: धूर्तता (dhoorttaa, formal), चालबाज़ी (chaalbaazii), शरारत (sharaarat, mischief, meaning scope narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, चालाकी (chaalaakii) is what you accuse a shopkeeper or a scheming relative of when they get one over on you -- it's the word behind the common warning होशियार रहो, यह चालाकी कर सकता है (hoshiyaar raho, yah chaalaakii kar saktaa hai, 'be careful, he might try something sly'). It shares a root with चालाक (chaalaak, 'clever/cunning' -- the adjective) and चाल (chaal, 'move, trick, gait'), so once you know one you can guess the family.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use चालाकी (chaalaakii) as a flat compliment for 'smart,' but in most everyday sentences it leans negative -- it implies someone got the better of another person through trickery, not just intelligence. 2) चालाकी करना (chaalaakii karnaa, 'to act cunningly/play a trick') is the common verb pairing -- learners sometimes reach for करना (karnaa) with the wrong object structure, forgetting चालाकी (chaalaakii) itself takes से (se) when describing the manner of doing something, as in चालाकी से (chaalaakii se, 'cunningly/slyly').

Sense 2 : quick-wittedness, presence of mind (positive sense)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: होशियारी (hoshiyaarii), फुर्ती (phurtii, meaning scope: quickness/agility more than mental cleverness)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this positive spin when someone praises a kid or an employee for wriggling out of a tight spot smartly -- उसने बड़ी चालाकी से मामला सुलझा लिया (usne badii chaalaakii se maamlaa suljhaa liyaa, 'he resolved the matter very cleverly'). Still, most native speakers reach for होशियारी (hoshiyaarii) or समझदारी (samajhdaarii) when they want unambiguous praise, keeping चालाकी (chaalaakii) for when there's at least a hint of scheming involved.
Common Mistakes: 1) This positive reading only surfaces when context clearly praises someone's resourcefulness (e.g. escaping a tricky situation); without that context, listeners default to the negative 'cunning' sense, so learners should not assume चालाकी (chaalaakii) is a safe, neutral compliment the way 'clever' is in English.
