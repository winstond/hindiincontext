ख़त्म (khatm)

adjective/verb An Urdu-origin loanword meaning 'finished, ended, over'; almost always used with होना (honaa, 'to become finished') or करना (karnaa, 'to finish/end') as a compound predicate rather than as a standalone adjective.
Sense 1 : finished, over, ended
adjective Almost always paired with होना (honaa) to mean 'to be over/finished' -- खाना ख़त्म हो गया (khaanaa khatm ho gayaa, 'the food got finished') -- rather than used as a standalone attributive adjective before a noun.
Synonyms: समाप्त (samaapt, formal), तमाम (tamaam, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily life -- दुकान का सामान ख़त्म हो गया (dukaan kaa saamaan khatm ho gayaa, 'the shop's stock ran out') or मेरा फ़ोन ख़त्म हो गया (meraa fon khatm ho gayaa, 'my phone died') -- Hindi speakers extend 'finished' to running out of things, batteries, patience, and money alike, much like English 'done' or 'out'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect ख़त्म (khatm) like a regular -आ adjective (e.g. adding ी/े for gender/number); it never changes form, no matter what it describes. 2) The nuqta on ख़ (kh) is frequently dropped in casual typing (ख़त्म becomes खत्म (khatm)) -- both are seen, but the dotted form is closer to the original Urdu pronunciation.

Sense 2 : to finish, use up, destroy (something)
verb, transitive Forms the compound verb ख़त्म करना (khatm karnaa); takes a direct object and behaves as an ordinary transitive verb in the perfective (ने-marked subject).
Synonyms: समाप्त करना (samaapt karnaa, formal), मिटाना (mitaanaa, 'to erase/destroy')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Parents telling kids खाना ख़त्म करो (khaanaa khatm karo, 'finish your food') is a household staple; the same phrase in a crime-drama headline about 'finishing off' a rival shows how far the same verb stretches in tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) In a harsher register, ख़त्म करना (khatm karnaa) can mean 'to kill/eliminate' someone, not just 'to finish' a task -- context (a person vs. a task) tells you which sense is meant, and learners sometimes miss the darker reading in news or dramatic contexts.
