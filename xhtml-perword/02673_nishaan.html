निशान (nishaan)

noun Mainly a physical mark or trace left on a surface or the body (nishaan as in a scar, stain, or footprint), extending to an abstract trace or sign of something, and to a target/mark you aim at.
Sense 1 : mark, scar, stain, trace left on a surface or body
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दाग (daag, stain specifically), चिह्न (chihn, formal), छाप (chhaap, imprint)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear at home when someone points to a childhood scar and says गिरने का निशान है (girne kaa nishaan hai, 'it's a mark from falling') -- Hindi loves to name a mark by the event that caused it, tracing the story right back through the postposition का (kaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दाग (daag) for any mark, but दाग (daag) leans toward a stain (like on clothes), while निशान (nishaan) covers scars, scratches, footprints, and marks of all kinds -- it's the more general word. 2) चोट का निशान (chot kaa nishaan, 'mark of an injury') is the natural phrase for a scar, not a literal one-word translation of 'scar'.
Sample Sentences:

(uske haath par jalne kaa nishaan hai।)

Sense 2 : trace, sign, evidence of something (abstract)
noun, masculine Common in the negative construction निशान न होना (nishaan n honaa) or निशान मिटना (nishaan mitnaa), meaning 'no trace to be found' or 'the trace to fade away'.
Synonyms: सुराग (suraag, clue, detective-story context), संकेत (sanket, indication), पता (pataa, whereabouts/trace, informal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in everyday drama, like when someone vanishes and a relative says उसका कोई निशान नहीं मिला (uskaa koii nishaan nahiin milaa, 'no trace of him was found') -- it carries a slight air of mystery or loss, useful for both real conversation and the many missing-person subplots in Hindi cinema.
Common Mistakes: 1) कोई निशान नहीं (koii nishaan nahiin) is the idiomatic way to say 'no trace at all' -- learners sometimes construct something clunkier when this fixed phrase is what a native speaker reaches for.
Sample Sentences:

(pulis ko abhii tak uskaa koii nishaan nahiin milaa।)

Sense 3 : target, mark to aim at
noun, masculine Chiefly seen in the fixed phrase निशाना लगाना (nishaanaa lagaanaa, 'to take aim') -- note this common collocation actually uses the related form निशाना (nishaanaa), not निशान (nishaan) itself; the two are closely linked but distinct words.
Synonyms: लक्ष्य (lakshy, goal/target, more formal and also used figuratively for 'aim in life')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense survives mostly in its cousin word निशाना (nishaanaa), used for aiming a gun or an arrow; निशान (nishaan) as 'target' itself is a bit dated, so treat this as background context rather than something to actively use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mix up निशान (nishaan) and निशाना (nishaanaa) -- for 'to aim at a target' you need निशाना (nishaanaa), while निशान (nishaan) itself rarely appears in the archery/shooting sense in everyday speech anymore.
