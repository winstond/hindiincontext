आदर्श (aadarsh)

noun/adjective A word borrowed straight from formal/moral vocabulary: as a noun it means 'an ideal' (a model or standard of excellence), and as an adjective it means 'ideal, model' (describing something that exemplifies excellence).
Sense 1 : an ideal, a model of excellence
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मानक (maanak, standard), प्रतिमान (pratimaan, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Indian schools, essays, and civics textbooks lean heavily on this word -- students are constantly asked to write about their 'आदर्श व्यक्तित्व' (aadarsh vyaktitv, 'ideal personality'), often Gandhi or a parent. It carries a slightly earnest, moralizing flavor, the kind of word a teacher or a political speech uses more than casual chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat आदर्श (aadarsh) as interchangeable with सपना (sapnaa, 'dream'), but आदर्श (aadarsh) is a standard or principle to live up to, not a wish or aspiration. 2) It is often used with रखना (rakhnaa, 'to hold/keep') or सामने रखना (saamne rakhnaa, 'to hold up as an example'), e.g. किसी को आदर्श मानना (kisii ko aadarsh maannaa, 'to regard someone as a role model').

Sense 2 : ideal, model, exemplary
adjective
Synonyms: उत्तम (uttam, excellent), अनुकरणीय (anukarniiy, literary, 'worthy of emulation')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this constantly in compound phrases like आदर्श पत्नी (aadarsh patnii, 'ideal wife') or आदर्श गाँव (aadarsh gaanv, 'model village') -- the latter tied to real government schemes for village development, which gives the word a civic, almost bureaucratic ring alongside its moral one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because it looks and sounds the same as the noun, learners sometimes struggle to tell from context whether आदर्श (aadarsh) is describing a noun ('an ideal teacher') or standing alone as 'an ideal' -- word order and the presence of a following noun (e.g. आदर्श शिक्षक (aadarsh shikshak, 'ideal teacher')) is the clue.
