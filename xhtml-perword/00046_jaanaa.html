जाना (jaanaa)

verb जाना (jaanaa) means 'to go' at its core, but two of its most important jobs are grammatical rather than lexical: it works as a completive/change-of-state vector verb attached to another verb's stem (V + जाना), and as the auxiliary that forms the passive voice (V + जाना). See also आना (aanaa, 'to come'), its natural pair.
Sense 1 : to go (motion from here to there)
verb, intransitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear जाना (jaanaa) constantly in Delhi: ऑफ़िस जाना (ofis jaanaa, 'to go to the office'), घर जाना (ghar jaanaa, 'to go home'). It's the natural partner of आना (aanaa, 'to come'), the same way English contrasts 'come' and 'go'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Concrete destinations usually skip को: स्कूल जाना है (skool jaanaa hai, 'have to go to school'), not स्कूल को जाना है -- को sounds stilted here even though the noun is a destination. 2) Don't confuse with चलना (chalnaa), which means 'to walk/move' rather than 'to go somewhere' as a destination verb.

Sense 2 : completive/change-of-state vector verb attached to another verb's stem (V + जाना)
verb Attaches to the stem of a main verb (V + जाना) to add a sense of completeness, suddenness, or a shift into a new state, rather than contributing its own literal 'go' meaning: हो जाना (ho jaanaa, 'to become'), सो जाना (so jaanaa, 'to fall asleep'), मर जाना (mar jaanaa, 'to die'), समझ जाना (samajh jaanaa, 'to suddenly get it'), चला जाना (chalaa jaanaa, 'to leave/depart'), गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa, 'to pass away/pass by').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb every learner needs to master compound verbs: सो जाना, मर जाना, समझ जाना, चला जाना all add a flavor of completeness or suddenness that the bare main verb alone doesn't carry. Think of जाना here as grammatical seasoning, not literal movement -- it's one of the most-used building blocks in natural spoken Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) ने-marking exception: वह खाना खा गया (vah khaanaa khaa gayaa, 'he ate up the food') takes no ने even though खाना (khaanaa) alone is a transitive, ने-taking verb -- because जाना itself is intransitive, agreement in the compound follows जाना, not the main verb. 2) Don't read चला जाना (chalaa jaanaa) or गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa) as जाना carrying a 'depart/pass away' meaning on its own -- that sense lives in the specific compound, not in जाना by itself.

Sense 3 : auxiliary forming the passive voice (V + जाना)
verb Combines with a main verb's perfective participle to build the passive: किया जाना (kiyaa jaanaa, 'to be done'), खाया जाना (khaayaa jaanaa, 'to be eaten'). The logical agent, if stated, is marked with से (se), not ने or को.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You meet this construction on signs and in slightly formal speech: यहाँ धूम्रपान नहीं किया जाता (yahaan dhoomrpaan nahiin kiyaa jaataa, 'smoking is not done here'). It's less common in casual chat than the active voice, but essential for reading notices, rules, and formal writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) The logical agent takes से, not ने: मुझसे यह काम नहीं किया गया (mujhse yah kaam nahiin kiyaa gayaa, 'this work couldn't be done by me') -- learners used to ने-marked agents in the simple past often reach for ने here by mistake. 2) This passive often reads as inability or involuntariness rather than a flat 'was done by': मुझसे चला नहीं जाता (mujhse chalaa nahiin jaataa, 'I'm not able to walk') describes incapacity, not just passive action.
