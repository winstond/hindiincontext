लोक- ([[लोक-]])

noun A combining prefix/root meaning 'folk, people, the common world' -- it rarely stands alone and instead attaches to other nouns to build compounds like लोकतंत्र ([[लोकतंत्र]], 'democracy') or लोकगीत ([[लोकगीत]], 'folk song'). One core sense covers all its compound uses: 'of the people, of the folk, of this world.'
Sense 1 : the common people, the folk, this world (combining form)
noun Functions as the first element of a compound noun rather than as a free-standing word; the second element supplies the specific meaning, e.g. लोक + गीत ([[गीत]], 'song') = लोकगीत ([[लोकगीत]], 'folk song'), लोक + कथा ([[कथा]], 'story') = लोककथा ([[लोककथा]], 'folk tale').
Synonyms: जन ([[जन]], combining form, 'people/folk'), जनता ([[जनता]], 'the public', standalone word not a prefix)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll meet लोक- ([[लोक-]]) constantly in serious nouns borrowed from Sanskrit-descended vocabulary: लोकतंत्र ([[लोकतंत्र]], 'rule by the people, democracy'), लोकसभा ([[लोकसभा]], the lower house of India's Parliament, literally 'assembly of the people'), and लोक कल्याण ([[लोक कल्याण]], 'public welfare'), a phrase you'll see on government schemes and banners. It also anchors a whole cultural vocabulary of grassroots tradition -- लोक संगीत ([[लोक संगीत]], 'folk music'), लोक नृत्य ([[लोक नृत्य]], 'folk dance') -- so think of it as the difference between 'state/formal' and 'of the people/traditional,' both senses riding on the same root.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use लोक- ([[लोक-]]) as a standalone noun meaning 'people' -- for that everyday sense, reach for लोग ([[लोग]]) or जनता ([[जनता]]) instead; लोक- ([[लोक-]]) almost always shows up glued to another word. 2) Don't confuse लोक- ([[लोक-]]) with लोग ([[लोग]], 'people') -- they look and sound similar but लोक- ([[लोक-]]) is the classical/compounding root while लोग ([[लोग]]) is the ordinary plural noun you'd use in conversation.
