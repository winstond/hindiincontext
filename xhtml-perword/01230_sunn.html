सुन्न (sunn)

adjective An adjective describing physical numbness (a limb 'gone to sleep', or deadened by cold/injury), extended figuratively to a mind or heart frozen by shock, grief, or fear.
Sense 1 : physically numb
adjective
Synonyms: जड़ (jad, of a limb, stiff/numb), शून्य (shoony, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Very common in everyday complaints about sitting cross-legged too long or stepping out into winter cold -- pair पैर (pair, foot/leg) or हाथ (haath, hand) with सुन्न हो जाना (sunn ho jaanaa) and every Delhi household will recognize the scene instantly, someone shaking out a 'dead' leg after sitting on the floor.
Common Mistakes: 1) सुन्न (sunn) describes the body part or person that has lost sensation, so it pairs with होना (honaa) -- हाथ सुन्न हो गया (haath sunn ho gayaa, 'the hand went numb') -- rather than being something you 'do' to the limb.

Sense 2 : mentally/emotionally numb, stunned into blankness
adjective
Synonyms: स्तब्ध (stabdh, formal/literary), बेसुध (besudh, loss of consciousness/awareness)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the physical sense (see word notes) stretched into feeling: shocking news is described as leaving the दिमाग (dimaag) or दिल (dil) 'numb', much like English 'stunned' or 'frozen' -- a natural bridge for English speakers to grasp instantly.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense सुन्न (sunn) usually modifies दिमाग (dimaag, mind) or दिल (dil, heart), not the whole person directly, so learners should say उसका दिमाग सुन्न हो गया (uskaa dimaag sunn ho gayaa, 'his mind went blank/numb') rather than treating the person as the grammatical subject.
