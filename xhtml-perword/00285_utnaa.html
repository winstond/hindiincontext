उतना (utnaa)

adjective/pronoun/adverb The correlative demonstrative meaning 'that much/so much,' the natural partner of जितना (jitnaa, 'as much as'); it inflects for gender/number/case like an adjective and can also stand alone as a pronoun.
Sense 1 : that much (quantity), correlative to जितना
adjective Forms the correlative pair जितना...उतना (jitnaa...utnaa, 'as much...that much') -- the जितना clause almost always comes first, with उतना (utnaa) picking it up in the main clause.
Synonyms: इतना (itnaa, 'this much' -- near-synonym for the near-deictic side)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the quantity-side of Hindi's correlative system, parallel to जैसा...वैसा (jaisaa...vaisaa) for manner and जहाँ...वहाँ (jahaan...vahaan) for place -- once a learner internalizes the जितना-उतना (jitnaa-utnaa) pattern, the whole correlative family starts to feel like one shape rather than separate rules.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop the जितना (jitnaa) half and use उतना (utnaa) alone where English just says 'that much' -- but in the correlative construction both halves are expected together, e.g. जितना समय है, उतना काम करो (jitnaa samay hai, utnaa kaam karo, 'do as much work as there is time for'). 2) The agreement with the noun it modifies (उतना/उतनी/उतने) is easy to forget under the pressure of building the correlative clause.

Sense 2 : that much (as a standalone quantity, not modifying a noun)
pronoun Used without a following noun when the quantity is already understood from context, e.g. बस उतना ही चाहिए (bas utnaa hii chaahie, 'that much alone is needed').
Synonyms: इतना (itnaa, 'this much')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Paired with ही (hii) as उतना ही (utnaa hii), it takes on the flavor of 'that much and no more/exactly that much' -- a very common way to set a limit politely, as when a shopkeeper or host says उतना ही ठीक है (utnaa hii thiik hai, 'that much is just fine').

Sense 3 : to that extent, so (before an adjective/adverb, often correlative with जितना)
adverb Modifies an adjective or adverb to express degree, as in उतना अच्छा नहीं (utnaa achchhaa nahiin, 'not that good'); frequently appears with नहीं (nahiin) in comparisons.
Synonyms: इतना (itnaa, 'so, this much')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A very natural way to soften a comparison in daily speech is वो उतना अच्छा नहीं है (vo utnaa achchhaa nahiin hai, 'it's not that good') -- this degree-adverb use of उतना (utnaa) is what makes such comparisons sound native rather than translated.
Common Mistakes: 1) In comparisons like 'not as good as X,' learners sometimes reach for a direct word-for-word translation of 'as' instead of the जितना...उतना (jitnaa...utnaa) or plain उतना...नहीं (utnaa...nahiin) frame that Hindi actually uses.
Sample Sentences:
