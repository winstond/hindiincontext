व्रत (vrat)

noun Mainly a religious/cultural noun meaning a fast or vow undertaken for religious or personal reasons; also used more loosely for any solemn personal resolve.
Sense 1 : religious fast
noun, masculine
Synonyms: उपवास (upvaas)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Vrat (vrat) is deeply woven into Hindu domestic life -- think of karva chauth (karvaa chauth) or navratri (navraatri) fasts, where women in many North Indian households skip grains or all food for a day as a vow tied to family welfare or devotion. It is less about willpower and more about a sacred promise (the word literally implies a vow), so it carries warmth and reverence rather than austerity alone.
Common Mistakes: 1) व्रत (vrat) is the noun for the observance itself; the common verb pairing is व्रत रखना (vrat rakhnaa, 'to keep a fast') or व्रत करना (vrat karnaa), not व्रत खाना (vrat khaanaa) or similar literal mismatches. 2) Learners sometimes confuse व्रत (vrat) with भूख हड़ताल (bhookh hadtaal, 'hunger strike') -- व्रत (vrat) is devotional/personal, not a protest.

Sense 2 : vow, solemn resolve
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रतिज्ञा (pratigyaa), संकल्प (sankalp)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Beyond fasting, व्रत (vrat) can describe any solemn personal vow, such as a student's vow of discipline or a devotee's vow of silence (मौन व्रत (maun vrat, 'vow of silence')). This sense overlaps with संकल्प (sankalp) but keeps a more religious or ascetic flavor.
