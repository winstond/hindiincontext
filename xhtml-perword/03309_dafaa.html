दफ़ा (dafaa)

noun Two main uses: as a noun meaning 'time/occasion' (as in counting how many times something happens) and as a noun meaning a legal 'section/clause' (as in a section of a law or the penal code).
Sense 1 : occasion, instance, time (as in 'once', 'twice')
noun, feminine Used with numerals or quantifiers, e.g. एक दफ़ा (ek dafaa, 'once'), कई दफ़ा (kaii dafaa, 'several times').
Synonyms: बार (baar), मर्तबा (martbaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday Delhi speech: फिर एक दफ़ा (phir ek dafaa, 'once more') or इस दफ़ा (is dafaa, 'this time') pop up in casual conversation just as often as their बार (baar) equivalents. It has a slightly more colloquial, conversational flavor than बार (baar).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to बार (baar) for 'time/occasion' and forget दफ़ा (dafaa) is a fully natural, common alternative in speech, not a rare or bookish synonym. 2) दफ़ा (dafaa) is feminine, so watch agreement: एक दफ़ा (ek dafaa) not एक दफ़ा वाला (ek dafaa vaalaa).
Sample Sentences:
इस दफ़ा मैं तुम्हारी बात नहीं मानूँगा। This time I won't listen to you.
(is dafaa main tumhaarii baat nahiin maanoongaa।)

Sense 2 : section or clause of a law
noun, feminine Common in legal and news contexts: दफ़ा 302 (dafaa 302, 'Section 302') referring to a section of the penal code.
Synonyms: धारा (dhaaraa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense comes from Persian/Urdu legal terminology and is deeply embedded in Indian police and courtroom culture -- you'll hear it in news reports and crime dramas, as in दफ़ा 420 (dafaa 420) which has become slang for a fraudster (from the cheating section of the old penal code).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with धारा (dhaaraa), which is actually the more standard/official term for legal sections in modern usage; दफ़ा (dafaa) is the older, still-common colloquial and journalistic equivalent, especially in police and crime contexts.
