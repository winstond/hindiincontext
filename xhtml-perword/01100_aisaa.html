ऐसा (aisaa)

adjective/adverb/conjunction A very common demonstrative meaning 'such, this kind of, like this'; it inflects like a regular adjective (ऐसा/ऐसे/ऐसी) and also stands alone as an adverb meaning 'like this, in this way'. Pairs naturally with जैसा (jaisaa) in correlative constructions.
Sense 1 : such, this kind of
adjective Agrees in gender/number with the noun it modifies, standing before it: ऐसा लड़का (aisaa ladkaa, 'such a boy'), ऐसी बात (aisii baat, 'such a thing/matter').
Synonyms: इस प्रकार का (is prakaar kaa, formal), इस तरह का (is tarah kaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear ऐसी बात नहीं है (aisii baat nahiin hai, 'it's not like that') constantly in Delhi conversation as a polite way to deny an accusation or soften a correction -- it's almost a reflex phrase before explaining what's really going on.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget to change the ending to match the noun's gender/number, saying ऐसा लड़की instead of ऐसी लड़की (aisii ladkii). 2) ऐसा (aisaa) points to a *kind* or *quality* ('this sort of'), while इतना (itnaa) points to *amount* ('this much') -- learners sometimes mix the two up when translating 'so much/such a...' from English.

(aisii baat nahiin hai, main bas thakaa huaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : in this way, like this
adverb Used invariantly (ऐसा (aisaa) form) before a verb to describe manner: ऐसा मत करो (aisaa mat karo, 'don't do it like this').
Synonyms: इस तरह (is tarah), इस प्रकार (is prakaar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: ऐसा क्यों? (aisaa kyon, 'why like this / why so?') is a handy short question you'll hear when someone reacts to a surprising turn of events -- much punchier than spelling out the whole sentence.

Sense 3 : correlative 'such...that' construction
conjunction Works with कि (ki) to form 'such...that' clauses: ऐसा हुआ कि... (aisaa huaa ki, 'it so happened that...'), or with जैसा (jaisaa) in 'as...so' correlatives: जैसा बोओगे, ऐसा काटोगे (jaisaa booge, aisaa kaatoge, 'as you sow, so shall you reap').
Synonyms: इतना (itnaa, used for degree rather than kind)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जैसा बोओगे, ऐसा काटोगे (jaisaa booge, aisaa kaatoge, 'as you sow, so shall you reap') is a proverb every Hindi speaker grows up hearing from parents and teachers -- a good anchor for how जैसा (jaisaa) and ऐसा (aisaa) work as a matched pair.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often leave out कि (ki) after ऐसा (aisaa) when building a result clause, producing an incomplete-sounding sentence. 2) The जैसा...ऐसा (jaisaa...aisaa) pairing is a fixed correlative frame; learners sometimes try to use ऐसा (aisaa) alone where the जैसा (jaisaa) half is required for the comparison to make sense.
