सहारा (sahaaraa)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'support, prop, reliance' (physical or emotional/financial), with a common idiomatic verb pairing सहारा लेना (sahaaraa lenaa, 'to take support/rely on').
Sense 1 : emotional, financial, or social support; reliance, dependence on someone
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भरोसा (bharosaa, trust/reliance), आसरा (aasraa, variant sense overlap), टेक (tek)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind lines like बुढ़ापे का सहारा (budhaape kaa sahaaraa, 'support for one's old age'), often said of children who are expected to look after aging parents -- a very common frame in Hindi family conversation and film dialogue about duty and dependence.
Common Mistakes: 1) सहारा है (sahaaraa hai) is often used with a को (ko)-marked person to mean 'X is Y's support/mainstay', e.g. वह मेरा सहारा है (vah meraa sahaaraa hai, 'he/she is my support') -- learners sometimes reach for भरोसा (bharosaa) here, which is closer to 'trust' than to 'being someone's mainstay.'

(betaa hii budhaape kaa sahaaraa hotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : physical support or prop (something to lean on or hold)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: टेक (tek), आधार (aadhaar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture an elderly person walking with a stick or holding a wall for balance -- that's the core image of सहारा (sahaaraa). It's a very tactile, physical sense before it extends to the emotional one below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the verb सहारा लेना (sahaaraa lenaa) is the natural way to say 'to take support (of something)' -- it takes का (kaa) on the thing being leaned on, e.g. दीवार का सहारा लेना (diivaar kaa sahaaraa lenaa, 'to lean on the wall for support'), not पर (par) as an English speaker might expect.

Sense 3 : pretext, excuse, or means used to justify or accomplish something (idiomatic extension)
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively in the frame X के सहारे (ke sahaare, 'by means of / relying on X'), where सहारे (sahaare) functions like a postposition-ish phrase meaning 'with the help of, on the strength of.'
Synonyms: बहाना (bahaanaa, closer to 'excuse' specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear के सहारे (ke sahaare) in reflective or slightly poetic speech, like झूठे वादों के सहारे (jhoothe vaadon ke sahaare, 'on the strength of false promises') -- it carries a note of precariousness, of getting by on something thin or uncertain.
Common Mistakes: 1) उम्मीद के सहारे जीना (ummiid ke sahaare jiinaa, 'to live on hope alone') uses के सहारे (ke sahaare) almost like 'on the strength of' -- learners sometimes try को (ko) or से (se) instead of this fixed के सहारे (ke sahaare) construction.
Sample Sentences:
वह झूठे वादों के सहारे चुनाव जीत गया। He won the election on the strength of false promises.
(vah jhoothe vaadon ke sahaare chunaav jiit gayaa।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
