मर्म (marm)

noun A literary/reflective word covering the 'inner essence or heart' of something (the real point or gist) and, closely related, the 'tender, vulnerable spot' of a person -- physical or emotional -- as in the idiom मर्म पर चोट करना (marm par chot karnaa, 'to strike at the heart/hurt deeply').
Sense 1 : the essential point, gist, or true inner meaning of something
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सार (saar), तत्व (tatv), मूल भाव (mool bhaav)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up a lot in discussions of scripture, poetry, or philosophy -- a teacher might say कहानी का मर्म (kahaanii kaa marm, 'the deeper meaning of the story') when pointing past the plot to the moral underneath. It has a slightly literary, thoughtful register rather than casual chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मतलब (matlab) or मीनिंग (miining) here, but मर्म (marm) is reserved for the deeper, almost hidden essence of a text, situation, or philosophy, not the everyday 'meaning of a word.' 2) It is most natural with verbs like समझना (samajhnaa, 'to understand') or पाना (paanaa, 'to grasp'), as in बात का मर्म समझना (baat kaa marm samajhnaa, 'to understand the real point of the matter').

इस कहानी का मर्म समझना इतना आसान नहीं है। Understanding the deeper meaning of this story is not so easy.
(is kahaanii kaa marm samajhnaa itnaa aasaan nahiin hai।)

Sense 2 : a person's emotionally vulnerable or tender spot
noun, masculine Almost always appears inside the fixed phrase मर्म पर चोट करना/लगना (marm par chot karnaa/lagnaa, 'to wound/strike at someone's tender spot') or मर्मस्थल (marmasthal) in compound form.
Synonyms: दुखती रग (dukhtii rag, idiomatic near-synonym)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in emotionally charged speech or writing -- a friend might say उसने मेरे मर्म पर चोट की (usne mere marm par chot kii, 'she struck at my most tender/vulnerable spot') after a cutting remark that landed deeper than expected. It carries real weight, so it is used sparingly and deliberately rather than in casual banter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'wound,' but मर्म (marm) here is the emotional core itself, not the injury -- चोट (chot) supplies the 'wound/blow' part in the common phrase. 2) Don't confuse with दिल (dil, 'heart') in its everyday romantic sense; मर्म (marm) is specifically about the point where someone is most easily hurt.
