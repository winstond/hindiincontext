जुलूस निकालना (juloos nikaalnaa)

verb A fixed verb-phrase meaning to hold or take out a procession/rally, built from जुलूस (juloos, 'procession') and निकालना (nikaalnaa, 'to take out'). One core sense; also used figuratively to mean publicly humiliating or dragging someone through the mud.
Sense 1 : to hold/take out a procession or rally
verb, transitive निकालना (nikaalnaa) is used here in its literal sense of 'bringing out/leading out onto the street' -- the procession is treated as something you extract into public space.
Synonyms: जुलूस निकालना (juloos nikaalnaa), रैली निकालना (railii nikaalnaa, protest march specifically), मार्च निकालना (maarch nikaalnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase is everywhere in Indian news coverage of protests, religious festivals, and political rallies -- from a Ganesh Chaturthi जुलूस (juloos) winding through a mohalla to farmers' unions announcing they will जुलूस निकालेंगे (juloos nikaalenge, 'will take out a procession') in Delhi. It carries a slightly formal, announcement-style register, the kind you'd hear on TV news or in a political speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try निकालना (nikaalnaa) alone to mean 'to march,' but the fixed collocation is जुलूस निकालना (juloos nikaalnaa) -- you take out a procession, you don't just 'go' it. 2) The subject who organizes/leads the procession takes ने (ne) in the past tense, as with any transitive verb with निकालना (nikaalnaa).

(kal shaam ganesh chaturthii kaa juloos nikaalaa jaaegaa।)

Sense 2 : to publicly humiliate, disgrace, or drag someone through the mud
verb, transitive Used with का/की (kaa/kii) marking the person being shamed, as in किसी का जुलूस निकालना (kisii kaa juloos nikaalnaa, 'to parade/humiliate someone'), echoing the old practice of parading a wrongdoer through town as punishment.
Synonyms: बेइज़्ज़त करना (beizzat karnaa), भद्द पिटवाना (bhadd pitvaanaa, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense harks back to a real historical punishment -- offenders were once literally marched through the streets in disgrace -- and the phrase preserves that image. You'll hear it in heated arguments: भरी मीटिंग में उसका जुलूस निकाल दिया (bharii miiting men uskaa juloos nikaal diyaa, 'he was humiliated in front of everyone at the meeting'), a satisfying, punchy way to describe someone being taken down a peg in public.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners take this literally as 'take out someone's procession' -- it actually means to expose and humiliate someone publicly, often in front of others at work, in a family dispute, or on social media. 2) The person shamed is marked with का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke), not से (se) or को (ko) -- e.g. उसका जुलूस निकाल दिया (uskaa juloos nikaal diyaa, 'thoroughly humiliated him').

(bos ne bharii miiting men uskaa juloos nikaal diyaa।)
