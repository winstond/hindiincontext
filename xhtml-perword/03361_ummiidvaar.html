उम्मीदवार (ummiidvaar)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'candidate' -- someone standing for election, applying for a job, or appearing for an exam. Related to उम्मीद (ummiid, 'hope'), since a candidate is literally 'one who holds hope' for a position.
Sense 1 : candidate, applicant
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रत्याशी (pratyaashii, formal, especially for elections), आवेदक (aavedak, for job/exam applicants)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly during Indian election season, on the news and on posters, as parties announce their उम्मीदवार (ummiidvaar) for each seat. It comes from Persian, built on उम्मीद (ummiid, 'hope') plus the agentive suffix -वार, so a candidate is quite literally 'a hope-bearer' -- a lovely image once you notice it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume this word is limited to election contexts because of its frequent use in news Hindi, but it is used just as naturally for job applicants (नौकरी के उम्मीदवार, naukrii ke ummiidvaar, 'job candidates') and exam candidates. 2) The gender is fixed masculine grammatically (ummiidvaar takes masculine agreement on verbs/adjectives) even when referring to a woman -- e.g. वह उम्मीदवार अच्छी है (vah ummiidvaar achchhii hai, 'that [female] candidate is good') shows the adjective agreeing with the woman's gender, but articles/verbs tied to the noun itself stay in the masculine pattern typical of such loan-based nouns.

इस सीट से हमारी पार्टी का उम्मीदवार कौन है? Who is our party's candidate from this seat?
(is siit se hamaarii paartii kaa ummiidvaar kaun hai?)
