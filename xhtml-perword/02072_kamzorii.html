कमज़ोरी (kamzorii)

noun A noun meaning weakness -- covers both physical/health weakness and a personal flaw or soft spot (including a fondness for something). Also spelled कमजोरी (kamjorii) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : physical weakness, feebleness
noun, feminine
Synonyms: निर्बलता (nirbaltaa, literary), दुर्बलता (durbaltaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear at the doctor's or from a worried mother: शरीर में कमज़ोरी है (shariir men kamzorii hai, 'there's weakness in the body') is a very common way to describe feeling run-down, often linked to not eating enough or recovering from illness.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I am weak' with a plain adjective when native speakers frequently prefer the noun in constructions like मुझे कमज़ोरी लग रही है (mujhe kamzorii lag rahii hai, 'I'm feeling weak/faint') -- using लगना (lagnaa) with a dative subject rather than होना (honaa).

बुखार के बाद शरीर में बहुत कमज़ोरी है। There's a lot of weakness in the body after the fever.
(bukhaar ke baad shariir men bahut kamzorii hai।)

Sense 2 : a personal flaw, shortcoming, or weak point
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कमी (kamii, meaning scope: also means 'lack/shortage')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Used the same way English uses 'weakness' in self-assessment -- मेरी कमज़ोरी है कि... (merii kamzorii hai ki..., 'my weakness is that...') is a stock phrase in job interviews and in more personal, reflective conversation alike.

Sense 3 : a soft spot, fondness, or weakness for someone/something
noun, feminine Takes के लिए (ke lie) or की (kii) to mark the object of fondness, e.g. मीठे की कमज़ोरी (miithe kii kamzorii, 'a weakness for sweets').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This affectionate sense shows up constantly in banter -- teasing someone about उसकी कमज़ोरी (uskii kamzorii, 'his/her weakness') for a particular food, film star, or person is a classic bit of Delhi small talk.
