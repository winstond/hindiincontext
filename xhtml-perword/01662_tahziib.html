तहज़ीब (tahziib)

noun A cultured, elevated word for refinement of manners and civilization -- one sense covering both 'good manners/etiquette' and the broader 'culture/civilization' it grows from.
Sense 1 : refined manners, etiquette, courtesy
noun, feminine
Synonyms: शिष्टता (shishttaa, formal), नफ़ासत (nafaasat, refers more to elegance/delicacy), सलीक़ा (saliiqaa, everyday word for 'proper way of doing things')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a word Delhi elders use with real weight -- an Urdu-rooted term tied to the old Mughal-Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb of Delhi and Lucknow, where how you spoke, ate, and greeted guests said everything about your upbringing. Calling someone "बेतहज़ीब" (betahziib, 'lacking manners') is a real insult in this cultural frame, much sharper than just saying they are rude.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शिष्टाचार (shishtaachaar) or अच्छे मैनर्स (achchhe mainars) when तहज़ीब (tahziib) would sound more natural and carry more weight -- it implies manners that come from upbringing and culture, not just politeness in the moment. 2) It is used with होना (honaa) or आना (aanaa) (तहज़ीब आना tahziib aanaa, 'to acquire manners') rather than करना (karnaa).

(uskii tahziib dekhkar har koii uskii taariif kartaa hai।)

Sense 2 : culture, civilization
noun, feminine
Synonyms: संस्कृति (sanskriti)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In this broader sense, तहज़ीब (tahziib) refers to an entire way of life -- you'll hear phrases like गंगा-जमुनी तहज़ीब (gangaa-jamunii tahziib, 'the composite Hindu-Muslim culture of the Ganga-Yamuna plains'), a phrase every North Indian recognizes as shorthand for shared, syncretic culture.

(lakhnaoo kii gangaa-jamunii tahziib poorii duniyaa men mashahoor hai।)
