श्री (shrii)

noun A respectful title placed before a man's name (like 'Mr.'), and also a formal/religious noun meaning auspiciousness, wealth, or splendor, often used as an honorific prefix for deities and revered texts.
Sense 1 : honorific title, 'Mr.'
noun, masculine Placed directly before the name with no inflection: श्री शर्मा (shrii sharmaa, Mr. Sharma).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see श्री (shrii) constantly on wedding invitations, official letters, and nameplates outside Delhi homes -- it carries the same formal courtesy as 'Mr.' or 'Shri' in Indian English. It is also used as a title for revered religious figures and gurus, e.g. श्री राम (shrii raam).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use श्री (shrii) for women, but the feminine equivalent is श्रीमती (shriimtii, Mrs.) for married women or सुश्री (sushrii, Ms.) as a neutral/unmarried option -- श्री (shrii) itself is male-only. 2) It is a written/formal-address convention (letters, invitations, forms, name plates) rather than something said aloud in casual conversation -- you would not typically call a colleague 'श्री (shrii) Verma' out loud the way you might write it.

Sense 2 : auspiciousness, prosperity, splendor (formal/devotional)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: शोभा (shobhaa), समृद्धि (samriddhi, formal), लक्ष्मी (lakshmii, as a personification)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older sense survives mainly inside compounds and set phrases -- श्रीगणेश (shriignesh, 'auspicious start', literally invoking Lord Ganesh) is the classic example, used idiomatically for 'making a beginning' of any task. It also appears as a devotional prefix before deity and text names, like श्रीमद्भगवद्गीता (shriimadbhagvadgiitaa).
