लंगूर (langoor)

noun Refers to the langur, a large long-tailed monkey common across North Indian towns and temples; also used loosely and affectionately (or teasingly) for a mischievous person, especially a child.
Sense 1 : langur (the animal)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Langurs are a familiar sight around temples, government buildings, and hill stations in North India, and are sometimes kept or led around (historically) to scare off smaller, more destructive monkeys -- so you may still hear of a 'langur wallah' (langoor vaalaa) being called in to clear an area of monkeys.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse लंगूर (langoor) with बंदर (bandar, 'monkey' generally) -- लंगूर (langoor) specifically denotes the larger, long-tailed, often grey-faced langur, not any monkey.

Sense 2 : mischievous or gawky person (informal, often affectionate)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बंदर (bandar, used similarly for a mischievous person)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A parent scolding a naughty child might say कहाँ भाग गया, लंगूर? (kahaan bhaag gayaa, langoor?, 'where did you run off to, you little monkey?') -- the tone is fond exasperation, not real insult.
Common Mistakes: 1) This use is informal and can sound rude if directed at someone unfamiliar to you -- it works best affectionately within family or among close friends, similar to calling a child a 'little monkey' in English.
