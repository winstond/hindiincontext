मँगवाना (mangvaanaa)

verb The causative of मँगाना (mangaanaa) itself derived from मागना/माँगना (maangnaa, 'to ask for') -- मँगवाना (mangvaanaa) means 'to have something ordered/sent for' by getting someone else to do the fetching or ordering.
Sense 1 : to have (something) sent for / ordered, via an intermediary
verb, transitive Doubly causative in feel: it typically implies a chain of agency -- 'I had X ask for/order Y' -- often surfacing with से (se) marking the person deputized: उससे खाना मँगवाया (usse khaanaa mangvaayaa, 'had food ordered through him').
Synonyms: मँगाना (mangaanaa, less indirect -- one causative step removed)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word captures a very Indian household dynamic -- you rarely go out yourself for small things; you get someone (a servant, a younger sibling, a delivery boy) to do it. Saying घर पर खाना मँगवा लो (ghar par khaanaa mangvaa lo, 'just get food ordered in') is the classic line when nobody feels like cooking, and it implicitly means 'call up a restaurant/delivery person,' not go get it yourself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use मँगाना (mangaanaa) and मँगवाना (mangvaanaa) interchangeably, but मँगवाना (mangvaanaa) specifically foregrounds a third party doing the actual ordering/fetching on your behalf, while मँगाना (mangaanaa) can be more direct. 2) The person deputized to do the fetching is marked with से (se), not को (ko) -- मैंने वेटर से पानी मँगवाया (mainne vetar se paanii mangvaayaa, 'I had the waiter get water'), not मैंने वेटर को पानी मँगवाया.

माँ ने नौकर से बाज़ार से सब्ज़ी मँगवाई। Mom had the servant get vegetables from the market.
(maan ne naukar se baazaar se sabzii mangvaaii।)
