स्पष्ट (spasht)

adjective/adverb A formal-register word meaning 'clear' or 'obvious' -- most often heard as an adjective describing clarity of speech, thought, or fact, and also common as the adverbial base in the phrase स्पष्ट रूप से (spasht roop se, 'clearly, explicitly').
Sense 1 : clear, obvious, evident
adjective
Synonyms: साफ़ (saaf), स्पष्ट-सा (spasht-saa, rare), प्रत्यक्ष (pratyaksh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear a news anchor or a teacher use, not the word you will hear in the kitchen -- it carries a slightly formal, almost official flavor, the kind of word that shows up in government notices (स्पष्ट निर्देश (spasht nirdesh, 'clear instructions')) and school essays alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for साफ़ (saaf) in every context, but स्पष्ट (spasht) is the word you want in written or formal spoken Hindi -- a report, a policy, an explanation -- while साफ़ (saaf) leans more everyday and physical (a clean room, clear water) alongside its 'clear/obvious' sense. 2) Learners sometimes forget this adjective takes the regular -आ/-ी agreement pattern (स्पष्ट कारण (spasht kaaran, 'a clear reason') vs स्पष्ट बात (spasht baat, 'a clear matter')) -- check the morphology table for the full pattern.

Sense 2 : clearly, explicitly (as part of a fixed phrase)
adverb Functions adverbially almost only inside the fixed phrase स्पष्ट रूप से (spasht roop se, 'clearly, explicitly'), which modifies a verb the way an English adverb would -- स्पष्ट (spasht) alone is not used adverbially without रूप से (roop se).
Synonyms: साफ़ साफ़ (saaf saaf), स्पष्ट रूप से (spasht roop se, fixed phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear स्पष्ट रूप से (spasht roop se) constantly in the news and in formal speeches -- 'उन्होंने स्पष्ट रूप से कहा' (unhonne spasht roop se kahaa, 'he said clearly/explicitly') -- while its everyday cousin साफ़ साफ़ (saaf saaf) is what a mother says when telling her child to speak plainly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop रूप से (roop se) and try to use स्पष्ट (spasht) alone as an adverb (e.g. wanting to say 'clearly' before a verb) -- native speakers need the full phrase स्पष्ट रूप से (spasht roop se) or the more casual साफ़ साफ़ (saaf saaf) for that job.
