घमंड (ghamand)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'pride' or 'arrogance' -- almost always used negatively, unlike English 'pride' which can be positive.
Sense 1 : arrogance, conceit, excessive pride
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अभिमान (abhimaan), गुरूर (guroor), अहंकार (ahankaar, more philosophical, often tied to ego/self)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word behind the common moral scolding घमंड मत करो (ghamand mat karo, 'don't be so arrogant') that elders in Delhi-NCR households use freely -- money, looks, a new job, a foreign trip, all can invite the accusation of घमंड (ghamand). It carries a strong whiff of 'pride goes before a fall,' often paired in cautionary speech with words like नाश (naash, 'ruin') or गिरना (girnaa, 'to fall/be humbled').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for घमंड (ghamand) to translate positive 'pride' (like a parent's pride in a child) -- that sense needs गर्व (garv) instead, which is neutral-to-positive. घमंड (ghamand) is almost always a criticism. 2) The common verb pairing is घमंड करना (ghamand karnaa, 'to be arrogant/to boast') or घमंड होना (ghamand honaa, 'to have/feel pride', often with a को (ko)-marked subject: उसे घमंड है (use ghamand hai, 'he is arrogant')) -- not घमंड लगना (ghamand lagnaa), which is not idiomatic.
