जान निकलना (jaan nikalnaa)

verb A vivid compound expression built on जान (jaan, 'life/vital breath') and निकलना (nikalnaa, 'to emerge/exit'); used almost entirely for intense fear or relief, not literal death.
Sense 1 : to be terrified, scared out of one's wits
verb, intransitive Used with a dative subject: जान + possessor marked by का/की/के or expressed via की जान, e.g. मेरी जान निकल गई (merii jaan nikal gaii, 'I was scared to death'). The verb almost always appears in perfective form with निकल जाना (nikal jaanaa) rather than bare निकलना.
Synonyms: डर के मारे बुरा हाल होना (dar ke maare buraa haal honaa), हवा निकलना (havaa nikalnaa, colloquial), होश उड़ना (hosh udnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly after a near-miss on the road or a sudden jump-scare: मेरी तो जान ही निकल गई! (merii to jaan hii nikal gaii, 'my soul nearly left my body!'). It pairs naturally with दिल की धड़कन बढ़ना (dil kii dhadkan badhnaa) in the same breathless retelling of a scary moment.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat जान as the grammatical subject taking ने (ne), but निकलना (nikalnaa) is intransitive here and the experiencer is marked with का/की/के, not ने. 2) This phrase is figurative even when the trigger is minor (a loud noise, a scare in a movie) -- it does not imply real danger to life.

(achaanak kutte ke bhaunkne se merii to jaan hii nikal gaii!)

Sense 2 : to be utterly exhausted or drained (by hard work, worry, etc.)
verb, intransitive Often occurs with a cause expressed via में or से: काम करते-करते जान निकल गई (kaam karte-karte jaan nikal gaii, 'I was worn out from working').
Synonyms: थक कर चूर होना (thak kar choor honaa), दम निकलना (dam nikalnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A tired rickshaw-puller or an exam-weary student might sigh पढ़ते-पढ़ते जान निकल गई (padhte-padhte jaan nikal gaii, 'studying wore the life out of me') -- the same phrase as the fear-sense, but context (effort vs. shock) tells you which is meant.

Sense 3 : to die, to pass away (literal, of the life-breath leaving the body)
verb, intransitive Literal use is less common in casual conversation than the figurative fear/exhaustion senses; it appears more in narrative, storytelling, or solemn description of a death.
Synonyms: मर जाना (mar jaanaa), प्राण निकलना (praan nikalnaa, literary), दम टूटना (dam tootnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the phrase's original, literal meaning -- जान (jaan) as the vital breath leaving the body at the moment of death -- from which the everyday 'scared to death' sense grew as a natural exaggeration.
Common Mistakes: 1) This literal sense can be confused with the far more frequent figurative 'terrified' sense -- context (a death scene vs. an everyday scare) is the only real disambiguator, since the words themselves don't change.
