सरसों (sarson)

noun A feminine noun referring to the mustard plant and its seeds; also the common short name for mustard oil (sarson kaa tel) in everyday kitchen talk.
Sense 1 : mustard (the plant/crop, or its seed)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture endless yellow सरसों के खेत (sarson ke khet, 'mustard fields') across Punjab and Haryana in winter -- it is practically the visual symbol of the North Indian रबी (rabii) crop season, and the backdrop of countless Bollywood songs shot in mustard fields.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say सरसों का साग (sarson kaa saag, 'mustard greens') meaning the vegetable dish, but सरसों (sarson) alone usually points to the seed/oil-crop sense in casual speech unless the context is clearly about the winter saag. 2) It looks plural (ending in the nasalized ों) but is grammatically singular -- do not add a plural marker.

Sense 2 : mustard oil (short for सरसों का तेल)
noun, feminine In cooking contexts, सरसों (sarson) alone is frequently understood as शॉर्टहैंड for the oil, e.g. सरसों में तड़का लगाना (sarson men tadkaa lagaanaa, 'to temper in mustard oil').
Synonyms: सरसों का तेल (sarson kaa tel, full form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian kitchens, especially Punjabi and Bihari cooking, सरसों का तेल (sarson kaa tel) is the everyday cooking oil of choice, valued for its pungent flavor and used in pickles like आम का अचार (aam kaa achaar, 'mango pickle').
Common Mistakes: 1) Because सरसों (sarson) can mean either the seed or the oil depending on context, learners sometimes mis-hear a recipe instruction -- if someone says सरसों गरम करो (sarson garam karo, 'heat the mustard [oil]'), they mean the oil, not the raw seeds.
