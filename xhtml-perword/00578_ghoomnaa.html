घूमना (ghoomnaa)

verb An intransitive motion verb covering three related ideas: wandering/strolling around, spinning/rotating, and going out to visit or roam a place. All senses share the core image of circular or aimless movement.
Sense 1 : to roam, stroll, wander around
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: टहलना (tahalnaa, strolling specifically), फिरना (phirnaa, wandering/roaming)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind the classic Delhi weekend plan: chalo, ghoomne chalte hain (chalo, ghoomne chalte hain, 'let's go roam around') -- said before heading to a market, a park, or just driving around with no fixed plan. The idea of wandering purely for pleasure, with no destination required, is baked into the word itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चलना (chalnaa) when they mean casual wandering, but चलना (chalnaa) is about walking/moving as a manner of locomotion, while घूमना (ghoomnaa) carries the sense of moving around without a fixed destination. 2) With a place, this verb often takes no postposition at all -- घूमना (ghoomnaa) + place name directly, e.g. बाज़ार घूमना (baazaar ghoomnaa, 'to wander around the market') -- learners sometimes over-insert में (men) where it isn't needed.
Sample Sentences:
चलो, घूमने चलते हैं। Come on, let's go roam around.
(chalo, ghoomne chalte hain।)

Sense 2 : to travel, go sightseeing, visit (a place) for leisure
verb, intransitive Commonly used as the compound घूमने जाना (ghoomne jaanaa, 'to go visit/travel') or घूमने आना (ghoomne aanaa, 'to come visit'), pairing the oblique infinitive with a motion verb.
Synonyms: सैर करना (sair karnaa, excursion/outing framing), भ्रमण करना (bhraman karnaa, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb of the family holiday and the honeymoon alike: हम शिमला घूमने गए (ham shimlaa ghoomne gae, 'we went to visit Shimla') is a completely normal way to describe a whole vacation, no separate 'sightsee' word needed. It's also the polite small-talk question आजकल कहाँ घूम रहे हो? (aajkal kahaan ghoom rahe ho?, 'where have you been traveling these days?').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'I visited Goa' too literally with a verb like देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see'); native speakers say मैं गोवा घूमा (main govaa ghoomaa, 'I traveled around/visited Goa'), treating the trip itself as the roaming. 2) The perfective agrees with the subject as an ordinary intransitive verb -- no ने (ne) is used even though the sentence feels like it has an 'object' (the place visited).

(ham garmiyon men shimlaa ghoomne gae the।)

Sense 3 : to spin, rotate, revolve
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: चक्कर लगाना (chakkar lagaanaa, compound-verb framing, often for repeated circling), घूर्णन करना (ghoornan karnaa, technical/scientific)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a पंखा (pankhaa, 'ceiling fan') घूम रहा है (ghoom rahaa hai, 'is spinning') on a hot Delhi afternoon -- that's the everyday mental image for this sense. It naturally extends to disorientation: दुनिया घूम रही है (duniyaa ghoom rahii hai, 'the world is spinning') is how you'd describe feeling faint or overwhelmed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume this sense is only for machines, but it's equally natural for the head/world 'spinning' in dizziness: सिर घूम रहा है (sir ghoom rahaa hai, 'my head is spinning') -- an involuntary bodily sensation described with a dative-subject-style construction (सिर is the grammatical subject, not the person).
Sample Sentences:
