एकदम (ekdam)

adverb A very common intensifier/adverb meaning 'absolutely, completely' or 'suddenly, all at once' -- the two senses both come from the same idea of 'in one go' but split by whether the emphasis is on degree or on abruptness.
Sense 1 : absolutely, totally, completely (intensifier)
adverb Placed directly before the adjective or adverb it intensifies, e.g. एकदम सही (ekdam sahii, 'totally right').
Synonyms: बिल्कुल (bilkul), पूरी तरह (poorii tarah)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear a Delhi shopkeeper or friend use for emphasis constantly -- एकदम सही (ekdam sahii, 'exactly right'), एकदम बढ़िया (ekdam badhiyaa, 'absolutely great'). It has a punchy, conversational energy that बिल्कुल (bilkul) shares but पूरी तरह (poorii tarah) doesn't quite match.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बहुत (bahut) when they mean a strong, emphatic 'totally' -- एकदम (ekdam) hits harder and sounds more emphatic/colloquial than बहुत (bahut), which is more a plain 'very.' 2) It pairs naturally with words that are already somewhat absolute in meaning (सही sahii, गलत galat, नया nayaa), so 'एकदम बहुत' type stacking sounds off to native ears.

Sense 2 : suddenly, all at once, abruptly
adverb Typically modifies the verb of a clause describing an unexpected change, e.g. वह एकदम रुक गया (vah ekdam ruk gayaa, 'he stopped all of a sudden').
Synonyms: अचानक (achaanak), फ़ौरन (fauran, immediately -- close but emphasizes speed of response more than suddenness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Literally 'one breath/stroke' (एक ek + दम dam) -- the image is of something happening in a single breath, which is why it covers both 'in one complete go' (the intensifier sense) and 'in one sudden instant' (this sense). Knowing that root helps the two meanings feel like one word rather than two unrelated ones.
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this sense with the intensifier sense -- एकदम रुक गया (ekdam ruk gayaa) means 'stopped suddenly,' not 'stopped completely,' even though 'completely' is a tempting literal read for English speakers.
