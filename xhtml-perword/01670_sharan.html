शरण (sharan)

noun A noun meaning refuge or shelter, most often the protective kind you seek from a person, place, or power rather than a physical roof; it appears constantly in the fixed phrase शरण लेना (sharan lenaa) 'to take refuge.'
Sense 1 : refuge, shelter, protection (sought from someone/something)
noun, feminine Almost always used with की (kii) marking what is sought and में (men) or की ओर (kii or) marking where/whom one turns to, as in किसी की शरण में जाना (kisii kii sharan men jaanaa, 'to go into someone's refuge/protection').
Synonyms: पनाह (panaah), आश्रय (aashray, somewhat formal), शरणस्थल (sharnasthal, the place of refuge itself)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries a strong devotional and epic flavor -- in the Ramayana and Mahabharata, characters take शरण (sharan) of gods or kings when all other options are gone, and that sense of last-resort surrender still colors the modern political term शरणार्थी (sharnaarthii, 'refugee'). You will also hear it in bhajans where devotees say they have come to God's शरण (sharan) -- it is not a neutral, bureaucratic word like 'shelter,' it implies humility and total dependence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use it for ordinary physical shelter from rain or cold -- Hindi speakers reserve शरण (sharan) for protection from danger, oppression, or a difficult situation, and would use छत (chhat) or पनाह (panaah) for a literal roof over your head. 2) The verb pairing is शरण लेना (sharan lenaa, 'to take refuge') or शरण देना (sharan denaa, 'to give refuge') -- not शरण करना (sharan karnaa), which sounds wrong to a native ear.
