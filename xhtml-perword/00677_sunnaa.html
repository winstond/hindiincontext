सुनना (sunnaa)

verb The everyday verb for 'to hear' and 'to listen', extending into common senses of 'obeying/heeding' and 'finding out/being told'.
Sense 1 : to hear / to listen to
verb, transitive
Synonyms: श्रवण करना (shravan karnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first verbs learners meet because of the imperative सुनो (suno, 'listen!') or सुनिए (sunie, 'listen, please') -- used constantly in Delhi markets and homes to get someone's attention, much like 'excuse me' or 'listen' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add सुन (sun) as though 'listen to' needed a preposition like English 'to' -- the object is simply direct: गाना सुनना (gaanaa sunnaa, 'to listen to a song'), not गाने को सुनना (gaane ko sunnaa). 2) सुनना (sunnaa) covers both passive 'hearing' and active 'listening', where English sometimes wants two different verbs.
Sample Sentences:
सुनो, ज़रा इधर आओ। Listen, come here for a second.
(suno, zaraa idhar aao।)

Sense 2 : to heed / to obey
verb, transitive Typically used with की बात (kii baat, 'someone's word/advice') as in मेरी बात सुनो (merii baat suno, 'listen to what I say' / 'heed me').
Synonyms: मानना (maannaa), बात मानना (baat maannaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense is the backbone of countless scoldings in Hindi households: बड़ों की बात सुनो (badon kii baat suno, 'listen to your elders') is practically a cultural instruction in itself, tying respect for age to the act of 'hearing'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Parents and elders say तुम मेरी बात नहीं सुनते (tum merii baat nahiin sunte, 'you don't listen to me') to mean 'you don't obey me', not literally 'you can't hear me' -- learners sometimes miss this figurative shift.

Sense 3 : to find out / to be told (news, information)
verb, transitive Common in reported-speech frames like सुना है कि... (sunaa hai ki..., 'I've heard that...') using the perfective passive-like सुना है (sunaa hai) construction.
Synonyms: पता चलना (pataa chalnaa, different construction, dative-subject)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: सुना है (sunaa hai, 'I hear...' / 'rumor has it...') is the classic opener for gossip or secondhand news in Hindi conversation, much like English 'I heard that...' -- it distances the speaker slightly from vouching for the claim.
Common Mistakes: 1) मैंने सुना है (mainne sunaa hai, 'I have heard') keeps ने (ne) even though it feels like a stative report, because सुनना (sunnaa) is a regular transitive verb -- learners sometimes drop ने (ne) by analogy with dative-subject verbs like लगना (lagnaa).
Sample Sentences:
सुना है इस बार बोनस नहीं मिलेगा। I hear that there won't be any bonus this time.
(sunaa hai is baar bonas nahiin milegaa।)
