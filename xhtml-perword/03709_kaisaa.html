कैसा (kaisaa)

adjective/adverb An interrogative/exclamatory word with four senses: asking about condition or state ('how'), asking about type or quality ('what kind of'), an exclamatory use ('what a...!'), and a separate invariant adverb form meaning 'how, by what means' (asking about manner of an action). Inflects as कैसा/कैसी/कैसे (kaise) for the adjective senses; see Notes on the adverb sense for why it does not inflect there.
Sense 1 : condition or state -- 'how'
adjective Used with होना (honaa) to ask about someone's state or condition, agreeing in gender/number with the subject: तुम कैसे हो (tum kaise ho, 'how are you'), तुम कैसी हो (tum kaisii ho, 'how are you,' to a woman).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the first question in almost every friendly exchange in Delhi -- आप कैसे हैं (aap kaise hain) or the more casual क्या हाल है (kyaa haal hai) between friends. Getting the gender agreement right here is one of the small things that makes your Hindi sound natural rather than textbook-ish.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget to change कैसा (kaisaa) to agree with the person being addressed -- कैसे (kaise) for a male or plural, कैसी (kaisii) for a female. 2) Confusing this sense with the manner-adverb कैसे (kaise, 'by what means'), which looks identical in its plural/oblique form but asks a completely different question -- compare तुम कैसे हो (tum kaise ho, 'how are you') with तुमने यह कैसे किया (tumne yah kaise kiyaa, 'how did you do this').

Sense 2 : type or quality -- 'what kind of'
adjective Modifies a following noun and agrees with it in gender/number: कैसा आदमी (kaisaa aadmii, 'what kind of man'), कैसी किताब (kaisii kitaab, 'what kind of book').
Synonyms: किस तरह का (kis tarah kaa), किस प्रकार का (kis prakaar kaa, slightly more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in markets and shops -- आपको कैसा कपड़ा चाहिए (aapko kaisaa kapdaa chaahie, 'what kind of cloth do you want') is a shopkeeper's stock question in Delhi's bazaars.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place कैसा (kaisaa) after the noun it modifies instead of before it, unlike in English where 'what kind of' questions can be phrased more flexibly.

Sense 3 : manner or method -- 'how, by what means'
adverb Asks about the method or manner of an action, not the condition of a person or thing -- modifies the verb rather than a noun: तुमने यह कैसे किया (tumne yah kaise kiyaa, 'how did you do this?'), यह कैसे हुआ (yah kaise huaa, 'how did this happen?'). This form is invariant -- it does not change for gender or number, even though it happens to share its spelling with the masculine plural/oblique form of the adjective कैसा (kaisaa).
Synonyms: किस तरह (kis tarah), किस प्रकार (kis prakaar, slightly more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the कैसे (kaise) you reach for when you genuinely want to know the 'how-to' of something -- यह कैसे बनाते हैं (yah kaise banaate hain, 'how do you make this') is exactly the question you'd ask a friend's mother hovering over a pot in the kitchen, wanting the actual method, not a comment on how the dish is doing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with sense (1), the condition-adjective कैसा/कैसी/कैसे (kaisaa/kaisii/kaise) -- तुम कैसे हो (tum kaise ho, 'how are you,' asking about state) looks similar to तुमने यह कैसे किया (tumne yah kaise kiyaa, 'how did you do this,' asking about method), but they are grammatically different words that happen to overlap in spelling. 2) Because this adverb never changes form, learners sometimes mistakenly try to feminize or pluralize it to match the subject, which is unnecessary and incorrect here.

Sense 4 : exclamatory -- 'what a...!'
adjective Used exclamatorily before a noun, agreeing in gender/number, often with a following adjective: कैसा सुंदर दिन है (kaisaa sundar din hai, 'what a beautiful day it is!').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This exclamatory use carries real emotional color -- often said with a hand gesture or a shake of the head, the way you might say 'what a thing!' in English with real feeling behind it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use क्या (kyaa) here instead, which also has an exclamatory use but combines differently -- क्या दिन है (kyaa din hai) versus कैसा सुंदर दिन है (kaisaa sundar din hai); the two are not always interchangeable in fixed exclamations.
