नर्स (nars)

noun A borrowed word from English 'nurse' -- one straightforward sense, used exactly as in English.
Sense 1 : nurse (medical profession)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: नर्स (nars) is one of many English loanwords that walked straight into everyday Hindi via the colonial-era hospital and healthcare system, alongside डॉक्टर (doktar) and अस्पताल (asptaal, though the latter is originally Persian). In a Delhi hospital or clinic, you will hear this word used constantly and naturally, with no native Hindi equivalent in common use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to make this agree like a native feminine noun in -ई (ी) by changing its ending -- it doesn't inflect that way; it stays नर्स (nars) regardless of number, with plurality shown only on the verb or an added log (log)/log word if needed. 2) Because most nurses encountered in daily life are women, learners assume the word is grammatically restricted to females -- but नर्स (nars) is used for a male nurse too, simply written and pronounced the same way, with agreement following the real-world referent, not a fixed grammatical gender.
