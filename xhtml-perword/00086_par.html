पर (par)

conjunction/postposition Two distinct words sharing a spelling: a very common conjunction meaning 'but', and a postposition meaning 'on/at/upon' (a shorter, everyday alternative to ऊपर (oopar)).
Sense 1 : but, however
conjunction
Synonyms: लेकिन (lekin), मगर (magar), किंतु (kintu, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first 'but' words a learner picks up because it is short and everywhere: in chai-shop arguments, family debates, and Bollywood dialogue alike. Note that it looks identical to the postposition पर (par, 'on'), but the two are unrelated in meaning and you will never confuse them once you see them in a sentence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for लेकिन (lekin) and forget that पर (par) is just as common in speech, especially at the start of a clarifying or contrasting clause. 2) पर (par) can feel slightly softer or more conversational than लेकिन (lekin), but this is a matter of feel, not a hard rule -- both are used constantly in everyday talk.

Sense 2 : on, at, upon
postposition Takes the oblique case on the preceding noun, like other simple postpositions; also used for point-in-time and point-of-contact meanings (मेज़ पर (mez par, 'on the table'), शाम को पर बात करेंगे is wrong -- रविवार पर is also wrong; time expressions like this normally take को, not पर, so पर is reserved for surfaces, points of location, and certain fixed expressions).
Synonyms: ऊपर (oopar, often used for physical 'on top of')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Physically, पर (par) is the workhorse word for anything resting on a surface: चाय मेज़ पर है (chaay mez par hai, 'the tea is on the table'). It also shows up abstractly in expressions of dependence or basis, like इस बात पर (is baat par, 'on this matter/point'), which is a very natural extension once you picture 'on' as the point something rests or depends on.
Common Mistakes: 1) पर (par) is used for a resting surface or a point of contact (मेज़ पर किताब है (mez par kitaab hai, 'the book is on the table')), but learners often overextend it to general location where में (men) ('in') is correct -- कमरे में है, not कमरे पर है, for 'is in the room'. 2) It also marks the object of certain fixed expressions like ध्यान देना (dhyaan denaa) 'to pay attention to', e.g. इस पर ध्यान दो (is par dhyaan do, 'pay attention to this') -- the पर here doesn't translate literally as 'on' in English.
