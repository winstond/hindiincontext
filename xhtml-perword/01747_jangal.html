जंगल (jangal)

noun/adjective A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'forest/jungle,' also used figuratively for any wild, overgrown, or chaotic mess of a place.
Sense 1 : forest, jungle, wilderness
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वन (van, formal), अरण्य (arany, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: जंगल (jangal) is the word Delhi folks use for everything from a dense forest to an overgrown park corner nobody has trimmed in years. It's also the go-to metaphor for chaos: लोग कहते हैं यह तो जंगल राज है (yah to jangal raaj hai, 'this is total lawlessness/jungle rule') when describing a place with no order or authority.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for वन (van) as the default word, but जंगल (jangal) is what people actually say in conversation -- वन (van) shows up mostly in signage, forest-department names, and formal writing. 2) जंगल (jangal) is masculine, so watch agreement: बड़ा जंगल (badaa jangal, 'big forest'), not बड़ी.

Sense 2 : wild, uncultivated (of plants/animals), rough or uncouth (of a person)
adjective This adjectival use is really the derived form जंगली (janglii); जंगल (jangal) itself is not typically used attributively without that suffix.
Synonyms: जंगली (janglii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जंगली (janglii) carries a nice double life: for animals or plants it's neutral ('wild, undomesticated'), but aimed at a person it becomes a scolding word for rude or uncivilized behavior -- a mother might snap जंगली मत बनो (janglii mat bano, 'don't act like a savage') at a child misbehaving at the table.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use जंगल (jangal) directly as an adjective before a noun, e.g. to mean 'wild animal' -- the correct word is जंगली जानवर (janglii jaanvar, 'wild animal'), not जंगल जानवर.
