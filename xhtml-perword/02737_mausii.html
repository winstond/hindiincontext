मौसी (mausii)

noun Primarily a kinship term for one's mother's sister (maternal aunt); also used as a warm or respectful term of address for an older woman who is not a blood relative.
Sense 1 : maternal aunt (mother's sister)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मामी (maamii, different relation: maternal uncle's wife, not interchangeable), चाची (chaachii, different relation: paternal uncle's wife, not interchangeable)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian families track maternal versus paternal sides very precisely, so मौसी (mausii) always signals 'my mom's sister' specifically, unlike the catch-all English 'aunt.' Children often address any close family friend of their mother's generation as मौसी (mausii) too, which shows how kinship terms extend outward to build closeness with non-relatives.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use मौसी (mausii) as a blanket word for 'aunt,' but Hindi kinship terms are specific: मौसी (mausii) is only mother's sister, while चाची (chaachii), मामी (maamii), and बुआ (buaa) mark other distinct relations -- swapping them is a real error, not just a style choice.

Sense 2 : affectionate/respectful address for an older, unrelated woman
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आंटी (aantii, informal, English loanword, common in urban settings)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling a family friend, a neighbor's mother, or even a household helper's mother मौसी (mausii) is a common way of folding a stranger into the family circle -- it's warmer and more personal than the borrowed आंटी (aantii), which many Delhi households now use instead, especially with children.
