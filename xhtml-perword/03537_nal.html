नल (nal)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'tap/faucet'; also carries a technical sense as 'tube/pipe' and appears in the name of the mythological king नल (nal) from the Mahabharata.
Sense 1 : tap, faucet
noun, masculine
Synonyms: टोंटी (tontii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Indian homes and streets you'll hear नल (nal) constantly: सार्वजनिक नल (saarvajnik nal, 'public tap') is a fixture of neighborhood life in many towns, where people queue up with buckets, especially when piped supply is limited. नल खोलना/बंद करना (nal kholnaa / nal band karnaa, 'to turn the tap on/off') is one of the first practical verb-object pairs learners pick up.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse नल (nal) with नाली (naalii, 'drain/gutter') -- नल is the tap itself, नाली is where the water runs off after. 2) पानी का नल (paanii kaa nal, 'water tap') is the common phrase; don't drop the का (kaa) and just say पानी नल.

Sense 2 : pipe, tube (technical/plumbing)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पाइप (paaip)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In plumbing and construction contexts नल (nal) can mean the pipe or conduit itself, not just the tap at its end -- a plumber might talk about laying नल (nal) under the floor. This sense overlaps heavily with the borrowed word पाइप (paaip), which is more common in casual speech.
