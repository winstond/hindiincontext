रोज़ (roz)

adverb/noun One core meaning: 'day' as a unit of daily repetition, plus its very common adverbial use meaning 'daily/every day'. Also spelled रोज (roj) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : daily, every day
adverb
Synonyms: हर रोज़ (har roz), हर दिन (har din), प्रतिदिन (pratidin, formal/literary), रोज़ाना (rozaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly in daily complaints and routines: माँ रोज़ कहती है (maan roz kahtii hai, 'Mom says every day') -- मॉम's daily nagging, चाय रोज़ पीता हूँ (chaay roz piitaa hoon, 'I drink tea every day'), that sort of thing. It's the natural, spoken-register way to say 'every day', much more common in conversation than the more bookish प्रतिदिन (pratidin).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add a postposition after रोज़ (roz) as if it needs marking like a noun of time, e.g. saying रोज़ में (roz men) -- but as an adverb it stands alone: रोज़ आता है (roz aataa hai, 'comes every day'), not रोज़ में आता है (roz men aataa hai). 2) Confusing रोज़ (roz) with रोज़ाना (rozaanaa) -- both work as 'daily', but रोज़ (roz) is the more casual, spoken default, while रोज़ाना (rozaanaa) leans slightly more formal or written.
Sample Sentences:
मैं रोज़ सुबह चाय पीता हूँ। I drink tea every morning.
(main roz subah chaay piitaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : day (as a repeating unit, in fixed phrases)
noun, masculine Occurs mainly inside fixed time-phrases like आज-कल के रोज़ (aaj-kal ke roz) or दो-चार रोज़ (do-chaar roz, 'a few days'), rather than as a freestanding noun the way दिन (din) is used.
Synonyms: दिन (din)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in slightly older or more colloquial speech -- an elder saying दो-चार रोज़ रुक जाओ (do-chaar roz ruk jaao, 'stay a few more days') carries a warmer, more old-fashioned texture than the same line with दिन (din).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to दिन (din) for 'day' in every context, but रोज़ (roz) as a noun shows up specifically in expressions of a span of days, e.g. दो रोज़ में (do roz men, 'in a couple of days') -- it doesn't simply substitute for दिन (din) everywhere.
Sample Sentences:

(do-chaar roz ruk jaao, phir chale jaanaa।)
