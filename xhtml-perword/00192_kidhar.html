किधर (kidhar)

adverb An interrogative/relative adverb of direction and location, roughly 'which way' or 'where'; colloquially it overlaps heavily with कहाँ (kahaan) in casual speech.
Sense 1 : which way / in what direction (interrogative)
adverb
Synonyms: कहाँ (kahaan), किस तरफ (kis taraph), किस दिशा में (kis dishaa men, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi you will hear किधर से आ रहे हो? (kidhar se aa rahe ho?, 'which way are you coming from?') used as a casual greeting-style question among friends, almost like 'where've you been?' rather than a literal request for directions.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat किधर (kidhar) as fully interchangeable with कहाँ (kahaan); in casual speech they often are, but किधर (kidhar) carries a stronger sense of 'which direction' rather than just 'at what place', so it feels natural in questions like किधर जा रहे हो? (kidhar jaa rahe ho?, 'which way are you going?') where कहाँ (kahaan) would ask more about the destination itself. 2) Do not confuse with कब (kab, 'when') or कैसे (kaise, 'how') -- all are distinct question words that beginners sometimes jumble.
Sample Sentences:
तुम किधर जा रहे हो? Which way are you going?
(tum kidhar jaa rahe ho?)

Sense 2 : wherever / whichever way (relative/indefinite)
adverb Used in correlative pairs with जिधर (jidhar) ('wherever...there...') or on its own with भी (bhii) to mean 'anywhere/wherever' -- e.g. किधर भी चले जाओ (kidhar bhii chale jaao, 'go wherever you like').
Synonyms: जिधर (jidhar, correlative partner), कहीं भी (kahiin bhii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This pairs with जिधर (jidhar) the same way जहाँ (jahaan) pairs with वहाँ (vahaan) -- part of the same correlative family the learner has likely already met.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar only with the question-word sense may miss that adding भी (bhii) turns किधर (kidhar) into an indefinite 'wherever', not a question.

जिधर तुम जाओगे, मैं उधर आऊँगा। Wherever you go, I will come there.
(jidhar tum jaaoge, main udhar aaoongaa।)
