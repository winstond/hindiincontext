प्रयोग (prayog)

noun Mainly means 'use/usage' in everyday speech, but also carries a more formal sense of 'experiment,' especially in science and academic contexts.
Sense 1 : use, usage, application
noun, masculine Commonly paired with the verb करना (karnaa) as प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa, 'to use') -- a formal-register equivalent of इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa).
Synonyms: इस्तेमाल (istemaal), उपयोग (upyog)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word a lot on medicine labels and product instructions -- सावधानीपूर्वक प्रयोग करें (saavdhaaniipoorvak prayog karen, 'use carefully') -- because its formality suits official, printed language. In daily conversation among friends, इस्तेमाल (istemaal) is far more natural.
Common Mistakes: 1) प्रयोग (prayog) sounds noticeably more formal/bookish than इस्तेमाल (istemaal) -- fine in writing, instructions, or careful speech, but a bit stiff for casual chat about everyday objects. 2) Learners sometimes use प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa) where a native speaker would just say इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa) in casual contexts, making the sentence sound like a textbook or a manual.
Sample Sentences:
इस दवा का प्रयोग डॉक्टर की सलाह के बिना न करें। Do not use this medicine without a doctor's advice.
(is davaa kaa prayog doktar kii salaah ke binaa n karen।)

Sense 2 : experiment, trial
noun, masculine Common in science classroom contexts, e.g. प्रयोगशाला (prayogshaalaa, 'laboratory') is built directly on this sense.
Synonyms: परीक्षण (pariikshan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense every Indian schoolkid meets in the science lab -- प्रयोगशाला (prayogshaalaa) literally means 'place of experiments.' Outside school and science contexts, this meaning is much less common than the everyday 'use' sense above.
