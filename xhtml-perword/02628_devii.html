देवी (devii)

noun Basic meaning is 'goddess' -- a feminine noun with a strong devotional/mythological sense, plus an everyday honorific use for addressing or referring to a respected woman.
Sense 1 : goddess
noun, feminine
Synonyms: देविका (devikaa, rare), माता (maataa, overlaps in devotional contexts)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati, and Kali are all called देवी (devii), and you will see this word constantly at festival time -- दुर्गा पूजा (durgaa poojaa, 'Durga worship/festival') banners, temple names, and songs. Many Hindu women's names also incorporate देवी (devii) as a suffix (e.g. सीता देवी, siitaa devii), reflecting this same reverence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use देवी (devii) as a generic word for any female divine figure interchangeably with माता (maataa) or देवता (devtaa), but देवता (devtaa) is the male/generic form and not simply the masculine of this word in casual use -- it is its own lemma.

(durgaa poojaa men devii durgaa kii moorti banaaii jaatii hai।)

Sense 2 : respected/admired woman (honorific)
noun, feminine Used as a term of high respect or flattery for an ordinary woman, sometimes affectionately or with mild exaggeration.
Synonyms: देवी जी (devii jii, polite address form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling a woman देवी (devii) elevates her to near-divine status rhetorically -- you will hear this in wedding speeches, tributes to mothers, or even half-joking praise like गृह देवी (grih devii, 'goddess of the house') for someone who runs the household well.
Common Mistakes: 1) This honorific use can sound old-fashioned or overly formal/poetic if used casually about someone in everyday conversation -- it is more common in written praise, speeches, or affectionate exaggeration than in plain daily talk.
