प्रवृत्ति (pravritti)

noun A somewhat formal/literary noun covering two related ideas: an inborn tendency or inclination (psychological/behavioral), and, in a more abstract or philosophical register, a general trend or drift (of thought, of a movement, of the times).
Sense 1 : an inclination or tendency (in behavior/character)
noun, feminine Commonly takes a following oblique-infinitive or की + verb construction to specify the tendency, e.g. भागने की प्रवृत्ति (bhaagne kii pravritti, 'a tendency to run away').
Synonyms: झुकाव (jhukaav), रुझान (rujhaan), स्वभाव (svabhaav, overlaps with 'nature/temperament')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word a lot in the context of parenting and moral advice -- teachers and elders talk about a child's प्रवृत्ति (pravritti) toward laziness or kindness as something to be guided, reflecting a broader Hindi habit of treating inner tendencies almost like separate forces within a person that can be shaped.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्वभाव (svabhaav) when they mean a specific recurring behavior pattern -- स्वभाव is more about overall character/nature, while प्रवृत्ति (pravritti) points to a particular leaning or drift within that nature. 2) This word is used in formal or reflective speech (psychology, self-help talk, essays) rather than casual chat -- in everyday conversation about someone's habits, आदत (aadat) is far more natural.

Sense 2 : a general trend, drift, or movement (of thought, era, or field)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: धारा (dhaaraa, 'current/stream', figurative), रुझान (rujhaan)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in discussions of literature, art, or society -- for instance, आधुनिक प्रवृत्तियाँ (aadhunik pravrittiyaan, 'modern trends') is a phrase you'd meet in a college essay or a newspaper editorial about changing values, not in casual talk about fashion.
