वोट (vot)

noun An English loanword used in Hindi exactly as in political/civic contexts: as a noun meaning 'vote' and as part of the common verb phrase वोट देना (vot denaa, 'to vote').
Sense 1 : vote (the ballot/choice cast in an election)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मतदान (matdaan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear वोट (vot) constantly around election season in Delhi -- on TV news, on posters, and in taxi-driver conversations about which party 'is going to win the votes' (वोट किसके पड़ेंगे, vot kiske padenge, 'whose way will the votes fall'). The verb डालना (daalnaa, 'to drop/pour') paints the ballot as something physically dropped into a box, a nice bit of Hindi's love for concrete, physical verbs.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say वोट करना (vot karnaa) by analogy with English 'to vote,' but the standard everyday expression is वोट देना (vot denaa, 'to give a vote'); वोट डालना (vot daalnaa, 'to cast a vote,' literally 'to drop/put a vote') is also common and slightly more formal-sounding.
Sample Sentences:
मैंने आज सुबह अपना वोट डाला। I cast my vote this morning.
(mainne aaj subah apnaa vot daalaa।)

Sense 2 : voting right / a person's vote as political leverage
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मताधिकार (mataadhikaar, formal, 'right to vote')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In political speech you'll often hear phrases like मेरा वोट (meraa vot, 'my vote') used almost like a possession or a bargaining chip -- as in वोट बैंक (vot baink, 'vote bank'), a very common term in Indian political commentary for a demographic group whose votes a party relies on.
Sample Sentences:
यह पार्टी सालों से मुस्लिम वोट बैंक पर भरोसा करती आई है। This party has relied on the Muslim vote bank for years.
(yah paartii saalon se muslim vot baink par bharosaa kartii aaii hai।)
