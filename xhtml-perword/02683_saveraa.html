सवेरा (saveraa)

noun A noun meaning 'morning' or 'dawn', used mostly to evoke the early part of the day poetically or in everyday speech; closely related to सुबह (subah), which is the more neutral, everyday word for 'morning'.
Sense 1 : morning, dawn
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुबह (subah), भोर (bhor, literary), प्रातःकाल (praatahkaal, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi treats the arrival of morning as something that happens on its own, not something you do -- you'll hear सवेरा हो गया (saveraa ho gayaa, 'it has become morning') the same way English says 'day broke,' with the day as the active party rather than the observer. सुबह-सवेरे (subah-savere, 'very early in the morning') is a common paired expression that doubles up both words for emphasis, something you'll hear from parents nudging kids out of bed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सवेरा (saveraa) as a default translation of 'morning' the way they'd use सुबह (subah); in practice सुबह (subah) is the everyday word used in schedules, greetings, and small talk (सुबह आठ बजे subah aath baje, 'at eight in the morning'), while सवेरा (saveraa) leans slightly more descriptive or evocative, common in phrases about the day breaking or in songs and stories. 2) सवेरा (saveraa) is masculine, so watch adjective and verb agreement -- सवेरा हो गया (saveraa ho gayaa, 'morning has come'), not a feminine form.
Sample Sentences:
सवेरा हो गया, उठो! Morning has come, wake up!
(saveraa ho gayaa, utho!)
