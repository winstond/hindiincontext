ईंट (iint)

noun/phrase A concrete noun meaning 'brick,' with a lively figurative sense in the common idiom ईंट से ईंट बजाना (iint se iint bajaanaa, 'to smash to pieces').
Sense 1 : brick
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly around any construction site (निर्माण स्थल, nirmaan sthal) in Delhi-NCR, where stacks of red bricks (लाल ईंटें, laal iinten) piled by the roadside are a familiar sight during the endless building boom.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat ईंट (iint) as masculine by default since many object nouns are; it is feminine, so watch agreement: ईंट टूट गई (iint toot gaii, 'the brick broke'), not टूट गया (toot gayaa).

Sense 2 : to destroy utterly, wreak total havoc
phrase Used with an agentive subject and often ने-marking in the past: उन्होंने ईंट से ईंट बजा दी (unhonne iint se iint bajaa dii, 'they smashed it to pieces').
Synonyms: तबाह करना (tabaah karnaa), मिट्टी में मिलाना (mittii men milaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The image is of bricks in a structure being knocked against each other until the whole building collapses -- a vivid way to threaten or describe total destruction, often heard in political rhetoric or heated arguments, as in दुश्मन की ईंट से ईंट बजा देंगे (dushman kii iint se iint bajaa denge, 'we will raze the enemy to the ground').
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a fixed idiom -- learners sometimes try to swap in other objects (e.g. 'पत्थर से पत्थर बजाना') which does not carry the same idiomatic force; the brick-on-brick image is fixed.
