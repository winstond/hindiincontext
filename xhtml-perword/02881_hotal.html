होटल (hotal)

noun A borrowed word from English 'hotel', but in everyday Hindi it usually means a restaurant or eatery, not a lodging place -- that distinction is the main thing to learn here.
Sense 1 : restaurant/eatery
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रेस्टोरेंट (restorent), ढाबा (dhaabaa, smaller/roadside eatery), भोजनालय (bhojnaalay, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In much of North India, especially Delhi-NCR, when someone says वहाँ का होटल अच्छा है (vahaan kaa hotal achchhaa hai, 'that restaurant is good'), they are almost certainly talking about the food, not the rooms. Small local eateries, even very modest dhaba-style ones, are routinely called होटल (hotal) -- it is one of those English loanwords whose meaning drifted once it settled into Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) The default, most common sense of होटल (hotal) in spoken Hindi is a place to eat, not a place to stay -- saying चलो होटल में खाना खाते हैं (chalo hotal men khaanaa khaate hain, 'let's go eat at the restaurant') is completely normal and does not imply lodging. 2) Learners often assume होटल (hotal) always means 'hotel' in the English sense and get confused when someone suggests going to one just for a meal.

Sense 2 : lodging establishment
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सराय (saraay, older/traditional inn), धर्मशाला (dharmshaalaa, charitable/pilgrim lodging)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense closer to English usage and is common in travel, tourism, and business contexts -- booking a होटल (hotal) for a trip. Context (talk of रुकना (ruknaa, staying) or बुकिंग (buking, booking) versus खाना (khaanaa, eating)) tells you which sense is active.
Common Mistakes: 1) When the lodging sense is meant, speakers often clarify with रुकना (ruknaa, 'to stay') -- e.g. हम होटल में रुके थे (ham hotal men ruke the, 'we stayed at a hotel') -- because होटल (hotal) alone defaults to the restaurant meaning in casual speech.
