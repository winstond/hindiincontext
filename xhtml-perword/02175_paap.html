पाप (paap)

noun A noun meaning 'sin' or 'moral wrongdoing' in both religious and everyday ethical senses; also used as an adjective-like intensifier in a few fixed expressions of pity or reproach.
Sense 1 : sin, moral wrongdoing
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गुनाह (gunaah), अपराध (apraadh, more like 'crime/offense'), दोष (dosh, fault/blame)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Hindu moral thinking, पाप (paap) is paired with पुण्य (puny, 'merit/virtue') -- life is often framed as a running ledger between the two, and elders will invoke पाप (paap) to discourage anything from cruelty to animals to wasting food. You will also hear it in the mild exclamation पाप लगेगा (paap lagegaa, 'it will bring sin upon you'), said half-seriously when someone is about to do something unkind or wasteful.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अपराध (apraadh) when they mean a moral/religious sin -- पाप (paap) carries a spiritual or karmic weight that अपराध (apraadh) (closer to 'crime' or 'legal offense') does not. 2) पाप लगना (paap lagnaa, 'sin attaches/accrues') is a common construction -- sin is described as something that 'sticks to' a person, not something one 'does' in the direct English sense, even though पाप करना (paap karnaa) also exists.

Sense 2 : pity, shame (in fixed exclamatory expressions)
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively in the fixed phrase पाप है (paap hai, 'it's a shame/sin [to do this]'), used to express that some act would be a waste or a pity, distinct from a genuine moral-sin judgment.
Synonyms: अफ़सोस (afsos, regret/pity, not a direct synonym but overlaps in this use)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear a grandmother say खाना फेंकना पाप है (khaanaa phenknaa paap hai, 'throwing away food is a sin') without meaning anything theological at all -- it's a rhetorical nudge, much like an English speaker saying 'it's a crime to waste this.'
Common Mistakes: 1) पाप है (paap hai, 'it would be a shame') is often used lightly, e.g. about wasting good food, and should not be translated with the full religious weight of sense 1 -- it is closer to English 'what a shame' or 'that would be a crime' (in the loose sense).
