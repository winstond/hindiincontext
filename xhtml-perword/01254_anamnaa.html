अनमना (anamnaa)

adjective A single-sense adjective describing a listless, half-hearted, or reluctant state of mind -- someone whose heart just isn't in what they're doing.
Sense 1 : listless, half-hearted, reluctant, in low spirits
adjective Often paired with मन (man, 'mind/heart') in phrasing like मन अनमना होना (man anamnaa honaa, 'to feel listless/out of sorts'), since the word itself is built from मन (man) plus a negating/altering prefix.
Synonyms: उदास (udaas, sad/gloomy rather than reluctant), बेदिल (bedil, half-hearted), अनिच्छुक (anichchhuk, unwilling), खिन्न (khinn, glum)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word most often describing a child who's sulking over food, or an employee dragging their feet through a task they don't want to do -- it's that specific flavor of low-energy reluctance, not deep sadness. It pairs naturally with the dative-subject-style constructions you already know from लगना (lagnaa), since Hindi tends to locate this kind of mood in मन (man) or दिल (dil) rather than in the person as a direct actor.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उदास (udaas) when they mean अनमना (anamnaa) -- उदास (udaas) is sadness or gloom, while अनमना (anamnaa) is more about a lack of enthusiasm or will, like doing something without your heart in it. 2) It's frequently used with दिल (dil) or मन (man) as the grammatical subject rather than the person directly, e.g. उसका मन अनमना था (uskaa man anamnaa thaa, 'his mind was listless') -- learners sometimes force the person as subject instead.
