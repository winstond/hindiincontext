न x न (n x n)

pattern This is the correlative frame न (n) ... न (n) ... used to negate two alternatives at once, roughly 'neither X nor Y'; each slot is filled by whatever is being denied.
Sense 1 : neither...nor... (paired negation of two alternatives)
pattern The negator न (n) is simply repeated before each item being ruled out; the items can be nouns, verbs, or whole clauses, e.g. न चाय न कॉफ़ी (n chaay n kofii, 'neither tea nor coffee') or न वह आया न मैं गया (n vah aayaa n main gayaa, 'neither did he come nor did I go').
Synonyms: न...और न...(n...aur n..., adds emphasis with 'and')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this pattern constantly in everyday complaints and refusals -- न पैसा है न वक़्त (n paisaa hai n vaqt, 'there's neither money nor time') is the kind of line every Delhi uncle has said at some point. It also survives inside the fixed idiom न घर का न घाट का (n ghar kaa n ghaat kaa, 'belonging neither to home nor to the riverbank' -- i.e. fitting in nowhere), which is worth recognizing even though it's a fossilized phrase rather than a live template use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use नहीं (nahiin) in this frame instead of न (n) -- नहीं (nahiin) is the everyday negator for a single statement, but the paired न...न (n...n) frame specifically signals 'neither...nor' and sounds off if broken up with नहीं (nahiin). 2) Forgetting to repeat न (n) before the second item -- dropping it (न चाय कॉफ़ी) reads as incomplete or ungrammatical to a native ear.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे पास न पैसा है न वक़्त। I have neither money nor time.
(mere paas n paisaa hai n vaqt।)
