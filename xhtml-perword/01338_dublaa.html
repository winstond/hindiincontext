दुबला (dublaa)

adjective A simple descriptive adjective meaning thin/lean, most often heard in the fixed pair दुबला-पतला (dublaa-patlaa).
Sense 1 : thin, lean (of body)
adjective
Synonyms: पतला (patlaa), कमज़ोर (kamzor, leans toward 'weak/frail' rather than just slim), छरहरा (chharahraa, slim, usually complimentary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian aunties and mothers use दुबला (dublaa) constantly, usually with concern rather than judgment -- 'तुम बहुत दुबले हो गए हो' (tum bahut duble ho gae ho, 'you have become very thin') is practically a love language in many households, often followed by an offer of food. It carries none of the positive 'slim/fit' connotation that छरहरा (chharahraa) or पतला (patlaa) can carry in a flattering context -- दुबला (dublaa) leans toward 'too thin, undernourished-looking.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कमज़ोर (kamzor) to mean 'thin,' but कमज़ोर (kamzor) really means 'weak/frail' -- दुबला (dublaa) is about body build, not strength, so a person can be दुबला (dublaa) but still strong. 2) दुबला (dublaa) is almost always paired with पतला (patlaa) in casual speech -- दुबला-पतला (dublaa-patlaa) -- saying just दुबला (dublaa) alone can sound slightly clinical or blunt by comparison.

(tum bahut duble ho gae ho, thiik se khaanaa khaao।)
