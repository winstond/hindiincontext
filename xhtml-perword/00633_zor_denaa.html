ज़ोर देना (zor denaa)

verb A common compound verb built from ज़ोर (zor, 'force/emphasis') plus देना (denaa, 'to give'); it means 'to emphasize, stress, or insist on' something, whether in speech, effort, or attention.
Sense 1 : to emphasize, stress (a point, idea, or word)
verb, transitive Takes पर (par) to mark the thing being emphasized: किसी बात पर ज़ोर देना (kisii baat par zor denaa, 'to emphasize a point').
Synonyms: बल देना (bal denaa, more formal/literary), महत्व देना (mahatv denaa, 'to give importance')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in classrooms, news debates, and family arguments alike -- टीचर ने इस बात पर ज़ोर दिया (tiichar ne is baat par zor diyaa, 'the teacher emphasized this point') is a very natural, everyday sentence. It pairs naturally with बात (baat), मुद्दा (muddaa), और ज़रूरत (zaroorat) as the thing being stressed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try ज़ोर देना (zor denaa) with को (ko) instead of पर (par) -- the emphasized thing is always marked with पर (par), not को (ko). 2) As a transitive compound, it takes ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने ज़ोर दिया (usne zor diyaa, 'he emphasized'), which learners sometimes forget since देना (denaa) alone feels light.

Sense 2 : to insist on, push hard for (an action or demand)
verb, transitive Followed by a verb + के लिए (ke lie) or a किFinal clause: उसने जाने के लिए ज़ोर दिया (usne jaane ke lie zor diyaa, 'he insisted on going').
Synonyms: जिद करना (jid karnaa, stronger, implies stubbornness), आग्रह करना (aagrah karnaa, more formal/polite)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind a parent's classic माँ ने पढ़ाई पर ज़ोर दिया (maan ne padhaaii par zor diyaa, 'mom insisted on/stressed studies') -- it sits right at the boundary between 'emphasizing' and 'pushing someone to do something,' which is very natural in Hindi but takes English learners a moment to feel out.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this insisting sense with the emphasizing sense above -- context (a demand for action vs. a point in speech) is what distinguishes them, not the grammar. 2) The postposition पर (par) can still appear before an action noun, e.g. जाने पर ज़ोर देना (jaane par zor denaa, 'to insist on going'), which can look identical in form to sense 1 but means something different in context.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to exert physical force or effort (on a task, body part)
verb, transitive Used with पर (par) for the body part or object under strain: आँखों पर ज़ोर देना (aankhon par zor denaa, 'to strain one's eyes').
Synonyms: ताकत लगाना (taakat lagaanaa, more physical/casual), दबाव डालना (dabaav daalnaa, 'to apply pressure')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear डॉक्टर ने कहा आँखों पर ज़ोर मत दो (doktar ne kahaa aankhon par zor mat do, 'the doctor said, do not strain your eyes') in everyday health advice. Note the closely related sibling ज़ोर लगाना (zor lagaanaa, 'to exert force/try hard'), which overlaps here in meaning but uses लगाना (lagaanaa) instead of देना (denaa) -- worth knowing as a pair.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may not realize this physical-effort sense exists alongside the more abstract 'emphasize/insist' senses -- context (a body part or physical task as object) signals this reading. 2) Overusing this sense where महत्व देना (mahatv denaa) or जोर लगाना (jor lagaanaa, a close relative with लगाना (lagaanaa) instead of देना (denaa)) would sound more natural to a native speaker.
Sample Sentences:
