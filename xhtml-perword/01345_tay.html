तय (tay)

adjective An adjective (used only in fixed predicate slots with verbs like होना (honaa) and करना (karnaa)) meaning 'decided/fixed/settled.' Almost always met as part of the compound verbs तय होना (tay honaa, 'to be decided') and तय करना (tay karnaa, 'to decide/settle').
Sense 1 : decided, settled, fixed (of a plan, matter, price, etc.)
adjective Used predicatively with होना (honaa) to mean 'to be decided': बात तय है (baat tay hai, 'the matter is settled'). Invariant -- does not change for gender or number.
Synonyms: निश्चित (nishchit), पक्का (pakkaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear तय है (tay hai, 'it's settled') constantly in everyday planning talk -- fixing a meeting time, a price at the market, or wedding arrangements. Note the pairing with करना (karnaa) and होना (honaa) mirrors the familiar transitive/intransitive verb-pair pattern (like खुलना (khulnaa)/खोलना (kholnaa)), even though तय (tay) itself is not a verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to inflect तय (tay) like a regular -आ adjective (तया/तयी); it never changes form. 2) तय होना (tay honaa) and तय करना (tay karnaa) are often confused: तय होना (tay honaa) is intransitive ('it got decided,' often with no clear agent), while तय करना (tay karnaa) is transitive and needs an ने-marked agent who does the deciding.

Sense 2 : covered, traversed (of distance)
adjective Used with करना (karnaa) in the fixed phrase दूरी तय करना (doorii tay karnaa, 'to cover a distance') and with होना (honaa) for 'to have been covered.'
Synonyms: पूरा (pooraa, covers a broader sense of 'complete')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in travel talk and news reports: कितनी दूरी तय की (kitnii doorii tay kii, 'how much distance was covered'). It is the same root idea as sense 1 (something becoming 'fixed' or 'settled') extended metaphorically to ground covered on a journey.
