वक़्त (vaqt)

noun A common everyday word for 'time,' both in the abstract sense (time passing, time available) and in the sense of a particular occasion or moment; also appears in a handful of fixed time-related expressions.
Sense 1 : time (in general; duration, availability of time)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: समय (samay), वेला (velaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: वक़्त (vaqt) is an Urdu-origin word so thoroughly absorbed into everyday Hindi that most speakers don't register it as 'foreign' at all -- you will hear it constantly in Delhi markets, offices, and homes, often interchangeably with समय (samay). It also carries a slightly more emotional or reflective color in phrases about life and fate, which is part of why it shows up so often in film dialogue and song lyrics about time passing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for समय (samay) because textbooks introduce it first, but वक़्त (vaqt) is at least as common in spoken Hindi, especially in Delhi -- both are safe defaults. 2) In phrases like 'I don't have time,' the construction is dative-subject: मेरे पास वक़्त नहीं है (mere paas vaqt nahiin hai, 'I don't have time'), not a direct translation of the English subject-verb order.
Sample Sentences:

(mere paas abhii vaqt nahiin hai।)

Sense 2 : a particular time, moment, or occasion
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मौक़ा (mauqaa, 'occasion/opportunity, close in some contexts'), पल (pal, 'instant, narrower scope')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear वक़्त (vaqt) used to mark a specific juncture in someone's life, as in बुरे वक़्त में (bure vaqt men, 'in bad times') -- Hindi speakers lean on this word heavily when talking about difficult or fortunate periods, almost personifying time as something that treats you well or badly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used with इस (is) or उस (us) to mean 'at this/that time' -- इस वक़्त (is vaqt, 'at this time/right now') is extremely common and learners sometimes default to अभी (abhii) instead, which is fine but less flexible in longer sentences.
