राहत (raahat)

noun A feminine noun meaning relief or comfort, used both for personal ease (from pain, stress, tension) and for organized disaster/humanitarian relief.
Sense 1 : relief, comfort (from pain, tension, or discomfort)
noun, feminine Very commonly paired with the verb मिलना (milnaa) -- राहत मिलना (raahat milnaa, 'to get relief') -- where the person who feels relieved is marked with को (ko).
Synonyms: आराम (aaraam), सुकून (sukoon), चैन (chain)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear after a headache fades, after an exam ends, or after monsoon rain finally breaks a heatwave -- राहत मिली (raahat milii, 'relief was gotten/felt') is the standard way to express that sigh of relief. It sits close in feeling to सुकून (sukoon), though सुकून leans more toward inner peace while राहत is relief from a specific pressure or problem.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use राहत (raahat) with पाना (paanaa) as a direct translation of 'to get relief,' but राहत मिलना (raahat milnaa) is far more natural and idiomatic in everyday speech. 2) The person experiencing relief takes को (ko), not ने (ne) -- मुझे राहत मिली (mujhe raahat milii, 'I felt relief'), not मैंने राहत मिली।

Sense 2 : disaster/humanitarian relief, aid
noun, feminine Frequently appears in compounds like राहत कार्य (raahat kaary, 'relief work') and राहत शिविर (raahat shivir, 'relief camp'), especially in news contexts about floods, earthquakes, or other disasters.
Synonyms: सहायता (sahaaytaa), मदद (madad)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense constantly on the news during flood or earthquake season -- राहत सामग्री (raahat saamagrii, 'relief supplies') being distributed, or सरकार ने राहत का ऐलान किया (sarkaar ne raahat kaa ailaan kiyaa, 'the government announced relief'). It is the standard bureaucratic and journalistic term for organized aid.
