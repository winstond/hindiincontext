बजे (baje)

particle/verb Used to tell time in Hindi -- 'o'clock' -- and also the past-tense form of the verb बजना (bajnaa, 'to ring/strike/sound') meaning something rang, struck, or played.
Sense 1 : o'clock (time marker)
particle Follows a number to state clock time: पाँच बजे (paanch baje, 'at five o'clock'). With one o'clock, use एक बजे (ek baje) -- note this is still बजे (baje), not a singular form, despite एक (ek) being singular.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi auto driver 'कितने बजे मिलते हैं?' (kitne baje milte hain, 'what time shall we meet?') and बजे (baje) is doing all the work of 'o'clock.' It comes from the verb बजना (bajnaa) -- the idea being that a clock or bell 'sounds' the hour, so time in Hindi is quite literally described by the striking of a bell.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make this agree with the number like an adjective, but बजे (baje) is fixed in this time-telling use except for the एक बजे (ek baje) vs दो बजे (do baje) contrast, which is about the noun बजा (bajaa)/बजे (baje) itself being singular or plural, not about agreement with the numeral in the usual adjective sense. 2) Learners forget को (ko) is optional but common when specifying 'at' a time in a full sentence: पाँच बजे (को) आना (paanch baje (ko) aanaa, 'to come at five o'clock').
Sample Sentences:
अभी पाँच बजे हैं। It's five o'clock right now.
(abhii paanch baje hain।)

Sense 2 : rang, struck, sounded, played (past tense)
verb, intransitive This is the masculine plural/default past form of बजना (bajnaa); used for bells, clocks, alarms, and musical instruments 'sounding' on their own -- घंटी बजी (ghantii bajii, 'the bell rang'), घड़ी के बारह बजे (ghadii ke baarah baje, 'the clock struck twelve').
Synonyms: गूँजा (goonjaa, resonated/echoed)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This intransitive verb बजना (bajnaa) pairs with its transitive causative cousin बजाना (bajaanaa, 'to play/ring something', e.g. a tabla or a doorbell) -- a classic Hindi pattern where the thing 'sounds by itself' versus 'someone makes it sound.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बजाया (bajaayaa) (from the transitive बजाना (bajaanaa), 'to play/sound something') when they mean the instrument or bell sounded by itself -- घंटी बज गई (ghantii baj gaii, 'the bell rang') not घंटी बजाई (ghantii bajaaii) unless someone deliberately rang it.
Sample Sentences:
दरवाजे की घंटी दो बार बजी। The doorbell rang twice.
