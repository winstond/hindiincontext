के बाहर (ke baahar)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'outside of' -- one straightforward sense marking location or movement beyond a boundary.
Sense 1 : outside (of), beyond
postposition Governs the oblique case of the preceding noun, like other compound postpositions ending in के (ke): घर के बाहर (ghar ke baahar, 'outside the house'). Can also be used figuratively for limits: मेरी पहुँच के बाहर (merii pahunch ke baahar, 'beyond my reach').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pairs naturally with के अंदर (ke andar, 'inside of') and के भीतर (ke bhiitar, 'inside of') as the standard set of spatial postpositions -- worth learning together. You will hear it constantly in everyday directions, like दुकान के बाहर खड़े हो जाओ (dukaan ke baahar khade ho jaao, 'stand outside the shop').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop के (ke) and just say बाहर (baahar) when they actually need to name what the thing is outside of -- बाहर (baahar) alone means 'outside' in general, while के बाहर (ke baahar) is used with a specific noun: घर के बाहर (ghar ke baahar, 'outside the house'), not घर बाहर (ghar baahar). 2) With pronouns, remember to use the oblique possessive form, e.g. उसके बाहर (uske baahar, 'outside it/that'), not उसका बाहर (uskaa baahar).
