अचानक (achaanak)

adverb/adjective A single-meaning word: 'suddenly, unexpectedly.' It works both as an adverb modifying a verb and as an adjective describing a noun, with no change in form between the two.
Sense 1 : suddenly, all of a sudden
adverb
Synonyms: अनायास (anaayaas, literary), एकाएक (ekaaek), सहसा (sahsaa, literary), आकस्मिक रूप से (aakasmik roop se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Delhiites reach for constantly when talking about the city's chaos -- अचानक बारिश हो गई (achaanak baarish ho gaii, 'it suddenly started raining') or अचानक बिजली चली गई (achaanak bijlii chalii gaii, 'the power suddenly went') are everyday complaints. It pairs naturally with verbs of appearing, stopping, or changing, since Hindi likes to frame unexpected events as things that simply happen to you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place अचानक (achaanak) at the very end of the sentence as English speakers do with 'suddenly' -- in natural Hindi it usually sits right before the verb phrase, e.g. वह अचानक आ गया (vah achaanak aa gayaa, 'he suddenly came'), not वह आ गया अचानक (vah aa gayaa achaanak). 2) It often pairs with the compound verb construction (verb + जाना (jaanaa) / verb + पड़ना (padnaa)) to sharpen the sense of abruptness -- रो पड़ा (ro padaa, 'suddenly burst into tears') feels more natural with अचानक (achaanak) than a plain simple verb.

Sense 2 : sudden, unexpected (describing a noun)
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. अचानक मौत (achaanak maut, 'sudden death'); it does not inflect for gender or number in this use.
Synonyms: आकस्मिक (aakasmik, formal), अप्रत्याशित (apratyaashit, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this on news tickers a lot -- अचानक हमला (achaanak hamlaa, 'sudden attack') or अचानक फैसला (achaanak phaislaa, 'sudden decision') are standard headline phrasing, since अचानक (achaanak) carries a slightly more dramatic, newsworthy flavor than a plain 'unexpected.'
