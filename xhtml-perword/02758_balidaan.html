बलिदान (balidaan)

noun A weighty noun meaning 'sacrifice' -- giving up something precious, often life itself, for a higher cause. One core sense, but it carries strong emotional and civic resonance.
Sense 1 : sacrifice (especially of one's life) for a cause, nation, or higher purpose
noun, masculine Most often used with the verb देना (denaa) -- बलिदान देना (balidaan denaa, 'to give one's sacrifice/life') -- rather than as a subject doing something on its own.
Synonyms: त्याग (tyaag, broader: renunciation/giving up), कुर्बानी (kurbaanii, everyday and widely used, especially in speech), न्योछावर (nyochhaavar, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries deep patriotic and often martial weight in Hindi -- it is the word used for soldiers who die for the nation (शहीदों का बलिदान (shahiidon kaa balidaan, 'the sacrifice of martyrs')), and you will hear it constantly around Republic Day and Independence Day speeches. कुर्बानी (kurbaanii) is the word you will hear far more in daily conversation and Bollywood dialogue, while बलिदान sits a notch higher in register, closer to what gets etched on memorials.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for त्याग (tyaag) or कुर्बानी (kurbaanii) interchangeably with बलिदान (balidaan), but बलिदान specifically implies giving up something supreme, usually life, whereas त्याग can mean simply renouncing comforts or habits (like त्याग करना (tyaag karnaa) for giving up sugar). 2) It is a noun and needs करना (karnaa) or देना (denaa) to function as a verb phrase -- there is no standalone verb form.
Sample Sentences:
देश की रक्षा में सैनिकों का बलिदान कभी नहीं भुलाया जा सकता। The sacrifice of soldiers in defense of the nation can never be forgotten.
(desh kii rakshaa men sainikon kaa balidaan kabhii nahiin bhulaayaa jaa saktaa।)
