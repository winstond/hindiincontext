याद करना (yaad karnaa)

verb A compound built from the noun याद (yaad, 'memory/remembrance') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); covers both 'to remember/recall' and 'to memorize' as well as the affectionate 'to miss someone'.
Sense 1 : to remember, to recall
verb, transitive The thing remembered is usually marked with को (ko) or is simply the direct object; contrast with याद आना (yaad aanaa), where the memory arrives to the person (dative subject) rather than the person actively recalling it.
Synonyms: याद आना (yaad aanaa, involuntary recall -- different construction), स्मरण करना (smaran karnaa, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of याद (yaad) as a mental object you can actively 'do' something with -- यह याद करो (yah yaad karo, 'recall this') treats memory as an act of will, which is why this pairs so naturally with करना (karnaa) rather than होना (honaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for याद आना (yaad aanaa) when they actually mean the deliberate act of recalling something -- याद करना (yaad karnaa) is the active, willed version, while याद आना (yaad aanaa) just happens to you. 2) The person doing the remembering is the subject here (with ने (ne) in the past, since करना (karnaa) is transitive), not marked with को (ko) as it would be with याद आना (yaad aanaa).

Sense 2 : to memorize (by rote)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: कंठस्थ करना (kanthasth karnaa, formal), रटना (ratnaa, rote/mechanical memorization, often with a negative shade)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian schoolkid has heard a parent or teacher say कविता याद करो (kavitaa yaad karo, 'memorize the poem') -- this sense is deeply tied to rote learning culture around exams.
Common Mistakes: 1) In school contexts याद करना (yaad karnaa) is the neutral everyday way to say 'memorize'; रटना (ratnaa) implies mindless cramming, so swapping them changes the tone.

Sense 3 : to miss someone (think of them fondly/longingly)
verb, transitive The person missed is the direct object, e.g. मैं तुम्हें याद करता हूँ (main tumhen yaad kartaa hoon, 'I miss you') -- an active statement of missing, distinct from तुम्हारी याद आती है (tumhaarii yaad aatii hai, 'I find myself missing you'), where the missing arrives unbidden.
Synonyms: याद आना (yaad aanaa, the involuntary counterpart -- 'to be missed/come to mind')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind countless Hindi film lyrics and messages -- तुम्हारी याद आती है (tumhaarii yaad aatii hai) lets missing someone feel like it happens to you, which fits Hindi's general comfort with feelings as things that arrive rather than things you do.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers default to 'I miss you' and translate it literally, but तुम्हारी याद आती है (tumhaarii yaad aatii hai) is actually far more common in natural speech for this feeling than मैं तुम्हें याद करता हूँ (main tumhen yaad kartaa hoon), which can sound slightly more deliberate or formal.
