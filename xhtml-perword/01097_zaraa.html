ज़रा (zaraa)

adverb A small, friendly word that means 'a little' or 'just a moment' -- used constantly to soften requests and commands, and also as an intensifier meaning 'just try' or 'go ahead.'
Sense 1 : a little bit, slightly
adverb
Synonyms: थोड़ा (thodaa), कुछ (kuchh, meaning scope: also 'some')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the softening word you hear everywhere in Delhi markets and homes -- ज़रा इधर आना (zaraa idhar aanaa, 'come here for a second') sounds far gentler than a bare इधर आओ (idhar aao). Adding ज़रा (zaraa) before a request is a small but important politeness move in everyday Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for थोड़ा (thodaa) by default, but ज़रा (zaraa) is what native speakers actually use in polite requests like ज़रा सुनिए (zaraa sunie, 'listen for a moment') -- the two are largely interchangeable here, but ज़रा (zaraa) feels warmer and more conversational. 2) Unlike थोड़ा (thodaa), ज़रा (zaraa) does not inflect for gender or number, so learners sometimes mistakenly write ज़री (zarii) or ज़रे (zare).

Sense 2 : just, for a moment (softening a request or command)
adverb Placed directly before an imperative or request verb: ज़रा देखिए (zaraa dekhie, 'just take a look'), ज़रा रुकिए (zaraa rukie, 'just wait a moment').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is arguably the most useful sense for a learner: dropping ज़रा (zaraa) in front of almost any polite request instantly makes it sound more natural and less abrupt, much like English speakers add 'just' before a favor -- 'just hold on a sec.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate this sense too literally as 'a little' and miss that its real job is softening a command -- ज़रा बताइए (zaraa bataaie) means 'please tell me,' not 'tell me a small amount.' 2) It is easy to overuse थोड़ा (thodaa) here instead, which sounds stiffer and less natural in a spoken request.

Sense 3 : used as a challenge or provocation ('just try')
adverb Common in confrontational or teasing speech: ज़रा हाथ लगाना (zaraa haath lagaanaa, 'just try touching it') implies a dare or warning.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in a heated exchange, like a parent warning a child: ज़रा फिर से बोलना (zaraa phir se bolnaa, 'just say that again' -- meaning 'don't dare say that again'). The same little word that softens a request can also sharpen a threat, depending entirely on tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners miss this sense entirely and get confused when ज़रा (zaraa) appears in an angry or challenging sentence rather than a polite one -- the tone here is sharp, almost sarcastic, opposite of the softening sense above.
