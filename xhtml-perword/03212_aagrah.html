आग्रह (aagrah)

noun A noun meaning an insistent request or urging -- the warm, persistent pressing that a host applies when offering food, or more broadly a firm insistence on something.
Sense 1 : insistent request, urging (especially to eat/accept something)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ज़िद (zid, stubborn insistence), विनती (vintii, polite request)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word captures a very Indian hosting ritual: the host says खाइए, थोड़ा और लीजिए (khaaie, thodaa aur liijie, 'eat, take a little more') and keeps insisting even after you politely decline -- that repeated, affectionate pressure is आग्रह (aagrah). It is considered warm and caring, not rude, unlike overly persistent urging might seem in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अनुरोध (anurodh) when they mean a simple request -- आग्रह (aagrah) carries more warmth and pressure, especially in the context of hospitality, like insisting a guest take a second helping. 2) The verb pairing is करना (karnaa) -- आग्रह करना (aagrah karnaa) -- not a direct verb form of its own.

Sense 2 : firm insistence, adamant stance (on an idea, opinion, or condition)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हठ (hath, stubbornness), दुराग्रह (duraagrah, obstinate/unreasonable insistence)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Here आग्रह (aagrah) shifts from hospitality to attitude -- someone who has an आग्रह (aagrah) about doing things a certain way is holding firm, though usually still seen as principled rather than unreasonable. Compare with its harsher cousin दुराग्रह (duraagrah), which crosses into stubbornness for its own sake.
