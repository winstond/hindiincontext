ख़र्च करना (kharch karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to spend' (money, time, or effort), built from the noun ख़र्च (kharch, 'expense') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'). Related to the word ख़र्च (kharch) used alone as a noun.
Sense 1 : to spend money
verb, transitive
Synonyms: व्यय करना (vyay karnaa, formal), उड़ाना (udaanaa, colloquial, 'to blow money')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi households, this phrase comes up constantly around budgeting -- मत खर्च करो (mat kharch karo, 'don't spend') is something every parent says to a teenager with new pocket money. The noun ख़र्च (kharch) alone (as in घर का खर्च, ghar kaa kharch, 'household expenses') is worth knowing alongside this verb phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say पैसा खर्च होना (paisaa kharch honaa) when they mean the money got spent on its own (intransitive/impersonal), versus पैसा खर्च करना (paisaa kharch karnaa) when a person deliberately spends it (transitive) -- mixing these up changes who is doing the spending. 2) The amount spent takes पर (par) or में (men) before the item, as in किताबों पर खर्च करना (kitaabon par kharch karnaa, 'to spend on books'), which learners sometimes replace with के लिए (ke lie), giving a slightly off 'for the sake of' feel rather than 'spent on'.
Sample Sentences:
मैं हर महीने किराए पर बीस हज़ार रुपये ख़र्च करता हूँ। I spend twenty thousand rupees on rent every month.
(main har mahiine kiraae par biis hazaar rupye kharch kartaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : to spend time or effort
verb, transitive Used with abstract nouns like समय (samay, 'time'), ताकत (taakat, 'strength'), or मेहनत (mehnat, 'effort') as the object, extending the 'spending' idea from money to non-material resources.
Synonyms: बिताना (bitaanaa, for time specifically), लगाना (lagaanaa, colloquial, for effort/energy)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension treats time and energy like money -- a very natural metaphor in Hindi just as in English ('don't waste your time'), so it feels intuitive to English speakers once they notice the parallel.
Common Mistakes: 1) For time specifically, native speakers usually prefer बिताना (bitaanaa) over ख़र्च करना (kharch karnaa); using ख़र्च करना with समय (samay) is understood but can sound slightly bookish or deliberately emphatic about time as a limited resource being 'used up'.
