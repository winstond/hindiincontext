उलटा (ultaa)

adjective/adverb/verb Covers three related uses: an adjective meaning 'inverted/reverse/wrong-side-out', an adverb meaning 'instead, on the contrary', and a verb sense (from उलटना, ulatnaa) meaning 'to overturn/reverse'. Also spelled उल्टा (ultaa) with a hard 'l' -- both spellings are common.
Sense 1 : inverted, upside-down, wrong-side-out
adjective
Synonyms: विपरीत (vipriit, more formal), उल्टा (ultaa, variant spelling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every mother uses when your shirt is on inside-out: कमीज़ उलटी है (kamiiz ultii hai, 'the shirt is inside-out') -- notice it agrees in gender/number with the noun since it's a variable adjective. It pairs naturally with सीधा (siidhaa, 'straight/right-side'), and Hindi speakers reach for this सीधा-उलटा (siidhaa-ultaa) pair constantly, the way English pairs 'right side up' and 'upside down'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use सीधा (siidhaa) and उलटा (ultaa) only for physical orientation, missing the very common extended use for 'the opposite/wrong' way of doing something, e.g. उलटा जवाब (ultaa javaab, 'a contrary/back-answer').
Sample Sentences:

(tumhaarii kamiiz ultii hai।)

Sense 2 : on the contrary, instead, the other way around
adverb Used to flip an expected outcome: 'instead of X happening, the opposite happened' -- often placed at the start of the second clause.
Synonyms: बल्कि (balki, different nuance: 'rather'), इसके विपरीत (iske vipriit, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic 'I tried to help and it backfired' word: मैंने मदद की, उलटा वो नाराज़ हो गया (mainne madad kii, ultaa vo naaraaz ho gayaa, 'I helped, and instead he got angry'). Every Hindi speaker has a story that starts this way.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'instead' reflexively with बल्कि (balki), but उलटा (ultaa) specifically signals a reversal of expectation or outcome, not just an alternative choice. 2) In this adverbial use उलटा (ultaa) stays invariant -- it does not agree with anything, unlike the adjective sense.
Sample Sentences:
मैंने उसकी मदद की, उलटा वो मुझसे नाराज़ हो गया। I helped him, and instead he got angry with me.
(mainne uskii madad kii, ultaa vo mujhse naaraaz ho gayaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : to overturn, knock over, reverse (something)
verb, transitive This is the transitive counterpart of उलटना (ulatnaa, intransitive: 'to overturn/topple'); the causative-like pairing is worth noting to the learner even though they are technically the same verb root used transitively/intransitively.
Synonyms: पलटना (palatnaa, very close synonym), गिराना (giraanaa, focuses on knocking down/spilling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in kitchen mishaps: चाय उलट गई (chaay ulat gaii, 'the tea spilled') -- note the intransitive form takes ने-less agreement, while the transitive 'someone spilled it' version needs ने (ne) with the agent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पलटना (palatnaa) in every context, but उलटा (ultaa) verb forms carry more of a 'spilled/knocked over by accident' flavor, especially with liquids or containers, e.g. दूध का गिलास उलट दिया (doodh kaa gilaas ulat diyaa, 'knocked over the glass of milk').
Sample Sentences:
बच्चे ने गलती से दूध का गिलास उलटा दिया। The child accidentally knocked over the glass of milk.
(bachche ne galtii se doodh kaa gilaas ultaa diyaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
