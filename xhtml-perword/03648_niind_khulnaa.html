नींद खुलना (niind khulnaa)

verb A single verbal idiom meaning 'to wake up' -- literally 'for sleep to open/break'; frames waking as something that happens to a person rather than an act they perform.
Sense 1 : to wake up (spontaneously, not by an alarm or effort)
verb, intransitive The person who wakes is marked with की (kii) as possessor of नींद (niind), e.g. मेरी नींद खुल गई (merii niind khul gaii, 'my sleep broke/I woke up'), not with ने (ne) or को (ko) -- नींद (niind) itself is the grammatical subject.
Synonyms: जागना (jaagnaa, to be/become awake), उठना (uthnaa, to get up, wider sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic body-as-agent construction, much like भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa, 'to feel hungry') -- Hindi treats waking as something that happens to your sleep, not something you actively do. You will hear this constantly in daily chat: सुबह जल्दी नींद खुल गई (subah jaldii niind khul gaii, 'I woke up early this morning'), often followed by an explanation of what disturbed the sleep, like शोर से (shor se, 'because of the noise').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जागना (jaagnaa) or उठना (uthnaa) here, but नींद खुलना (niind khulnaa) specifically describes the involuntary moment of waking (often before you even get up), while उठना (uthnaa) is the physical act of getting out of bed. 2) The verb agrees with नींद (niind) (feminine), not with the person: मेरी नींद खुल गई (merii niind khul gaii, 'I woke up'), never a masculine agreement even for a male speaker.
