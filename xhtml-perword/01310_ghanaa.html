घना (ghanaa)

adjective An adjective describing thickness or density -- most often heard about hair, forests, fog, or crowds, and occasionally used figuratively for close/deep relationships.
Sense 1 : dense, thick (of physical things -- hair, forest, fog, crowd)
adjective
Synonyms: गाढ़ा (gaadhaa, used more for liquids/colors), भरा-पूरा (bharaa-pooraa, of a full/lush thing like hair or a crowd)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture घना जंगल (ghanaa jangal, 'dense forest') or घने बाल (ghane baal, 'thick hair') -- this is the word Indian shampoo ads love, promising बाल घने और काले (baal ghane aur kaale, 'hair thick and black'). It also shows up in winter news reports as घना कोहरा (ghanaa kohraa, 'dense fog'), a very real Delhi-NCR January experience.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for गाढ़ा (gaadhaa) when they mean घना (ghanaa) -- गाढ़ा (gaadhaa) suits liquids, colors, and relationships (गाढ़ी दोस्ती (gaadhii dostii, 'thick/close friendship')), while घना (ghanaa) is for things made of many close-packed strands or particles: hair, trees, fog, smoke, crowds.

Sense 2 : close, intimate (of a bond or relationship)
adjective
Synonyms: गहरा (gahraa, more common for this sense), गाढ़ा (gaadhaa, as in गाढ़ी दोस्ती (gaadhii dostii, 'close friendship'))
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative extension treats a relationship as if it had the same close-packed density as thick hair or a dense forest -- घना रिश्ता (ghanaa rishtaa, 'close/intimate relationship'). It's less common than गहरा (gahraa) for this meaning, so learners will hear it less often, but it's not wrong.

(un donon bhaaiyon kaa rishtaa bahut ghanaa hai।)
