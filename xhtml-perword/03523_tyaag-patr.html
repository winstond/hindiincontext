त्याग-पत्र (tyaag-patr)

noun A compound noun meaning 'resignation letter' -- literally a 'renunciation letter,' built from त्याग (tyaag, 'renunciation/giving up') plus पत्र (patr, 'letter/document'). One core sense, used mainly in the fixed phrase त्याग-पत्र देना (tyaag-patr denaa).
Sense 1 : resignation letter
noun, masculine Almost always appears as the object of देना (denaa, 'to give') or the intransitive verb होना (honaa), e.g. त्याग-पत्र देना (tyaag-patr denaa, 'to submit one's resignation').
Synonyms: इस्तीफ़ा (istiifaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: इस्तीफ़ा (istiifaa) is the word you'll actually hear most in spoken Hindi and in news headlines about politicians and officials resigning; त्याग-पत्र (tyaag-patr) is the more formal, written-document term you'd use for the actual letter itself, like the one you'd hand your HR department. The two are near-perfect synonyms, but त्याग-पत्र (tyaag-patr) leans slightly more bureaucratic/official in feel because of its literal 'renunciation' root.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for छोड़ना (chhodnaa, 'to leave/quit') alone when they mean 'to resign,' but the standard formal phrase is त्याग-पत्र देना (tyaag-patr denaa) -- छोड़ना (chhodnaa) describes leaving the job itself, not the act of formally submitting the letter. 2) The word is written with or without the hyphen (त्याग-पत्र (tyaag-patr) or त्यागपत्र (tyaagpatr)) -- both are seen in print, so don't assume one is wrong.

मैंने आज सुबह अपना त्याग-पत्र दे दिया। I submitted my resignation letter this morning.
(mainne aaj subah apnaa tyaag-patr de diyaa।)
