की ख़ातिर (kii khaatir)

postposition A postpositional phrase meaning 'for the sake of' -- follows the genitive की (kii) plus ख़ातिर (khaatir, 'sake'), used to mark purpose or benefit, similar in feel to के लिए (ke lie) but warmer and more emphatic.
Sense 1 : for the sake of, for the benefit of
postposition Attaches to a noun/pronoun already marked with the genitive की/के/की (kii) matching gender/number of the possessor construction, e.g. उसकी ख़ातिर (uskii khaatir, 'for his/her sake'); functions exactly like के लिए (ke lie) but adds emotional weight, often implying sacrifice or devotion.
Synonyms: के लिए (ke lie), की भलाई के लिए (kii bhalaaii ke lie, more explicit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this often in emotional appeals -- a mother might say बच्चों की ख़ातिर (bachchon kii khaatir, 'for the children's sake') to explain why she endures hardship, and it carries more heart than the plain के लिए (ke lie). The word ख़ातिर (khaatir) itself comes from Persian/Urdu and also independently means 'hospitality' or 'regard' (as in ख़ातिर करना (khaatir karnaa), 'to look after/entertain a guest'), so native speakers feel a warm, almost personal texture in this phrase beyond the neutral 'for'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use यह के लिए-style literal translation and forget the phrase must follow the genitive form (की/के/की), not the plain noun -- it is कि ख़ातिर (uskii khaatir), never उस ख़ातिर. 2) ख़ातिर (khaatir) alone (without की) is also used colloquially to mean 'for the sake of' in casual speech, e.g. दोस्ती की ख़ातिर (dostii kii khaatir, 'for the sake of friendship') vs. just ख़ातिर (khaatir) in looser speech -- both are heard, but की ख़ातिर is the fuller, more standard form.
Sample Sentences:
वह अपने बच्चों की ख़ातिर दिन-रात मेहनत करती है। She works day and night for the sake of her children.
(vah apne bachchon kii khaatir din-raat mehnat kartii hai।)
