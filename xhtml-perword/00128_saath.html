साथ (saath)

postposition/noun/adverb Mainly used as a noun meaning 'company/togetherness' and, far more frequently, inside the postposition phrase के साथ (ke saath) meaning 'with.' Both uses share the same root idea of accompaniment.
Sense 1 : with (accompaniment/instrument)
postposition Occurs as the compound postposition के साथ (ke saath) governing the oblique case, e.g. उसके साथ (uske saath, 'with him/her').
Synonyms: समेत (samet, formal, means 'along with/including')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers reach for के साथ (ke saath) constantly in daily life -- मेरे साथ चलो (mere saath chalo, 'come with me') is one of the first phrases friends and family use to invite someone along, whether to the market, a wedding, or just next door for chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop के (ke) and just say साथ (saath) after a noun -- the के (ke) is required except after pronoun forms like मेरे साथ (mere saath, 'with me'), जिसके साथ (jiske saath), etc. where the possessive form itself already carries it. 2) Confusing साथ (saath, 'with a person/thing') with से (se) when the meaning is closer to 'by means of' -- चाकू से काटना (chaakoo se kaatnaa, 'to cut with a knife') uses से (se), not साथ (saath), because it is an instrument, not a companion.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे साथ चलो, बाज़ार जाना है। Come with me, I need to go to the market.
(mere saath chalo, baazaar jaanaa hai।)

Sense 2 : company, companionship
noun, masculine Common in the phrase साथ देना (saath denaa, 'to support/stand by someone') and साथ रहना (saath rahnaa, 'to live/stay together').
Synonyms: साहचर्य (saahchary, literary), संगति (sangti, somewhat formal, often of company/influence)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: साथ (saath) as a noun carries warm emotional weight in Hindi -- तुम्हारा साथ चाहिए (tumhaaraa saath chaahie, 'I need your company/support') is the kind of line you hear in both everyday conversation and in film dialogue about friendship or family bonds.
Common Mistakes: 1) साथ देना (saath denaa) does not literally mean 'to give company' in a passive sense -- it means to actively support or back someone up, e.g. in a difficult time, and learners sometimes underuse it in favor of a literal 'support' translation like समर्थन (samarthan).

Sense 3 : together, simultaneously
adverb Used adverbially without के (ke) when it modifies a verb directly to mean 'together,' as in साथ खाना खाते हैं (saath khaanaa khaate hain, 'we eat together').
Synonyms: एक साथ (ek saath, more emphatic, 'all together/at once'), मिलकर (milkar, 'jointly, by combining')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in the very common phrase साथ साथ (saath saath, 'along together, side by side'), often repeated for emphasis -- हम साथ साथ चलेंगे (ham saath saath chalenge, 'we'll walk along together') has that comfortable, companionable ring Hindi likes to give to shared journeys, literal or metaphorical.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert के (ke) here by analogy with the postposition sense, producing an incorrect *उसके साथ खाना खाते हैं* when the intended meaning is simply 'we eat together' (no specific companion named) -- here साथ (saath) alone functions as the adverb, and के साथ (ke saath) would need a following noun/pronoun object to be grammatical.

चलो, आज हम सब साथ खाना खाते हैं। Come on, today let's all eat together.
