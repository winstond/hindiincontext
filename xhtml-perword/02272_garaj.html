गरज (garaj)

noun Two related but distinct senses: the physical/literal roar of thunder or a lion (a sound), and the figurative sense of 'need, self-interest, or motive' that drives a person to act. Also connects to the verb गरजना (garajnaa, 'to roar/thunder').
Sense 1 : self-interest, need, ulterior motive
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मतलब (matlab), स्वार्थ (svaarth), ज़रूरत (zaroorat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the well-known saying गरज़ पड़ना (garaz padnaa, 'for a need/interest to fall upon someone'), often used cynically about people who only show up when they want something -- जब गरज़ होती है तब याद करते हैं (jab garaz hotii hai tab yaad karte hain, 'they remember you only when they need you'). It captures a very Delhi-market kind of worldliness about self-interested behavior.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only know गरज (garaj) as 'thunder' and miss this completely separate figurative sense of 'selfish need/motive' -- as in the common saying गरज़ है तो सब कुछ (garaz hai to sab kuchh, 'if there's a need, everything follows'). 2) This sense pairs heavily with the postposition से in idiomatic phrases like किसी से गरज़ होना (kisii se garaz honaa, 'to have a need/interest in someone') -- the से marks who the need is directed toward, not a cause.
Sample Sentences:
जब गरज़ होती है तब ही लोग याद करते हैं। People remember you only when they need something.
(jab garaz hotii hai tab hii log yaad karte hain।)

Sense 2 : roar, thunder, loud rumbling sound
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गड़गड़ाहट (gadagdaahat), दहाड़ (dahaad, typically for an animal's roar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the literal sound sense, as in बादल की गरज (baadal kii garaj, 'the roar of the clouds/thunder') or शेर की गरज (sher kii garaj, 'the lion's roar'). It shares its root with the verb गरजना (garajnaa, 'to thunder, to roar'), and Hindi speakers often pair it with बरसना (barasnaa, 'to rain') in the old saying about empty threats: बहुत गरजने वाले बादल कम बरसते हैं (bahut garajne vaale baadal kam baraste hain, 'clouds that thunder a lot rain little') -- used for people who talk big but do little.
