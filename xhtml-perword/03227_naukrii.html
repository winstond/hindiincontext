नौकरी (naukrii)

noun A single core sense: paid employment or a job/post. Learners will meet it constantly in set phrases about working, applying, and quitting.
Sense 1 : job, employment, service
noun, feminine
Synonyms: काम (kaam, broader, also means 'work' generally), नौकरी-चाकरी (naukrii-chaakrii, colloquial, emphatic doubling), पद (pad, meaning 'post/position' rather than the job itself), मुलाज़मत (mulaazmat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR households, नौकरी (naukrii) carries a specific cultural weight: it usually implies a steady salaried post (especially government, सरकारी नौकरी sarkaarii naukrii) as opposed to व्यापार (vyaapaar, business) or freelance काम (kaam). Parents anxious about a child's future will ask नौकरी लगी या नहीं? (naukrii lagii yaa nahiin?, 'did the job come through or not?') -- notice how the job is framed as something that arrives or lands on you, not something you actively seize.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say करना (karnaa) with नौकरी (naukrii) when they mean 'to work at a job' -- नौकरी करना (naukrii karnaa, 'to have/do a job') is correct and very common, but do not confuse it with काम करना (kaam karnaa, 'to do work/a task'), which is about the activity, not the employment status. 2) 'To get a job' is नौकरी लगना (naukrii lagnaa, 'the job to land [on someone]') or नौकरी मिलना (naukrii milnaa), both using dative-subject constructions with को (ko) -- learners default to करना (karnaa) here, which sounds off. 3) 'To lose a job' is नौकरी छूटना (naukrii chhootnaa, intransitive, 'the job slips away') or नौकरी जाना (naukrii jaanaa), not a direct translation of 'lose.'
Sample Sentences:
मुझे पिछले महीने एक नई नौकरी मिली। I got a new job last month.
(mujhe pichhle mahiine ek naii naukrii milii।)
