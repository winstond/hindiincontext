ठेठ (theth)

adjective/adverb An adjective/adverb describing something as pure, authentic, or unmixed -- typically used for language, dialect, or manner, meaning 'genuine to the core' or 'thoroughgoing.'
Sense 1 : pure, authentic, unmixed (usually of language/dialect/style)
adjective Most often modifies a noun for language or regional identity, e.g. ठेठ हिंदी (theth hindii, 'pure/authentic Hindi'), ठेठ देहाती (theth dehaatii, 'thoroughly rustic').
Synonyms: शुद्ध (shuddh), खरा (kharaa), असली (aslii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word a lot when people praise someone's speech as ठेठ देहाती (theth dehaatii, 'pure village-style') or ठेठ मुंबईया (theth mumbaiiyaa, 'true-blue Mumbai style') -- it carries a warm, almost proud sense of 'unfiltered and real,' not a negative one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse ठेठ (theth) with शुद्ध (shuddh); शुद्ध (shuddh) suggests grammatical/formal correctness, while ठेठ (theth) suggests unfiltered, native, close-to-the-soil authenticity -- it can even describe a rustic or unpolished accent approvingly. 2) It is invariant in form, so learners should not try to inflect it like a typical -आ adjective.
Sample Sentences:
वह ठेठ देहाती हिंदी बोलता है। He speaks pure, rustic Hindi.
(vah theth dehaatii hindii boltaa hai।)

Sense 2 : right up to, all the way to (extent/distance)
adverb Used before a place or point to stress that something goes all the way there without stopping short, e.g. ठेठ दिल्ली तक (theth dillii tak, 'all the way to Delhi').
Synonyms: बिल्कुल (bilkul), एकदम (ekdam)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This usage pops up in travel talk and storytelling -- ठेठ गाँव तक (theth gaanv tak, 'right into the village') gives a sense of going deep or far without any shortcuts.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense pairs naturally with तक (tak); dropping तक (tak) can make the sentence sound incomplete to native ears.
