छेद (chhed)

noun A concrete noun meaning a hole, puncture, or perforation, with a natural extension to a figurative 'flaw/loophole' sense -- both covered below.
Sense 1 : hole, puncture, perforation
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुराख़ (suraakh), छिद्र (chhidr, formal/technical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly around the house -- a mother checking a sweater for छेद (chhed) before winter, or a mechanic pointing out a टायर में छेद (taayar men chhed, 'a hole in the tire'). छिद्र (chhidr) is the more Sanskritized, textbook-y version of the same idea, common in biology class (pores, follicles) but less natural in everyday chatter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse छेद (chhed) with गड्ढा (gaddhaa) -- छेद (chhed) is a hole that goes through something (like a puncture in cloth or a pierced ear), while गड्ढा (gaddhaa) is a pit or depression in the ground.
Sample Sentences:
इस मोज़े में एक बड़ा छेद है। There's a big hole in this sock.
(is moze men ek badaa chhed hai।)

Sense 2 : flaw, defect, loophole (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the fixed phrase छेद निकालना (chhed nikaalnaa, 'to find fault/pick holes') and छेद ढूँढना (chhed dhoondhnaa, 'to look for a flaw').
Synonyms: कमी (kamii), ख़ामी (khaamii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image as English 'picking holes in an argument' -- Hindi speakers use छेद निकालना (chhed nikaalnaa) exactly this way when someone nitpicks a plan or an excuse. You'll hear parents or bosses use it: उसमें बहुत छेद हैं (usmen bahut chhed hain, 'there are a lot of flaws in it').
Sample Sentences:

(uskii kahaanii men bahut chhed hain।)
