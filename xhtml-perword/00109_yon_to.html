यों तो (yon to)

conjunction A discourse connector meaning 'well, technically...' or 'though in general...' -- it concedes a general truth before pivoting to a contrast, usually followed by लेकिन (lekin) or पर (par).
Sense 1 : concessive discourse marker introducing a general truth before a contrast
conjunction Sets up a concession that is almost always completed by a following clause with लेकिन (lekin), पर (par), or मगर (magar) -- the two halves work as a pair, much like 'sure, X, but Y.'
Synonyms: वैसे तो (vaise to), हालांकि (haalaanki, more formal, often frontal without needing a paired but-clause)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic hedge you will hear constantly in everyday Delhi conversation, especially when someone is about to soften a criticism or admit a general rule has exceptions -- like a mother saying यों तो वो अच्छा लड़का है, लेकिन... (yon to vo achchhaa ladkaa hai, lekin..., 'he's a good boy, generally speaking, but...') right before the complaint comes out.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use यों तो (yon to) alone without a following contrast clause, which sounds incomplete to a native speaker -- the phrase sets up an expectation that something will push back against the initial claim. 2) It is sometimes confused with यूं ही (yoon hii, 'just like that, for no reason'), which is unrelated in meaning despite the similar-looking यों (yon) / यूं (yoon).
Sample Sentences:
यों तो वो अच्छा लड़का है, लेकिन कभी-कभी बहुत ज़िद्दी हो जाता है। Generally speaking he's a good boy, but sometimes he gets very stubborn.
(yon to vo achchhaa ladkaa hai, lekin kabhii-kabhii bahut ziddii ho jaataa hai।)
