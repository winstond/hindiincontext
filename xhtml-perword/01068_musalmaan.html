मुसलमान (musalmaan)

noun/adjective A noun meaning 'Muslim' (a follower of Islam), and by extension used as an adjective describing something as Muslim/Islamic; the same word serves both functions without changing form.
Sense 1 : a Muslim person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: मुस्लिम (muslim)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, मुसलमान (musalmaan) and मुस्लिम (muslim) are used interchangeably in daily life, with मुस्लिम (muslim) feeling slightly more common in news and formal writing. You'll hear this word constantly in conversations about neighborhoods, festivals like ईद (iid), and India's religious diversity, where people casually refer to हिन्दू (hindoo) and मुसलमान (musalmaan) neighbors living side by side.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुसलमान (musalmaan) itself is neutral and standard in everyday speech; learners sometimes worry it might be impolite compared to मुस्लिम (muslim), but both are perfectly respectful in ordinary conversation. 2) As a noun the masculine plural form मुसलमान (musalmaan) stays identical to the singular in casual speech, which can confuse learners expecting a visible plural marker.

Sense 2 : Muslim, Islamic (describing people, customs, or things)
adjective Used invariantly before a noun, e.g. मुसलमान परिवार (musalmaan parivaar, 'a Muslim family'), without gender or number agreement.
Synonyms: मुस्लिम (muslim)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear phrases like मुसलमान त्योहार (musalmaan tyohaar, 'Muslim festival') or मुसलमान इलाका (musalmaan ilaakaa, 'Muslim neighborhood') in everyday Delhi speech, especially when talking about local geography or festival calendars.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect मुसलमान (musalmaan) like a regular adjective (adding आ/ई endings); it never changes form regardless of the noun's gender or number.
