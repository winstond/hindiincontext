नादान (naadaan)

adjective A single-sense adjective (with a matching noun-like use for people) meaning naive, innocent, or immature -- typically used with affection toward children, or gently critical toward adults who acted without understanding the consequences.
Sense 1 : naive, innocent, immature (lacking worldly understanding)
adjective
Synonyms: भोला (bholaa), मासूम (maasoom), अनजान (anjaan, meaning scope: unaware/ignorant, not always innocent-natured), बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof, meaning scope: foolish, harsher and not affectionate)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word a parent or elder uses with real tenderness -- 'अरे, यह तो नादान है' (are, yah to naadaan hai, 'oh, he/she is just naive/innocent') excuses a child's or young person's mistake by pointing to their lack of years, not their lack of sense. It carries none of the sting of calling someone stupid; if anything, it asks the listener to be gentle in return.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof) when they mean नादान (naadaan) -- but बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof) means 'stupid' and can be an insult, while नादान (naadaan) is soft and forgiving, closer to 'doesn't know better yet.' 2) It is very commonly paired with बच्चा (bachchaa) as नादान बच्चा (naadaan bachchaa, 'innocent/naive child') -- learners sometimes miss that this pairing is almost a fixed phrase in everyday speech.
Sample Sentences:
अरे, यह तो नादान है, इसे माफ कर दो। Oh, he's just naive, forgive him.
(are, yah to naadaan hai, ise maaph kar do।)
