दिलचस्प (dilchasp)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'interesting'; from Persian dil ('heart') + chasp ('sticking to'), literally 'that which sticks to the heart'.
Sense 1 : interesting, engaging
adjective
Synonyms: रोचक (rochak), मनोरंजक (manoranjak, leans toward 'entertaining')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a lovely example of how Persian imagery embedded itself in everyday Hindi: dil (dil) is 'heart', and chasp comes from a root meaning 'to stick' or 'cling' -- so something दिलचस्प (dilchasp) is quite literally 'heart-sticking', the thing your attention clings to. You'll hear it constantly in casual conversation about books, movies, people, and gossip alike -- it's a real workhorse word, not at all formal or bookish despite its Persian pedigree.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like a regular -आ (aa) adjective (दिलचस्पा, दिलचस्पे); it is invariant and stays दिलचस्प (dilchasp) regardless of the noun's gender or number. 2) दिलचस्पी (dilchaspii) is the related noun ('interest'), often used with the pattern दिलचस्पी होना (dilchaspii honaa) or दिलचस्पी लेना (dilchaspii lenaa) -- learners sometimes reach for the adjective दिलचस्प (dilchasp) where the noun construction is what's actually needed, e.g. to say 'I'm interested in...' you need मुझे ... में दिलचस्पी है (mujhe ... men dilchaspii hai, 'I have interest in...'), not a direct calque with the adjective.
