यार (yaar)

noun/interjection Primarily a warm, casual term for 'friend/buddy' used constantly among peers, but it also doubles as a filler exclamation of exasperation, emphasis, or camaraderie, and has an older literary sense of 'lover/beloved'.
Sense 1 : close friend, buddy, pal
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दोस्त (dost), मित्र (mitr, formal), साथी (saathii, companion)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you hear on every Delhi street corner and college campus -- चलो यार, कहाँ हो यार (chalo yaar, kahaan ho yaar, 'come on yaar, where are you') -- it is the glue of casual friendship talk. It works almost like a verbal handshake among friends, signaling closeness without needing to say anything else.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use यार (yaar) with strangers or elders out of a desire to sound friendly -- it is strictly for peers and close acquaintances, and using it with someone senior or unfamiliar can sound disrespectful or overly familiar. 2) It skews slightly more common among men in casual speech, though women use it freely too, especially in urban settings.

Sense 2 : filler expressing frustration, emphasis, or camaraderie
interjection Used tacked onto the end (or start) of a sentence purely for emotional color, without referring to any actual person being addressed.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the Hindi equivalent of English 'man' or 'dude' used as a sigh -- ओह यार (oh yaar, 'oh man') when the metro is late, or यार, क्या करूँ (yaar, kyaa karoon, 'man, what do I do') when venting to no one in particular. It carries an intimate, informal register -- you would not use it in a formal complaint or with your boss.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because this use does not literally address a 'friend,' learners sometimes miss it when it's used with strangers in frustration, e.g. अरे यार, ये लाइन खत्म ही नहीं होती (are yaar, ye laain khatm hii nahiin hotii, 'ugh man, this line just won't end') -- here यार (yaar) is not calling the listener a friend at all, it is pure exasperation.

Sense 3 : lover, beloved (literary/poetic)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रेमी (premii, m, lover), माशूक (maashook, literary), सजन (sajan, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older sense survives mainly in Urdu-influenced poetry, ghazals, and old Bollywood songs, where यार (yaar) means 'beloved' rather than 'friend' -- a reminder that the everyday 'buddy' sense actually grew out of this romantic root.
