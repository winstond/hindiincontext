ससुर (sasur)

noun/interjection Primarily means 'father-in-law,' a key member of the in-law family vocabulary; also has a common, rougher colloquial use as a mild abusive/exasperated term for a man.
Sense 1 : father-in-law
noun, masculine
Synonyms: श्वसुर (shvasur, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a typical North Indian household, ससुर (sasur) is spoken of with a mix of respect and formality -- a daughter-in-law traditionally would not call him by name, and many people avoid saying the word directly in his presence, using respectful alternatives like पिताजी (pitaajii, 'father,' used for the father-in-law too) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mix up ससुर (sasur, father-in-law) with सास (saas, mother-in-law) -- remember ससुर (sasur) is male, sास is female. 2) Do not confuse with ससुराल (sasuraal), which means 'in-laws' home/family' as a whole, not the father-in-law himself.

Sense 2 : mild abusive/exasperated exclamation about a man
interjection Used standalone or attached to a noun/pronoun to express annoyance, contempt, or rough familiarity toward a man, e.g. ससुर कहीं मिलता नहीं (sasur kahiin miltaa nahiin, 'the wretch is nowhere to be found').
Synonyms: साला (saalaa, colloquial, similar rough usage)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Like साला (saalaa), this is one of several in-law kinship terms in Hindi that get repurposed as casual, blokey exclamations -- you will hear it in markets, among friends, or in Bollywood dialogue exchanges where men are ribbing each other, but it carries a rough, informal edge.
Common Mistakes: 1) This use is mildly coarse and male-register -- learners should avoid using it in polite or mixed-formality company, even though it is common in casual banter among men. 2) It is not literally accusing someone of being a father-in-law; the kinship meaning is completely bleached out here.
