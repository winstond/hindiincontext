तेज़ी (tezii)

noun A noun meaning 'speed/quickness' or, by extension, 'sharpness/intensity' -- and in market/economic contexts, a 'boom' or 'upswing'. Related to the adjective तेज़ (tez, fast/sharp).
Sense 1 : speed, quickness
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जल्दी (jaldii), फुर्ती (phurtii, nimbleness), रफ़्तार (raftaar, pace)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear तेज़ी से (tezii se) constantly -- 'तेज़ी से बढ़ना (tezii se badhnaa, to grow rapidly)' is the go-to phrase for anything from traffic to inflation to a child growing up fast. It is the noun form of तेज़ (tez), so if you know that adjective, this word is just its abstract-quality version.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जल्दी (jaldii) for 'speed,' but जल्दी (jaldii) usually means 'haste/hurry' as an action, while तेज़ी (tezii) names the quality of speed itself -- so तेज़ी से (tezii se, 'quickly, with speed') is the natural adverbial phrase, not just जल्दी से (jaldii se).

Sense 2 : sharpness, intensity (of pain, heat, taste, light, etc.)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: तीव्रता (tiivrtaa, slightly more formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as तेज़ (tez) describes a sharp chili, a bright light, or a strong fever, तेज़ी (tezii) is used for the degree of that sharpness -- दर्द की तेज़ी (dard kii tezii, 'the intensity of the pain') or बुखार की तेज़ी (bukhaar kii tezii, 'the severity of the fever'). Think of it as the noun you reach for whenever तेज़ (tez) needs to become a quantity rather than a description.

Sense 3 : market boom, economic upswing
noun, feminine
Synonyms: उछाल (uchhaal, surge, esp. in prices)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Flip on any Hindi business news channel and you will hear शेयर बाज़ार में तेज़ी (sheyar baazaar men tezii, 'a rally in the stock market') almost daily -- तेज़ी (tezii) and मंदी (mandii) form the standard boom/bust vocabulary pair, much like 'bull' and 'bear' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) In business news this is a fixed opposite pair with मंदी (mandii, slowdown/recession) -- सोने में तेज़ी (sone men tezii, 'a boom in gold prices') is a standard headline pattern learners should recognize as a set collocation, not build word-by-word.
