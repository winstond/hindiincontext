शिमला (shimlaa)

noun A proper noun: the city of Shimla, capital of Himachal Pradesh, famous as a former British colonial hill station. Also seen in the compound शिमला मिर्च (shimlaa mirch), the everyday Hindi name for bell pepper/capsicum.
Sense 1 : the city of Shimla
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Shimla was the summer capital of British India, and older Delhi-NCR families still talk about it as the classic honeymoon or summer-vacation hill station, along with Manali (manaalii) and Nainital (nainiitaal). If you hear someone say हम शिमला घूमने गए (ham shimlaa ghoomne gae, 'we went to Shimla to sightsee'), it is almost always about a holiday trip, not business.

Sense 2 : bell pepper / capsicum (short for शिमला मिर्च)
noun, feminine In casual kitchen and market speech, शिमला (shimlaa) alone can stand in for शिमला मिर्च (shimlaa mirch), much like English speakers say 'pepper' for 'bell pepper.'
Synonyms: शिमला मिर्च (shimlaa mirch, full form), कैप्सिकम (kaipsikam)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The vegetable has nothing to do with the actual growing region historically -- it is simply named after the city, the way English uses place-names for foods. At a Delhi sabzi mandi (sabzii mandii) you will hear vendors call out शिमला ले लो (shimlaa le lo, 'take some capsicum'), shortening the full name in fast market chatter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not confuse this शिमला (shimlaa) with hot chili peppers -- मिर्च (mirch) alone usually means the spicy green/red chili, while शिमला मिर्च (shimlaa mirch) specifically means the mild, sweet bell pepper.

(bhaiyaa, do kilo shimlaa de denaa।)
