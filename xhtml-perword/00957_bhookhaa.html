भूखा (bhookhaa)

adjective A common adjective meaning 'hungry,' with an extended figurative sense meaning 'craving/greedy for' something non-physical, as in power or attention.
Sense 1 : hungry (for food)
adjective, masculine/feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian mother uses the moment you walk in the door: तुम भूखे हो? (tum bhookhe ho, 'are you hungry?') is practically a greeting in many homes, food being the default love language. It pairs naturally with its noun root भूख (bhookh, 'hunger') -- feel free to think of भूखा (bhookhaa) as simply 'having भूख.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मुझे भूखा है (mujhe bhookhaa hai) by analogy with other को-marked feelings, but भूखा (bhookhaa) agrees with the subject in gender/number and takes a normal subject: मैं भूखा हूँ (main bhookhaa hoon, 'I am hungry'), not a dative-subject construction. 2) Do not confuse with the verb भूखना (bhookhnaa) or the noun भूख (bhookh, 'hunger') -- भूखा (bhookhaa) itself is the adjective describing the state, and the two are often mixed up by beginners reaching for 'I am hunger' instead of 'I am hungry.'

(kyaa tum bhookhe ho?)

Sense 2 : craving, greedy, starved for (something abstract)
adjective, masculine/feminine Takes का (kaa) to mark what one is hungry for, e.g. पैसे का भूखा (paise kaa bhookhaa, 'hungry for money').
Synonyms: लालची (laalchii, greedy -- broader, not just 'hungry for')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this used pointedly about people, often with a critical edge: सत्ता का भूखा (sattaa kaa bhookhaa, 'power-hungry') or तारीफ़ का भूखा (taariif kaa bhookhaa, 'hungry for praise') both carry a faint disapproval, much like the English 'power-hungry' does.
