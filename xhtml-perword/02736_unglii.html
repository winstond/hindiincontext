उँगली (unglii)

noun A single, everyday sense: the finger (or, by extension, toe). Also commonly spelled अंगुली (angulii) or उंगली (unglii, with an anusvara instead of a candrabindu).
Sense 1 : finger
noun, feminine
Synonyms: अंगुली (angulii, variant form), उंगली (unglii, variant form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word lives inside several vivid idioms every learner runs into fast: उँगली उठाना (unglii uthaanaa, 'to point/accuse'), उँगलियों पर नचाना (ungliyon par nachaanaa, 'to make someone dance on your fingers' -- i.e., to control someone completely), and उँगली पकड़ कर चलना (unglii pakad kar chalnaa, 'to walk holding someone's finger') which is exactly the tender image Hindi uses for a parent guiding a toddler by the finger, more common than 'holding hands' for that scene.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that this word takes feminine agreement throughout -- मेरी उँगली (merii unglii), not मेरा। 2) उँगली उठाना (unglii uthaanaa) does not mean 'to raise a finger' casually as in English -- it specifically means 'to point a finger of blame/suspicion at someone,' so it is used more narrowly than its literal English translation suggests. 3) The word also covers toes in casual speech (पैर की उँगली, pair kii unglii, 'toe' -- literally 'finger of the foot'); there is no separate everyday word for toe.
