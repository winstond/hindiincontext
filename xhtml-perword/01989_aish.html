ऐश (aish)

noun A colloquial word centered on living it up and enjoying life's comforts, most alive today in the phrase ऐश करना (aish karnaa) and the compound ऐश-आराम (aish-aaraam).
Sense 1 : luxury, pleasure, enjoyment, comfortable living
noun, feminine Most commonly seen inside the verb phrase ऐश करना (aish karnaa, 'to enjoy/live it up') rather than standing alone as a plain object of other verbs.
Synonyms: आराम (aaraam), मज़ा (mazaa), भोग-विलास (bhog-vilaas, formal), ऐश-ओ-आराम (aish-o-aaraam, fixed phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ऐश (aish) carries a slightly cheeky, indulgent flavor in everyday Delhi speech -- friends tease each other with lines like ऐश कर रहे हो? (aish kar rahe ho, 'living the good life, are you?') when someone is relaxing while others work. It pairs naturally with आराम (aaraam) in the common combo ऐश-आराम (aish-aaraam), 'creature comforts.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use ऐश (aish) as a generic word for 'happiness,' but it specifically implies indulgent, carefree enjoyment or luxury, not just any happy feeling -- खुशी (khushii) is the word for plain happiness. 2) The set phrase ऐश करना (aish karnaa) is used almost like a single unit meaning 'to live the good life' or 'to have a blast' -- learners sometimes try to modify ऐश (aish) with adjectives the way they would a regular noun, which sounds odd; it works best in this fixed collocation.
