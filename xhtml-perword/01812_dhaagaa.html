धागा (dhaagaa)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'thread/string,' with a common figurative extension for a bond or connection between people.
Sense 1 : thread, string
noun, masculine
Synonyms: डोरी (dorii, thinner or twine-like), सूत (soot, spun yarn)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian household has a सुई-धागा (suii-dhaagaa, 'needle and thread') box tucked away for quick repairs -- a button popping off is met with 'धागा ला (dhaagaa laa, 'bring the thread')' rather than any fuss. The raakhii thread tied on Raksha Bandhan and the sacred जनेऊ (janeoo) thread both draw on this same everyday word for their symbolic power.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse धागा (dhaagaa) with डोरी (dorii) -- डोरी tends to be used for a slightly thicker cord or string (like a kite string), while धागा is the everyday word for sewing thread. 2) In tailoring and craft contexts, सुई-धागा (suii-dhaagaa, 'needle and thread') is a set phrase, always in that order.

Sense 2 : bond, connection (figurative)
noun, masculine Used with verbs like जुड़ा होना (judaa honaa, 'to be connected') or टूटना (tootnaa, 'to break') to describe an emotional or relational bond, much like English 'thread' of connection.
Synonyms: रिश्ता (rishtaa, relationship/bond), नाता (naataa, tie/link)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image English speakers use in 'hanging by a thread,' but Hindi extends it warmly to relationships too -- रिश्तों का धागा (rishton kaa dhaagaa, 'the thread of relationships') is a common way to talk about family ties that hold people together across distance.
