हरियाली (hariyaalii)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'greenery' or 'lushness' -- both the literal sight of green vegetation and, by extension, a festive/seasonal association with the monsoon and fresh growth.
Sense 1 : greenery, lush vegetation
noun, feminine
Synonyms: हरा-भरापन (haraa-bharaapan, rare), वनस्पति (vanaspti, formal, refers more to flora/plant-life than the visual greenness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Delhi-wallahs use the moment the monsoon breaks and the parks, medians, and Ridge forest turn from dusty brown to green almost overnight -- लोग कहते हैं, 'बारिश के बाद कितनी हरियाली आ गई है' (baarish ke baad kitnii hariyaalii aa gaii hai, 'how much greenery has come after the rain'). It also names the Hariyali Teej (hariyaalii tiij) festival, celebrating the monsoon's arrival.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat हरियाली (hariyaalii) as just the adjective हरा (haraa, 'green') with a suffix stuck on and try to use it adjectivally -- it is a standalone noun and needs की/से etc. to link to other nouns, e.g. हरियाली की चादर (hariyaalii kii chaadar, 'a blanket of greenery').

Sense 2 : freshness, flourishing/prosperous state (figurative)
noun, feminine Used figuratively to describe a thriving or rejuvenated state, often of a place, relationship, or period of life, by extension from literal greenery.
Synonyms: खुशहाली (khushahaalii, closer to 'prosperity' than literal green)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in a slightly poetic or hopeful register -- someone might say ज़िंदगी में हरियाली आ गई (zindgii men hariyaalii aa gaii, 'greenery/freshness has come into life') to mean things have turned good and full of promise again, much like English 'a new lease of life.'
