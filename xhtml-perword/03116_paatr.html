पात्र (paatr)

noun/adjective Two main strands: a concrete noun for a vessel/container/utensil, and an abstract noun meaning a deserving/eligible/suitable person; also used as an adjective meaning 'fit, worthy, eligible.' A third sense as 'character' (in a play/story) is common in literary and media contexts.
Sense 1 : a person who is eligible, deserving, or suitable (for something)
noun, masculine Typically used with के लिए (ke lie) or a genitive-like construction to specify what the person is eligible for, e.g. सहायता का पात्र (sahaaytaa kaa paatr, 'eligible/entitled to help').
Synonyms: योग्य व्यक्ति (yogy vyakti, descriptive phrase), उपयुक्त व्यक्ति (upyukt vyakti, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will meet constantly in government and scholarship notices -- पात्रता (paatrtaa, 'eligibility') is built directly from this sense, so recognizing पात्र (paatr) here unlocks a whole family of bureaucratic vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is often confused with पात्र (paatr) as adjective (below) -- as a noun it means 'the deserving one' itself, so आप पात्र हैं (aap paatr hain, 'you are eligible') treats it almost like an adjective in practice, and learners get tripped up on whether to add a further noun after it or not.
Sample Sentences:
इस योजना के लिए कौन पात्र है? Who is eligible for this scheme?
(is yojnaa ke lie kaun paatr hai?)

Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Future -- Voice: Passive

Sense 2 : vessel, container, utensil
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बर्तन (bartan), भाँड़ा (bhaandaa, regional/colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of पात्र (paatr) as the 'proper' or slightly bookish word for a container, the kind you would read on a science textbook diagram or a government form, while बर्तन (bartan) is what your mother actually shouts at you to wash.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बर्तन (bartan) as the default word for 'container' and miss that पात्र (paatr) is the more formal/written register word for the same idea -- you will see it on lab equipment, in recipes, or in official language rather than in casual kitchen talk.

Sense 3 : fit, worthy, eligible, deserving
adjective
Synonyms: योग्य (yogy), उपयुक्त (upyukt), लायक (laayak)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in formal announcements: वह पुरस्कार का पात्र है (vah puraskaar kaa paatr hai, 'he is worthy/deserving of the award') -- a slightly stiff, official register word rather than something you would casually say about a friend.
Common Mistakes: 1) As an adjective it is invariant in form regardless of the noun's gender/number, unlike योग्य (yogy) and उपयुक्त (upyukt) which behave the same way -- but learners sometimes expect it to inflect like कड़ा (kadaa)-type adjectives and try to change its ending.
Sample Sentences:

(vah is puraskaar kaa paatr hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
क्या मैं इस योजना के लिए पात्र हूँ? Am I eligible for this scheme?

Sense 4 : a character (in a play, film, story, or novel)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: किरदार (kirdaar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in reviews and discussions of films and serials -- मुख्य पात्र (mukhy paatr, 'main character/protagonist') is a phrase you will see in almost every article about a new show, right alongside its close cousin किरदार (kirdaar).

इस फ़िल्म का मुख्य पात्र बहुत दिलचस्प है। The main character of this film is very interesting.
