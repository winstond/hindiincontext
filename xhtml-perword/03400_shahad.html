शहद (shahad)

noun A simple, single-sense word meaning 'honey' -- both the literal substance and, in a common figurative use, sweetness of speech or manner.
Sense 1 : honey (the substance bees make)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मधु (madhu, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian household keeps a bottle of शहद (shahad) close at hand, usually reached for the moment someone has a cold or sore throat, often stirred into गरम पानी (garam paanii, 'warm water') or licked straight off a spoon. It is also the go-to home remedy paired with अदरक (adrak, 'ginger') or नींबू (niimboo, 'lemon') in winter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes guess a feminine gender by analogy with other food words ending in a consonant, but शहद (shahad) is masculine: शहद मीठा है (shahad miithaa hai, 'honey is sweet'), not शहद मीठी है (shahad miithii hai).

Sense 2 : figurative: sweetness, honeyed quality (of words, voice, or manner)
noun, masculine Used almost always in comparison or description, e.g. describing someone's words as 'dripping with honey' -- बातों में शहद घोलना (baaton men shahad gholnaa, 'to mix honey into one's words', i.e. to speak sweetly/flatteringly).
Synonyms: मिठास (mithaas)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense shows up in idiom rather than plain description -- बातों में शहद घोलना (baaton men shahad gholnaa) is the classic image for someone who talks sweetly, often with a hint that the sweetness is calculated or flattering rather than sincere, much like the English 'honey-tongued.'
