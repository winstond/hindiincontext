आदर करना (aadar karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the noun आदर (aadar, 'respect, regard') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do') -- literally 'to do respect,' i.e. 'to respect, to show respect to someone.'
Sense 1 : to respect, to show respect/deference to someone
verb, transitive The person being respected is marked with की (kii) as in किसी की आदर करना is not standard; the correct frame takes the respected person's possessive form only indirectly -- in practice speakers say किसी का आदर करना (kisii kaa aadar karnaa, 'to respect someone'), with का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) agreeing with आदर, not with the person.
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त करना (izzat karnaa), सम्मान करना (sammaan karnaa, slightly more formal), मान देना (maan denaa, idiomatic variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the verb you hear in the classic moral-science-class refrain बड़ों का आदर करो (badon kaa aadar karo, 'respect your elders') -- it carries a slightly formal, almost textbook-and-values-lesson flavor compared to the more everyday इज़्ज़त करना (izzat karnaa). Parents and teachers reach for आदर when talking about respecting elders, teachers, or traditions rather than casual personal respect between friends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for इज़्ज़त करना (izzat karnaa) instead, which is fine, but आदर करना (aadar karnaa) sits at a slightly more formal/written register and is common in moral or instructional contexts (respecting elders, teachers, traditions). 2) The object of respect takes का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) before आदर, not को (ko) -- learners used to verb-object patterns sometimes wrongly mark the person with को.
