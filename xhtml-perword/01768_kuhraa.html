कुहरा (kuhraa)

noun A concrete, everyday noun meaning 'fog/mist' -- mostly used in its literal weather sense, with a natural figurative extension to mental or perceptual haziness.
Sense 1 : fog, mist (atmospheric)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धुंध (dhundh), कोहरा (kohraa, variant form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every winter, Delhi-NCR mornings from late December into January are dominated by talk of कुहरा (kuhraa) -- flight delays, slow trains, and headlines about 'घना कुहरा' (ghanaa kuhraa, 'dense fog') are a shared seasonal experience here, so this word carries real everyday resonance rather than being just a textbook weather term. Note the common spelling variant कोहरा (kohraa), which is at least as frequent, if not more so, in Delhi speech and writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse कुहरा (kuhraa) with धुआँ (dhuaan, smoke) -- कुहरा is natural weather fog, not man-made smoke. 2) The verb typically paired with it is पड़ना (padnaa) or छाना (chhaanaa), as in कुहरा छाया है (kuhraa chhaayaa hai, 'fog has settled/spread'), not होना (honaa) alone.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : haze, mental fog, confusion (figurative)
noun, masculine Used metaphorically for unclear thinking or a blurred, uncertain situation, e.g. दिमाग़ में कुहरा छाना (dimaagh men kuhraa chhaanaa, 'a fog to settle over the mind').
Synonyms: धुंध (dhundh), उलझन (uljhan, meaning scope: confusion rather than haze)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just as English speakers say 'my mind is foggy,' Hindi speakers extend कुहरा (kuhraa) from weather to thought -- a nice parallel that makes this figurative sense easy to grasp once the literal one is familiar.
Sample Sentences:
