ढकना (dhaknaa)

verb/noun Two related things live under this spelling: the verb 'to cover' and the noun 'lid/cover' (the thing you cover something with). Inline Hindi as Devanagari (dhaknaa).
Sense 1 : to cover, put a covering over something
verb, transitive
Synonyms: छिपाना (chhipaanaa, to hide/conceal, not identical), ढाँपना (dhaampnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a Delhi kitchen: दाल (daal) simmering on the stove, and someone says बर्तन ढक दो (bartan dhak do, 'cover the pot'), often as a compound with दो (denaa) to mean 'go ahead and cover it for me/for now.' The related noun sense, ढकना (dhaknaa, 'lid'), is the thing you'd use to do this very action -- the noun and verb share the same body.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for छिपाना (chhipaanaa) when they mean a physical cover -- छिपाना (chhipaanaa) is about hiding/concealing (an object, a fact, an emotion), while ढकना (dhaknaa) is about physically covering something with a lid, cloth, or blanket. 2) The past-tense object marking follows the standard transitive pattern (उसने बर्तन ढका, usne bartan dhakaa, 'he covered the pot') -- nothing unusual here, but learners occasionally forget ने (ne) because the verb feels 'light.'
Sample Sentences:
दाल उबल रही है, बर्तन ढक दो। The lentils are boiling, cover the pot.
(daal ubal rahii hai, bartan dhak do।)

Sense 2 : lid, cover (the object)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ढक्कन (dhakkan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This noun sense is simply the object you use to perform the verb ढकना (dhaknaa, 'to cover') -- a पतीले का ढकना (patiile kaa dhaknaa, 'the lid of a pot') sitting on the kitchen shelf. Many learners are surprised the same spelling covers both the action and the thing.
Common Mistakes: 1) ढक्कन (dhakkan) is the far more common everyday word for a bottle-cap or jar-lid in casual speech; ढकना (dhaknaa) as a noun is understood but less frequently reached for by native speakers, who often default to ढक्कन (dhakkan).
