थोड़ा-बहुत (thodaa-bahut)

adjective/adverb A reduplicated pair of थोड़ा (thodaa, 'a little') and बहुत (bahut, 'a lot') that together mean 'a little bit, some small amount' -- softer and more casual than थोड़ा (thodaa) alone. Used as both an adjective (modifying a noun) and an adverb (modifying a verb/degree).
Sense 1 : a small amount of (something)
adjective
Synonyms: कुछ (kuchh), ज़रा सा (zaraa saa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly when someone is being modest about their skills: थोड़ी-बहुत अंग्रेज़ी आती है (thodii-bahut angrezii aatii hai, 'I know a little English') is the classic humble-brag reply. It softens a claim in the same way 'a bit of' does in English, and native speakers reach for it instead of plain थोड़ा (thodaa) whenever they want to sound unassuming.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect बहुत (bahut) separately for gender/number the way they would with थोड़ा (thodaa) alone; in this fixed pair, बहुत (bahut) stays as is while only थोड़ा (thodaa) agrees with the noun, e.g. थोड़ी-बहुत हिंदी (thodii-bahut hindii, 'a little bit of Hindi').
Sample Sentences:
मुझे थोड़ी-बहुत अंग्रेज़ी आती है। I know a little bit of English.
(mujhe thodii-bahut angrezii aatii hai।)

Sense 2 : to a small extent, somewhat
adverb
Synonyms: कुछ कुछ (kuchh kuchh), ज़रा (zaraa)
Frequency: medium
