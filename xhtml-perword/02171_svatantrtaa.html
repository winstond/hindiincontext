स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa)

noun A formal, high-register noun meaning 'freedom' or 'independence' -- the word you see on national monuments and hear in speeches, as opposed to the everyday word for personal freedom.
Sense 1 : independence (national/political)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आज़ादी (aazaadii), मुक्ति (mukti, liberation, more spiritual/philosophical sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word on the national holiday स्वतंत्रता दिवस (svatantrtaa divas, 'Independence Day', August 15), and in the Constitution's language about rights. In daily conversation, though, people almost always reach for आज़ादी (aazaadii) instead -- स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa) has a slightly bookish, official flavor, the kind of word a schoolteacher or a news anchor uses rather than a friend.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for आज़ादी (aazaadii) in speech, which is correct and far more common in conversation; स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa) is the word used in formal, written, or official contexts like स्वतंत्रता दिवस (svatantrtaa divas, 'Independence Day'). 2) It is feminine, which affects agreement on any adjective or verb describing it: स्वतंत्रता मिली (svatantrtaa milii, 'independence was received/attained').

(bhaarat ko 1947 men svatantrtaa milii thii।)

Sense 2 : freedom, liberty (general/personal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आज़ादी (aazaadii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used in a broader sense too, as in अभिव्यक्ति की स्वतंत्रता (abhivyakti kii svatantrtaa, 'freedom of expression') -- this legalistic, rights-based flavor is where स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa) tends to appear over आज़ादी (aazaadii), even in speech, because it echoes the constitutional phrasing people grew up hearing.
