उन्नीस (unniis)

numeral/adjective The number nineteen (19); also lives on in a common idiom about small differences.
Sense 1 : the number nineteen
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Numbers between eleven and ninety-nine in Hindi are historically fused forms (not simple ten-plus-unit combinations like English), so उन्नीस (unniis) just has to be learned as its own word rather than derived from नौ (nau, 'nine') and दस (das, 'ten').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like an adjective for gender or number -- it does not change: उन्नीस लड़के (unniis ladke, 'nineteen boys') and उन्नीस लड़कियाँ (unniis ladkiyaan, 'nineteen girls') both keep the same form.

Sense 2 : slightly lesser, a shade worse (in the idiom 'nineteen-twenty')
adjective Occurs almost exclusively inside the fixed comparison उन्नीस-बीस (unniis-biis, 'nineteen-twenty'), describing two things that differ only marginally.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of उन्नीस-बीस (unniis-biis) the way English speakers say 'six of one, half a dozen of the other,' or 'splitting hairs' -- it is the go-to phrase in a Delhi bazaar haggle when comparing two nearly identical items, like two similar-looking mangoes at a फल वाला (phal vaalaa, 'fruit seller')'s cart.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not used as a standalone descriptive adjective the way English 'slightly worse' is -- it needs the paired बीस (biis) to make sense: कीमत में उन्नीस-बीस का फ़र्क़ है (kiimat men unniis-biis kaa farq hai, 'there is a marginal difference in price') is natural, but उन्नीस चीज़ (unniis chiiz) alone to mean 'inferior thing' is not idiomatic.
