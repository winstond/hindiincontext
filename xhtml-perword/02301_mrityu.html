मृत्यु (mrityu)

noun A single-sense, formal/literary noun meaning 'death'; the everyday spoken word for the same idea is मौत (maut).
Sense 1 : death
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मौत (maut, everyday word), निधन (nidhan, formal, used for respected/notable people), देहांत (dehaant, formal, respectful), मरण (maran, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet मृत्यु (mrityu) constantly in news headlines, official announcements, and philosophical or religious discussion (as in the phrase जीवन और मृत्यु (jiivan aur mrityu, 'life and death')), while मौत (maut) is what people actually say at home or on the street. Choosing between them is a nice early lesson in Hindi's formal/everyday register split, much like जल (jal) versus पानी (paanii) for water.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मृत्यु (mrityu) in casual speech where a native speaker would simply say मौत (maut); मृत्यु (mrityu) sounds bookish or formal in everyday conversation, more at home in news reports, obituaries, or solemn contexts. 2) For someone's passing in polite conversation, निधन (nidhan) or देहांत (dehaant) is often preferred over मृत्यु (mrityu) or मौत (maut) as a mark of respect.
Sample Sentences:
दुर्घटना में तीन लोगों की मृत्यु हो गई। Three people died in the accident.
(durghatnaa men tiin logon kii mrityu ho gaii।)
