सज्जन (sajjan)

noun/adjective A respectful noun meaning 'gentleman, good/decent person'; it also works as an adjective meaning 'virtuous, well-mannered.'
Sense 1 : a gentleman, a good/decent person
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भला आदमी (bhalaa aadmii), शरीफ़ आदमी (shariif aadmii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in slightly formal contexts -- introducing an elder, describing a stranger who helped you, or in a speech: 'वे एक सज्जन व्यक्ति हैं' (ve ek sajjan vyakti hain, 'he is a gentleman/decent person'). It has a old-world courtesy to it, the kind of word your grandfather might use approvingly about a neighbor.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use it casually like अंग्रेज़ी (angrezii) 'gentleman,' but सज्जन (sajjan) carries a moral tone (good character), not just polish or manners -- calling someone सज्जन (sajjan) is a compliment about their nature, not their dress sense.

(ve ek sajjan vyakti hain।)

Sense 2 : virtuous, well-mannered, decent
adjective Commonly used to modify person-nouns, as in सज्जन व्यक्ति (sajjan vyakti, 'decent person') or सज्जन पुरुष (sajjan purush, 'gentleman/virtuous man').
Synonyms: भला (bhalaa), शरीफ़ (shariif), नेक (nek)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: As an adjective it pairs naturally with व्यक्ति (vyakti) or पुरुष (purush) to describe someone's inner character rather than outward behavior -- it's the word you'd use to praise someone's सज्जनता (sajjantaa, the related noun 'gentlemanliness/decency').
