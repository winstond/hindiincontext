हो चलना (ho chalnaa)

verb A compound verb built from होना (honaa, 'to be/become') plus चलना (chalnaa) as a vector verb, meaning a gradual process or state has already begun and is steadily progressing -- 'to have started becoming, to be well on the way to.'
Sense 1 : to have begun to become, to be gradually setting in
verb, intransitive हो stays fixed while चलना (chalnaa) conjugates for the gender/number of the subject: हो चला (ho chalaa), हो चली (ho chalii), हो चले (ho chale), हो चलीं (ho chaliin). Commonly used with time-of-day nouns and adjectives describing states that creep up gradually (age, health, weather, darkness).
Synonyms: होने लगना (hone lagnaa), हो जाना (ho jaanaa, less gradual, more sudden completion)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a favorite construction for describing the slow creep of time or age: शाम हो चली है (shaam ho chalii hai, 'evening has begun to set in') or वह बूढ़ा हो चला है (vah boodhaa ho chalaa hai, 'he has begun to turn old') both capture that unmistakably Hindi sense of a state quietly arriving rather than switching on all at once. It pairs naturally with चलना's (chalnaa) own core meaning of ongoing movement -- the state is 'walking along' toward completion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for हो जाना (ho jaanaa) instead, but that frames the change as complete or sudden; हो चलना (ho chalnaa) specifically frames the change as still underway, already in motion. 2) Because हो is frozen and only चलना (chalnaa) carries the agreement, learners sometimes mistakenly try to inflect हो itself (e.g. writing हुई चलना) -- only the second part changes.
Sample Sentences:
शाम हो चली है, अब हमें घर लौट जाना चाहिए। Evening has begun to set in, we should head back home now.
(shaam ho chalii hai, ab hamen ghar laut jaanaa chaahie।)
