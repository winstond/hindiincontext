सेंकना (senknaa)

verb A kitchen-and-body verb built around applying heat: literally to roast/toast/bake food, and by extension to warm a body part at a fire or in the sun (haath senknaa, 'to warm one's hands'). Both senses are transitive.
Sense 1 : to roast, toast, or bake (food) over direct heat
verb, transitive
Synonyms: भूनना (bhoonnaa), भूनना (bhoonnaa, more common for roasting spices/grains)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi mother and she'll tell you रोटी सेंकना (rotii senknaa, 'to toast the roti') is a small daily ritual -- flipping the bread by hand right over the flame until it puffs up, a motion every kitchen in the region knows by feel rather than by recipe.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for भूनना (bhoonnaa) for everything cooked with dry heat; सेंकना (senknaa) is the natural choice specifically for flatbreads like रोटी (rotii) toasted directly on or near a flame, where भूनना (bhoonnaa) would sound off. 2) The base form describes hands-on tending of the food over heat (turning a रोटी (rotii) on a तवा (tavaa)), not just 'cooking' in general -- learners overuse it for boiling or frying, which take different verbs.

Sense 2 : to warm (a body part) at a fire, stove, or in the sun
verb, transitive Almost always appears with a body-part object, e.g. हाथ सेंकना (haath senknaa, 'to warm one's hands') or पीठ सेंकना (piith senknaa, 'to warm one's back'), naming the part being warmed rather than the heat source.
Synonyms: गरमाना (garmaanaa, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a Delhi winter morning: neighbors gathered around a roadside अलाव (alaav, 'bonfire'), rubbing and stretching out their hands -- that scene is exactly what हाथ सेंकना (haath senknaa) evokes, and it is also used figuratively for someone 'warming their hands' opportunistically off someone else's trouble or wealth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the heat source (आग (aag, 'fire') or धूप (dhoop, 'sunshine')) the direct object; instead it takes से (se) -- आग से हाथ सेंकना (aag se haath senknaa, 'to warm one's hands at the fire') -- while the body part stays the object.
