नक़ल (naqal)

noun A common noun covering both 'copy/imitation' (including cheating in exams) and 'a copy/duplicate of a document.' The nuqta under क is often dropped in casual writing (नकल).
Sense 1 : cheating (copying answers, e.g. in an exam)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चोरी (chorii, in exam context)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Indian student and they will have a नक़ल (naqal) story -- chits hidden in socks, answers written on the underside of a ruler. Exam-copying scandals are a recurring theme in Indian news, so this word carries strong cultural resonance beyond the classroom.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this sense with चोरी (chorii) alone, but नक़ल (naqal) is the specific word used for exam-copying; चोरी (chorii) by itself usually means theft in general. 2) The common verb frame is नक़ल करना (naqal karnaa, 'to cheat/copy') -- learners sometimes wrongly reach for a literal 'copy' verb instead.

(pariikshaa men naqal karnaa sakht manaa hai।)

Sense 2 : imitation, mimicry (of a person's mannerisms, voice, etc.)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: नकल (nakal, variant spelling without nuqta), नाटक (naatak, only when performative)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for the party-trick skill of mimicking a teacher's voice or a politician's speech pattern -- a much-loved form of entertainment at Indian family gatherings and in stand-up comedy.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense नक़ल उतारना (naqal utaarnaa, 'to do an impression of someone') is the natural compound-verb frame, distinct from the plainer नक़ल करना (naqal karnaa) used for the cheating sense.

Sense 3 : copy, duplicate (of a document or object)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रतिलिपि (pratilipi, formal), डुप्लीकेट (dupliiket, colloquial, from English)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this at a government office when a clerk asks for नक़ल (naqal) of a document like an ID or land record -- a certified copy is called नक़ल (naqal) or असल की नक़ल (asal kii naqal, 'copy of the original') in bureaucratic Hindi.
