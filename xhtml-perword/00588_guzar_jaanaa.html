गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa)

verb A compound verb built on गुज़रना (guzarnaa, 'to pass') plus जाना (jaanaa) as the completive helper; covers time/events passing, physically passing through or by, and the euphemistic sense of someone passing away.
Sense 1 : for time to pass/elapse
verb, intransitive Subject is typically a time expression (साल, दिन, वक़्त): साल गुज़र गया (saal guzar gayaa, 'the year passed').
Synonyms: बीत जाना (biit jaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in reflective small talk -- वक़्त कैसे गुज़र गया, पता ही नहीं चला (vaqt kaise guzar gayaa, pataa hii nahiin chalaa, 'I didn't even notice how time passed') is a very Delhi way of expressing nostalgia about how fast life moves.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सिर्फ़ गुज़रना (guzarnaa) without जाना (jaanaa); adding जाना (jaanaa) is what gives the sense of completion, 'it's over and done' -- plain गुज़रना (guzarnaa) alone can sound incomplete or literary in casual speech. 2) This sense is intransitive, so despite describing a completed event it never takes ने (ne) -- the subject (साल, वक़्त) is not an agent.
Sample Sentences:
वक़्त कैसे गुज़र गया, पता ही नहीं चला। I didn't even notice how time passed.
(vaqt kaise guzar gayaa, pataa hii nahiin chalaa।)

Sense 2 : to pass by/through (a place)
verb, intransitive Takes से (se) for the route or point passed: इस रास्ते से गुज़र जाना (is raaste se guzar jaanaa, 'to pass by this route').
Synonyms: निकल जाना (nikal jaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Useful for giving directions or describing a journey -- ऑटो वाला बाज़ार से गुज़र जाता है (oto vaalaa baazaar se guzar jaataa hai, 'the auto passes through the market') is the kind of line you'd hear from a Delhi rickshaw driver explaining his route.

Sense 3 : to pass away, die (euphemistic)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: चल बसना (chal basnaa, euphemistic), मर जाना (mar jaanaa, blunt, direct), स्वर्गवासी होना (svargvaasii honaa, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you use when speaking with or about a grieving family -- दादाजी गुज़र गए (daadaajii guzar gae, 'grandfather passed away') is gentle and respectful in a way that mirrors English euphemism almost exactly, which makes it an easy one for English speakers to adopt naturally.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is the polite, softened way to say someone died -- using मर जाना (mar jaanaa) instead in condolence contexts can sound abrupt or even rude, much like the English distinction between 'passed away' and 'died'.
Sample Sentences:
दादाजी गुज़र गए। Grandfather passed away.
(daadaajii guzar gae।)
