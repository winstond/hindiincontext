नन्हे-मुन्ने (nanhe-munne)

adjective An affectionate, singsong reduplicated phrase meaning 'little ones' or 'tiny tots' -- used almost exclusively for small children. It appears mainly in its plural/oblique form, which is why it is entered here as नन्हे-मुन्ने (nanhe-munne) rather than the dictionary-style masculine singular नन्हा-मुन्ना (nanhaa-munnaa).
Sense 1 : tiny, little (of small children, affectionate)
adjective Used almost always before a noun like बच्चे (bachche) or as a noun-phrase itself referring to children; the reduplication with मुन्ना (munnaa) exists purely for rhythmic, affectionate emphasis and has no separate meaning of its own.
Synonyms: छोटे-छोटे (chhote-chhote), नन्हे (nanhe, shorter variant without reduplication)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you hear at a school function, a park, or in a lullaby context -- teachers and grandparents love saying things like नन्हे-मुन्ने बच्चे (nanhe-munne bachche, 'the tiny tots') with real warmth in the voice. The doubled, rhyming structure (नन्हा (nanhaa) + मुन्ना (munnaa)) is a classic Hindi affection-pattern, similar in spirit to English 'itty-bitty' -- it exists to sound cute, not to add precise meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use नन्हे-मुन्ने (nanhe-munne) for small objects or animals in general -- it is reserved almost entirely for babies and young children, not for 'small' in a neutral sense (for that, छोटा (chhotaa) is the word). 2) The base form नन्हा-मुन्ना (nanhaa-munnaa, masculine singular) is rarely heard alone in speech; the plural/oblique नन्हे-मुन्ने (nanhe-munne) is what you will actually encounter, often functioning as a noun phrase ('the little ones') rather than modifying a stated noun.
