टेकना (teknaa)

verb A physical verb meaning to lean, prop, or rest something (like a stick, hand, or knee) against a support -- most familiar to learners through the fixed phrase घुटने टेकना (ghutne teknaa, 'to kneel/give in').
Sense 1 : to kneel down (as an act of submission or surrender), used almost only in the fixed idiom घुटने टेकना
verb, transitive Almost always appears as the fixed collocation घुटने टेकना (ghutne teknaa, 'to kneel'), where घुटने (ghutne, 'knees') is the object being 'propped' on the ground.
Synonyms: हार मानना (haar maannaa, to accept defeat), झुकना (jhuknaa, to bow/bend)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This idiom carries the same emotional weight as its English counterpart 'to go down on one's knees' or 'to surrender' -- you will hear it in news reports about armies or political rivals 'kneeling' (घुटने टेकना, ghutne teknaa) rather than literally describing prayer posture.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'knees rested' rather than recognizing it as an idiom for defeat or surrender, similar to English 'to go down on one's knees.' 2) The idiom is used figuratively far more often than literally -- घुटने टेकना (ghutne teknaa) in a sentence about an argument or war means 'surrendered,' not necessarily a physical kneeling.
Sample Sentences:
आखिरकार दुश्मन सेना को घुटने टेकने पड़े। In the end, the enemy army had to kneel down (surrender).
(aakhirkaar dushman senaa ko ghutne tekne pade।)

Sense 2 : to prop, lean, or rest something against a support
verb, transitive Takes an object plus a location marked with पर (par): X को Y पर टेकना (X ko Y par teknaa).
Synonyms: सहारा लेना (sahaaraa lenaa, take support), टिकाना (tikaanaa, to make something rest against)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this most in physical, everyday contexts: an elderly person propping a cane -- लाठी टेकना (laathii teknaa) -- or someone resting a hand on a table to steady themselves. It is a very concrete, body-in-space verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse टेकना (teknaa) with टिकना (tiknaa) -- टिकना is intransitive ('to rest, to hold up on its own') while टेकना is transitive ('to prop something against something'). 2) The object being propped (a stick, hand, knee) needs को or is simply the direct object; the surface being propped against takes पर (par), not पे casually swapped without thought in careful writing.

(budhiyaa diivaar par haath tekkar uthii।)
