सिद्ध (siddh)

adjective/noun A Sanskrit-derived word with two main lives: as an adjective meaning 'proven/established' (used with होना (honaa) or करना (karnaa)), and as a noun meaning a spiritually accomplished or perfected being (a 'siddha') in yogic and mystical traditions.
Sense 1 : proven, established (as true)
adjective Commonly appears in the frame X सिद्ध होना (X siddh honaa, 'X turns out to be / is proven'), where सिद्ध functions almost like a predicate adjective paired with होना rather than an ordinary standalone descriptive adjective.
Synonyms: साबित (saabit), प्रमाणित (pramaanit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in newspapers, court reports, and school essays -- साबित (saabit) is what you'd say at the dinner table, सिद्ध (siddh) is what the news anchor or your history textbook says. It also shows up in the compound असिद्ध (asiddh, 'unproven'), following the common Sanskrit अ- negation pattern.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for साबित करना (saabit karnaa) for 'to prove' in casual speech and forget that सिद्ध करना (siddh karnaa) is the more formal/written equivalent -- both are correct but सिद्ध leans bookish, academic, or legal in tone. 2) सिद्ध होना (siddh honaa) often means 'turns out to be' (as in a result revealing itself) rather than just 'is proven' -- e.g. यह फैसला सही सिद्ध हुआ (yah phaislaa sahii siddh huaa, 'this decision turned out to be right') -- learners sometimes translate too literally as 'was proven' when 'turned out' fits the sense better.

Sense 2 : a spiritually perfected being, an accomplished yogi/sage
noun, masculine
Synonyms: योगी (yogii, overlapping but broader), महात्मा (mahaatmaa, overlapping but broader)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense comes straight from yogic and tantric tradition -- a सिद्ध (siddh) is someone believed to have mastered सिद्धियाँ (siddhiyaan, mystical powers or accomplishments) through intense sadhana. You will hear it in the context of Nath yogis, Himalayan ascetics, or old folk tales about wandering sadhus with miraculous abilities -- it carries a tone of awe and reverence rather than everyday description.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this noun sense with the adjective sense 'proven' -- in spiritual/religious contexts सिद्ध describes a person who has attained supernatural or perfected powers through discipline, not something that has been factually established.
