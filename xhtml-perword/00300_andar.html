अंदर (andar)

adverb/postposition/noun One core meaning of physical or figurative interiority, splitting into an adverb of place ('inside'), a postposition governing a following noun ('inside of'), and a noun sense ('the inside/interior').
Sense 1 : inside, indoors
adverb
Synonyms: भीतर (bhiitar), भीतर (bhiitar, slightly more formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Said on its own, अंदर आओ (andar aao, 'come inside') or अंदर जाओ (andar jaao, 'go inside') is what you hear constantly at doorways and gates across Delhi homes and offices -- it is the everyday command form, much more common than any fancier synonym.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add a postposition after अंदर (andar) when it is used alone as an adverb, e.g. saying घर के अंदर के अंदर (ghar ke andar ke andar) by mistake -- as a standalone adverb it needs no के (ke) before it: अंदर आओ (andar aao, 'come inside').

Sense 2 : inside (of), within
postposition Takes a preceding noun in the oblique marked by के (ke): घर के अंदर (ghar ke andar, 'inside the house').
Synonyms: भीतर (bhiitar)
Frequency: high

Sense 3 : the inside, interior (often figurative, as in one's inner self)
noun, masculine Used with possessive/oblique markers, e.g. मेरे अंदर (mere andar, 'inside me') to talk about inner feelings or states, distinct from physical location.
Synonyms: भीतर (bhiitar), अंतर्मन (antarman, literary, 'inner mind')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative use -- मेरे अंदर (mere andar, 'inside me') -- is very common in songs, self-help talk, and everyday emotional speech, treating feelings as things located within a person, which fits the broader Hindi habit of framing emotions and sensations as arriving or residing rather than being actively done.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'I feel...inside' too literally with भीतर (bhiitar) or 'inside' word order from English; the natural Hindi frame is X के अंदर + feeling verb, as in मेरे अंदर डर है (mere andar dar hai, 'there is fear inside me'), putting the feeling as the grammatical subject rather than 'I'.
