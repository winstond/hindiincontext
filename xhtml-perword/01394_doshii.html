दोषी (doshii)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning 'guilty/at fault,' also used as a noun for 'the guilty person/culprit'; related to दोष (dosh, 'fault, flaw, blame').
Sense 1 : guilty, at fault
adjective Commonly used with होना (honaa) as दोषी होना (doshii honaa, 'to be guilty') and with पाना (paanaa) in दोषी पाया जाना (doshii paayaa jaanaa, 'to be found guilty'), and with ठहराना (thahraanaa) in दोषी ठहराना (doshii thahraanaa, 'to declare/hold guilty').
Synonyms: कसूरवार (kasoorvaar), अपराधी (apraadhii, leans toward 'criminal/offender')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in courtroom dramas and news reports -- अदालत ने उसे दोषी ठहराया (adaalat ne use doshii thahraayaa, 'the court held him guilty') is standard legal-register phrasing. But it is just as at home in a family argument: माँ हमेशा मुझे ही दोषी ठहराती है (maan hameshaa mujhe hii doshii thahraatii hai, 'mom always blames only me') -- a very relatable line for anyone with siblings.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अपराधी (apraadhii) when they mean दोषी (doshii) -- अपराधी (apraadhii) suggests a criminal/offender by identity, while दोषी (doshii) is about being at fault in a specific matter, which can be minor (like a domestic argument) as easily as a court case. 2) The postposition of blame is का (kaa), not की (kii) or को (ko): वह इस गलती का दोषी है (vah is galtii kaa doshii hai, 'he is guilty of this mistake') -- learners sometimes wrongly say इस गलती को दोषी (is galtii ko doshii).
Sample Sentences:

(adaalat ne use doshii thahraayaa।)

Sense 2 : the guilty one, culprit
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: अपराधी (apraadhii, criminal/offender), कसूरवार (kasoorvaar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This noun use is basically the adjective standing on its own as 'the guilty person' -- असली दोषी कौन है? (aslii doshii kaun hai?, 'who is the real culprit?') is a very natural, everyday-sounding question, not just legal jargon.
