छोटा (chhotaa)

adjective A very common adjective covering both physical size ('small') and age/status ('younger', 'junior') -- the two senses split below because they steer different real-world usage.
Sense 1 : small, little (size)
adjective
Synonyms: छोटा-सा (chhotaa-saa, slightly softened/diminutive form), नन्हा (nanhaa, cute/tender-small, used for babies and small creatures), लघु (laghu, literary/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, छोटा (chhotaa) shows up constantly in food and market talk -- छोटा पैग (chhotaa paig, 'a small peg [of a drink]'), छोटी बोतल (chhotii botal, 'a small bottle'), एक छोटा कप चाय (ek chhotaa kap chaay, 'a small cup of tea'). It is one of the first adjectives every learner masters because it is everywhere.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for छोटा (chhotaa) when नन्हा (nanhaa) would sound warmer -- for a baby or kitten, नन्हा (nanhaa) carries affection that छोटा (chhotaa) lacks. 2) छोटा-सा (chhotaa-saa) is not just 'small' -- the -सा (-saa) softens it to 'a little small thing', often used to downplay size, e.g. describing a modest request or a tiny gift.
Sample Sentences:
यह कमरा बहुत छोटा है। This room is very small.
(yah kamraa bahut chhotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : younger, junior (age or rank)
adjective Commonly used as a noun-like modifier in kinship terms: छोटा भाई (chhotaa bhaaii, 'younger brother'), छोटी बहन (chhotii bahan, 'younger sister') -- Hindi has no single word for 'brother' or 'sister' without specifying relative age.
Synonyms: कनिष्ठ (kanishth, formal/bureaucratic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Family hierarchy is baked into vocabulary here -- you cannot just say 'my brother' in Hindi; you must say छोटा भाई (chhotaa bhaaii) or बड़ा भाई (badaa bhaaii), because relative age carries real social weight (who touches whose feet, who gets addressed with तुम (tum) versus आप (aap)). This sense pairs directly with बड़ा (badaa, 'big/elder') as its natural opposite.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers instinctively reach for 'brother' or 'sister' without an age marker, but Hindi requires छोटा (chhotaa) or बड़ा (badaa) before भाई (bhaaii) or बहन (bahan) in almost all natural speech -- omitting it sounds incomplete. 2) In the workplace, छोटा (chhotaa) can mean 'junior in rank' rather than 'younger in age' -- छोटा अफ़सर (chhotaa afsar) means a low-ranking officer, not necessarily a young one.

Sense 3 : minor, trivial, insignificant
adjective
Synonyms: मामूली (maamoolii, ordinary/trivial), तुच्छ (tuchchh, literary, dismissive -- 'petty, worthless')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear छोटी बात है (chhotii baat hai, 'it's a small matter') or कोई छोटी बात नहीं (koii chhotii baat nahiin, 'it's no small thing') used to manage social weight in a conversation -- minimizing an apology, or conversely, stressing that an achievement deserves real credit.
Common Mistakes: 1) छोटी बात (chhotii baat) means 'a trivial matter', not 'a small sentence' -- learners sometimes take बात (baat) too literally as 'word' or 'sentence' rather than 'matter/issue'.
Sample Sentences:
अरे, कोई बात नहीं, यह तो बहुत छोटी बात है। Oh, don't worry about it, it's a very small matter.
(are, koii baat nahiin, yah to bahut chhotii baat hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
