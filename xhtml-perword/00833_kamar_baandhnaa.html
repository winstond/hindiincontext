कमर बाँधना (kamar baandhnaa)

verb Literally 'to tie the waist,' this compound expression is used figuratively to mean getting ready or determined to take on a task or challenge.
Sense 1 : to prepare oneself resolutely, to gear up (for a task/challenge)
verb, transitive Takes की (kii) before the purpose: कमर बाँधना (kamar baandhnaa) is typically followed by a genitive phrase naming the task, e.g. लड़ने की कमर बाँधना (ladne kii kamar baandhnaa, 'to gird up to fight').
Synonyms: तैयार होना (taiyaar honaa), कटिबद्ध होना (katibaddh honaa, formal/literary), जुट जाना (jut jaanaa, to throw oneself into)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This idiom paints determination in very physical, almost warrior-like terms -- picture someone tightening their kamar (waist/lower back) before a fight or heavy labor, the way a wrestler or soldier would cinch their belt before battle. It is commonly used for anything from exam prep to a family gearing up for a wedding's chaos, and pairs naturally with the compound-verb form कमर बाँध लेना (kamar baandh lenaa) to stress the completeness of the resolve.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as physically tying a belt, missing that it is almost always figurative in modern speech. 2) The verb बाँधना (baandhnaa) here is transitive, so it takes ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने कमर बाँध ली (usne kamar baandh lii, 'he/she girded up') -- learners sometimes forget the ने (ne) because the idiom feels like a fixed phrase rather than an ordinary transitive verb.
Sample Sentences:
चुनाव नज़दीक आते ही सभी पार्टियों ने कमर बाँध ली है। With elections approaching, all the parties have girded up for the fight.
(chunaav nazdiik aate hii sabhii paartiyon ne kamar baandh lii hai।)
