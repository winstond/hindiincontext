मज़ा आना (mazaa aanaa)

verb A conjunct expression meaning 'to enjoy/have fun' -- literally 'enjoyment comes' -- built from मज़ा (mazaa, 'enjoyment, fun') plus आना (aanaa, 'to come'), and it uses the dative-subject pattern the learner already knows from लगना (lagnaa).
Sense 1 : to enjoy oneself, have fun, find something delightful
verb, intransitive Follows the same dative-subject frame as लगना (lagnaa): the person who enjoys is marked with को (ko), and मज़ा (mazaa) is the grammatical subject that agrees with the verb -- e.g. मुझे मज़ा आया (mujhe mazaa aayaa, 'I enjoyed it').
Synonyms: आनंद आना (aanand aanaa, slightly more formal), लुत्फ़ आना (lutf aanaa, literary/formal), अच्छा लगना (achchhaa lagnaa, broader meaning, 'to feel good')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase is everywhere in casual talk about food, movies, trips, and parties in Delhi-NCR -- asking क्या मज़ा आया? (kyaa mazaa aayaa?, 'did you have fun?') after any outing is almost reflexive. It sits in the same family as मज़ा लेना (mazaa lenaa, 'to take enjoyment from, to relish/tease'), which is a related but distinct expression using a different verb and a slightly more agentive, sometimes teasing, flavor.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person the subject, saying something like मैं मज़ा आया (main mazaa aayaa) instead of the correct dative-subject form मुझे मज़ा आया (mujhe mazaa aayaa). 2) The source of enjoyment is marked with में (men) or से (se) depending on framing -- खेलने में मज़ा आया (khelne men mazaa aayaa, 'enjoyed playing') versus उससे मिलकर मज़ा आया (usse milkar mazaa aayaa, 'enjoyed meeting him') -- and learners tend to default to just one preposition for every case. 3) The negative is मज़ा नहीं आया (mazaa nahiin aayaa, 'didn't enjoy it'), not a form built on ना-verb negation patterns from other verbs.

मुझे यह फ़िल्म देखकर बहुत मज़ा आया। I really enjoyed watching this movie.
(mujhe yah film dekhkar bahut mazaa aayaa।)
