बिकना (biknaa)

verb The intransitive/passive-sense partner of बेचना (bechnaa, 'to sell'): बिकना (biknaa) means 'to be sold, to sell (of goods, as a market fact)'. One sense, used both literally (of goods) and figuratively (of a person 'selling out' their principles).
Sense 1 : to be sold; to sell (of goods, in the market)
verb, intransitive The subject is the thing sold, marked with ने-marking never applying (it is intransitive); the seller, if mentioned, appears with से (se) or a phrase like हाथों (haathon, 'at the hands of'), not as a direct agent.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Shopkeepers and vendors use this constantly: माल बिक गया (maal bik gayaa, 'the stock got sold') or यह चीज़ बहुत बिकती है (yah chiiz bahut biktii hai, 'this item sells a lot') -- it's the natural way to talk about how well something moves in the market, much more common in that context than any passive of बेचना (bechnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for a passive construction of बेचना (bechnaa) (like बेचा जाता है, bechaa jaataa hai) when बिकना (biknaa) is the simpler, more natural native choice for 'this sells well' or 'this is sold here' -- यह यहाँ बिकता है (yah yahaan biktaa hai, 'this is sold here') sounds far more idiomatic than the periphrastic passive. 2) Because it's intransitive, it never takes ने (ne) in the perfective even though its meaning feels 'passive' -- सामान बिका (saamaan bikaa, 'the goods sold/were sold'), not सामान ने बिका.
Sample Sentences:
यह चीज़ बहुत बिकती है। This item sells a lot.
(yah chiiz bahut biktii hai।)

Sense 2 : to sell out, to be bought off (figurative, of a person's loyalty or principles)
verb, intransitive Often occurs with पैसों के हाथों (paison ke haathon, 'at the hands of money') or दाम पर (daam par, 'for a price') to specify what someone was bought with.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word behind common political and moral criticism in Hindi media and conversation -- calling a politician or official बिका हुआ (bikaa huaa, 'a sellout, bought') is a sharp, everyday insult you'll hear in news debates and casual complaints about corruption alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense (a person 'selling out') is fully idiomatic in Hindi just as in English, but learners sometimes hesitate to apply बिकना (biknaa) to people, expecting it to stay literal to goods -- वह बिक गया (vah bik gayaa, 'he sold out/was bought off') is completely natural.
Sample Sentences:
