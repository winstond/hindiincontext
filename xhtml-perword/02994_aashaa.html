आशा (aashaa)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'hope' or 'expectation'; also used as a common Hindu girl's given name. The verb sense you'll actually hear most is the phrase आशा है (aashaa hai), 'it is hoped', used to express a wish politely.
Sense 1 : hope, expectation
noun, feminine Frequently appears in the frame X से आशा है (X se aashaa hai, 'there is hope from X') or आशा है कि... (aashaa hai ki, 'it is hoped that...') to politely voice an expectation.
Synonyms: उम्मीद (ummiid), आस (aas, everyday, slightly poetic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see आशा है (aashaa hai) at the start or end of letters and emails in Hindi, much like 'I hope this finds you well' -- it has that same formal, wishing-well flavor. Note also that आशा (aashaa) is one of the most common Hindu girls' names in North India, so context will tell you quickly whether someone is talking about hope or talking about their friend Asha.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उम्मीद (ummiid) by default since it is more common in casual speech; आशा (aashaa) is not wrong but sits a notch more formal, so it shows up often in written wishes, speeches, and letters rather than everyday chit-chat. 2) The polite closing formula आशा है ((aashaa hai, 'I hope...')) taking कि (ki) plus a full clause can trip up learners who try to use a direct object instead.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे आशा है कि आप ठीक होंगे। I hope you are doing well.
(mujhe aashaa hai ki aap thiik honge।)

Sense 2 : given name Asha
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: As a name, आशा (aashaa) carries the same hopeful meaning as the common noun -- parents choosing it are literally naming their daughter 'Hope.' Singer Asha Bhosle is probably the most famous bearer of this name in India.
