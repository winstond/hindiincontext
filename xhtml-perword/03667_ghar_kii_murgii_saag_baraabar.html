घर की मुर्गी साग बराबर (ghar kii murgii saag baraabar)

phrase A proverb meaning 'familiarity breeds contempt' -- literally, 'the hen at home is [worth only as much as] a vegetable.' Often heard in its short form घर की मुर्गी (ghar kii murgii) alone.
Sense 1 : familiarity breeds contempt; something valuable seems ordinary once it's easily available
phrase
Synonyms: घर की मुर्गी (ghar kii murgii, short form, same meaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the Hindi cousin of 'familiarity breeds contempt' or 'the grass is greener on the other side,' but with a very Indian-household image: a hen you already own and can eat any day feels as unremarkable as a plate of साग (saag, 'leafy greens'), while a neighbor's hen seems like a feast. You'll hear it used affectionately by parents or elders about a person, skill, or possession that's underappreciated simply because it's always been around.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate this literally into English conversation ('my home chicken is like vegetable') -- it only works as a fixed proverb, never as a fresh descriptive sentence about an actual chicken. 2) The full proverb is often clipped to just घर की मुर्गी (ghar kii murgii) with साग बराबर (saag baraabar) left unsaid but understood, so don't be thrown off when you hear only the first half.
