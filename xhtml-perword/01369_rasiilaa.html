रसीला (rasiilaa)

adjective Primarily describes fruit or food that is juicy/succulent; extends figuratively to voice, poetry, or personality that is 'juicy' in the sense of rich, sweet, or full of flavor and charm.
Sense 1 : juicy, succulent (of fruit or food)
adjective
Synonyms: रसदार (rasdaar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly at the fruit cart (thele, thele) in any Delhi market -- a vendor praising his mangoes will almost always reach for रसीला (rasiilaa) rather than a plainer word, because it evokes the juice running down your hand, not just moisture.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for जूसी (joosii), an English borrowing that some younger speakers use casually, but रसीला (rasiilaa) is the standard word and works in all registers, so it should be the default choice, not the borrowed one.

(ye aam bahut rasiile hain।)

Sense 2 : rich, sweet, charming (of voice, music, poetry, or personality)
adjective
Synonyms: मधुर (madhur), सुरीला (suriilaa, mainly for voice/music)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Applied to a voice or a piece of poetry, रसीला (rasiilaa) draws on the classical idea of रस (ras) -- the 'essence' or aesthetic flavor that Hindi and Sanskrit poetics treat as the whole point of art. Calling a singer's voice रसीला (rasiilaa) is a compliment that reaches back to this old idea of art having 'juice' or emotional flavor to offer.
