नज़र डालना (nazar daalnaa)

verb A compound verb literally meaning 'to throw a glance' -- built from नज़र (nazar, 'gaze/glance/sight') plus डालना (daalnaa, 'to put/throw'). It has one core sense: to cast a quick look or glance at something.
Sense 1 : to glance at, to take a quick look at
verb, transitive Takes the object with पर (par): नज़र डालना पर (nazar daalnaa par) -- 'to cast a glance ON something.' The thing looked at is marked with पर, not accusative को.
Synonyms: देखना (dekhnaa, plain 'to look/see'), नज़र मारना (nazar maarnaa, colloquial), निगाह डालना (nigaah daalnaa, variant form using निगाह)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of those lovely Hindi expressions where the eye's action is expressed as 'throwing' something -- नज़र (nazar) is the gaze itself, treated almost like an object you can toss onto a thing. You'll hear it constantly in practical contexts: ज़रा इस पर नज़र डाल लो (zaraa is par nazar daal lo, 'just take a quick look at this') is something a boss, teacher, or parent says dozens of times a day in any Delhi office or home. It pairs naturally with related idioms like नज़र लगना (nazar lagnaa, 'to be affected by an evil eye') -- same root noun, very different flavor, so don't confuse the two.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try को where पर (par) is needed -- you say किताब पर नज़र डाली (kitaab par nazar daalii, 'glanced at the book'), not किताब को. 2) This phrase implies a brief, cursory look, not a sustained one -- for longer looking, ताकना (taaknaa) or घूरना (ghoornaa) fit better, so don't overuse नज़र डालना (nazar daalnaa) for staring or careful examination unless that quick-glance sense is intended.

(zaraa is par nazar daal lo।)
