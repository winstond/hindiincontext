संत (sant)

noun/adjective संत (sant) refers to a saint or holy person -- someone revered for spiritual wisdom and moral purity, often used for the great Bhakti-era poet-saints of North India.
Sense 1 : saint, holy/pious person
noun, masculine
Synonyms: साधु (saadhu), महात्मा (mahaatmaa), साध्वी (saadhvii, for a female ascetic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In North Indian culture, संत (sant) is closely tied to the Bhakti tradition -- names like कबीर (kabiir) and तुलसीदास (tulsiidaas) are called संत (sant) rather than just poets, because their verses are treated as spiritual teaching, not mere literature. You'll often hear the phrase संत-महात्मा (sant-mahaatmaa, 'saints and holy men') used together as a set pair when talking about revered spiritual figures.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use संत (sant) loosely for any religious figure; it specifically implies recognized spiritual attainment or moral greatness, not just someone who is religious or performs rituals. 2) Don't confuse with पंडित (pandit, priest/scholar) or पुजारी (pujaarii, temple priest) -- those describe a role or occupation, while संत (sant) describes a person's spiritual/moral stature.

Sense 2 : saintly, virtuous, gentle-natured
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. संत स्वभाव (sant svabhaav, 'saintly nature'), describing a person's temperament rather than formally calling them a saint.
Synonyms: सज्जन (sajjan), पवित्र (pavitr, more about purity than character)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use is less common in casual speech than the noun sense; you're more likely to hear it in descriptions of someone's calm, forgiving temperament, as in बड़े संत स्वभाव के हैं (bade sant svabhaav ke hain, 'he's a very saintly/gentle sort of person').
