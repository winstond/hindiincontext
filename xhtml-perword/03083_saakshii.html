साक्षी (saakshii)

noun A noun meaning 'witness' -- someone who has personally seen or observed an event, especially used in legal, formal, or reflective contexts. Related to साक्ष्य (saakshy, 'evidence/testimony').
Sense 1 : witness (a person who observes or testifies)
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: गवाह (gavaah)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In courtroom Hindi (as heard in countless Bollywood trial scenes), the judge or lawyer says साक्षी (saakshii) rather than गवाह (gavaah) when the register is meant to sound official. Outside the courtroom, you will also meet it in phrases like मैं साक्षी हूँ (main saakshii hoon, 'I am a witness/I can testify to this'), used almost poetically to mean 'I have seen this happen with my own eyes.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to गवाह (gavaah) for every context, but साक्षी (saakshii) is the word you will see in courtroom drama, formal writing, and set phrases like साक्षी देना (saakshii denaa, 'to give testimony') where गवाह (gavaah) sounds slightly more everyday. 2) साक्षी (saakshii) is grammatically m/f but often appears unmarked in the singular regardless of the referent's gender in more formal registers -- do not assume it always inflects like a typical feminine noun ending in ई.

Sense 2 : witnessing/testimony (abstract, spiritual sense of bearing witness)
noun, masculine/feminine Used in reflective or spiritual registers, often in the phrase साक्षी भाव (saakshii bhaav, 'the witness attitude' -- observing without judgment or attachment).
Synonyms: साक्ष्य (saakshy, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In yoga and meditation contexts, साक्षी भाव (saakshii bhaav) describes the practice of observing your own thoughts and feelings like a neutral witness, rather than reacting to them -- a concept you will hear discussed in spiritual discourses and self-help writing across Delhi-NCR's many yoga and wellness circles.
