देहाती (dehaatii)

adjective/noun An adjective (and by extension a noun for a person) describing something as rural, rustic, or village-style; it can be a neutral geographic descriptor or carry a mildly judgmental 'unsophisticated/backward' tone depending on context.
Sense 1 : rural, of/from the village
adjective
Synonyms: गंवई (ganvaii, colloquial), ग्रामीण (graamiin, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Say देहाती खाना (dehaatii khaanaa, 'village-style food') and most Delhi folks picture something wholesome and hearty, like sarson ka saag with makki ki roti -- here the word is affectionate, even aspirational. But call someone's clothes or manners देहाती (dehaatii), and the tone flips to mild mockery, implying 'unrefined' or 'behind the times.' Context and tone of voice do a lot of work with this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use देहाती (dehaatii) as a neutral synonym for ग्रामीण (graamiin) in formal writing (e.g. government reports, statistics), where ग्रामीण (graamiin) is the expected register; देहाती (dehaatii) usually belongs in casual speech and often carries a hint of judgment.

Sense 2 : a villager, rustic person
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun by dropping the modified noun; agrees in gender/number with the person referred to, e.g. एक देहाती (ek dehaatii, 'a villager (m.)') vs. एक देहाती औरत (ek dehaatii aurat, 'a village woman').
Synonyms: गंवार (ganvaar, derogatory, unsophisticated), ग्रामीण (graamiin, formal, neutral)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In city banter, calling someone एक नंबर का देहाती (ek nambar kaa dehaatii, 'a total bumpkin') is a common jab at someone who seems clueless about city ways -- said among friends it is teasing, but from a stranger it can sting.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used as a plain noun for 'a person from a village,' this word can land as an insult even when the speaker means it descriptively -- unlike ग्रामीण (graamiin), which stays neutral in that same slot.
