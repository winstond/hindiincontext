मदद करना (madad karnaa)

verb A common compound expression meaning 'to help' -- built from the noun मदद (madad, 'help') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'). One core sense, with the postposition it takes toward the person helped being the main thing to learn.
Sense 1 : to help, to assist
verb, transitive The person being helped is marked with की (kii) as part of the possessive frame -- मदद करना (madad karnaa) literally patterns as 'to do X's help', so it appears as किसी की मदद करना (kisii kii madad karnaa, 'to help someone'), not with को (ko) as an English speaker might expect.
Synonyms: सहायता करना (sahaaytaa karnaa, formal), साथ देना (saath denaa, 'to lend support/stand by')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi life you will hear this constantly -- from a shopkeeper's ज़रा मदद कर दो (zaraa madad kar do, 'just help me out a bit') to a dramatic बचाओ, कोई मदद करो! (bachaao, koii madad karo, 'help, someone help!'). Note the family link to मददगार (madadgaar, 'helpful, a helper'), a related adjective/noun worth knowing alongside this phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for को (ko) on the person helped -- मुझे मदद करो (mujhe madad karo) -- but the correct frame uses की (kii): मेरी मदद करो (merii madad karo, 'help me'). 2) मदद (madad) is feminine, so past-tense agreement lands on it, not on the helper or the person helped -- उसने मेरी मदद की (usne merii madad kii, 'he/she helped me'), with की (kii) agreeing with मदद (madad).

(kyaa tum merii madad kar sakte ho?)
