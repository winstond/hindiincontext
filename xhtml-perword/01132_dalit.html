दलित (dalit)

noun/adjective Primarily a modern sociopolitical noun/adjective referring to communities historically labeled 'untouchable' under the caste system; it also carries a literal, less common adjective sense of 'crushed, oppressed.'
Sense 1 : member of a formerly 'untouchable' caste community
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: अनुसूचित जाति (anusoochit jaati, official/administrative term), हरिजन (harijan, dated, avoid -- see note)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: दलित (dalit) literally means 'broken/crushed' and was reclaimed in the 20th century, especially through the Dalit movement associated with Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, as a term of assertion rather than shame. You will see it constantly in Indian news, politics, and reservation/affirmative-action discussions (आरक्षण, aarakshan), so it is essential vocabulary for reading a newspaper, even though it is a sensitive and weighty word rather than everyday chit-chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) हरिजन (harijan) was Gandhi's term but is now widely seen as patronizing and is avoided in respectful usage; दलित (dalit) is the term communities themselves use and prefer. 2) This is a self-chosen political/social identity term, not a neutral descriptive word -- use it the way you would a specific, respected group name, not as a casual synonym for 'poor' or 'low-caste.'

Sense 2 : crushed, oppressed, downtrodden (literal/general sense)
adjective
Synonyms: पीड़ित (piidit), शोषित (shoshit), कुचला (kuchlaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the root literal meaning -- 'crushed underfoot' -- from which the social-identity noun sense grew; in formal or literary writing you may still see दलित वर्ग (dalit varg, 'the oppressed/downtrodden class') used more generally, but in everyday speech the noun sense (a Dalit person) dominates.
