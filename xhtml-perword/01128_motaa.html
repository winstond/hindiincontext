मोटा (motaa)

adjective/noun Primarily a physical-description adjective covering 'fat/overweight,' 'thick,' and 'coarse/crude,' plus a figurative extension to 'rough/approximate' and a colloquial noun-ish use for 'big money.' All senses share the core idea of bulk or lack of fineness.
Sense 1 : fat, overweight (of a person or animal)
adjective
Synonyms: मोटा-ताज़ा (motaa-taazaa, describes a person as healthily plump), भारी (bhaarii, heavy-set), फूला हुआ (phoolaa huaa, puffed up/bloated)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In many Indian families, especially with elders, commenting directly on someone's weight -- 'तू तो मोटा हो गया है' (too to motaa ho gayaa hai, 'you have gotten fat') -- is treated as ordinary conversation rather than an insult, which often surprises Western learners used to avoiding the topic entirely.
Common Mistakes: 1) Calling someone मोटा (motaa) to their face is much blunter in Hindi than 'chubby' in English -- among family it can be affectionate, but with strangers or acquaintances it usually reads as rude, so learners should be careful about tone. 2) For a softer, more polite way to describe someone as pleasantly plump, मोटा-ताज़ा (motaa-taazaa) is often preferred over plain मोटा (motaa).

Sense 2 : thick (of an object, book, rope, wall, etc.)
adjective
Synonyms: गाढ़ा (gaadhaa, of liquids/consistency), चौड़ा (chaudaa, wide, partial overlap)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday shopping talk -- मोटी रोटी (motii rotii, 'a thick roti') versus पतली रोटी (patlii rotii, 'a thin roti') is a real point of preference at the dinner table in North Indian homes.
Common Mistakes: 1) मोटा (motaa) describes physical thickness of an object (a rope, a book, a wall), while गाढ़ा (gaadhaa) is used for the thickness/consistency of a liquid like milk or gravy -- learners often reach for मोटा (motaa) in both contexts by analogy with English 'thick.'

Sense 3 : coarse, crude, unrefined (of texture, cloth, grain, or manners)
adjective
Synonyms: भद्दा (bhaddaa, crude/unsightly), खुरदरा (khuradraa, rough-textured)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: मोटा कपड़ा (motaa kapdaa, 'coarse cloth') or मोटा अनाज (motaa anaaj, 'coarse grain,' i.e. millets like बाजरा) both carry a whiff of 'basic, unrefined, everyday-people's stuff' as opposed to fine or refined -- useful cultural shorthand when discussing food or fabric quality.

Sense 4 : rough, approximate (of an estimate, calculation, or understanding)
adjective Common in the fixed phrase मोटा-मोटा हिसाब (motaa-motaa hisaab, 'a rough calculation') or मोटे तौर पर (mote taur par, 'roughly speaking'), where मोटा (motaa) extends from physical coarseness to conceptual coarseness.
Synonyms: अनुमानित (anumaanit, estimated), लगभग (lagbhag, approximately, adverb not adjective)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same mental leap English makes with 'rough' -- something physically coarse becomes something conceptually imprecise. मोटे तौर पर (mote taur par) is extremely common in spoken explanations, meetings, and news commentary when someone gives a ballpark figure.
Common Mistakes: 1) मोटे तौर पर (mote taur par) is a fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'broadly speaking' -- learners sometimes try to build it with other case endings, but it is best learned as a set chunk.
Sample Sentences:

(mote taur par hamen biis hazaar rupye chaahie honge।)

Sense 5 : big money, a large sum (colloquial)
noun, masculine Used almost always in the phrase मोटा पैसा (motaa paisaa, 'big/fat money') or मोटी कमाई (motii kamaaii, 'fat earnings') -- functions adjectivally before पैसा (paisaa) or कमाई (kamaaii) rather than standing alone as a noun.
Synonyms: बड़ी रकम (badii rakam, large sum, neutral register), मालामाल (maalaamaal, loaded with money, adjective not noun)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: मोटा पैसा कमाना (motaa paisaa kamaanaa, 'to earn big money') is a very common colloquial phrase in business and career talk, echoing English 'fat paycheck' -- the same bigness-equals-wealth metaphor shows up in both languages.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense almost never appears as a bare noun; it is really the adjective मोटा (motaa) modifying पैसा (paisaa) or कमाई (kamaaii) -- learners should not expect to use मोटा (motaa) alone to mean 'wealth.'
