प्रति (prati)

postposition/noun Mainly a formal postposition meaning 'towards/per', but also shows up as a prefix meaning 'counter-/against' in compound words, and as a standalone noun meaning 'copy' (of a document).
Sense 1 : towards, for (attitude or feeling directed at someone/something)
postposition Governs the oblique case like other postpositions; typically used after abstract nouns of feeling (प्यार, सम्मान, कर्तव्य) rather than in casual physical-direction contexts, where की ओर (kii or) or तरफ (taraph) is preferred.
Synonyms: के लिए (ke lie), की ओर (kii or)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word constantly in newspaper editorials and formal speeches: देश के प्रति कर्तव्य (desh ke prati kartavy, 'duty towards the country') is a classic phrase in patriotic or moral writing. In daily bazaar chatter, though, people reach for की तरफ (kii taraph) instead -- so think of प्रति (prati) as the podium version.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use प्रति (prati) in casual spoken sentences where की तरफ (kii taraph) or के लिए (ke lie) would sound far more natural -- प्रति (prati) has a formal, almost written-register flavor. 2) It pairs with feelings/attitudes (सम्मान, प्रेम, कर्तव्य) rather than physical movement -- 'walking towards the market' does not use प्रति (prati).

Sense 2 : per, for each (rate or distribution)
postposition Used before the noun it modifies to express a rate: प्रति व्यक्ति (prati vyakti, 'per person'), प्रति किलो (prati kilo, 'per kilo').
Synonyms: हर (har, less formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this everywhere on price tags and government statistics: प्रति व्यक्ति आय (prati vyakti aay, 'per capita income') is a stock phrase in news reports about the economy. In the vegetable market, though, vendors are more likely to just say '50 रुपये किलो' (50 rupye kilo, '50 rupees a kilo') without प्रति (prati) at all.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense sits right before the noun, unlike most postpositions which follow their noun -- प्रति दिन (prati din, 'per day'), not दिन प्रति (din prati). Learners used to postpositions trailing the noun get tripped up by this reversal.

Sense 3 : copy (of a document, letter, etc.)
noun, feminine Used mainly in formal/bureaucratic contexts for a copy of a letter, application, or official document, e.g. एक प्रति (ek prati, 'one copy').
Synonyms: कॉपी (kopii, colloquial, more common for photocopies)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense turns up on official forms and government correspondence -- कार्यालय की प्रति (kaaryaalay kii prati, 'office copy') is standard bureaucratic phrasing. For an everyday photocopy at the local shop, people just say कॉपी (kopii) or फोटोकॉपी (photokopii).
