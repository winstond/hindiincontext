छपना (chhapnaa)

verb An intransitive verb meaning 'to be printed / to be published' -- used for books, newspapers, photos, and designs going into print. Closely related to the transitive छापना (chhaapnaa, 'to print'), which is a separate lemma; छपना (chhapnaa) is what happens to the thing being printed, not what the printer does.
Sense 1 : to be printed, to be published
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: प्रकाशित होना (prakaashit honaa, formal, 'used for books/articles specifically')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Newspaper readers in India say things like "आज अख़बार में मेरी तस्वीर छपी" (aaj akhbaar men merii tasviir chhapii, 'my photo was printed in the paper today') with real pride -- getting छपना (chhapnaa) in print, especially in a local paper, is still a small social event in many families.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for छापना (chhaapnaa) when they mean this word: use छपना (chhapnaa) when the subject is the book/article/photo itself ("किताब छपी" (kitaab chhapii, 'the book got printed')), and छापना (chhaapnaa) only when a person or press is the one doing the printing. 2) Because it is intransitive, it never takes ने (ne) marking on its subject, unlike its transitive partner छापना (chhaapnaa).

(aaj akhbaar men merii tasviir chhapii hai।)

Sense 2 : to be imprinted, stamped, or patterned (on fabric, skin, etc.)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: उभरना (ubharnaa, 'used more for marks/impressions surfacing')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of block-printed fabric at a Rajasthani market: the design is described as छपना (chhapnaa) onto the cloth, e.g. "साड़ी पर फूल छपे हैं" (saadii par phool chhape hain, 'flowers are printed on the sari'). The same verb extends naturally to a mark left on skin or memory, like a scar or a lasting impression.
