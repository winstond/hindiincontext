नन्हा-मुन्ना (nanhaa-munnaa)

adjective An affectionate reduplicated adjective meaning 'tiny, little' -- almost always used when cooing over small children or baby animals. It is built from नन्हा (nanhaa, 'tiny') plus its rhyming echo मुन्ना (munnaa, which also independently means 'little boy').
Sense 1 : tiny, adorably small (of a child, baby, or small creature)
adjective
Synonyms: छोटा सा (chhotaa saa), नन्हा (nanhaa, same root, less doting), प्यारा सा (pyaaraa saa, emphasizes cuteness over size)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is classic baby-talk vocabulary -- the kind of word a Delhi grandmother uses while pinching a toddler's cheeks, as in नन्हा-मुन्ना बच्चा (nanhaa-munnaa bachchaa, 'a tiny little child'). The reduplication itself carries the affection; saying just नन्हा (nanhaa) is fine but noticeably less warm and cooing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use this for any small object -- a small phone, a small bag -- but नन्हा-मुन्ना (nanhaa-munnaa) is reserved almost exclusively for living, endearing things: babies, children, kittens, chicks. For a small inanimate object, छोटा (chhotaa) is the right word. 2) Because it inflects like a regular -आ adjective, learners sometimes forget to inflect *both* halves together: it becomes नन्ही-मुन्नी (nanhii-munnii) for feminine/plural agreement, not just the second word.
