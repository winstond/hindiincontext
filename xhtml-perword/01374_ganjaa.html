गंजा (ganjaa)

adjective/noun Means 'bald' -- describes a head with no hair. Also used, often teasingly or insultingly, as a nickname/label for a bald person.
Sense 1 : bald
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गंजा (ganjaa) is a very direct, everyday word -- Hindi doesn't soften baldness the way English sometimes does with 'thinning' or 'follicly challenged.' Calling someone गंजा (ganjaa) to their face, especially among friends or family, is common good-natured teasing, though it can sting if the person is sensitive about it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use गंजा (ganjaa) as a noun meaning 'baldness' -- that abstract sense is गंजापन (ganjaapan), not गंजा (ganjaa) itself. 2) Remember to inflect for gender/number when describing a noun: गंजा आदमी (ganjaa aadmii, 'bald man') but गंजी औरत (ganjii aurat, 'bald woman') is unusual since baldness is mostly discussed for men in casual speech -- but grammatically it still agrees like any variable adjective.

Sense 2 : bald person (as a nickname or label)
noun, masculine Used as a noun referring to a person, typically as a nickname, taunt, or casual identifier -- 'the bald guy.'
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Indian schoolyards, offices, and neighborhoods, nicknames based on physical traits are common and गंजा (ganjaa) is a classic one -- said with affection among close friends, but it can be genuinely hurtful from strangers or in a professional setting.
Common Mistakes: 1) This noun use is often teasing or mildly rude -- learners should be cautious using गंजा (ganjaa) to refer to a stranger directly, as it can come across as impolite, similar to shouting 'baldy' in English.
