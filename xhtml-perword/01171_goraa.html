गोरा (goraa)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective describing fair/light skin tone; also used as a noun for a fair-skinned person, and colloquially (often plural गोरे (gore)) as a term for white/European people, especially the British in a historical context.
Sense 1 : fair-skinned, light-colored (complexion)
adjective
Synonyms: गोरा-चिट्टा (goraa-chittaa, emphatic compound form), उजला (ujlaa, more about brightness/cleanliness than skin tone)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Fair skin carries strong positive associations in much of Hindi-speaking society, visible in the huge market for products marketed as 'fairness creams' and in matrimonial ads that mention गोरा रंग (goraa rang, 'fair complexion') as a selling point. As a teacher, I'd gently flag this to learners: the word itself is neutral, but its cultural weight is not, and conversations about it can get sensitive.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat गोरा (goraa) as a neutral descriptor the way 'fair' can be in English, but in everyday Hindi it is loaded with social meaning around beauty standards -- it is very frequently used as a compliment or a marker of desirability, especially in matrimonial and beauty-product contexts. 2) Do not confuse with गोल (gol, 'round') -- similar sound, unrelated meaning.
Sample Sentences:
उसका रंग बहुत गोरा है। Her complexion is very fair.
(uskaa rang bahut goraa hai।)

Sense 2 : a fair-skinned person
noun, masculine Functions as a noun by using the adjective on its own, agreeing in gender/number with the person referred to (गोरा (goraa) for a man, गोरी (gorii) for a woman).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this used affectionately too, like calling a fair-skinned baby मेरा गोरा बेटा (meraa goraa betaa, 'my fair-skinned son') -- it is not always about race or foreignness, sometimes it is just describing someone in the family.

Sense 3 : a white/European person (especially historically, the British)
noun, masculine Almost always heard in the plural, गोरे (gore), when referring to white people or the British collectively (e.g. in the colonial context).
Synonyms: अंग्रेज़ (angrez, specifically 'Englishman/Briton', narrower in scope), विदेशी (videshii, 'foreigner', broader and not about skin color)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In conversations about India's independence movement, गोरों का राज (goron kaa raaj, 'the rule of the whites') is a common way older generations and history textbooks refer to British rule -- it carries a distinct historical charge beyond simply describing someone's skin color.
Common Mistakes: 1) In historical or political speech, गोरे (gore) often specifically means the British colonizers, not just 'white people' in general -- context from independence-era history often narrows the meaning this way.
Sample Sentences:
अंग्रेजों के जमाने में यहाँ बहुत गोरे रहते थे। In the time of the British, many white people used to live here.
(angrejon ke jamaane men yahaan bahut gore rahte the।)
