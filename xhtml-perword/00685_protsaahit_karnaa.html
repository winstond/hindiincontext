प्रोत्साहित करना (protsaahit karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the Sanskrit-derived adjective/participle प्रोत्साहित (protsaahit, 'encouraged/incited') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means 'to encourage' or 'to motivate' someone toward an action.
Sense 1 : to encourage, to motivate
verb, transitive Takes the person encouraged as direct object, often followed by के लिए (ke lie) plus an infinitive or verbal noun to specify what they're being encouraged to do, e.g. पढ़ने के लिए प्रोत्साहित करना (padhne ke lie protsaahit karnaa, 'to encourage to study').
Synonyms: बढ़ावा देना (badhaavaa denaa), हिम्मत बढ़ाना (himmat badhaanaa), उत्साहित करना (utsaahit karnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll see on school report cards, NGO posters, and government schemes -- 'प्रोत्साहित करने के लिए' (protsaahit karne ke lie, 'in order to encourage') is classic bureaucratic and educational register. For everyday warmth -- a mother cheering on a nervous child before an exam -- हौसला बढ़ाना (hauslaa badhaanaa) or पीठ थपथपाना (piith thapathpaanaa, 'to pat on the back') feel closer to the heart of Delhi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उत्साहित करना (utsaahit karnaa) interchangeably, but प्रोत्साहित करना (protsaahit karnaa) leans more toward encouraging someone to *take action or persist* (like a teacher encouraging a student), while उत्साहित करना (utsaahit karnaa) leans toward making someone *excited or enthusiastic*. 2) This is a formal, somewhat bookish/institutional verb -- in casual speech, हिम्मत देना (himmat denaa) or हौसला बढ़ाना (hauslaa badhaanaa) sound warmer and more natural among friends and family.
