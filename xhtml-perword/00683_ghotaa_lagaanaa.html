ग़ोता लगाना (ghotaa lagaanaa)

verb A compound verb built from the noun ग़ोता (ghotaa, 'dive/plunge') plus लगाना (lagaanaa, 'to apply/give'); it means 'to take a dive' or 'to plunge,' used both literally (into water) and figuratively (into a task, thought, or trouble).
Sense 1 : to dive (into water)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: डुबकी लगाना (dubkii lagaanaa), डूबना (doobnaa, focuses on sinking rather than the act of diving)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture boys jumping off a ghat wall into the Yamuna or a village pond -- ग़ोता लगाना (ghotaa lagaanaa) is exactly that image: a full-body plunge, not a gentle wade in. It is the natural verb for swimmers, divers, and even ducks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use ग़ोता (ghotaa) alone as a verb -- it needs लगाना (lagaanaa) to function as the action; ग़ोता (ghotaa) by itself is just the noun 'dive.' 2) The place dived into takes में (men), e.g. नदी में ग़ोता लगाना (nadii men ghotaa lagaanaa, 'to dive into the river') -- learners sometimes reach for पर (par) or को (ko) instead.

Sense 2 : to plunge into (figurative: deep engagement)
verb, intransitive Used with में (men) to mark what one is immersing oneself in, e.g. काम में ग़ोता लगाना (kaam men ghotaa lagaanaa, 'to dive into work').
Synonyms: डूब जाना (doob jaanaa, focuses on being absorbed rather than the initial plunge), लीन होना (liin honaa, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just as English speakers 'dive into' a project, Hindi speakers use ग़ोता लगाना (ghotaa lagaanaa) the same way -- the water image carries over neatly into the abstract, so a student might कल की पढ़ाई में ग़ोता लगाना (kal kii padhaaii men ghotaa lagaanaa, 'dive into tomorrow's studying') the night before an exam.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is less common than the plain 'dive into water' meaning, so overusing it where a simpler verb like में डूब जाना (men doob jaanaa) or में लग जाना (men lag jaanaa) would sound more natural to a native speaker.
