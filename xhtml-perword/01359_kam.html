कम (kam)

adjective/adverb/postposition Mainly an adjective/adverb meaning 'less/little/few', with a common idiomatic use in से कम (se kam) 'no less than' and a few fixed expressions built around scarcity or insufficiency.
Sense 1 : little in quantity, few, insufficient
adjective
Synonyms: थोड़ा (thodaa), न्यून (nyoon, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in markets and kitchens: नमक कम है (namak kam hai, 'there's not enough salt') or थोड़ा कम कर दो (thodaa kam kar do, 'turn it down a bit,' e.g. a stove flame or the TV volume). Its opposite ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) is its constant conversational partner -- Hindi speakers love pairing them, as in कम या ज़्यादा (kam yaa zyaadaa, 'more or less').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect कम (kam) like a regular -आ adjective (कमा, कमे, कमी); it never changes form regardless of the noun's gender or number -- कम रोटियाँ (kam rotiyaan, 'few rotis'), कम पैसे (kam paise, 'little money'). 2) कम से कम (kam se kam) means 'at least,' not 'less than less' -- learners sometimes misparse it literally.

Sense 2 : not much, rarely, to a small degree
adverb Modifies verbs and adjectives directly, e.g. कम खाओ (kam khaao, 'eat less') or कम अच्छा (kam achchhaa, 'less good').
Synonyms: थोड़ा (thodaa), शायद ही (shaayad hii, meaning scope: 'hardly ever')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Used to describe a quiet or reserved person: वह कम बोलता है (vah kam boltaa hai, 'he doesn't talk much') is a very common way Indians describe someone shy or introverted, often said approvingly about a well-behaved child or respectfully about a serious adult.
Common Mistakes: 1) वह कम बोलता है (vah kam boltaa hai, 'he speaks little/rarely') can sound to learners like it needs से (se) for comparison, but on its own कम (kam) just means 'rarely/not much' with no comparison implied.

Sense 3 : less than (in comparisons of quantity)
postposition Occurs in the frame X से कम (X se kam), 'less than X' -- the से-marked noun is the standard of comparison.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this on shop signs and price boards constantly: दो सौ रुपये से कम में (do sau rupye se kam men, 'for less than two hundred rupees') is classic bargaining language in Delhi markets.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop से (se) and just say दस कम (das kam) intending 'less than ten' -- the से (se) is required: दस से कम (das se kam).
Sample Sentences:
यह कुर्ता दो सौ रुपये से कम में मिल जाएगा। This kurta will be available for less than two hundred rupees.
(yah kurtaa do sau rupye se kam men mil jaaegaa।)
