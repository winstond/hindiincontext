मन लगना (man lagnaa)

verb A dative-subject expression built from मन (man, 'mind/heart') and लगना (lagnaa, 'to attach/feel'); it describes feeling engaged or settled in an activity or place, and by extension its negative flip describes restlessness or homesickness.
Sense 1 : to feel absorbed/engaged in something, to enjoy doing something
verb, intransitive Takes a dative subject with को (ko): the person who feels engaged is marked with को, e.g. मुझे पढ़ाई में मन लगता है (mujhe padhaaii men man lagtaa hai, 'I feel engaged in studying'). The activity or place is usually marked with में (men).
Synonyms: दिलचस्पी होना (dilchaspii honaa, 'to have interest'), रुचि होना (ruchi honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Parents and teachers use this constantly: पढ़ाई में मन लगाओ (padhaaii men man lagaao, 'put your mind into your studies') is a classic scolding. The idiom treats attention as something that 'attaches' to an activity rather than something you actively direct, which is a very Hindi way of framing focus.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person the subject with ने (ne) as if it were an ordinary transitive verb; it is always a को-marked dative construction, like लगना (lagnaa) itself. 2) Do not confuse with मन करना (man karnaa), which expresses a passing desire ('feel like doing') rather than sustained engagement or enjoyment.

(aaj meraa padhne men man nahiin lag rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to feel settled/at home in a place (often negated: to feel restless, not settle in)
verb, intransitive Very commonly used in the negative to describe homesickness or restlessness: यहाँ मेरा मन नहीं लगता (yahaan meraa man nahiin lagtaa, 'I don't feel settled/at home here'). Place is marked with में (men) or यहाँ/वहाँ-type locatives.
Synonyms: जी लगना (jii lagnaa, close synonym)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase behind the common complaint of students or new hostel-goers: होस्टल में मन नहीं लगता, घर जाना है (hostal men man nahiin lagtaa, ghar jaanaa hai, 'I don't feel settled in the hostel, I want to go home'). It captures a very relatable ache of displacement that English usually needs a whole sentence to express.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this literally as 'my mind is not attaching' and miss that it idiomatically means homesickness or discomfort in a place, closer to 'I can't settle in' than any literal sense. 2) The subject is still दिल/मन (man), not the person directly -- the person appears only via the possessive मेरा/उसका (meraa/uskaa) or a dative को.
