विष (vish)

noun A formal/literary-leaning word for poison, used both literally and in common figurative expressions about bitterness or venomous feeling; the everyday word ज़हर (zahar) is its more colloquial counterpart.
Sense 1 : poison, venom (literal toxic substance)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ज़हर (zahar), गरल (garal, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: विष (vish) carries a mythological weight: think of समुद्र मंथन (samudr manthan, 'the churning of the ocean'), where the god शिव (shiv) drinks the world-poison to save creation and earns the name नीलकंठ (niilkanth, 'blue-throated one'). That story is why the word feels a shade more epic than its plain cousin ज़हर (zahar).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to ज़हर (zahar) in speech, which is correct -- विष (vish) is not wrong but sounds more bookish or formal, more likely in a news report, a mythological story, or a doctor's warning than in casual kitchen or market talk.

Sense 2 : figurative venom or bitterness (in speech, feelings, or relationships)
noun, masculine Often appears in fixed phrases like विष घोलना (vish gholnaa, 'to dissolve poison,' i.e. to poison a relationship or atmosphere with malice) and मन में विष भरना (man men vish bharnaa, 'to fill the heart with venom').
Synonyms: कड़वाहट (kadvaahat, covers emotional bitterness specifically), ज़हर (zahar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in emotionally charged or dramatic speech -- a mother warning against gossip that विष घोलता है (vish gholtaa hai, 'sows poison') in a family, or a political speech accusing rivals of spreading विष (vish) in society. It is a favorite word of Hindi journalism and moral speeches for exactly this reason.
