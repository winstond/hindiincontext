नतीजा (natiijaa)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'result, outcome, consequence' -- used for exam results, the upshot of a decision, or the consequence of an action.
Sense 1 : result, outcome (of an exam, event, or process)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: परिणाम (parinaam), फल (phal, literary), नतीजा (natiijaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian student knows the anxious phrase नतीजा निकला (natiijaa niklaa, 'the result came out') -- board exam results are a huge family event, with neighbors and relatives all asking नतीजा क्या रहा? (natiijaa kyaa rahaa, 'what was the result?'). The word also works fine for non-academic outcomes, like the result of a business decision or a cricket match.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say परीक्षा का जवाब (pariikshaa kaa javaab) for 'exam result' by mistake -- the correct word is नतीजा (natiijaa) or परिणाम (parinaam), not जवाब (javaab) which means 'answer/reply.' 2) The verb पाना (paanaa) or निकलना (nikalnaa) is used with नतीजा (natiijaa) -- e.g. नतीजा निकलना (natiijaa nikalnaa, 'for a result to come out') -- rather than a literal 'to come' verb like आना (aanaa).
Sample Sentences:
आज बोर्ड का नतीजा निकला है। Today the board exam result came out.
(aaj bord kaa natiijaa niklaa hai।)

Sense 2 : consequence, upshot (of an action, especially a negative one)
noun, masculine Common in the frame X का नतीजा यह हुआ कि... (X kaa natiijaa yah huaa ki, 'the consequence of X was that...') to link a cause to its fallout.
Synonyms: अंजाम (anjaam), परिणाम (parinaam)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in parental warnings and moralizing speech: बुरे काम का नतीजा बुरा होता है (bure kaam kaa natiijaa buraa hotaa hai, 'a bad deed has a bad consequence') is the kind of line every Hindi-speaking kid has heard. It shares its root with the 'result' sense -- both frame an outcome as something that 'emerges' (निकलना, nikalnaa) from a prior situation.
Common Mistakes: 1) In warnings or scoldings, नतीजा भुगतना (natiijaa bhugatnaa, 'to bear/suffer the consequence') is the natural idiom -- learners sometimes try to translate 'face the consequences' too literally with सामना (saamnaa) instead.
