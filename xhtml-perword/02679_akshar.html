अक्षर (akshar)

noun/adjective Mainly a noun meaning 'letter/character' (of a script) or, more broadly, 'syllable'; it also has a rarer literary adjective sense meaning 'imperishable, eternal'.
Sense 1 : letter (of the alphabet), written character
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वर्ण (varn, formal/linguistic term), हर्फ़ (harf, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear when someone talks about a child learning to write: अक्षर लिखना सीखना (akshar likhnaa siikhnaa, 'to learn to write letters'). It is also used for the letters of Hindi's own script, देवनागरी (devnaagrii), so you will meet it constantly in any conversation about learning Hindi itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use अक्षर (akshar) when they mean शब्द (shabd, 'word') -- अक्षर (akshar) is only the individual letter/character, not a whole word. 2) In the fixed phrase अक्षर ज्ञान (akshar gyaan, 'basic literacy'), अक्षर (akshar) refers to letters in the sense of literacy itself, not a countable single letter.

Sense 2 : syllable
noun, masculine Used in discussions of Hindi/Sanskrit meter and phonetics, where a syllable is counted as one अक्षर (akshar).
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense mostly if you get into poetry or the study of Sanskrit chhand (chhand, 'meter'), where lines are measured in अक्षर (akshar) count rather than by word.

Sense 3 : imperishable, eternal, unchanging
adjective
Synonyms: अमर (amar), अविनाशी (avinaashii, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense plays on the literal root: something that cannot be 'worn away' letter by letter, i.e. that endures forever -- it appears in devotional and philosophical writing, for instance describing the eternal soul or the divine.
