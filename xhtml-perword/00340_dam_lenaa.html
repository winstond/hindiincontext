दम लेना (dam lenaa)

verb A compound verb built on दम (dam, 'breath') plus लेना (lenaa, 'to take'); its main everyday sense is 'to take a rest/breather,' with a related literal sense about catching one's breath.
Sense 1 : to take a rest, pause for a breather
verb, intransitive Often used after a stretch of hard work or exertion, frequently in imperative or suggestion form: दम लेना (dam lenaa) chाहिए/लो.
Synonyms: आराम करना (aaraam karnaa), सुस्ताना (sustaanaa, colloquial), साँस लेना (saans lenaa, to catch one's breath)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly from elders and coworkers alike -- after carrying groceries up the stairs or finishing a task, someone will say ज़रा दम ले लो (zaraa dam le lo, 'just catch your breath a bit'). It has the same warm, practical concern as English 'catch your breath' but is used more broadly for any kind of tiredness, not just physical exertion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'to take breath' in a medical/physical sense (like a doctor checking breathing) -- in everyday speech it almost always means 'take a short break,' not a clinical statement about respiration. 2) It pairs naturally with थोड़ा (thodaa, 'a little') as थोड़ा दम ले लो (thodaa dam le lo, 'take a little rest') -- dropping थोड़ा doesn't change grammaticality but changes the casual, encouraging tone native speakers usually intend.
Sample Sentences:
ज़रा दम ले लो, फिर काम शुरू करेंगे। Take a rest for a bit, we'll start work again after.
(zaraa dam le lo, phir kaam shuroo karenge।)

Sense 2 : to catch one's breath (literal, after exertion)
verb, intransitive Used right after physical exertion like running or climbing, describing the literal act of recovering normal breathing before continuing.
Synonyms: साँस लेना (saans lenaa), हाँफना (haamphnaa, to pant, related but distinct)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with the 'rest' sense above -- दम (dam) itself straddles 'breath' and 'stamina/energy,' so दम लेना (dam lenaa) naturally slides between 'catch your breath' and 'take a break.' Context (a runner pausing mid-race vs. a worker pausing mid-task) tells you which shade is meant.
