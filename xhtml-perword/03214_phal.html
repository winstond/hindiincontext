फल (phal)

noun Primarily 'fruit' in the everyday sense; also carries the extended/figurative sense of 'result, outcome, consequence' (as in the fruit of one's actions).
Sense 1 : fruit (the food item)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मेवा (mevaa, usually dried fruit/nuts)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Any Delhi fruit-seller's cart is called a फल की रेहड़ी (phal kii rehdii, a fruit cart), and फल (phal) is the word you will hear shouted out in markets and on trains alike. Offering फल (phal) as प्रसाद (prasaad) at a temple or as a gift when visiting someone is a very common social gesture.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use फल (phal) for any produce, but vegetables are सब्ज़ी (sabzii) or सब्ज़ियाँ (sabziyaan), a separate category in everyday Hindi shopping talk.

Sense 2 : result, outcome, consequence (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the fixed idea of कर्म का फल (karm kaa phal, 'the fruit of one's actions/karma'), where actions are said to 'bear fruit.'
Synonyms: परिणाम (parinaam), नतीजा (natiijaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense grows directly out of the literal 'fruit' meaning: just as a tree yields fruit, actions are believed to yield फल (phal) -- this is the whole idea behind the famous concept of कर्मफल (karmphal, the fruit of one's karma), which underlies a lot of everyday moralizing you'll hear from elders.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for परिणाम (parinaam) or नतीजा (natiijaa) by default in this sense, forgetting that फल (phal) is the natural word specifically in moral/karmic or reward-and-consequence contexts, not for something like an exam result.
