आजकल (aajkal)

adverb A single-sense time adverb meaning 'these days, nowadays' -- a compound of आज (aaj, today) and कल (kal, yesterday/tomorrow) that together point to the general present period.
Sense 1 : nowadays, these days
adverb
Synonyms: इन दिनों (in dinon), वर्तमान में (vartmaan men, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आजकल (aajkal) literally stitches together 'today' and 'tomorrow/yesterday' (aaj and kal) into a single idea of 'the present era' -- a nice example of how Hindi builds time words by pairing adjacent days rather than coining a separate abstract word. You will hear it constantly in small talk: आजकल क्या चल रहा है? (aajkal kyaa chal rahaa hai?, 'what's going on these days?') is a classic way to ask someone what they have been up to.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अभी (abhii) when they mean 'these days' -- अभी (abhii) points to this exact moment ('right now'), while आजकल (aajkal) spans a whole recent period ('this month, this year'). 2) It typically opens or follows early in the sentence as a scene-setting time word, not tucked in right before the verb the way English 'nowadays' can float anywhere.
