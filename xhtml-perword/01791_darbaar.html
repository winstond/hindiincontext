दरबार (darbaar)

noun A noun referring to a royal court or formal assembly, and by extension the crowd of people or hangers-on gathered around an important person; also used for a divine 'court' in devotional contexts (e.g. a saint's shrine).
Sense 1 : royal court / formal assembly held by a ruler or high official
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राजसभा (raajasbhaa, formal/historical), सभा (sabhaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of Mughal or Rajput history classes: the emperor holding दरबार (darbaar) meant nobles, petitioners, and officials all gathered to seek favor or give reports. The word still carries that flavor of formal, hierarchical audience-granting.
Common Mistakes: 1) दरबार (darbaar) refers to the institution or the assembly itself, not a physical palace building -- learners sometimes use it where महल (mahal, 'palace') is meant.

Sense 2 : a saint's or deity's shrine, viewed as their court
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दरगाह (dargaah, specific to Sufi shrines), मंदिर (mandir, not interchangeable, general 'temple')
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: You will hear devotees say चलो दरबार में चलते हैं (chalo darbaar men chalte hain, 'let's go to the shrine/court') about a Sufi dargah or a revered saint's resting place -- the idea is that the saint still 'holds court' spiritually, receiving petitioners just like a king would.

Sense 3 : the retinue or crowd of followers/flatterers around an important person
noun, masculine Often used with a slight edge, implying sycophancy -- e.g. describing the flatterers who surround a politician or boss as their दरबार (darbaar).
Synonyms: चेले-चमचे (chele-chamche, colloquial, mildly derogatory), जमघट (jamghat, gathering, less specific)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In everyday office or political gossip, someone might say उसका अपना दरबार है (uskaa apnaa darbaar hai, 'he has his own court') about a boss surrounded by yes-men -- a wry, modern extension of the old royal-court image.
