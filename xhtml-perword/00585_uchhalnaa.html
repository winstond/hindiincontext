उछलना (uchhalnaa)

verb One core sense: to jump/leap/bounce, used for people, animals, and objects, and extended figuratively to things like prices or emotions 'jumping up.'
Sense 1 : to jump, leap, bounce
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: कूदना (koodnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this used very physically -- a child उछलना (uchhalnaa, 'jumping') on a bed, a ball bouncing on the road -- but also emotionally, as in दिल उछल पड़ा (dil uchhal padaa, 'the heart leapt'), which mirrors the English idiom of a heart leaping with joy or fright.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with कूदना (koodnaa); उछलना (uchhalnaa) leans more toward a sudden, springy, often involuntary bounce (like a ball or a startled body), while कूदना (koodnaa) is the more general, deliberate act of jumping (like jumping over a wall or into water). 2) As an intransitive verb, it does not take ने (ne) in the perfective -- learners who over-apply the ने-rule to any past-tense action verb sometimes add it here by mistake.
Sample Sentences:

(bachche bistar par uchhal rahe hain।)

Sense 2 : to shoot up, spike suddenly (prices, values, figures)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: बढ़ना (badhnaa, general rise, less sudden)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear on the news or in the sabzi mandi (sabzii mandii, 'vegetable market') when prices suddenly jump -- दाम उछल गए (daam uchhal gae, 'the prices shot up') -- capturing the same springy, sudden-jerk quality as the physical sense, just applied to numbers.
Sample Sentences:
