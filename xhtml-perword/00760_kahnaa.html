कहना (kahnaa)

verb/noun The basic verb 'to say/tell'; also covers 'to call/name something' and appears in a set of very common frozen forms and idioms built on the same root.
Sense 1 : to say, to tell, to speak (words)
verb, transitive Takes an indirect object with से (se) for the person addressed: उससे कहना (usse kahnaa, to say to him/her) -- not को (ko) as an English speaker might expect from 'tell him'.
Synonyms: बोलना (bolnaa), बताना (bataanaa, to tell/inform)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first verbs learners meet, and it anchors reported speech: उसने कहा कि... (usne kahaa ki..., 'He said that...') is the standard way to open a quoted or reported sentence in Hindi conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the listener with को (ko) by analogy with 'tell him', but कहना (kahnaa) wants से (se): उससे कहो (usse kaho, tell him/her), not उसको कहो. 2) In the past tense this verb is transitive and takes ने (ne): मैंने कहा (mainne kahaa, I said) -- learners sometimes drop ने here by confusing it with intransitive speech-verbs.

मेरी माँ हमेशा कहती है कि सुबह जल्दी उठो। My mother always says to wake up early in the morning.
(merii maan hameshaa kahtii hai ki subah jaldii utho।)

Sense 2 : to call, to name, to refer to as
verb, transitive Frame: X को Y कहना (X ko Y kahnaa) = 'to call X Y', e.g. उसे पागल कहना (use paagal kahnaa, to call him crazy).
Synonyms: बुलाना (bulaanaa, to call/summon -- different frame, not interchangeable)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: इसे क्या कहते हैं? (ise kyaa kahte hain?, 'What is this called?') is one of the most useful survival phrases for a learner pointing at objects and asking for vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget the को (ko) marking on the thing/person being named: इसे हिंदी में क्या कहते हैं? (ise hindii men kyaa kahte hain?, 'What do you call this in Hindi?') is the natural frame, not a direct word-for-word translation from English.

Sense 3 : word, saying (as in 'a saying goes')
noun, feminine Appears mainly in the fixed idiom कहने को (kahne ko, 'so to speak' / 'in name only') and in किसी की कहनी (kisii kii kahnii) type literary usage; the everyday idiomatic form is what learners will actually encounter.
Synonyms: बात (baat, broader meaning: talk/matter)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: कहने को तो... (kahne ko to..., 'Nominally/supposedly...') is a common hedge in spoken Hindi, e.g. introducing a claim the speaker doesn't fully buy: कहने को तो वह डॉक्टर है (kahne ko to vah doktar hai, 'He's supposedly a doctor').
