भजन (bhajan)

noun A devotional song sung in praise of a deity; the word also carries the deeper sense of 'worship' or 'reverence' from which the song-genre meaning grows.
Sense 1 : devotional song
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कीर्तन (kiirtan, sung with call-and-response and instruments), स्तुति (stuti, literary/formal, more 'hymn of praise')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture an evening in a Delhi colony: elderly neighbors gathered on someone's veranda, a harmonium and a pair of tabla out, singing भजन (bhajan) to Krishna or Ram late into the night -- this is called a भजन संध्या (bhajan sandhyaa, 'an evening of devotional singing') and is a common sight in North Indian neighborhoods, especially around festivals or in old age.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume भजन (bhajan) means any religious song, but आरती (aartii) is a distinct short ritual song sung while waving a lit lamp before an idol -- भजन (bhajan) is usually longer and more like a devotional ballad. 2) भजन (bhajan) is grammatically masculine even though singing is often imagined as a soft, gentle act -- do not default to feminine agreement.

Sense 2 : worship, devotion (act of)
noun, masculine This sense appears mainly in set phrases like भजन करना (bhajan karnaa, 'to worship/adore') and in compound formations, rather than as freely chosen vocabulary in casual speech.
Synonyms: पूजा (poojaa, more ritual-specific, involves formal offerings), आराधना (aaraadhnaa, literary, more formal/reverential)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older, broader sense of भजन (bhajan) -- devotion or reverence itself, not just the song -- survives today mostly inside the compound verb भजन करना (bhajan karnaa) and in the etymological root shared with भक्ति (bhakti, 'devotion'), both from the same Sanskrit root meaning 'to share in, partake of, revere.'
