लिखना (likhnaa)

verb A core, everyday transitive verb meaning 'to write'; it also extends naturally into a couple of common figurative and idiomatic uses (fate being 'written', a name being 'entered' somewhere).
Sense 1 : to write (letters, words, a document)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first 'action' verbs every learner meets, usually paired with पढ़ना (padhnaa, 'to read/study') in classroom contexts -- लिखना-पढ़ना (likhnaa-padhnaa) together evoke basic literacy and schooling in everyday speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget ने (ne) marking in the perfective: since लिखना (likhnaa) is transitive, past-tense sentences need ने (ne) on the subject, e.g. उसने चिट्ठी लिखी (usne chitthii likhii, 'she wrote a letter'), with the verb agreeing with चिट्ठी (chitthii), not the subject. 2) The thing written takes a direct object with no postposition (चिट्ठी लिखना (chitthii likhnaa)), while the recipient takes को (ko): उसे चिट्ठी लिखी (use chitthii likhii, 'wrote her a letter').
Sample Sentences:
वह हर दिन अपनी डायरी में लिखता है। He writes in his diary every day.
(vah har din apnii daayrii men likhtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to be fated/destined (as if written by fate)
verb, intransitive Typically appears passively/impersonally with में (men) marking the fate-holder: उसकी क़िस्मत में लिखा था (uskii qismat men likhaa thaa, 'it was written in her fate').
Synonyms: होना (honaa, general 'to happen/be'), मुक़द्दर में होना (muqaddar men honaa, fate-specific phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense taps into a very common cultural idea in Hindi-speaking India: that destiny is something pre-written by God or fate, as in भाग्य में लिखा है (bhaagy men likhaa hai, 'it is written in [one's] fate'). You will hear this in everyday consolation, like जो लिखा है वही होगा (jo likhaa hai vahii hogaa, 'whatever is written [in fate] will happen').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use this sense actively with a doer, but native speakers almost always phrase it impersonally -- 'X is written in Y's fate' -- rather than 'someone wrote X for Y'. 2) The verb here is usually in perfective passive-like form (लिखा है (likhaa hai), लिखा था (likhaa thaa)) rather than conjugated for an explicit subject.
