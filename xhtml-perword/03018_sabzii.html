सब्ज़ी (sabzii)

noun A single core sense: 'vegetable' as an ingredient, and by extension the cooked vegetable dish itself, as served at an Indian meal.
Sense 1 : vegetable (raw, as produce)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: तरकारी (tarkaarii, regional/older term)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every neighborhood in Delhi-NCR has its सब्ज़ी वाला (sabzii vaalaa, 'vegetable seller') who comes around with a cart shouting out today's stock -- आलू, प्याज़, टमाटर (aaloo, pyaaz, tamaatar, 'potato, onion, tomato') and so on. Asking सब्ज़ी लेनी है? (sabzii lenii hai?, 'need to buy vegetables?') is a completely ordinary daily-errand question.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume सब्ज़ी (sabzii) is always singular in meaning like English 'a vegetable' -- but it is regularly used as a mass/collective noun, as in सब्ज़ी लाना (sabzii laanaa, 'to bring vegetables'), covering a whole basket of mixed produce, not just one item. 2) The nuqta on ज़ is often dropped in casual writing (सब्जी), which is common and acceptable, not a different word.

Sense 2 : cooked vegetable dish (as part of a meal)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: At almost any North Indian meal, रोटी-सब्ज़ी (rotii-sabzii, 'bread and vegetable dish') is the everyday staple pairing -- when someone asks आज क्या सब्ज़ी बनी है? (aaj kyaa sabzii banii hai?, 'what vegetable dish was made today?'), they mean the cooked preparation, not the raw ingredient.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate सब्ज़ी (sabzii) too literally as 'vegetable' even when it clearly refers to the cooked side dish on the plate -- e.g. रोटी और सब्ज़ी (rotii aur sabzii, 'bread and [cooked] vegetable dish') means the finished curry, not raw produce.
