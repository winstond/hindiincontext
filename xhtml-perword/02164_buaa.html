बुआ (buaa)

noun A single kinship term: father's sister (paternal aunt). Also used as a respectful/affectionate address to older women of that relation, and by extension informally to family friends of that generation.
Sense 1 : father's sister (aunt)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: फूफी (phoophii, regional variant), पितृ-भगिनी (pitri-bhaginii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian families, बुआ (buaa) holds a special affectionate status -- she's often the one who spoils her brother's kids the most, and there's a well-known cultural sweetness to the बुआ-भतीजा/भतीजी (buaa-bhatiijaa/bhatiijii) bond, especially around festivals and weddings where बुआ (buaa) plays a ritual role (like in Punjabi and North Indian wedding customs). Children usually call her directly बुआ (buaa) rather than using her name, just as they would with चाची (chaachii) or मामी (maamii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use बुआ (buaa) for any aunt, but Hindi distinguishes kin by side and type: मामी (maamii) is maternal uncle's wife, चाची (chaachii) is paternal uncle's (father's younger brother's) wife, and मौसी (mausii) is mother's sister. बुआ (buaa) is specifically father's sister -- getting the side wrong is a very common slip. 2) The husband of बुआ (buaa) is called फूफा (phoophaa), not मौसा (mausaa) or मामा (maamaa), which learners sometimes mix up.

Sense 2 : respectful/affectionate term for an older woman of one's father's generation, not a blood relative
noun, feminine Used as a polite address or reference term, extending the kinship word to family friends or neighbors of the appropriate generation, similar to how चाची (chaachii) or मामी (maamii) get extended.
Synonyms: आंटी (aantii, informal, not kin-specific)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This extension of kinship terms to non-relatives is very typical in Hindi -- calling a family friend बुआ (buaa) instead of by name signals warmth and respect, and situates her socially as being 'like family' on the father's side, rather than the mother's side (which would call for मौसी (mausii) instead).
