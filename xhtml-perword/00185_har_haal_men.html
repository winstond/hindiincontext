हर हाल में (har haal men)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'no matter what, come what may, in any case' -- used to stress that something will happen regardless of circumstances. Built from हर (har, 'every') + हाल (haal, 'condition/state') + में (men, 'in').
Sense 1 : no matter what, in any case, come what may
adverb Typically placed before the verb phrase it modifies, often paired with a future or imperative to stress determination or an unavoidable requirement: हर हाल में आना है (har haal men aanaa hai, 'you must come no matter what').
Synonyms: किसी भी सूरत में (kisii bhii soorat men), हर सूरत में (har soorat men), जैसे भी हो (jaise bhii ho)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot from parents, bosses, and officials -- हर हाल में समय पर पहुँचना (har haal men samay par pahunchnaa, 'arrive on time no matter what') is the kind of line that brooks no excuses. It carries a slightly stern, no-negotiation tone, similar in force to English 'come hell or high water.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this word-for-word as 'in every condition' and use it in neutral descriptive sentences; in practice it is almost always used to add emphasis or urgency to a promise, demand, or requirement, not to describe a state of affairs. 2) It is easy to confuse with हर हाल (har haal) alone (without में (men)), which is not how this phrase is used; the में (men) is fixed and cannot be dropped.
Sample Sentences:
हमें हर हाल में समय पर पहुँचना है। We have to arrive on time no matter what.
(hamen har haal men samay par pahunchnaa hai।)
