कंधा (kandhaa)

noun A concrete body-part noun meaning 'shoulder,' with a common figurative extension to bearing responsibility or emotional support.
Sense 1 : shoulder (body part)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a father walking through a mela with his child perched on his कंधा (kandhaa) -- कंधे पर बैठाना (kandhe par baithaanaa, 'to carry on the shoulders') is a very common, affectionate image in Hindi households and films alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget this noun is masculine and takes the -e oblique/plural pattern like कमरा (kamraa) -> कमरे (kamre), so 'on the shoulders' is कंधों पर (kandhon par), not a feminine-pattern form. 2) The postposition पर (par) is used for 'on the shoulder' (कंधे पर बैठाना, kandhe par baithaanaa, 'to seat on the shoulder'), where learners sometimes reach for में (men) by mistake.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे कंधे में बहुत दर्द हो रहा है। My shoulder is hurting a lot.
(mere kandhe men bahut dard ho rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative: support, responsibility borne by someone
noun, masculine Common in the frame कंधे से कंधा मिलाना (kandhe se kandhaa milaanaa, 'to stand shoulder to shoulder, to support together') and कंधों पर ज़िम्मेदारी होना (kandhon par zimmedaarii honaa, 'to have responsibility on one's shoulders').
Synonyms: जिम्मा (jimmaa, responsibility, not a body part), सहारा (sahaaraa, support)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use mirrors English 'shoulder the burden,' but Hindi leans on it constantly in speeches and news headlines -- कंधे से कंधा मिलाकर काम करना (kandhe se kandhaa milaakar kaam karnaa, 'to work shoulder to shoulder') is a go-to phrase for unity, whether between siblings, colleagues, or a whole nation.
Common Mistakes: 1) कंधे से कंधा मिलाना (kandhe se kandhaa milaanaa) is a fixed idiom for standing in solidarity or working together -- learners sometimes translate it too literally as just physical touching rather than teamwork or moral support.
