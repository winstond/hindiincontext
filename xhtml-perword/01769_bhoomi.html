भूमि (bhoomi)

noun Mainly means 'land, ground, earth' in the physical/geographical sense, and extends to a figurative 'ground, basis' for something (as in पृष्ठभूमि (prishthbhoomi, background)). It is a more formal/literary register word than the everyday जमीन (jamiin).
Sense 1 : land, ground, earth (physical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जमीन (jamiin), ज़मीन (zamiin), धरती (dhartii), भू (bhoo, literary, used mainly in compounds)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word constantly in official and written contexts -- भूमि अधिग्रहण (bhoomi adhigrahan, land acquisition) is a common phrase in news about government projects, and land records offices use भूमि (bhoomi) rather than जमीन (jamiin). Think of भूमि (bhoomi) as the word you'd find on a form or in a newspaper headline, while जमीन (jamiin) is what you'd actually say to your neighbor about the plot next door.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for भूमि (bhoomi) in everyday speech where a native speaker would simply say जमीन (jamiin) -- भूमि (bhoomi) sounds bookish or official in casual conversation, more at home in writing, news, or government contexts (land records, agriculture, real estate) than in a market or family chat.

Sense 2 : basis, ground, foundation (figurative)
noun, feminine This figurative sense appears heavily inside compounds like पृष्ठभूमि (prishthbhoomi, background) and कार्यभूमि (kaarybhoomi, field/site of action) rather than standing alone in a sentence.
Synonyms: आधार (aadhaar), नींव (niinv, literal foundation, used figuratively too)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense rarely stands on its own -- it lives inside compound words. पृष्ठभूमि (prishthbhoomi) literally means 'back-ground' (पृष्ठ (prishth, back/page) + भूमि (bhoomi)) and is the everyday Hindi word for 'background,' whether you're talking about someone's family background or the backdrop of a stage.
