हवा (havaa)

noun Primarily 'air/wind' in the physical sense, but the word carries a rich set of figurative extensions -- mood/atmosphere, rumor, and vanity/arrogance -- all built on the same core image of something invisible moving or filling a space.
Sense 1 : air, wind (physical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पवन (pavan, literary), वायु (vaayu, formal/literary), बयार (bayaar, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi life you'll hear हवा (havaa) constantly -- खिड़की खोलो, हवा आने दो (khidkii kholo, havaa aane do, 'open the window, let the air in') on a hot day, or ठंडी हवा (thandii havaa, 'cool breeze') when the weather turns pleasant. Many older family members also worry about हवा लगना (havaa lagnaa), the folk idea that a draft of air can make you sick.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for वायु (vaayu) or पवन (pavan) in everyday speech where हवा (havaa) is the natural choice -- those alternatives sound bookish or poetic in conversation. 2) हवा लगना (havaa lagnaa, 'to catch a chill/draft') is a common health-related idiom -- Indians often blame a cold or fever on 'हवा लग गई' (havaa lag gaii, 'the wind/draft got to me') rather than germs.
Sample Sentences:

(khidkii kholo, havaa aane do।)

Sense 2 : atmosphere, mood, general feeling or trend
noun, feminine Common in phrases like हवा बदलना (havaa badalnaa, 'the mood/climate to change') or हवा का रुख (havaa kaa rukh, 'the direction the wind is blowing', i.e. the way things are trending).
Synonyms: माहौल (maahaul), रुझान (rujhaan, trend/leaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in political and social commentary: चुनाव की हवा किसके पक्ष में है (chunaav kii havaa kiske paksh men hai, 'which way is the election wind blowing') is a very natural thing to hear on the news, treating public opinion as literally a current of air.

Sense 3 : rumor, unverified talk
noun, feminine Occurs mainly in the fixed phrase हवा फैलना/उड़ना (havaa phailnaa / havaa udnaa, 'for a rumor to spread/fly') and हवा उड़ाना (havaa udaanaa, 'to spread a rumor').
Synonyms: अफ़वाह (afvaah), चर्चा (charchaa, talk/buzz)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in casual gossip: बाज़ार में हवा है कि दुकान बंद हो रही है (baazaar men havaa hai ki dukaan band ho rahii hai, 'word around the market is that the shop is closing') -- the rumor is pictured as something carried on the wind from person to person.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use अफ़वाह (afvaah) exclusively for 'rumor' and miss that हवा (havaa) in phrases like हवा है कि (havaa hai ki, 'word is that...') carries the same meaning colloquially.
Sample Sentences:
बाज़ार में हवा है कि दुकान बंद हो रही है। Word around the market is that the shop is closing.
(baazaar men havaa hai ki dukaan band ho rahii hai।)

Sense 4 : vanity, conceit, an inflated sense of self
noun, feminine Almost always appears in the idiom हवा में उड़ना / हवा में होना (havaa men udnaa / havaa men honaa, 'to be flying/floating in the air', i.e. to be conceited or full of oneself) or हवा हो जाना (havaa ho jaanaa used differently -- see disappearance sense).
Synonyms: घमंड (ghamand), गुरूर (guroor, literary/formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A classic parental scolding: सफलता मिली तो सिर पर हवा मत चढ़ने देना (saphaltaa milii to sir par havaa mat chadhne denaa, 'don't let success go to your head') -- here हवा (havaa) is the puffed-up feeling of pride rising like air into one's head.
Common Mistakes: 1) सिर पर हवा चढ़ना (sir par havaa chadhnaa, 'for air/wind to rise to one's head') means 'to become conceited/big-headed' -- learners may misread it literally rather than as this idiom of vanity.
Sample Sentences:
सफलता मिली तो सिर पर हवा मत चढ़ने देना। If you get success, don't let it go to your head.
(saphaltaa milii to sir par havaa mat chadhne denaa।)

Sense 5 : sudden disappearance, vanishing (idiomatic)
noun, feminine Fixed idiom हवा हो जाना (havaa ho jaanaa, 'to vanish/disappear suddenly'), used for people, objects, or even fear/nervousness disappearing quickly.
Synonyms: ग़ायब (ghaayab, disappeared -- adjective, used together as ग़ायब हो जाना)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Very common in casual speech: जैसे ही पुलिस आई, चोर हवा हो गया (jaise hii pulis aaii, chor havaa ho gayaa, 'as soon as the police arrived, the thief vanished into thin air') -- a vivid, everyday way to say someone disappeared fast.
Common Mistakes: 1) This idiom is easy to confuse with the 'vanity' sense above (हवा में होना) -- हवा हो जाना (havaa ho jaanaa) means 'to vanish,' not 'to become conceited,' despite the similar wording.
