राष्ट्रभाषा (raashtrbhaashaa)

noun A compound noun meaning 'national language,' built from राष्ट्र (raashtr, nation) plus भाषा (bhaashaa, language). One straightforward sense, but the term carries real political weight in India.
Sense 1 : national language
noun, feminine
Synonyms: राजभाषा (raajbhaashaa, official language, not exactly the same concept)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word shows up constantly in Indian civics classes and political debates, and the gap between what people *call* Hindi and what the Constitution actually says is a genuine sore point in states like Tamil Nadu -- calling Hindi 'राष्ट्रभाषा (raashtrbhaashaa)' in front of a non-Hindi speaker can spark an argument, so it is worth knowing the term is popular usage rather than legal fact.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume Hindi is constitutionally India's 'राष्ट्रभाषा (raashtrbhaashaa)' -- in fact the Indian Constitution names Hindi (and English) as राजभाषा (raajbhaashaa, official language) of the Union, not as a national language; India has no constitutionally declared राष्ट्रभाषा (raashtrbhaashaa). 2) Do not confuse this with मातृभाषा (maatribhaashaa, mother tongue), which is personal/regional, not national.
