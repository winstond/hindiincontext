स्तंभ (stambh)

noun Primarily a physical 'pillar/column,' extended figuratively to a written 'column' (in a newspaper) and to a 'pillar' figure who supports or upholds something (also spelled स्तम्भ (stambh) with the anusvaar written as the full nasal consonant).
Sense 1 : pillar, column (physical structure)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खंभा (khambhaa), खंभा (khambhaa), सतून (satoon, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the great stone pillars at Qutub Minar or in old temple halls -- स्तंभ (stambh) carries that weight of grandeur and antiquity, unlike the humble खंभा (khambhaa) you'd use for a bus-stop pole or a clothesline post.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for खंभा (khambhaa) in everyday speech about a physical pillar or pole (like a lamppost or electric pole) and save स्तंभ (stambh) for more formal or grand contexts -- a temple pillar, a monument, an ancient column -- rather than an ordinary street pole.

Sense 2 : column (in a newspaper or publication)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कॉलम (kolam, common in casual speech)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Open any Hindi newspaper and you'll see headings like 'संपादकीय स्तंभ' (sampaadkiiy stambh, 'editorial column') -- this is the standard formal word in print journalism, even though people chatting about the newspaper will often just say कॉलम (kolam).

Sense 3 : pillar (figurative: a person or thing that supports/upholds)
noun, masculine Used in fixed phrases like देश का स्तंभ (desh kaa stambh, 'pillar of the nation') to praise someone as a foundational support of a family, institution, or country.
Synonyms: आधार (aadhaar, broader sense of 'foundation/basis')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in speeches and tributes -- calling someone समाज का स्तंभ (samaaj kaa stambh, 'a pillar of society') is a classic bit of Hindi rhetorical praise, putting a person on the same solid footing as the pillars holding up a building.
