कम से कम (kam se kam)

adverb A fixed comparative phrase built from कम (kam, 'less') repeated with से (se) -- it has two everyday senses: 'at least' (a minimum quantity/expectation) and 'at the very least' (a minimum concession in an argument).
Sense 1 : at least (minimum quantity)
adverb
Synonyms: न्यूनतम (nyoontam, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in markets and while planning: दस रुपये कम से कम लगेंगे (das rupye kam se kam lagenge, 'it will cost at least ten rupees'). It pairs naturally with numbers, time, and quantities the way English 'at least' does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate 'at least' word-for-word with अधिकतम (adhiktam, 'at most') logic reversed -- remember कम से कम (kam se kam) always marks a floor, not a ceiling; ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा (zyaadaa se zyaadaa) is its 'at most' counterpart.
Sample Sentences:

(is kaam men kam se kam do ghante lagenge।)

Sense 2 : at the very least (concession/minimal expectation)
adverb Used to introduce the smallest acceptable option in a sentence, often at the start of a clause, when other possibilities have been ruled out.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind a scolding parent's line like कम से कम फ़ोन तो कर देते (kam se kam fon to kar dete, 'you could have at least called') -- it carries a note of mild reproach or resignation, pointing to the bare minimum someone should have done.
