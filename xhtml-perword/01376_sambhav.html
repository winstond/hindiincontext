संभव (sambhav)

adjective/noun One core sense: 'possible.' Mostly used as an adjective in fixed frames like संभव है (sambhav hai) and जहाँ तक संभव हो (jahaan tak sambhav ho).
Sense 1 : possible, feasible
adjective Commonly paired with है (hai) as संभव है (sambhav hai, 'it is possible') to open a sentence, functioning like an impersonal opener similar to 'maybe' or 'it's possible that.'
Synonyms: मुमकिन (mumkin), साध्य (saadhy, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear जितना संभव हो सके (jitnaa sambhav ho sake, 'as much as possible') and जहाँ तक संभव हो (jahaan tak sambhav ho, 'as far as possible') constantly in polite requests and official notices in Delhi -- these are the everyday home of this word far more than standalone use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make संभव (sambhav) agree in gender/number like a normal adjective, but it is invariant -- it stays संभव (sambhav) regardless of the noun it describes. 2) संभव है (sambhav hai) at the start of a sentence means 'it's possible that,' not 'it is possible' describing a specific noun just mentioned -- learners sometimes misplace it expecting it to modify the previous word directly.
Sample Sentences:
यह काम समय पर पूरा करना संभव है। It is possible to finish this work on time.
(yah kaam samay par pooraa karnaa sambhav hai।)

Sense 2 : possibility (rare as standalone noun)
noun, feminine The dedicated noun for 'possibility' is संभावना (sambhaavnaa); संभव (sambhav) itself is rarely used as a standalone noun in everyday speech.
Synonyms: संभावना (sambhaavnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: If you mean the noun 'possibility,' reach for संभावना (sambhaavnaa) instead -- native speakers keep संभव (sambhav) as the adjective and let its cousin संभावना (sambhaavnaa) carry the noun sense.
