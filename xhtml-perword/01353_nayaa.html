नया (nayaa)

adjective The everyday adjective for 'new,' used constantly across contexts from clothes to relationships; it also has a common figurative sense meaning 'inexperienced' or 'unfamiliar with something.'
Sense 1 : new (freshly made, acquired, or recent)
adjective
Synonyms: नवीन (naviin, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: नया साल (nayaa saal, 'new year') is the phrase you will hear everywhere around January 1, and नई दिल्ली (naii dillii, 'New Delhi') is a name every Delhi-ite says daily without a second thought. Note that this adjective is fully variable, so its form shifts constantly with the noun it describes -- pay close attention to those endings, they are what native ears listen for.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget to inflect the ending: नया (nayaa) becomes नई (naii) before feminine nouns and नए (nae) before masculine plural/oblique nouns -- e.g. नई किताब (naii kitaab, 'new book'), नए कपड़े (nae kapde, 'new clothes'). 2) For 'What's new?' Hindi speakers say क्या नया है? (kyaa nayaa hai, 'what's new'), not a literal translation of English small talk phrasing.
Sample Sentences:

(mainne kal ek nayaa fon khariidaa।)

Sense 2 : new/inexperienced (person unfamiliar with a role, place, or activity)
adjective Often used with में (men) to mark the domain of inexperience, as in इस काम में नया (is kaam men nayaa, 'new to this work').
Synonyms: अनजान (anjaan, unfamiliar), अनुभवहीन (anubhvahiin, lacks experience)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A boss might say नया लड़का है, सिखाना पड़ेगा (nayaa ladkaa hai, sikhaanaa padegaa, 'he's new, we'll have to teach him') -- this figurative use of नया (nayaa) for 'the new guy' at work or in a neighborhood is extremely common in everyday Delhi speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अनजान (anjaan) when they mean 'inexperienced' -- नया (nayaa) specifically frames someone as a newcomer to a role or skill, while अनजान (anjaan) leans toward simply not knowing a fact or person.

(vo yahaan nayaa hai, islie use sab kuchh samjhaanaa padtaa hai।)
