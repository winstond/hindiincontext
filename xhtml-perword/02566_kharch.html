ख़र्च (kharch)

noun/verb Primarily a noun meaning 'expense/expenditure,' also used as part of the common compound verb ख़र्च करना (kharch karnaa, 'to spend') and, less commonly, as an intransitive-feeling verb ख़र्च होना (kharch honaa, 'to be spent').
Sense 1 : expense, expenditure, cost
noun, masculine
Synonyms: व्यय (vyay, formal), लागत (laagat, cost/expenditure of production)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in household contexts -- घर का ख़र्च (ghar kaa kharch, 'household expenses') is a staple phrase when families talk budget at the dinner table. Note the nuqta: ख़र्च (kharch) with the dot under ख is the standard educated pronunciation, though खर्च (kharch) without it is also commonly written and understood.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use ख़र्च (kharch) as a verb by itself -- remember it needs करना (karnaa) or होना (honaa) to function as a verb; the noun alone just names the expense. 2) Don't confuse with दाम (daam) or क़ीमत (qiimat), which mean 'price' -- ख़र्च (kharch) is about money going out overall (rent, groceries, travel), not the price tag on a single item.

Sense 2 : to spend (money, time, energy)
verb, transitive Functions as a compound verb: ख़र्च (kharch) + करना (karnaa), with करना (karnaa) carrying the normal transitive ने (ne)-marking in the perfective.
Synonyms: व्यय करना (vyay karnaa, formal), उड़ाना (udaanaa, colloquial, 'to blow money')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पैसे ख़र्च करना (paise kharch karnaa, 'to spend money') is the everyday phrase you'll use constantly while shopping or discussing budgets -- much more natural in speech than the more bookish व्यय करना (vyay karnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop करना (karnaa) entirely, saying just ख़र्च (kharch) as if it were a standalone verb -- it always needs करना (karnaa) (transitive) or होना (honaa) (intransitive) attached.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to be spent, get used up (of money)
verb, intransitive Formed with होना (honaa) as ख़र्च होना (kharch honaa); the subject (the money) is not ने-marked since होना (honaa) is intransitive.
Synonyms: उड़ना (udnaa, colloquial, 'to fly away/get blown')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this when someone laments where their salary went: पूरी तनख़्वाह ख़र्च हो गई (poorii tanakhvaah kharch ho gaii, 'the whole salary got spent') -- a classic complaint at the end of the month.
