व्यर्थ (vyarth)

adjective/adverb One core idea -- 'useless / in vain' -- expressed both as an adjective (describing something pointless) and as an adverb (describing an action done pointlessly).
Sense 1 : useless, pointless, futile
adjective
Synonyms: बेकार (bekaar), फ़िज़ूल (fizool), निरर्थक (nirarthak, formal), बेफ़ायदा (befaaydaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot in moralizing or advice-giving speech -- parents, teachers, and motivational speakers love saying things like समय व्यर्थ मत करो (samay vyarth mat karo, 'don't waste time'). It carries a slightly bookish, sermon-like flavor compared to the more everyday बेकार (bekaar).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बेकार (bekaar) in every context, but व्यर्थ (vyarth) sounds a notch more formal/literary and is common in advice, warnings, or reflective statements rather than casual complaints about broken objects. 2) As a predicate adjective it is invariant -- it does not change form for gender or number, unlike a typical -आ adjective.

Sense 2 : in vain, uselessly, for nothing
adverb Modifies a verb directly, typically placed just before it: व्यर्थ गया (vyarth gayaa, 'went to waste') or मत जाओ व्यर्थ (vyarth) constructions with करना.
Synonyms: बेकार (bekaar), फ़िज़ूल में (fizool men), बेवजह (bevjah, without reason)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A classic pairing is मेहनत व्यर्थ गई (mehnat vyarth gaii, 'the effort went to waste') -- you will hear this whenever plans fall through despite hard work, a very relatable sentiment in exam-and-job-obsessed Delhi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert में (men) after व्यर्थ (vyarth) by analogy with फ़िज़ूल में (fizool men), but व्यर्थ (vyarth) alone already functions adverbially and needs no postposition.
