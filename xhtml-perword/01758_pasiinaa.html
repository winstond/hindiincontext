पसीना (pasiinaa)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'sweat/perspiration,' with a very common extended sense in the fixed phrase पसीना आना (pasiinaa aanaa) describing the involuntary act of sweating, and figurative use for hard toil (as in पसीना बहाना (pasiinaa bahaanaa)).
Sense 1 : sweat, perspiration (the bodily fluid)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वेद (sved, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi treats sweating the same way it treats feeling cold or hungry -- as something that happens *to* you (दे रहा, आ रहा) rather than something you actively do, which fits the broader pattern of dative-subject, involuntary-experience verbs like लगना (lagnaa) and भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Sweating is expressed with पसीना आना (pasiinaa aanaa, 'sweat to come') rather than a verb meaning 'to sweat' -- पसीना is the grammatical subject, and the person who is sweating takes को (ko): मुझे पसीना आ रहा है (mujhe pasiinaa aa rahaa hai, 'I am sweating'). Learners often try to force an English-style 'I sweat' construction instead.
Sample Sentences:
गर्मी में मुझे बहुत पसीना आता है। I sweat a lot in the summer.
(garmii men mujhe bahut pasiinaa aataa hai।)

Sense 2 : hard toil, exertion (figurative)
noun, masculine Lives mainly inside set phrases such as पसीना बहाना (pasiinaa bahaanaa, 'to sweat, to toil hard') and पसीना निकलना (pasiinaa nikalnaa, 'to be worked to exhaustion, to break into a sweat from effort or fear').
Synonyms: मेहनत (mehnat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English says 'blood, sweat, and tears,' Hindi reaches for the same image: किसी चीज़ के लिए पसीना बहाना (kisii chiiz ke lie pasiinaa bahaanaa, 'to sweat for something') is the everyday way to praise someone's hard-earned success, whether it is a student before exams or a laborer on a construction site.
Common Mistakes: 1) पसीना बहाना (pasiinaa bahaanaa) is about effort and labor, not literal sweat production -- a learner might undertranslate it as simply 'to sweat' and miss that it usually means 'worked hard for something,' close to English 'broke a sweat over it.'
