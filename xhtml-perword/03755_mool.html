मूल (mool)

adjective/noun Covers the noun sense of 'root/origin/source/capital' and the adjective sense of 'original/basic/root-'; both senses share the same conceptual core of something foundational that other things derive from.
Sense 1 : original, basic, root (as a modifier)
adjective
Synonyms: असली (aslii), आधारभूत (aadhaarbhoot), बुनियादी (buniyaadii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll meet this constantly in civic and legal contexts -- मूल अधिकार (mool adhikaar, 'fundamental rights') is straight out of the Indian Constitution and shows up in school textbooks and news alike. It shares its root with the noun sense of मूल (mool) below -- both point back to the idea of a 'source' or 'foundation'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for असली (aslii) when they mean 'genuine/real', but मूल (mool) specifically points to the 'source' or 'starting point' version of something -- e.g. मूल पाठ (mool paath, 'the original text') versus a translation or copy, not fake versus real. 2) मूल (mool) is commonly used as a prefix-like modifier in compounds such as मूल अधिकार (mool adhikaar, 'fundamental rights') and मूल कारण (mool kaaran, 'root cause') -- learners sometimes miss these fixed collocations and substitute a synonym that doesn't fit the set phrase.

Sense 2 : root, origin, source
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जड़ (jad), स्रोत (srot), उद्गम (udgam, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of मूल (mool) as the word Hindi reaches for whenever something needs to be traced back to its source -- a word's etymology, a dispute's root cause, or a loan's principal amount at the bank. It's a common building block in compounds like मूल्य (mooly, 'value/price') and मूलतः (mooltah, 'basically/originally'), so recognizing it helps you decode a whole family of related words.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to जड़ (jad) for every 'root', but मूल (mool) is preferred for abstract origins (a problem's root, a word's origin) while जड़ (jad) leans toward the literal, physical root of a plant. 2) In financial contexts मूल (mool) means 'principal' (as opposed to interest) -- e.g. मूल और ब्याज (mool aur byaaj, 'principal and interest') -- a sense learners often don't expect from the everyday 'origin' meaning.
