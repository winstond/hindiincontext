रोगी (rogii)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning 'patient, sick person'; it also works as an adjective meaning 'diseased/sickly,' describing a person or thing prone to or afflicted with illness.
Sense 1 : a sick person, patient
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: बीमार (biimaar), मरीज़ (mariiz)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear मरीज़ (mariiz) far more often at an actual clinic or hospital counter, while रोगी (rogii) has a slightly more formal or written feel, the kind of word you might see on a hospital sign like रोगी विभाग (rogii vibhaag, 'patient department') or hear from a doctor speaking somewhat formally.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बीमार (biimaar) as a noun ('the sick person'), but बीमार (biimaar) is mainly used as an adjective ('sick, ill'); मरीज़ (mariiz) and रोगी (rogii) are the natural nouns for 'patient,' with मरीज़ (mariiz) being the more everyday, hospital-register word. 2) रोगी (rogii) does not change form for gender in the singular oblique-looking way some -ई nouns do -- it stays रोगी (rogii) for both a male and a female patient in casual speech, though careful writing may mark agreement elsewhere in the sentence.

(is asptaal men har din saikdon rogii aate hain।)

Sense 2 : diseased, sickly, prone to illness
adjective
Synonyms: बीमार (biimaar), अस्वस्थ (asvasth, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use extends beyond people -- you might describe a रोगी मन (rogii man, 'a diseased/troubled mind') or a रोगी समाज (rogii samaaj, 'a sick society') in more reflective or literary speech, giving रोगी (rogii) a slightly broader metaphorical reach than the plain-spoken बीमार (biimaar).
Sample Sentences:
यह पेड़ रोगी लग रहा है, इसकी पत्तियाँ पीली पड़ गई हैं। This tree looks diseased, its leaves have turned yellow.
(yah ped rogii lag rahaa hai, iskii pattiyaan piilii pad gaii hain।)
