निकलना (nikalnaa)

verb An essential intransitive verb built around the core idea of 'coming/going out or forth'; it stretches from literal exit (leaving a place) to emergence (sun, results, tears) to turning-out-to-be (a fact revealed) and several idiomatic extensions.
Sense 1 : to exit, leave, go/come out (of a place)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: बाहर जाना (baahar jaanaa), रवाना होना (ravaanaa honaa, departing on a journey)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb Delhi folks use constantly for daily comings and goings -- चलो, निकलते हैं (chalo, nikalte hain, 'come on, let's head out') is an extremely common way to signal it's time to leave a gathering, more casual and immediate-sounding than जाना (jaanaa) alone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बाहर जाना (baahar jaanaa) by default, but निकलना (nikalnaa) is the more natural everyday choice for 'heading out' of the house, e.g. मैं घर से निकल रहा हूँ (main ghar se nikal rahaa hoon, 'I'm leaving the house'). 2) The place left is marked with से (se), not से before which learners sometimes insert को (ko) by analogy with destinations -- घर से निकलना (ghar se nikalnaa), not घर को निकलना.
Sample Sentences:
चलो, निकलते हैं, बहुत देर हो गई है। Come on, let's head out, it's gotten really late.
(chalo, nikalte hain, bahut der ho gaii hai।)

Sense 2 : to emerge, appear, come out (sun, tooth, plant, result, etc.)
verb, intransitive Used with a wide range of natural/bodily/abstract subjects: सूरज निकलना (sooraj nikalnaa, 'the sun to come out'), दाँत निकलना (daant nikalnaa, 'a tooth to come in'), परिणाम निकलना (parinaam nikalnaa, 'a result to come out').
Synonyms: प्रकट होना (prakat honaa, more formal), उगना (ugnaa, specifically for plants/sun rising)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense captures a very Hindi way of framing emergence as something that happens on its own, almost independent of an agent -- आँसू निकल आए (aansoo nikal aae, 'tears welled up') treats the tears as the actor, not 'me crying', which echoes the involuntary-verb pattern seen elsewhere in the language (compare याद आना, yaad aanaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'the results came out' too literally with आना (aanaa) when निकलना (nikalnaa) is the natural verb for results, rashes, tears, sun, etc. emerging -- परिणाम निकल गया (parinaam nikal gayaa, 'the result came out').
Sample Sentences:
सुबह सूरज निकलता है। The sun rises in the morning.
(subah sooraj nikaltaa hai।)

Sense 3 : to turn out to be, to prove to be (on discovery)
verb, intransitive Common frame: [noun/adjective] निकलना (nikalnaa) -- वह झूठा निकला (vah jhoothaa niklaa, 'he turned out to be a liar').
Synonyms: साबित होना (saabit honaa, more formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense is beloved in storytelling and gossip -- वह चोर निकला (vah chor niklaa, 'he turned out to be a thief') has that satisfying 'plot twist' flavor Hindi speakers reach for when recounting how someone was unmasked.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to होना (honaa) for 'turned out to be', missing the nuance that निकलना (nikalnaa) specifically implies a discovery or revelation after the fact, not a simple state -- यह नकली निकला (yah naklii niklaa, 'this turned out to be fake', implies it was initially believed genuine).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 4 : to be removed, extracted, or pulled out
verb, intransitive Functions as the intransitive/inchoative counterpart to निकालना (nikaalnaa, 'to take out'): काँटा निकल गया (kaantaa nikal gayaa, 'the thorn came out').
Synonyms: निकाला जाना (nikaalaa jaanaa, explicit passive form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pairing shows up everywhere in daily life -- from stain removal to pulling a tooth to money coming out of an ATM (पैसे निकल आए, paise nikal aae) -- and mastering the निकलना (nikalnaa)/निकालना (nikaalnaa) pair is a genuine milestone in learning Hindi verb pairs.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is one of the classic transitive/intransitive pairs (निकलना (nikalnaa) intransitive vs. निकालना (nikaalnaa) transitive) -- learners who know ने-marking rules should note this pair explicitly, since mixing them up is a very frequent error: दाग निकल गया (daag nikal gayaa, 'the stain came out') vs. मैंने दाग निकाला (mainne daag nikaalaa, 'I removed the stain').
Sample Sentences:
यह दाग़ नहीं निकल रहा है। This stain is not coming out.

Sense 5 : to set out, depart (on a journey or errand)
verb, intransitive Often paired with a destination via के लिए (ke lie): हम दिल्ली के लिए निकल रहे हैं (ham dillii ke lie nikal rahe hain, 'we're setting off for Delhi').
Synonyms: रवाना होना (ravaanaa honaa), चल पड़ना (chal padnaa, compound verb, more sudden/decisive)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Very practical travel-planning language -- सुबह जल्दी निकलना (subah jaldii nikalnaa, 'to set out early in the morning') is the standard way Indians talk about beating traffic or heat on a road trip.
