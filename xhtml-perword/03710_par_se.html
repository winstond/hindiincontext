पर से (par se)

postposition A compound postposition combining पर (par, 'on') and से (se, 'from'), meaning 'from on/off (a surface or position)' -- used when something moves off or away from a raised or elevated location.
Sense 1 : from on top of, off of
postposition Marks the noun as the elevated surface or position from which motion away or downward originates, typically with verbs of falling, jumping, or getting down: X पर से गिरना/कूदना/उतरना.
Synonyms: से नीचे (se niiche, less common, emphasizes downward direction)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of पर (par) as anchoring 'on' a surface and से (se) as pulling the motion 'away from' it -- so घोड़े पर से गिरना (ghode par se girnaa, 'to fall off a horse') literally tracks the fall as leaving the horse's back. This combination is very common with vehicles, animals, furniture, and walls -- anywhere you can be perched 'on' something before coming off it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use just से (se) alone, but when the starting point is a surface or elevated position (a horse, a wall, a roof), पर से (par se) is the natural choice -- से (se) alone would sound like the thing is a mere source or origin rather than something one was on. 2) Do not confuse with से पहले (se pahle, 'before') -- पर से (par se) is purely spatial/positional, not temporal.
