जीवन (jiivan)

noun A single core meaning -- 'life' -- used both literally (a person's lifespan/existence) and in the broader philosophical/reflective sense of 'life' as a concept. No split by grammar here; the richness is in register and collocation, covered below.
Sense 1 : life (existence, lifespan, the course of a person's life)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ज़िंदगी (zindgii), हयात (hayaat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: जीवन (jiivan) and ज़िंदगी (zindgii) are near-synonyms but have a slightly different feel: ज़िंदगी (zindgii) is the word for the lived, day-to-day, emotional texture of life (song lyrics, casual chat), while जीवन (jiivan) leans toward life as a subject of reflection, discussed in school, in advice from elders, or in spiritual and philosophical contexts. You will hear it constantly in phrases about how to live well -- जीवन जीना (jiivan jiinaa, 'to live life'), जीवन का उद्देश्य (jiivan kaa uddeshy, 'the purpose of life').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for ज़िंदगी (zindgii) since it comes up more in casual speech; जीवन (jiivan) is just as common but leans slightly more reflective/written, so it shows up heavily in advice, philosophy, school essays, and formal speech -- worth building active use of both rather than defaulting to one. 2) जीवन (jiivan) is grammatically masculine, so possessives and adjectives agree accordingly: मेरा जीवन (meraa jiivan, my life), not मेरी.
Sample Sentences:
इंसान को अपना जीवन ईमानदारी से जीना चाहिए। A person should live their life with honesty.
(insaan ko apnaa jiivan iimaandaarii se jiinaa chaahie।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Subjunctive -- Voice: Active
जीवन का उद्देश्य क्या है, यह सवाल हर धर्म में पूछा गया है। What is the purpose of life -- this question has been asked in every religion.
(jiivan kaa uddeshy kyaa hai, yah savaal har dharm men poochhaa gayaa hai।)
Register: Literary -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Passive
