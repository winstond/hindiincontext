प्रभाव (prabhaav)

noun A single core idea -- 'influence/effect' -- used both concretely (an effect on health, on a situation) and abstractly (one person's or thing's influence over another). One noun sense covers both, since English also uses 'effect' and 'influence' for the same underlying concept.
Sense 1 : influence, effect, impact
noun, masculine Commonly used with पड़ना (padnaa) -- 'X का प्रभाव पड़ना (X kaa prabhaav padnaa, 'X's effect falls/lands') -- to say something has an effect on someone or something.
Synonyms: असर (asar), प्रभुत्व (prabhutv, power/dominance, narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear प्रभाव (prabhaav) constantly in the context of social or political influence -- someone's प्रभाव (prabhaav) in their community, their रसूख़ (rasookh)-like clout, or a parent's influence on a child's upbringing. It carries a slightly more weighty, formal register than असर (asar), so news anchors and essay-writers reach for it more than someone chatting over chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for असर (asar) in every context since it is more common in casual speech; प्रभाव (prabhaav) is the word of choice in slightly more formal or written contexts (news, essays, official speech) though the two overlap heavily. 2) The verb that pairs naturally with प्रभाव (prabhaav) is पड़ना (padnaa) (something's influence 'falls' on you) rather than होना (honaa) alone, e.g. उस पर प्रभाव पड़ा (us par prabhaav padaa, 'it had an effect on him'), not a literal 'effect happened' construction.
Sample Sentences:
इस दवा का प्रभाव कुछ घंटों तक रहता है। The effect of this medicine lasts for a few hours.
(is davaa kaa prabhaav kuchh ghanton tak rahtaa hai।)
