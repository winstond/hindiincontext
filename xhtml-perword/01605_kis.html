किस (kis)

pronoun/adjective The oblique form of कौन (kaun, 'who/which') used before postpositions -- appears in questions like किसने (kisne, 'who'), किसको (kisko, 'to whom'), and किस से (kis se, 'from whom/what').
Sense 1 : interrogative pronoun (oblique 'who/what/which')
pronoun This is the oblique form of कौन (kaun); it never stands alone and is always followed by a postposition (को, से, में, पर, etc.) or, when modifying a noun directly, functions as an interrogative adjective (see next sense).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this constantly in everyday questions like किससे बात कर रहे हो? (kisse baat kar rahe ho, 'who are you talking to?') -- Hindi puts the postposition right after किस (kis) rather than stranding it at the end the way English does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use कौन (kaun) before a postposition -- but the oblique किस (kis) is required: किससे (kisse, 'from whom'), not कौनसे (kaunse) in that sense. 2) किसने (kisne) is the fused form for 'who' as an ने-marked subject -- learners sometimes write कौनने (kaunne), which is wrong.

Sense 2 : interrogative adjective ('which')
adjective Used directly before a noun to ask 'which' when the noun phrase itself takes a postposition, e.g. किस शहर में (kis shahar men, 'in which city'), किस लड़की को (kis ladkii ko, 'to which girl').
Synonyms: कौन सा (kaun saa, used when not immediately followed by a postposition)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of किस (kis) as the 'plain' oblique version of कौन सा (kaun saa) -- reach for it whenever a postposition is coming right after the noun, which is most of the time in real conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to कौन सा (kaun saa) even when a postposition follows the noun -- native speakers prefer किस + noun + postposition, e.g. किस किताब में (kis kitaab men, 'in which book'), not कौन सी किताब में (kaun sii kitaab men).
