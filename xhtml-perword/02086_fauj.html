फ़ौज (fauj)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'army' or 'military force', used both literally and in casual figurative talk about a large crowd of people.
Sense 1 : army, military force
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सेना (senaa), सिपाही (sipaahii, army of soldiers -- related term, not a direct synonym)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: फ़ौज (fauj) is the word you will hear on the street and in family conversation when someone talks about a relative 'in the army' (fauj men hai, 'is in the army'), while सेना (senaa) shows up more in official and news contexts like भारतीय सेना (bhaartiiy senaa, 'Indian Army'). The word comes from Persian/Urdu and carries that everyday, slightly informal warmth in Hindi speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse फ़ौज (fauj) with सेना (senaa) as if one were formal and the other casual -- in practice both are used interchangeably in everyday speech and news, so there is no strict register split to memorize. 2) The nuqta on फ़ (fauj) is often dropped in casual writing as फौज (phauj); both are read and understood the same way.

Sense 2 : a large crowd or horde (figurative)
noun, feminine Used metonymically for any large, overwhelming group, e.g. मच्छरों की फ़ौज (machchhron kii fauj, 'an army of mosquitoes') or बच्चों की फ़ौज (bachchon kii fauj, 'a horde of children').
Synonyms: भीड़ (bhiid, crowd -- close in sense), झुंड (jhund, herd/swarm)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same affectionate exaggeration English speakers make with 'an army of...' -- a Delhi mother might say मेहमानों की पूरी फ़ौज आ गई (mehmaanon kii poorii fauj aa gaii, 'a whole army of guests has arrived') when relatives descend unannounced for a festival.
