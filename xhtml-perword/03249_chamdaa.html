चमड़ा (chamdaa)

noun Mainly means 'leather' (the tanned material used for shoes, bags, belts); by extension also used colloquially and a bit crudely for human/animal 'skin' or 'hide'.
Sense 1 : leather (tanned animal hide used as material)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चर्म (charm, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly at a bazaar when buying जूते (joote, 'shoes') or a बेल्ट (belt, 'belt') -- shopkeepers proudly say असली चमड़ा (aslii chamdaa, 'genuine leather') to distinguish it from रेक्सिन (reksin), the cheap synthetic substitute sold everywhere in Indian markets.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चर्म (charm) in everyday speech; that word is fine in compounds like चर्मकार (charmkaar, 'leatherworker') but sounds bookish as a standalone word for 'leather' -- चमड़ा (chamdaa) is what you'll hear at a shoe shop or belt stall.

Sense 2 : skin or hide (of a person or animal), used bluntly or crudely
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खाल (khaal), त्वचा (tvachaa, neutral/formal for human skin)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in scolding or threatening speech -- an angry parent or boss might growl चमड़ी उतार दूँगा (chamdii utaar doongaa, 'I'll skin you alive'), using the diminutive चमड़ी (chamdii) rather than चमड़ा (chamdaa) itself, but both share the same rough, physical register.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't use चमड़ा (chamdaa) for someone's skin in a neutral or polite context -- it's coarse and can sound insulting or comic, unlike त्वचा (tvachaa) or चेहरे की त्वचा (chehre kii tvachaa, 'facial skin') which are neutral.
