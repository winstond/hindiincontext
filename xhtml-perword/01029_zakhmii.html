ज़ख़मी (zakhmii)

adjective An adjective meaning 'wounded' or 'injured', used for both physical injuries and, in extended use, emotional hurt. Related to the noun ज़ख़्म (zakhm, 'wound'), which is processed separately.
Sense 1 : physically wounded or injured
adjective
Synonyms: घायल (ghaayal), जख़्मी (jakhmii, variant spelling without nuqta)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news reports about accidents or violence: कई लोग ज़ख़मी हो गए (kaii log zakhmii ho gae, 'many people were injured') is standard reporting language. It sits at a slightly more literary/formal register than the everyday घायल (ghaayal), though both are widely used and understood.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say ज़ख़मी होना (zakhmii honaa) but forget that the person affected is the grammatical subject, not marked with को (ko) -- unlike लगना (lagnaa) constructions describing the injury itself, e.g. उसे चोट लगी (use chot lagii, 'he got hurt'). 2) Confusing ज़ख़मी (zakhmii) with the noun ज़ख़्म (zakhm) -- the adjective describes the person or body part, while the noun names the wound itself.
Sample Sentences:
सड़क हादसे में कई लोग ज़ख़मी हो गए। Many people were injured in the road accident.
(sadak haadse men kaii log zakhmii ho gae।)

Sense 2 : emotionally hurt, wounded in feelings
adjective
Synonyms: आहत (aahat, literary), दुखी (dukhii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi frequently borrows the language of physical injury to talk about emotional pain, so दिल ज़ख़मी होना (dil zakhmii honaa, 'the heart to be wounded') is a natural and common way to describe heartbreak, especially in songs and ghazals where love and injury are closely intertwined.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी बेरुख़ी से मेरा दिल ज़ख़मी हो गया। His indifference left my heart wounded.
(uskii berukhii se meraa dil zakhmii ho gayaa।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
