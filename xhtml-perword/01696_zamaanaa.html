ज़माना (zamaanaa)

noun/verb Primarily a noun meaning 'time/era/world (as in the people around you)'; also has a less common verb sense meaning 'to establish or set firmly.' The noun sense is far more common in everyday speech and idiom.
Sense 1 : era, times, the world/society around one
noun, masculine Often used with पुराना (puraanaa) or नया (nayaa) to contrast 'old days' vs. 'modern times,' and frequently personified as if it has opinions or moods, e.g. ज़माना क्या कहेगा (zamaanaa kyaa kahegaa, 'what will people/the world say').
Synonyms: दौर (daur), युग (yug, literary), वक़्त (vaqt, overlaps in 'time period' sense), दुनिया (duniyaa, when meaning 'people/society')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries a wistful, philosophical weight in Hindi-Urdu speech and especially in Bollywood lyrics and shayari, where ज़माना (zamaanaa) is cast almost as a character -- judgmental, changing, indifferent to lovers. You'll hear elders sigh आज का ज़माना और है (aaj kaa zamaanaa aur hai, 'today's world/generation is different') to mark generational change.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate ज़माना (zamaanaa) narrowly as 'time' and miss its second core sense of 'people/society/the world at large' -- as in ज़माना बहुत आगे बढ़ गया है (zamaanaa bahut aage badh gayaa hai, 'the world/society has moved far ahead'), which isn't about clock-time at all. 2) It is masculine, so possessives and adjectives agree accordingly (इस ज़माने में (is zamaane men), not the feminine forms).

Sense 2 : to establish, fix firmly, set up
verb, transitive This is the causative of जमना (jamnaa, 'to set/gel/take hold') -- used for making something set (like curd, jamaanaa doodh se dahii, doodh se dahii jamaanaa, 'to set curd from milk') or metaphorically establishing one's position or influence (अपना असर जमाना (apnaa asar jamaanaa, 'to establish one's influence')).
Synonyms: जमाना (jamaanaa, same word without nuqta, standard causative sense), स्थापित करना (sthaapit karnaa, formal/more literal 'establish')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in kitchen contexts (दही जमाना (dahii jamaanaa, 'to set yogurt')) and in social ones, like जमाना असर (asar jamaanaa, 'making an impression/establishing sway') -- both senses share the core idea of 'making something take hold.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this verb sense with the much more common noun ज़माना (zamaanaa, 'era/world') since they're spelled identically once the nuqta is dropped in casual writing -- context (presence of an object, verb position) disambiguates them. 2) This spelling with the nuqta is less standard for the verb; जमाना (jamaanaa) without the nuqta is the usual way to write this verb sense.
