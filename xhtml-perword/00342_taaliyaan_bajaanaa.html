तालियाँ बजाना (taaliyaan bajaanaa)

verb A fixed verb phrase meaning 'to clap hands, to applaud' -- literally 'to strike/play claps'; ताली (taalii) is the single clap and बजाना (bajaanaa) is the ordinary transitive verb used for playing instruments or making things sound.
Sense 1 : to clap, to applaud (with hands)
verb, transitive The noun ताली (taalii) is almost always used in its plural form तालियाँ (taaliyaan) when talking about the general act of clapping/applauding, though the singular ताली बजाना (taalii bajaanaa) is just as commonly heard.
Synonyms: ताली बजाना (taalii bajaanaa, singular form, equally common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Clapping in India carries extra cultural freight beyond simple applause: at weddings and celebrations, rhythmic ताली बजाना (taalii bajaanaa) accompanies singing and dancing, and in schools children are taught songs with clapping games. It is also the customary way to show appreciation after a speech, performance, or even a good joke in a classroom or office setting, much like in English, but the phrase itself is used far more freely in everyday narration -- 'sab ne taaliyaan bajaayin' shows up constantly in stories about events.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try हाथ बजाना (haath bajaanaa) by literal translation from 'clap hands' -- Hindi always uses ताली/तालियाँ (taalii/taaliyaan), never हाथ (haath), for this action. 2) The object तालियाँ (taaliyaan) takes ने-marking on the subject in the perfective, just like any other transitive verb with बजाना (bajaanaa) -- e.g. उसने तालियाँ बजाईं (usne taaliyaan bajaaiin, 'she clapped'), which surprises learners who think of clapping as a spontaneous, almost intransitive action.
Sample Sentences:
जब गाना खत्म हुआ, तो सबने तालियाँ बजाईं। When the song ended, everyone clapped.
(jab gaanaa khatm huaa, to sabne taaliyaan bajaaiin।)
