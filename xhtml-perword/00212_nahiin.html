नहीं (nahiin)

adverb/particle The basic Hindi negator, meaning 'no' or 'not'; also used at the end of a statement to turn it into a question, like English 'isn't it?'
Sense 1 : negation ('not')
adverb Placed before the verb (or before the element being negated) to negate a statement; in the future tense and with चाहिए (chaahie) it commonly appears before the verb, while in habitual/continuous past constructions it often precedes the auxiliary.
Synonyms: न (n, more formal/literary in this use)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first words every learner absorbs, and Hindi speakers use it constantly as a standalone reply to a yes/no question, much like English 'no.' Notice that Hindi has no single word for 'yes' either -- हाँ (haan) fills that role -- so नहीं (nahiin) and हाँ (haan) form the basic yes/no pair a learner should master together.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often put नहीं (nahiin) right after the subject instead of right before the verb phrase, e.g. saying मैं नहीं जाता हूँ (main nahiin jaataa hoon, 'I do not go') is fine, but placement shifts meaning/emphasis depending on what is being negated -- नहीं (nahiin) sits immediately before whatever it negates. 2) With the future tense, learners sometimes forget that नहीं (nahiin) still precedes the verb rather than the subject: वह नहीं जाएगा (vah nahiin jaaegaa, 'he will not go'), not वह जाएगा नहीं for a neutral negative statement (that order shifts emphasis to 'he will NOT go').

Sense 2 : standalone 'no' (answer to a question)
particle Used alone as a complete utterance in answer to a question, without attaching to a verb.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: As a one-word answer, नहीं (nahiin) can sound a little blunt on its own in polite conversation, so speakers often soften it with जी (jii) -- जी नहीं (jii nahiin, 'no, sir/madam') -- especially when talking to elders or strangers, or repeat it as नहीं नहीं (nahiin nahiin, 'no, no') to sound more emphatic and conversational rather than curt.

Sense 3 : tag-question marker ('isn't it?', 'right?')
particle Attached to the end of a statement to invite agreement or confirmation, functioning like an English tag question.
Synonyms: क्या (kyaa, different construction, sentence-initial), न (n, lighter/softer tag)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This tag-question use is extremely common in spoken Hindi -- tacking on ...है ना? (hai naa) or ...है नहीं? (hai nahiin) at the end of a sentence is the everyday equivalent of English 'right?' or 'isn't it?', and you will hear it constantly in casual Delhi conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this नहीं (nahiin) as negating the sentence it follows, but in this use it does not change the statement's truth value at all -- वह अच्छा है, नहीं? (vah achchhaa hai, nahiin?, 'he is good, isn't he?') remains a positive statement, just seeking confirmation.
