वीर (viir)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'brave, heroic'; the same word is also used as a noun meaning 'a hero, a brave man,' and shows up as a form of address or honorific tag in names and speeches.
Sense 1 : brave, heroic, valiant
adjective
Synonyms: बहादुर (bahaadur), साहसी (saahsii), निडर (nidar, fearless), दिलेर (diler, somewhat literary/formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet वीर (viir) constantly around Republic Day and Independence Day speeches, war memorials, and school textbooks describing freedom fighters -- it carries a formal, almost storybook grandeur that बहादुर (bahaadur) lacks. Calling someone बहादुर (bahaadur) in daily life feels natural; calling them वीर (viir) feels like you are writing their epitaph or introducing them at a ceremony.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बहादुर (bahaadur) by default since it is more common in casual speech; वीर (viir) sits a register higher and is favored in patriotic, historical, or ceremonial contexts (like describing soldiers or freedom fighters) rather than everyday praise of a friend's courage.

Sense 2 : a hero, a brave/valiant man
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बहादुर (bahaadur), योद्धा (yoddhaa, warrior), शूरवीर (shoorviir, compound form, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This noun sense shows up in compounds and titles you will recognize instantly: वीर सावरकर (viir saavarkar) uses वीर (viir) as an honorific prefix much like 'the brave' or 'the valiant' before a name, and परमवीर चक्र (paramviir chakr, India's highest military honor) literally builds on this same root. It signals someone whose courage is being publicly honored, not just a casually brave person.
