बरबाद करना (barbaad karnaa)

verb A compound verb meaning 'to ruin, destroy, waste' -- बरबाद (barbaad, 'ruined, wasted') combines with करना (karnaa, 'to do') to form the transitive action of causing ruin. Also spelled बर्बाद करना (barbaad karnaa).
Sense 1 : to destroy, ruin (a thing, place, life)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: नष्ट करना (nasht karnaa), तबाह करना (tabaah karnaa), चौपट करना (chaupat karnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly about relationships, careers, and health: ज़िंदगी बरबाद कर दी (zindgii barbaad kar dii, 'ruined [my/his/her] life') is a classic dramatic complaint, often aimed at a person blamed for someone's misfortune, from a bad marriage to a bad decision. Bollywood dialogue loves this phrase for exactly that reason.
Common Mistakes: 1) बरबाद (barbaad) itself is an adjective/participle, not a verb -- learners sometimes try to conjugate it directly instead of pairing it with करना (karnaa). 2) Do not confuse with बरबाद होना (barbaad honaa), the intransitive counterpart ('to be ruined') -- करना (karnaa) versions need an agent who does the ruining, marked with ने (ne) in the perfective.
Sample Sentences:
उसने अपने बेटे की ज़िंदगी बरबाद कर दी। She ruined her son's life.
(usne apne bete kii zindgii barbaad kar dii।)

Sense 2 : to waste (time, money, opportunity)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बर्बाद करना (barbaad karnaa, variant spelling), ज़ाया करना (zaayaa karnaa, less common), फ़िज़ूल खर्च करना (fizool kharch karnaa, money-specific)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: समय बरबाद मत करो (samay barbaad mat karo, 'don't waste time') is something every Hindi-speaking parent or teacher says on repeat -- it carries a slightly scolding, urgent tone, more forceful than the neutral समय बचाओ (samay bachaao, 'save time').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for खोना (khonaa, 'to lose') when they mean 'waste' -- वक्त बरबाद करना (vakt barbaad karnaa) is the natural way to say 'to waste time,' not वक्त खोना (vakt khonaa).
