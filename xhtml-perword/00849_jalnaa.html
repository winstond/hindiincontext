जलना (jalnaa)

verb An intransitive verb centered on burning: (1) literal combustion or being on fire, (2) a body part or skin stinging/burning from heat or spice, and (3) a common figurative sense of burning with jealousy or envy. Its transitive causative partner is जलाना (jalaanaa, 'to burn something').
Sense 1 : to burn, be on fire
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: भड़कना (bhadaknaa, to flare up), सुलगना (sulagnaa, to smolder)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around Diwali and everyday cooking talk -- दीया जल रहा है (diiyaa jal rahaa hai, 'the lamp is burning') or गैस जल रही है (gais jal rahii hai, 'the gas [stove] is on'). Note how Hindi treats a lit stove or lamp as 'burning' rather than 'being on,' which feels natural once you get used to it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for जलाना (jalaanaa) when describing a fire that is simply burning on its own -- घर जल रहा है (ghar jal rahaa hai, 'the house is burning') needs जलना (jalnaa), the transitive जलाना (jalaanaa) is only for someone burning something. 2) As an intransitive verb, it never takes ने (ne) in the past tense -- लकड़ी जली (lakdii jalii, 'the wood burned'), not लकड़ी ने जली (lakdii ne jalii).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to sting, feel a burning sensation
verb, intransitive Common with a को (ko)-marked experiencer: आँखों में जलन हो रही है (aankhon men jalan ho rahii hai, 'my eyes are burning/stinging') or मुझे आँख जल रही है (mujhe aankh jal rahii hai, 'my eye is stinging').
Synonyms: चुभना (chubhnaa, to prick/sting), जलन होना (jalan honaa, phrase form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Very alive in kitchen and body-talk: बहुत मिर्च है, मुँह जल रहा है (bahut mirch hai, munh jal rahaa hai, 'it's too spicy, my mouth is burning') is something you'll hear at almost any dinner table with a bit too much chili.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to दर्द होना (dard honaa, 'to hurt') for any discomfort, but a burning/stinging sensation -- from chili, soap, or a wound -- specifically calls for जलना (jalnaa) or the noun phrase जलन होना (jalan honaa).

Sense 3 : to burn with envy or jealousy
verb, intransitive Frequently used with से (se) marking the cause: उसकी तरक्की से जलना (uskii tarakkii se jalnaa, 'to burn [with envy] over his promotion').
Synonyms: कुढ़ना (kudhnaa, to fret/sulk with envy), ईर्ष्या करना (iirshyaa karnaa, more formal phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind the common jibe जलने वाले जलते रहेंगे (jalne vaale jalte rahenge, 'the jealous ones will keep burning'), a phrase people toss around lightly when teasing a friend who is envious of their good news.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense can be missed entirely by learners who only know the literal 'burn' meaning -- वह मुझसे जलता है (vah mujhse jaltaa hai) means 'he is jealous of me,' not that he is literally on fire because of you.
