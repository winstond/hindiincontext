भयंकर (bhayankar)

adjective An adjective meaning 'terrible, dreadful, ferocious' -- used for genuinely frightening or intense things -- and by extension, in casual speech, as an intensifier for anything extreme, good or bad.
Sense 1 : dreadful, terrifying, ferocious
adjective
Synonyms: डरावना (daraavnaa), खतरनाक (khatarnaak), भीषण (bhiishan, formal), खौफनाक (khauphnaak, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for a truly menacing storm (toofaan), accident (durghatnaa), or disease (biimaarii) -- something that genuinely threatens life or safety, not just something spooky. You will hear it a lot in news reports: भयंकर बारिश (bhayankar baarish, severe rain), भयंकर आग (bhayankar aag, massive fire).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for डरावना (daraavnaa) when भयंकर (bhayankar) is meant -- डरावना (daraavnaa) leans toward 'scary in appearance' (a horror movie, a mask), while भयंकर (bhayankar) suits large-scale or violent danger like a storm, accident, or disease.

Sense 2 : extreme, intense (as a colloquial intensifier)
adjective In this sense भयंकर (bhayankar) functions almost like an adverbial intensifier before another adjective, e.g. भयंकर गर्मी (bhayankar garmii, 'terrible/intense heat'), rather than describing something frightening in itself.
Synonyms: जबरदस्त (jabardast, colloquial), बहुत ज़्यादा (bahut zyaadaa), भारी (bhaarii, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Just like English speakers say 'terribly cold' or 'a monster of a headache,' Delhi folks throw भयंकर (bhayankar) in front of all kinds of things -- भयंकर भूख (bhayankar bhookh, 'terrible hunger'), भयंकर ट्रैफिक (bhayankar traiphik, 'awful traffic') -- as a everyday way to dial up the intensity, without any real sense of danger involved.
Common Mistakes: 1) This casual, exaggerated use ('भयंकर ठंड है' bhayankar thand hai, 'it's brutally cold') is very common in spoken Hindi but can sound overly dramatic in formal writing -- reserve it for informal speech.
