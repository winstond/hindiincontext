अरे (are)

interjection A very common interjection used to call attention, express surprise, mild annoyance, or affectionate familiarity, depending on tone.
Sense 1 : expression of surprise or realization
interjection
Synonyms: अरे वाह (are vaah, surprised/pleased), ओहो (oho)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sound of genuine, unfiltered reaction in Hindi -- you will hear it constantly on the street, in kitchens, in offices, the moment someone notices something unexpected: अरे, ये तो बहुत सस्ता है! (are, ye to bahut sastaa hai, 'oh, this is really cheap!'). The exact same word can carry delight, shock, or dismay; the emotion rides entirely on tone, not on the word itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often flatten अरे (are) to a single all-purpose 'oh', missing that the same word can shift from delighted surprise to sharp annoyance depending on tone and context -- listen for pitch and what follows. 2) It is not rude by default; treating it as always brusque or always casual-friendly leads to misjudging the speaker's actual mood.

Sense 2 : attention-getting call, address to a person
interjection
Synonyms: ऐ (ai, sharper/less warm), सुनो (suno, meaning 'listen', not a direct synonym but often used similarly)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic way Indians hail someone across a room, a street, or a dinner table -- अरे भाई, इधर आओ! (are bhaaii, idhar aao, 'hey brother, come here!'). Pairing it with भाई (bhaaii), यार (yaar), or a name softens it into warm familiarity, which is exactly why it feels so at home between friends and family but can jar when aimed at someone you should be formal with.
Common Mistakes: 1) Using अरे (are) to address someone senior or a stranger with respect is a real misstep -- it is fine with peers, juniors, or people you know well, but can sound presumptuous or rude toward an elder, a boss, or someone you'd otherwise address with आप (aap). 2) Learners sometimes treat it as a name-substitute; it is a call for attention, not a term of address like 'sir' or 'ji'.

Sense 3 : mild protest, exasperation, or dismissal
interjection
Synonyms: अरे यार (are yaar, softer, exasperated-but-affectionate), छोड़ो (chhodo, 'leave it', related dismissive move)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the अरे (are) of a mother swatting away a child's excuse, or a friend brushing off over-thanks -- अरे, कोई बात नहीं! (are, koii baat nahiin, 'oh come on, it's nothing!'). It carries a light, deflecting energy rather than real anger, which is part of why the same syllable can sound so different across these three senses.
Common Mistakes: 1) This exasperated use overlaps heavily with the surprise sense, and learners often cannot tell them apart from the transcript alone -- in real speech the clipped, slightly drawn-out tone and a following correction or complaint (अरे नहीं, ऐसे नहीं! (are nahiin, aise nahiin, 'no no, not like that!')) mark this sense.
Sample Sentences:
अरे, कोई बात नहीं! Oh come on, it's nothing!
