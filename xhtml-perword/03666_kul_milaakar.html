कुल मिलाकर (kul milaakar)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'overall, all things considered' -- literally 'having mixed/joined the total.' Single sense.
Sense 1 : overall, all things considered
adverb Functions as a sentence-opening or clause-opening adverb summarizing what follows or was just said; it does not connect to a preceding clause the way a conjunction would, so it can freely start a sentence on its own.
Synonyms: कुल मिलाकर के (kul milaakar ke, colloquial variant), सब मिलाकर (sab milaakar), संक्षेप में (sankshep men, 'in short')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Very common in spoken Hindi when someone is wrapping up an opinion or a story, much like an English speaker saying 'all in all' or 'bottom line.' You will hear it constantly on news debate shows and in casual conversation alike, e.g. someone summing up a movie review or a day's events with कुल मिलाकर, फिल्म अच्छी थी (kul milaakar, philm achchhii thii, 'overall, the movie was good').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a conjunction linking two clauses (like 'however' or 'so'), but it does not join anything grammatically -- it simply frames a summary judgment, so it can stand alone at the start of a sentence. 2) Overusing it as a direct translation of 'in total' in a literal counting sense (e.g. adding up a bill) is unnatural; for that, जोड़कर (jodkar) or कुल (kul) alone fits better.
Sample Sentences:
कुल मिलाकर, फिल्म अच्छी थी। Overall, the movie was good.
(kul milaakar, philm achchhii thii।)
