कुर्सी (kursii)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'chair,' with a widely used figurative extension meaning 'position/seat of power,' especially in political contexts.
Sense 1 : chair (furniture)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in daily life -- from a shopkeeper offering कुर्सी पर बैठिए (kursii par baithie, 'please sit on the chair') to a host arranging seating for guests. It is one of the first pieces of furniture vocabulary every learner picks up.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget कुर्सी (kursii) is feminine, leading to wrong adjective/verb agreement, e.g. saying यह कुर्सी अच्छा है (yah kursii achchhaa hai) instead of यह कुर्सी अच्छी है (yah kursii achchhii hai, 'this chair is good').

Sense 2 : position of power, seat/office (especially political)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पद (pad, focuses on official rank rather than power itself), गद्दी (gaddii, more traditional/regal image of a throne or seat of authority)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear on the news constantly: कुर्सी बचाना (kursii bachaanaa, 'to save one's seat/position') or कुर्सी की लड़ाई (kursii kii ladaaii, 'a fight for power') describe politicians clinging to office. The chair becomes a metonym for the power itself, much like 'the throne' or 'the seat' in English political commentary.
