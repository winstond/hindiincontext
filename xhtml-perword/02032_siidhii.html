सीढ़ी (siidhii)

noun A concrete noun meaning a staircase, stairs, or a ladder -- one physical object covering both meanings, the way English splits them into two words.
Sense 1 : staircase, stairs, steps
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जीना (jiinaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Most Delhi flats and older houses have narrow, steep सीढ़ियाँ (siidhiyaan) leading up to the छत (chhat, 'rooftop'), where families gather in the evenings -- so this word carries a warm, everyday domestic feel, not just an architectural one.
Common Mistakes: 1) सीढ़ी (siidhii) is often used with चढ़ना (chadhnaa, 'to climb') and उतरना (utarnaa, 'to descend') -- learners sometimes reach for ऊपर जाना (oopar jaanaa) instead, which is understandable but less idiomatic when stairs are specifically involved. 2) In the plural sense ('the stairs' as a structure), speakers often still say सीढ़ी (siidhii) in singular or use सीढ़ियाँ (siidhiyaan) interchangeably depending on whether they mean the staircase as a whole or the individual steps.
Sample Sentences:
छत पर जाने के लिए सीढ़ियाँ चढ़नी पड़ती हैं। You have to climb the stairs to get to the rooftop.
(chhat par jaane ke lie siidhiyaan chadhnii padtii hain।)

Sense 2 : ladder
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The same word covers a portable ladder -- the kind an electrician or painter carries -- so context (a fixed structure versus a movable object) tells you which meaning is intended, much like English 'stairs' versus 'ladder' but unified in Hindi.

Sense 3 : a step or means toward advancement (figurative)
noun, feminine Used with सफलता की सीढ़ी (saphaltaa kii siidhii, 'the ladder of success') and similar genitive constructions to frame progress as climbing.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English says 'climbing the ladder of success,' Hindi uses सीढ़ी (siidhii) the same way -- तरक्की की सीढ़ी चढ़ना (tarakkii kii siidhii chadhnaa, 'to climb the ladder of progress') is a common motivational phrase heard from parents and teachers alike.
