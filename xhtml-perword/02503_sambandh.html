संबंध (sambandh)

noun A formal-leaning word for 'relationship' or 'connection' -- covers personal/family ties, abstract logical connections, and (in its plural-ish use) romantic or physical relations. Related to the more colloquial रिश्ता (rishtaa).
Sense 1 : relationship (personal/social)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रिश्ता (rishtaa), नाता (naataa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in news broadcasts and official speech -- 'भारत-पाकिस्तान संबंध' (bhaarat-paakistaan sambandh, 'India-Pakistan relations') is a stock phrase. In daily family chat, though, people lean on रिश्ता (rishtaa) instead, which feels warmer and more personal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for रिश्ता (rishtaa) in speech and संबंध (sambandh) in writing or formal contexts; both are correct, but संबंध (sambandh) sounds noticeably more formal in casual conversation. 2) The relationship between two people is usually phrased as X से संबंध (X se sambandh) -- 'a relationship with X' -- using से (se), not को (ko) or का (kaa) alone.

Sense 2 : connection, correlation (abstract/logical)
noun, masculine Used for abstract links between ideas, events, or facts, often in constructions like इस बात का उस बात से संबंध है (is baat kaa us baat se sambandh hai, 'this matter is connected to that matter').
Synonyms: जुड़ाव (judaav), लगाव (lagaav, emotional connection, not identical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will meet in academic or analytical writing -- a teacher explaining that two historical events have a संबंध (sambandh), or that cause and effect are संबंधित (sambandhit, 'related/connected') to each other.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : romantic or physical relations
noun, masculine Frequently appears as शारीरिक संबंध (shaariirik sambandh, 'physical relations') or संबंध बनाना (sambandh banaanaa, 'to have relations') -- a euphemistic register for sex or an affair, common in news reports and legal language.
Synonyms: रिश्ता (rishtaa), नाजायज़ संबंध (naajaayaz sambandh, illicit relationship, specific phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Indian newspapers reporting on scandals or court cases will almost always say 'अवैध संबंध' (avaidh sambandh, 'illicit relationship') rather than anything more explicit -- this euphemistic register is exactly why the word is so common in that context.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is often used euphemistically -- native speakers reach for संबंध (sambandh) precisely because it is less blunt than more direct words, so it shows up a lot in news and courtroom contexts rather than casual talk.
