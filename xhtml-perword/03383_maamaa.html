मामा (maamaa)

noun Refers to a mother's brother -- one word in the Hindi kinship system that keeps this relationship distinct from a father's brother; also has a common extended/idiomatic use for a sly or manipulative man.
Sense 1 : maternal uncle
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Hindi-speaking families, the मामा (maamaa) side of the family is treated with special warmth -- especially at weddings, where the maternal uncle has specific ceremonial roles like carrying the bride's palanquin (डोली, dolii) or gifting during rituals. Kids often use मामा (maamaa) affectionately for their mother's brother, and it doubles as a respectful way to address an older stranger, similar to how 'uncle' is used loosely in Indian English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for 'uncle' generically, but Hindi kinship terms are specific: मामा (maamaa) is only the mother's brother, not the father's brother (चाचा, chaachaa) or an uncle by marriage. 2) The wife of मामा (maamaa) is मामी (maamii), not to be confused with मामा (maamaa) itself.
Sample Sentences:

(mere maamaa dillii men rahte hain।)

Sense 2 : sly or manipulative man (slang)
noun, masculine Used almost exclusively in casual, spoken registers, often in phrases like बड़ा मामा बनता है (badaa maamaa bantaa hai, 'he acts like a big shot/con man').
Synonyms: उल्लू का पट्ठा (ulloo kaa patthaa, idiomatic insult, different sense), बदमाश (badmaash, scoundrel)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a colloquial extension of the kinship term, likely playing on the idea of a mama's boy or someone who throws his weight around using connections -- you will hear young men in Delhi use it teasingly among friends, as in वो तो पूरा मामा है (vo to pooraa maamaa hai, 'that guy's a real operator').
Common Mistakes: 1) This slang sense can sound rude or mocking if used with someone the speaker doesn't know well -- it is very informal and context-dependent, unlike the neutral kinship sense.
Sample Sentences:

(vo to pooraa maamaa hai, kisii par bharosaa mat kar।)
