बाँध (baandh)

noun/verb Primarily a noun meaning 'dam' or 'embankment'; it also appears as the stem/imperative form of the verb बाँधना (baandhnaa, 'to tie/bind'), which learners will meet constantly in its own conjugated forms.
Sense 1 : dam, embankment
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बंधान (bandhaan, rare), जलबंध (jalbandh, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Big dams like भाखड़ा बाँध (bhaakhdaa baandh, 'the Bhakra Dam') are common reference points in school geography and news, so this word shows up early for anyone reading about Indian rivers and irrigation projects.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse बाँध (baandh) with बंद (band, 'closed/off') because of the similar sound -- the nasal vowel ('ँ') on बाँध (baandh) is the key distinguishing mark.
Sample Sentences:
भाखड़ा बाँध सतलुज नदी पर बना है। The Bhakra Dam is built on the Sutlej river.
(bhaakhdaa baandh satluj nadii par banaa hai।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 2 : imperative/stem form: tie, bind, fasten
verb, transitive This is the bare stem of बाँधना (baandhnaa), used directly as the intimate/familiar imperative ('tie it!') and as the base onto which all other endings attach.
Synonyms: बाँधना (baandhnaa, full infinitive form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this stem constantly in everyday commands like पगड़ी बाँध (pagdii baandh, 'tie the turban') or बाल बाँध (baal baandh, 'tie up your hair'), said casually between family members.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often expect this bare form to be a full independent verb sense distinct from बाँधना (baandhnaa) -- it is not a separate lexical item, just the stem, so its meaning and usage are identical to that of the parent verb.
Sample Sentences:
अपनी पगड़ी बाँध। Tie your turban.
(apnii pagdii baandh।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

(jaraa apne baal baandh le, bahut bikhre hue hain।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active
