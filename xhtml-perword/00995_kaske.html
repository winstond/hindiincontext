कसके (kaske)

adverb This is the adverbial/absolutive form built from the verb कसना (kasnaa, 'to tighten') -- it works as an adverb meaning 'tightly, firmly' and also shows up colloquially as an intensifier meaning 'a lot, really hard.'
Sense 1 : tightly, firmly
adverb Formed from the conjunctive participle of कसना (kasnaa) plus के (ke), it functions as a manner adverb describing how something is held, tied, or gripped.
Synonyms: मज़बूती से (mazbootii se), जोर से (jor se)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in physical, hands-on contexts: a mother telling a child पकड़ो कसके (pakdo kaske, 'hold on tight') on a scooter, or someone tightening a knot or a lid. It paints a vivid image of gripping something firmly, almost like the English 'hold on for dear life.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to conjugate कसके (kaske) like a verb -- it is frozen as an adverb and does not change for tense, gender, or number. 2) Don't confuse this with कसकर (kaskar), which means exactly the same thing and is just as common; the two are freely interchangeable.

(skootar par baitho to mujhe kaske pakadnaa।)

Sense 2 : very hard, a lot (intensifier)
adverb Used colloquially to intensify a verb, especially with feelings or reactions, e.g. कसके डाँट पड़ी (kaske daant padii, 'got scolded hard') -- the literal 'tightly' sense has been stretched into a general intensifier.
Synonyms: बहुत (bahut), ज़ोरदार तरीके से (zordaar tariike se, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi college-and-street slang -- कसके मज़ा आया (kaske mazaa aayaa, 'had a blast') or कसके पढ़ना (kaske padhnaa, 'study hard') both lean on this punchy, informal intensifier feel, similar to how English speakers use 'hardcore' or 'seriously' loosely.
Common Mistakes: 1) This use is casual/spoken register -- in formal writing, stick to बहुत (bahut) or ज़ोर से (zor se) instead. 2) Learners may not realize this figurative, intensifying sense exists at all and only know the literal 'tightly' meaning.
