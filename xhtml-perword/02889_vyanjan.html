व्यंजन (vyanjan)

noun Two main senses: a savory dish/culinary preparation (the everyday food sense), and a consonant in grammar/phonetics (the technical linguistic sense).
Sense 1 : a cooked dish, a food preparation
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पकवान (pakvaan), खाना (khaanaa, everyday, broader), भोजन (bhojan, formal, broader)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this word on restaurant menus and in phrases like स्वादिष्ट व्यंजन (svaadisht vyanjan, 'tasty dish') or क्षेत्रीय व्यंजन (kshetriiy vyanjan, 'regional dishes'), especially in food TV shows and articles about regional cuisines -- it has a slightly formal, descriptive flavor compared to the everyday खाना (khaanaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use खाना (khaanaa) for 'food' in general and reach for व्यंजन (vyanjan) too broadly; व्यंजन (vyanjan) is better reserved for a specific named dish or preparation (like 'a South Indian dish' or 'a festive dish'), not food in general. 2) It sounds slightly more formal/menu-like than खाना (khaanaa), so using it in casual chat about 'what's for dinner' can feel a bit stiff.

Sense 2 : a consonant (in grammar/phonetics)
noun, masculine Used in contrast with स्वर (svar, 'vowel') when discussing the Devanagari alphabet or Hindi phonetics.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Every Hindi student meets this word early on, in the pairing स्वर और व्यंजन (svar aur vyanjan, 'vowels and consonants') when learning the Devanagari script -- it's the standard grammar-class term, showing up in schoolbooks rather than everyday talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a classroom/grammar term, not something you'd use in casual conversation -- don't confuse it with the food sense, which is far more common day-to-day.
