मोल (mol)

noun/verb Primarily a noun meaning 'price/cost/value', but it lives mainly in a set of common idiomatic phrases (मोल लेना (mol lenaa), मोल-भाव (mol-bhaav), मुँह-माँगा मोल (munh-maangaa mol)) rather than in plain standalone use.
Sense 1 : price, cost
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कीमत (kiimat), दाम (daam), भाव (bhaav)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear मोल-भाव (mol-bhaav) constantly at a Delhi sabzi mandi or Sunday bazaar -- it's the whole ritual of haggling, not just asking the price once. A shopkeeper who won't budge might say यह मुँह-माँगा मोल है (yah munh-maangaa mol hai, 'this is exactly the asked price'), implying no further bargaining is possible.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कीमत (kiimat) by default and forget मोल (mol) is equally common in spoken bargaining contexts, especially in the fixed phrase मोल-भाव (mol-bhaav, 'haggling/bargaining'). 2) मोल (mol) itself does not inflect for plural in the way some nouns do when used abstractly for 'price' -- this is covered further in the morphology table.

Sense 2 : value, worth (figurative)
noun, masculine Used abstractly for the worth of a person, relationship, effort, or time, distinct from a literal shopping price.
Synonyms: कीमत (kiimat), महत्व (mahatv)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the मोल (mol) you hear in advice from elders: रिश्तों का मोल समझो (rishton kaa mol samjho, 'understand the worth of relationships'). It carries a slightly reflective, almost moralizing tone, common in the kind of gentle scolding a Hindi-speaking parent or grandparent gives.
Common Mistakes: 1) In sentences like समय का मोल (samay kaa mol, 'the value of time'), learners sometimes substitute दाम (daam), which sounds odd here -- दाम (daam) stays tied to literal monetary cost, while मोल (mol) extends naturally to abstract worth.

Sense 3 : to buy, to purchase (in the fixed expression मोल लेना)
verb, transitive Occurs almost exclusively as the compound मोल लेना (mol lenaa); मोल (mol) itself is the noun 'price' here, paired with लेना (lenaa, 'to take') to mean 'to buy'.
Synonyms: खरीदना (khariidnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: झगड़ा मोल लेना (jhagdaa mol lenaa, 'to buy a quarrel') is a classic scolding line -- कोई क्यों झगड़ा मोल ले? (koii kyon jhagdaa mol le?, 'why would anyone go looking for a fight?') -- treating trouble as something you can literally purchase for yourself.
Common Mistakes: 1) मोल लेना (mol lenaa) is also used figuratively for bringing trouble on oneself, as in दुश्मनी मोल लेना (dushmnii mol lenaa, 'to invite enmity') -- learners who only know खरीदना (khariidnaa) miss this figurative extension entirely.
