मिज़ाज (mizaaj)

noun A word for temperament or mood, used both for a person's underlying nature and their momentary state of mind, plus a common idiomatic sense about airs/attitude.
Sense 1 : temperament, disposition (a person's basic nature)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वभाव (svabhaav), प्रकृति (prakriti, formal), तबीयत (tabiiyat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in phrases like गरम मिज़ाज (garam mizaaj, 'hot temper') or शाही मिज़ाज (shaahii mizaaj, 'royal/regal temperament') -- Delhi loves describing people's mizaaj, especially when gossiping about a relative's short fuse or a friend's laid-back nature.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्वभाव (svabhaav) by default; मिज़ाज (mizaaj) is just as common in speech and carries a slightly more colorful, personality-flavored feel, especially when describing someone as hot-tempered or easygoing.
Sample Sentences:

(uskaa mizaaj bahut garam hai।)

Sense 2 : mood, state of mind (how someone feels right now)
noun, masculine Often appears in the polite inquiry क्या मिज़ाज है (kyaa mizaaj hai, 'how are you feeling/what's your mood') as a warm, slightly playful greeting among familiars.
Synonyms: मूड (mood, colloquial), तबीयत (tabiiyat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Asking क्या मिज़ाज है (kyaa mizaaj hai) to a friend is a warm, almost teasing way of saying 'what's up, how are you feeling today' -- it has a bit more flavor than the plain कैसे हो (kaise ho).
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with the temperament sense above; context (a greeting vs. a character description) is what tells them apart, not a different word form.

Sense 3 : airs, haughty attitude (used critically)
noun, masculine Common in the phrase मिज़ाज दिखाना (mizaaj dikhaanaa, 'to show attitude/put on airs') and मिज़ाज बिगड़ना (mizaaj bigadnaa, 'to become haughty/short-tempered').
Synonyms: नखरे (nakhre, colloquial), घमंड (ghamand, meaning scope: pride specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: If someone says उसका मिज़ाज बदल गया है (uskaa mizaaj badal gayaa hai, 'his attitude has changed'), they're usually complaining that a person has gotten too big for their boots since some success -- a very Delhi way of gently cutting someone down to size.
Common Mistakes: 1) This critical sense is signaled by context and tone (often said with irritation about someone acting superior), not by any change in the word itself, so learners should listen for the surrounding complaint rather than expecting a marked form.
Sample Sentences:
उसका मिज़ाज बदल गया है, एक प्रमोशन में ही। His attitude has changed, and just from one promotion.
