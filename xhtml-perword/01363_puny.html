पुण्य (puny)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning religious/moral merit earned through good deeds -- the positive counterpart to पाप (paap, sin). Also used as an adjective describing something holy or virtuous.
Sense 1 : religious/moral merit
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुकृत (sukrit, literary), नेकी (nekii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday Hindu thinking, पुण्य (puny) works almost like a spiritual bank balance: feeding a beggar, bathing in the Ganga, or helping an elder all 'deposit' पुण्य, while पाप (paap) withdraws from it. You will hear elderly relatives say things like इससे पुण्य मिलता है (isse puny miltaa hai, 'one gets merit from this') about small acts of charity, tying the word closely to karma and the afterlife.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate पुण्य (puny) simply as 'good deed', but it more precisely means the spiritual credit or merit *earned* by a good deed -- the deed itself is often called पुण्य कर्म (puny karm, 'meritorious act') or नेक काम (nek kaam, 'good deed'). 2) It is frequently used with कमाना (kamaanaa, to earn) or मिलना (milnaa, to be gotten), as in पुण्य कमाना (puny kamaanaa, 'to earn merit') -- learners sometimes reach for करना (karnaa) instead, which sounds odd here.

Sense 2 : holy, virtuous, sacred
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. पुण्य तिथि (puny tithi, 'holy/anniversary date'), पुण्य आत्मा (puny aatmaa, 'virtuous soul').
Synonyms: पवित्र (pavitr), पावन (paavan, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use shows up in set phrases tied to religious occasions, like पुण्य तिथि (puny tithi), the term used for a saint's or great person's death anniversary -- a reminder that पुण्य (puny) colors both actions and the days/things associated with them as sacred.
