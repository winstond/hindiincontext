लँगड़ा (langdaa)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'lame' or 'limping', describing a person or animal with an impaired leg; also used as a noun for a lame person, and colloquially/figuratively for something 'defective' or 'weak' (like an excuse or argument).
Sense 1 : lame, limping (of a person or animal)
adjective
Synonyms: लंगड़ा (langdaa, variant spelling without chandrabindu), अपाहिज (apaahij, broader: disabled/handicapped)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is everyday, blunt language you would hear in a market or among neighbors describing someone's gait, not a clinical or formal term -- a doctor or an official form would use अपाहिज (apaahij) or दिव्यांग (divyaang, differently-abled, the modern polite term) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for अपाहिज (apaahij) as a direct synonym, but अपाहिज (apaahij) covers general physical disability while लँगड़ा (langdaa) specifically means impaired in the leg/limping. 2) Using this word to describe a person directly in front of them can sound blunt or insensitive -- it is common in casual speech but not considered polite or formal vocabulary.

Sense 2 : a lame person
noun, masculine Functions as the adjective used substantively -- 'the lame one' -- inflecting the same way the adjective does.
Synonyms: अपाहिज (apaahij, broader: disabled person)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this used as a nickname or blunt descriptor in village or street speech, e.g. pointing someone out as लँगड़ा (langdaa) rather than by name -- it is casual and can feel unkind, so it is not a word to use carelessly about someone within earshot.

Sense 3 : weak, feeble, unconvincing (figurative, of an excuse, argument, or effort)
adjective
Synonyms: कमज़ोर (kamzor, weak, more general-purpose), बेदम (bedam, breathless/feeble, closer register match)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just like English lets 'lame' slide from a physical limp to a lame joke or lame excuse, Hindi does the same with लँगड़ा (langdaa) -- you will hear लँगड़ी दलील (langdii daliil, 'a lame argument') in an animated family debate over dinner.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense closely mirrors English 'a lame excuse', which can tempt learners to overuse it -- in natural Hindi, कमज़ोर बहाना (kamzor bahaanaa, 'a weak excuse') is at least as common as लँगड़ा बहाना (langdaa bahaanaa, 'a lame excuse').
