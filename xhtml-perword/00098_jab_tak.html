जब तक (jab tak)

conjunction A correlative conjunction meaning 'as long as / until', usually paired with तब तक (tab tak, 'until then') and often working with negation to mean 'until'.
Sense 1 : until (with negative verb)
conjunction This sense requires a negative verb in the जब तक (jab tak) clause: जब तक तुम न आओ (jab tak tum n aao, 'until you come') literally reads 'as long as you don't come'. English speakers often drop the negative, which changes the meaning in Hindi.
Synonyms: जब तक ... न (jab tak ... n, correlative frame)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This 'until' sense is extremely common in everyday warnings and instructions -- a mother saying जब तक खाना न खा लो, टीवी नहीं (jab tak khaanaa n khaa lo, tiivii nahiin, 'no TV until you finish your food') is a very typical household sentence in Delhi-NCR homes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say जब तक तुम आओगे (jab tak tum aaoge) meaning 'until you come', but without न (n) this actually means 'as long as you keep coming' -- the negative is essential for the 'until' sense. 2) The verb in the जब तक (jab tak) clause is usually in the subjunctive or simple present, not the future tense, even though the English 'until you come' feels future-oriented.
Sample Sentences:
जब तक तुम खाना न खा लो, टीवी नहीं चलेगा। The TV won't run until you finish eating.
(jab tak tum khaanaa n khaa lo, tiivii nahiin chalegaa।)

Sense 2 : as long as (duration/condition)
conjunction Here the जब तक (jab tak) clause has a positive (non-negated) verb, and often pairs with तब तक (tab tak) in the main clause: जब तक साँस तब तक आस (jab tak saans tab tak aas, 'as long as there's breath, there's hope').
Synonyms: जब तक ... तब तक (jab tak ... tab tak, full correlative pair)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pairs naturally with तब तक (tab tak, 'until then/that long') to form a balanced 'as long as X, that long Y' structure, a very common correlative pattern in Hindi alongside जो...वह (jo...vah) and जैसा...वैसा (jaisaa...vaisaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing this sense with the 'until' sense above purely based on the presence or absence of न (n) in the second clause -- the negative marker is the key signal, not the English translation you have in mind.

जब तक तुम साथ हो, मुझे कोई डर नहीं है। As long as you are with me, I have no fear.
(jab tak tum saath ho, mujhe koii dar nahiin hai।)
