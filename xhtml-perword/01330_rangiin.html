रंगीन (rangiin)

adjective An adjective meaning 'colorful' or 'colored' -- used both literally for objects with color and figuratively for a lively, fun-loving personality or a vivid mood/atmosphere.
Sense 1 : having color, colored (literal)
adjective
Synonyms: रंग-बिरंगा (rang-birangaa, multicolored), बहुरंगी (bahurangii, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly with टीवी (tiivii) and फ़ोटो (foto) -- रंगीन टीवी (rangiin tiivii, 'color TV') was a real marker of household status in the 1980s-90s, so older speakers still use the phrase with a bit of nostalgic weight, contrasting it with काला-सफ़ेद (kaalaa-safed, 'black and white').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse रंगीन (rangiin) with रंग-बिरंगा (rang-birangaa): रंगीन (rangiin) just means 'having color' (as opposed to black-and-white or plain), while रंग-बिरंगा (rang-birangaa) specifically means 'multicolored, many different colors together.' A single red shirt is रंगीन (rangiin) but not really रंग-बिरंगा (rang-birangaa).

Sense 2 : vivid, lively, fun-loving (of mood, personality, or life)
adjective
Synonyms: जीवंत (jiivant, vibrant, more formal), मज़ेदार (mazedaar, fun-loving)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone रंगीन (rangiin) or saying उसकी तबीयत रंगीन है (uskii tabiiyat rangiin hai, 'he has a colorful/pleasure-loving nature') is a classic Delhi way of teasingly hinting that someone enjoys the good life a bit too much -- parties, romance, style -- said with a smile, sometimes with side-eye.
Common Mistakes: 1) रंगीन तबीयत (rangiin tabiiyat, 'colorful/pleasure-loving nature') and रंगीन मिज़ाज़ (rangiin mizaaz, 'colorful temperament') are set phrases describing someone fond of pleasure, fun, or (with an edge) romance/flirtation -- they carry a knowing, slightly gossipy tone rather than being a neutral compliment.
