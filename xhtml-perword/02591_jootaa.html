जूता (jootaa)

noun A single common-noun sense: shoe. Almost always heard in the plural जूते (joote) since footwear comes in pairs, and figures in a well-known idiom about beating someone with a shoe.
Sense 1 : shoe
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बूट (boot, boot-style footwear)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Taking off your जूते (joote) before entering a home, temple, or gurdwara is such basic etiquette that you will see a shoe rack (जूता स्टैंड, jootaa staind) right by the door in most Indian houses. There is also a famous idiom, जूते मारना (joote maarnaa, 'to hit with shoes'), used to describe someone being publicly humiliated or thrashed -- shoes carry a strong insult connotation in Hindi culture, since they touch the ground and are considered unclean.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use जूता (jootaa) in the singular when talking about a pair of shoes; native speakers default to the plural जूते (joote) since you wear two at once -- 'मेरे जूते कहाँ हैं' (mere joote kahaan hain, 'where are my shoes'), not the singular, unless referring to just one shoe. 2) जूता (jootaa) refers to closed shoes generally; sandals and slippers are usually called चप्पल (chappal) instead, and mixing the two up is a common learner slip.
