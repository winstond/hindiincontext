कसरत (kasrat)

noun Mainly means physical exercise/workout, with an extended everyday sense of 'a lot of effort/trouble' put into getting something done.
Sense 1 : physical exercise, workout
noun, feminine
Synonyms: व्यायाम (vyaayaam, formal), वर्ज़िश (varzish)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi parks fill up every morning with people doing कसरत (kasrat) -- walking briskly, stretching, or doing desi-style calisthenics near the local park's benches. It's a slightly more everyday, less clinical word than व्यायाम (vyaayaam), which you would see written on a yoga institute's signboard.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use करना (karnaa) automatically since it is the default light verb, but that is in fact correct here -- कसरत करना (kasrat karnaa, 'to exercise') is the standard collocation, so no surprise there; the real trap is reaching for खेलना (khelnaa) or बनाना (banaanaa) by analogy with English 'do/make exercise,' which sound wrong to a native ear.
Sample Sentences:
मैं रोज़ सुबह कसरत करता हूँ। I exercise every morning.
(main roz subah kasrat kartaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : great effort, trouble, elaborate exertion
noun, feminine Used with करना (karnaa) in the sense of 'to go through a lot of trouble/maneuvering' to get something done, often with a mildly exasperated or impressed tone.
Synonyms: मेहनत (mehnat), जुगत (jugat, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this when someone describes the runaround of getting a document made or a ticket confirmed -- 'बड़ी कसरत करनी पड़ी टिकट कन्फर्म कराने में' (badii kasrat karnii padii tikat kanpharm karaane men, 'I had to go through a real ordeal to get the ticket confirmed'). It paints the effort as almost physically exhausting, echoing the word's exercise sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense surprises learners who only know the 'exercise' meaning -- हमें बहुत कसरत करनी पड़ी (hamen bahut kasrat karnii padii, 'we had to go through a lot of trouble') has nothing to do with physical workouts.
