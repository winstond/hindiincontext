भार (bhaar)

noun Primarily means physical weight or load; extends naturally into the figurative sense of a burden, responsibility, or emotional weight.
Sense 1 : burden, responsibility, emotional weight
noun, masculine Often appears in the pattern X पर भार होना/डालना (X par bhaar honaa/daalnaa, 'to be/put a burden on X'), treating a duty or expense as a weight placed on someone.
Synonyms: बोझ (bojh), ज़िम्मेदारी (zimmedaarii, responsibility)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative sense is everywhere in Hindi discourse about duty -- a son taking on घर का भार (ghar kaa bhaar, 'the burden/responsibility of the household') after a father's retirement or death is a very familiar cultural image, tied to ideas of family duty and izzat.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use भार (bhaar) only in the physical sense and miss its very common figurative use for financial or emotional burden, as in परिवार का भार (parivaar kaa bhaar, 'the burden of the family') -- a phrase you'll hear often in serious or emotional conversation.
Sample Sentences:
पिता के जाने के बाद घर का सारा भार उसके कंधों पर आ गया। After his father passed away, the entire burden of the household fell on his shoulders.
(pitaa ke jaane ke baad ghar kaa saaraa bhaar uske kandhon par aa gayaa।)

Sense 2 : physical weight/load
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वज़न (vazan), बोझ (bojh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see भार (bhaar) on official signage and documents -- like a truck's load limit or a courier's weight slip -- more than you'll hear it in casual kitchen or market talk, where वज़न (vazan) rules.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for वज़न (vazan) by default since it's the everyday word for weight; भार (bhaar) is not wrong but leans slightly more formal/technical, common in contexts like physics, shipping, or written registers rather than casual chat about how heavy a bag is.
