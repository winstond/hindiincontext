आपत्ति (aapatti)

noun A formal-ish noun meaning 'objection' or, less commonly, 'trouble/calamity' -- the objection sense is by far the more useful one in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : objection, protest
noun, feminine Commonly used in the pattern आपत्ति करना (aapatti karnaa, 'to raise an objection') or आपत्ति होना (aapatti honaa, 'to have an objection', with a को-marked person who objects, e.g. मुझे आपत्ति है (mujhe aapatti hai, 'I have an objection')).
Synonyms: एतराज़ (etraaz), विरोध (virodh, stronger, 'opposition')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in formal or bureaucratic settings -- a lawyer says आपत्ति (aapatti) the way an English speaker says 'objection' in a courtroom, and government notices invite the public to 'file' an आपत्ति (aapatti) against a proposal within so many days.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं आपत्ति है (main aapatti hai) instead of the correct dative-subject form मुझे आपत्ति है (mujhe aapatti hai) -- the person who objects takes को (ko), not the plain subject case. 2) आपत्ति (aapatti) is more formal than एतराज़ (etraaz); in casual speech एतराज़ (etraaz) is heard more often, while आपत्ति (aapatti) shows up in meetings, official letters, and legal contexts.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे इस फैसले पर आपत्ति है। I have an objection to this decision.
(mujhe is phaisle par aapatti hai।)

Sense 2 : trouble, calamity, adversity
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मुसीबत (musiibat), आपदा (aapdaa, more formal, 'disaster')
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older sense survives mainly in set phrases and slightly literary or devotional speech, such as आपत्ति में सहायक (aapatti men sahaayak, 'a helper in times of trouble') -- a phrase you might hear describing a deity or a true friend, but rarely in casual chat.
