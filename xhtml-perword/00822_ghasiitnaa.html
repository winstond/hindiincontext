घसीटना (ghasiitnaa)

verb A physical verb meaning 'to drag' something along the ground, with a common figurative extension to 'dragging' someone into a matter (like a legal case or argument) against their will.
Sense 1 : to drag, pull along the ground
verb, transitive
Synonyms: खींचना (khiinchnaa, overlaps but focuses more on pulling toward oneself rather than dragging along a surface)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture someone dragging a heavy sack of aata (aataa, 'flour') across a kirana (kiraanaa, 'grocery store') floor, or kids dragging a charpai (chaarpaaii, 'cot') out into the courtyard on a summer evening -- that scraping, effortful motion is exactly what this word captures.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for खींचना (khiinchnaa) for any kind of pulling, but घसीटना (ghasiitnaa) specifically evokes something heavy or resistant being dragged across a surface, often with effort or roughness -- a sack, a body, furniture -- not a light tug like pulling a door or a rope.
Sample Sentences:
वह बोरी को ज़मीन पर घसीटते हुए ले गया। He dragged the sack along the ground and took it away.
(vah borii ko zamiin par ghasiitte hue le gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to drag someone into an unwanted situation (figurative)
verb, transitive Commonly used with में (men) marking the situation someone is being dragged into, e.g. मुझे इस झगड़े में घसीटना (mujhe is jhagde men ghasiitnaa, 'to drag me into this quarrel').
Synonyms: खींचना (khiinchnaa, also used figuratively for pulling someone into a matter)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this a lot in the context of family disputes and property cases -- कोर्ट-कचहरी में घसीटना (kort-kachahrii men ghasiitnaa, 'to drag into court proceedings') is a very Indian complaint about relatives who turn small disagreements into years-long legal battles.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is very common in the context of legal or family disputes -- अदालत में घसीटना (adaalat men ghasiitnaa, 'to drag into court') -- and learners sometimes miss that it always carries a negative, unwilling connotation; you would not use it for someone happily invited into something.
