गुलाम (gulaam)

noun Primarily a noun meaning 'slave/servant,' with a common figurative sense of being someone's helpless devotee or someone controlled by a habit or emotion.
Sense 1 : slave, bondsman
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दास (daas, literary), बंदा (bandaa, colloquial, self-referential)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries real historical weight in India, tied to memories of colonial rule -- गुलामी (gulaamii) 'slavery/servitude' is the word used for the British era before 1947, so गुलाम (gulaam) has a heavier, more political ring than English 'servant' would suggest.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use नौकर (naukar) and गुलाम (gulaam) interchangeably, but नौकर (naukar) is a paid servant while गुलाम (gulaam) implies ownership or total lack of freedom, historically or figuratively.

Sense 2 : someone helplessly devoted to or controlled by something (figurative)
noun, masculine Typically used in the construction X का गुलाम होना (X kaa gulaam honaa, 'to be a slave to X'), where X is a habit, emotion, or person.
Synonyms: दीवाना (diivaanaa, besotted, narrower scope), आशिक़ (aashiq, narrower scope, romantic context only)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in everyday moralizing -- a father might say आदत का गुलाम मत बनो (aadat kaa gulaam mat bano, 'don't become a slave to habit') -- and in romantic contexts, तेरा गुलाम (teraa gulaam, 'your slave') shows up often in film lyrics and dialogue as a term of devoted love.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the का (kaa) linking the person/thing one is enslaved to -- आदत का गुलाम (aadat kaa gulaam, 'a slave to habit') needs that possessive link, not a direct object construction.

Sense 3 : the Jack in a deck of playing cards
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In card games like रम्मी (rammii, rummy) or तीन पत्ती (tiin pattii, teen patti), the Jack is called गुलाम (gulaam) rather than जैक (jaik) -- a nice example of an old loanword settling comfortably into everyday Hindi far from its original 'slave' sense.
