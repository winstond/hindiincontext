देर से (der se)

adverb A common adverbial phrase meaning 'late' -- built from देर (der, 'delay/lateness') plus the postposition से (se). One core sense, used constantly to talk about arriving or doing things late.
Sense 1 : late (in time)
adverb
Synonyms: विलंब से (vilamb se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR daily life, देर से (der se) is the go-to phrase for the universal complaint about traffic and trains: बस देर से आई (bas der se aaii, 'the bus came late'). It pairs naturally with सॉरी (sorii) or माफ़ करो (maaf karo) when apologizing for being late yourself, as in माफ़ करो, देर से आया (maaf karo, der se aayaa, 'sorry, I came late').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say देर (der) alone when they mean 'late' as an adverb, but देर से (der se) is the phrase actually used with verbs of arriving/coming: वह देर से आया (vah der se aayaa, 'he came late') -- देर (der) by itself functions more like a noun ('a delay/a while'), as in मुझे देर हो गई (mujhe der ho gaii, 'I got delayed'). 2) Do not confuse देर से (der se) 'late' with देर तक (der tak) 'for a long time' -- से (se) marks the late starting point, तक (tak) marks extended duration.
