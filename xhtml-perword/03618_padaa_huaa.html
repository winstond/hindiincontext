पड़ा हुआ (padaa huaa)

adjective This is the perfective participle of पड़ना (padnaa, 'to lie/fall') used as a descriptive adjective meaning 'lying (there)' or 'in a state of having fallen/been left' -- describing something's position or neglected state.
Sense 1 : lying somewhere, positioned/left in a spot
adjective, masculine/feminine Describes the resulting state of something having come to rest somewhere -- a book, a body, an object -- and is typically followed by a locative postposition phrase telling where: मेज़ पर पड़ा हुआ (mez par padaa huaa, 'lying on the table').
Synonyms: रखा हुआ (rakhaa huaa, for objects placed rather than fallen), बिखरा हुआ (bikhraa huaa, scattered)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly about clutter and mess -- कपड़े ज़मीन पर पड़े हुए हैं (kapde zamiin par pade hue hain, 'the clothes are lying on the floor') is a classic line from an Indian mother surveying her child's room. The word carries a faint judgment of untidiness or neglect that 'lying there' in English doesn't automatically carry.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for रखा हुआ (rakhaa huaa) when they mean something was deliberately placed, but पड़ा हुआ (padaa huaa) suggests it just ended up there, often carelessly or by chance -- the nuance is about intention, not position. 2) As a compound past participle, it agrees in gender/number with the noun it describes, which learners sometimes forget when the noun is feminine or plural: किताब पड़ी हुई है (kitaab padii huii hai, 'the book is lying there'), not पड़ा हुआ (padaa huaa).

Sense 2 : idle, unused, sitting neglected
adjective, masculine/feminine Extends the literal 'lying' sense to things that sit unused over time -- a room, a plot of land, a machine, a skill -- implying waste or missed potential.
Synonyms: बेकार पड़ा हुआ (bekaar padaa huaa, emphasizes uselessness), खाली पड़ा हुआ (khaalii padaa huaa, for empty/vacant spaces)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in conversations about property and resources going to waste -- ज़मीन खाली पड़ी है (zamiin khaalii padii hai, 'the land is lying vacant') is something you might hear discussing an uncle's ancestral plot back in the village. There's often a tone of mild regret or criticism attached, as if the thing should be doing something.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'unused' with केवल इस्तेमाल नहीं (keval istemaal nahiin) or similar literal phrasing when a native speaker would simply say घर खाली पड़ा है (ghar khaalii padaa hai, 'the house sits empty') -- पड़ा हुआ (padaa huaa) is the natural, idiomatic choice here, not a workaround.
