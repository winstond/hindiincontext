धोखा (dhokhaa)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'deceit/betrayal' or 'accident/mishap', and the core of the very common verb phrase धोखा देना (dhokhaa denaa, 'to deceive/cheat').
Sense 1 : deception, betrayal, cheating
noun, masculine Almost always appears with देना (denaa) 'to give/deal (someone) a deceit' or खाना (khaanaa) 'to receive/fall for a deceit' -- the object of deception takes को.
Synonyms: छल (chhal), फ़रेब (fareb), दगा (dagaa, colloquial), विश्वासघात (vishvaasghaat, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: धोखा देना (dhokhaa denaa) is the everyday phrase for betrayal in relationships, business, and politics alike -- you will hear it constantly in TV dramas and real gossip about unfaithful partners or cheating business deals. The idiom धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa) nicely captures how Hindi frames being deceived as something that happens to you, almost like an involuntary experience, rather than something you actively do.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say धोखा करना (dhokhaa karnaa) by analogy with other noun+करना (karnaa) constructions, but the natural verb here is देना (denaa) -- धोखा देना (dhokhaa denaa), not धोखा करना (dhokhaa karnaa). 2) To describe being the victim, use धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to eat a deceit' = to be deceived), not a passive of देना (denaa).

उसने अपनी पत्नी को धोखा दिया। He betrayed his wife.
(usne apnii patnii ko dhokhaa diyaa।)

Sense 2 : accident, mishap, unintentional error
noun, masculine Used in phrases like धोखे से (dhokhe se, 'by mistake, accidentally') -- the oblique + से marks the unintentional manner.
Synonyms: ग़लती (ghaltii, not exact but overlaps for 'unintentional error'), भूल (bhool, overlaps, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will often hear धोखे से हो गया (dhokhe se ho gayaa, 'it happened by mistake') when someone is explaining away a small blunder, like knocking over a cup of chai -- it softens the admission by framing the error as something that just happened, not something done on purpose.
Common Mistakes: 1) धोखे से (dhokhe se) means 'accidentally/by mistake' -- learners sometimes confuse this with the deceit sense and mistranslate it as 'through deception,' but here it simply flags an unintentional slip.
