दर्जन (darjan)

noun A borrowed counting word meaning 'dozen' -- used just like in English, for a group of twelve, and often loosely for 'a bunch of' something.
Sense 1 : a group of twelve
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly at the sabzi mandi (sabzii mandii) or from a fruitwala haggling over kelे (kele, bananas) or ande (ande, eggs) -- दर्जन (darjan) is one of those everyday loanwords (from English 'dozen') that has been fully absorbed into bazaar Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that दर्जन (darjan) itself stays invariant when counting -- you say दो दर्जन केले (do darjan kele, 'two dozen bananas'), not a plural form of दर्जन (darjan) itself. 2) Unlike English 'a dozen eggs,' Hindi does not need एक (ek) before दर्जन (darjan) when just one dozen is meant in casual speech -- दर्जन भर अंडे (darjan bhar ande, 'a dozen eggs' or literally 'dozen-full eggs') is a common pattern using भर (bhar).

Sense 2 : a good number of, many (approximate/figurative)
noun, masculine Often doubled as दर्जनों (darjnon) to mean 'dozens of' in a loose, non-exact sense, similar to English 'dozens of complaints.'
Synonyms: बहुत (bahut, general 'many', not exclusive to counting), ढेर (dher, a heap/lots)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same shift English makes when it says 'dozens of people showed up' -- a precise number word sliding into a vague 'a lot.' You will hear दर्जनों बार (darjnon baar, 'dozens of times') from an exasperated parent or teacher scolding someone for repeating a mistake.
Common Mistakes: 1) दर्जनों (darjnon) is the form used for this vague 'many' sense -- learners sometimes use the plain singular दर्जन (darjan) here, which sounds odd since this figurative use expects the oblique plural.

(mainne tumhen darjnon baar samjhaayaa hai, phir bhii tum nahiin sunte।)
