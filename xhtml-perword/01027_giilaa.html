गीला (giilaa)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'wet, damp, moist'; also used figuratively for eyes welling up with tears.
Sense 1 : wet, damp, moist
adjective
Synonyms: नम (nam, slightly formal/drier than गीला), भीगा (bhiigaa, soaked, implies having been drenched)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear गीला (giilaa) constantly in daily domestic life -- गीले कपड़े (giile kapde, 'wet clothes') on the line, फर्श गीला है (pharsh giilaa hai, 'the floor is wet') as a common household warning, or गीला कचरा (giilaa kachraa, 'wet/organic waste') on municipal garbage bins in Delhi-NCR, paired with सूखा कचरा (sookhaa kachraa, 'dry waste') for segregation. Figuratively, आँखें गीली होना (aankhen giilii honaa, 'eyes to become moist') is a gentle, common way to describe someone getting emotional or teary-eyed without saying रोना (ronaa, 'to cry') outright.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for भीगा (bhiigaa) and गीला (giilaa) interchangeably, but भीगा (bhiigaa) usually implies something got wet through an action or event (rain, a dip in water), while गीला (giilaa) is the plain descriptive state of being wet, regardless of how it got that way -- a गीला कपड़ा (giilaa kapdaa, 'wet cloth') might just be freshly washed, not rained on. 2) Don't confuse with ठंडा (thandaa, 'cold') -- a wet thing is often cold too, but गीला (giilaa) specifically flags moisture, not temperature.
