साँवला (saanvlaa)

adjective A single-sense descriptive adjective for dusky/dark-toned complexion, used mostly of people, most often admiringly rather than negatively.
Sense 1 : dusky/dark-complexioned (of skin tone)
adjective
Synonyms: काला (kaalaa, blunter, can sound negative), श्याम (shyaam, literary), गेहुँआ (gehunaa, wheat-toned, a lighter shade than साँवला)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Hindi film songs and everyday flirtation alike, साँवला (saanvlaa) (and its feminine साँवली (saanvlii)) is practically a compliment -- think of how often lyrics praise a साँवली सूरत (saanvlii soorat, 'a dusky face'). It carries none of the harshness that काला (kaalaa) can carry when applied to a person's skin, which is worth knowing given how loaded complexion talk can be in Indian society.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for काला (kaalaa) to describe someone's complexion, but काला (kaalaa) can sound blunt or even rude about a person's skin, while साँवला (saanvlaa) is the polite, often admiring word Hindi speakers actually use in everyday conversation and even in matrimonial or descriptive contexts. 2) साँवला (saanvlaa) is used for people (and sometimes for dusky evening light, as in साँवली शाम (saanvlii shaam, 'dusky evening')), not for ordinary dark-colored objects -- for those, काला (kaalaa) is normal and neutral.
