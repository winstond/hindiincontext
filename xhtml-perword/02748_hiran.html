हिरन (hiran)

noun A common noun meaning 'deer'; used both literally for the animal and figuratively in a couple of set expressions about being startled or vanishing.
Sense 1 : deer
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मृग (mrig, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हिरन (hiran) is the word you will hear at a zoo (chidiyaaghar) or wildlife sanctuary, while मृग (mrig) has a more storybook, classical feel -- think of the mrig-tanha (mirage) idea or the golden deer from the Ramayan that Sita wanted.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मृग (mrig) as the everyday word because it appears in textbooks and mythology stories (like the golden deer of the Ramayan), but in ordinary spoken Hindi हिरन (hiran) is what people actually say.
Sample Sentences:
चिड़ियाघर में बच्चों ने हिरन को देखा। The children saw a deer at the zoo.
(chidiyaaghar men bachchon ne hiran ko dekhaa।)

Sense 2 : someone easily startled or frightened (figurative)
noun, masculine Used mainly inside the fixed idiom हिरन हो जाना (hiran ho jaanaa, 'to bolt/vanish out of fear') rather than as a free-standing description of a person.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The image is very physical: a deer's instinct to freeze and then dart away at the slightest sound. Hindi speakers reach for this same picture when describing someone who gets spooked and disappears, much like English 'to bolt like a rabbit.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense barely occurs on its own -- learners should not try to use हिरन (hiran) alone as an adjective for 'timid'; it only carries this meaning inside the idiom with हो जाना (ho jaanaa).
Sample Sentences:
पुलिस को देखते ही वह हिरन हो गया। The moment he saw the police, he bolted like a startled deer.
