मलाई (malaaii)

noun Mainly the cream/malai that forms on boiled milk, with a figurative sense meaning easy profit or the 'best cut' of something -- often heard in the idiom मलाई खाना (malaaii khaanaa).
Sense 1 : the thick cream that forms on top of boiled/cooled milk
noun, feminine
Synonyms: क्रीम (kriim, loanword, used for packaged cream)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most Indian homes, milk is boiled daily and then left to cool, and the layer of मलाई (malaaii) that forms on top is a small prized thing -- kids often fight over who gets to eat it with a spoon or have it stirred into their morning milk. It is also the base for घर की मलाई से बनी मिठाई (ghar kii malaaii se banii mithaaii, 'sweets made from homemade cream'), like मलाई कोफ्ता (malaaii kophtaa) or मलाई कुल्फी (malaaii kulphii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume मलाई (malaaii) means only the Western 'cream' sold in cartons -- in everyday Indian kitchens it usually refers to the layer that forms naturally on boiled milk once it cools, which is skimmed off by hand.

Sense 2 : easy or illicit profit; the best/easiest share of something (figurative)
noun, feminine Almost always appears with खाना (khaanaa) in the fixed expression मलाई खाना (malaaii khaanaa, 'to skim off the cream/profit'), used about someone benefiting easily -- often at others' expense -- from a position, deal, or job.
Synonyms: फ़ायदा (faaydaa), मुनाफ़ा (munaafaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic office-gossip word: अफ़सर सारी मलाई खा जाता है (afsar saarii malaaii khaa jaataa hai, 'the officer eats up all the cream') is the kind of line you hear about a boss or bureaucrat who takes the easy benefits while others do the hard work. It draws directly on the kitchen image -- the top, richest layer being skimmed off before anyone else gets a share.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only really works paired with खाना (khaanaa) -- learners sometimes try to use मलाई (malaaii) alone as an abstract noun for 'profit,' which sounds off; you need the verb to trigger the idiom. 2) It carries a mildly negative or cynical shade (implying someone is getting an unfair or effortless cut), so it is not a neutral synonym for सामान्य फ़ायदा (saamaany faaydaa, 'ordinary profit').
Sample Sentences:

(afsar saarii malaaii khaa jaataa hai, baakii sab mehnat karte rahte hain।)
