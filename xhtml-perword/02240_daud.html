दौड़ (daud)

noun Primarily the noun 'run/race,' closely tied to the verb दौड़ना (daudnaa, 'to run') -- also appears in a few common idioms about ambition and effort.
Sense 1 : a run, race, or the act of running
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भागदौड़ (bhaagdaud, running around, busy activity), रेस (res, competitive race)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear दौड़ (daud) constantly around school sports days -- सौ मीटर की दौड़ (sau miitar kii daud, 'the 100-meter race') is a fixture of every Indian school's annual sports day (वार्षिक खेल दिवस). The word also shows up in the common phrase भाग-दौड़ (bhaag-daud), describing the hectic running-around of daily city life -- something every Delhi commuter knows intimately.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use भागना (bhaagnaa, 'to flee/run away') interchangeably with दौड़ (daud) -- but दौड़ (daud) is neutral running/racing, while भागना (bhaagnaa) implies fleeing or escaping. 2) दौड़ (daud) is the noun; the verb form is दौड़ना (daudnaa), and beginners occasionally try to use दौड़ (daud) itself as a verb stem in sentences where दौड़ना (daudnaa) is needed.

(skool men kal sau miitar kii daud hai।)

Sense 2 : a competitive struggle or pursuit (figurative 'race')
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रतिस्पर्धा (pratispardhaa, formal, competition), होड़ (hod, rivalry)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: इस दुनिया की दौड़ (is duniyaa kii daud, 'the race of this world') is a phrase you'll hear from elders lamenting the modern scramble for money, status, or jobs -- दौड़ (daud) here captures the same sense as 'rat race' in English, the endless competitive chase.
