उस्ताद (ustaad)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning 'master/teacher' (especially of music, craft, or a skill), with a common extended adjective use meaning 'crafty, shrewd, expert at tricks.'
Sense 1 : master teacher, guru of a craft or art
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गुरु (guru), शिक्षक (shikshak, formal, esp. school context)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries deep resonance in Hindustani classical music and Urdu/Persian-influenced artistic traditions -- a sitar or tabla maestro is addressed as उस्ताद जी (ustaad jii), with जी (jii) added for respect. It sits alongside गुरु (guru), which leans more Sanskritic/spiritual, while उस्ताद (ustaad) leans more toward the Mughal-court and qawwali/classical-music heritage.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शिक्षक (shikshak) as a direct equivalent, but उस्ताद (ustaad) is reserved for a master of a craft, art, or skill (music, cooking, wrestling, etc.), not a regular school teacher. 2) It is used as a respectful title before a name, e.g. उस्ताद बिस्मिल्लाह खान (ustaad bismillaah khaan, 'Ustad Bismillah Khan'), similar to how one might say 'Maestro' in English.

Sense 2 : crafty, cunning, expert at tricks (often mischievous)
adjective
Synonyms: चालाक (chaalaak), शातिर (shaatir, stronger, implies deviousness)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this thrown around in Delhi households and bazaars with a smile -- कह के गया, बड़ा उस्ताद निकला (kah ke gayaa, badaa ustaad niklaa, 'he said one thing and turned out to be quite the sly one') captures the semi-admiring tone this word often carries, unlike a purely negative word like धोखेबाज़ (dhokhebaaz).
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is often used affectionately or teasingly about someone who is clever at getting their way, e.g. about a child who always manages to avoid trouble -- it is not necessarily an insult. 2) Don't confuse with the noun sense: तू तो बड़ा उस्ताद है (too to badaa ustaad hai, 'you're a real crafty one') is calling someone sly, not calling them a maestro.
