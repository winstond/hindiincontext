गप (gap)

noun An informal noun meaning idle chat or a tall tale/gossip -- almost always heard in its doubled form गप्पें (gappen) or in the compound गप्पबाज़ी (gappbaazii), 'chit-chat.'
Sense 1 : idle/casual chat, chit-chat
noun, feminine Almost always used with मारना (maarnaa) or हाँकना (haanknaa) as the verb -- गप मारना (gap maarnaa) or plural गप्पें मारना (gappen maarnaa) is the standard way to say 'to chat/gossip.'
Synonyms: बातचीत (baatchiit), बकबक (bakbak, colloquial), गपशप (gapshap, one-word variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for the lazy, meandering conversation over chai on a veranda or at a tea stall -- गप्पें मारना (gappen maarnaa) evokes friends with nothing urgent to do, just talking for the pleasure of it. It has a warm, unhurried, sociable connotation rather than a negative one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use गप (gap) with करना (karnaa) the way they would with बात (baat), but native speakers strongly prefer मारना (maarnaa) or हाँकना (haanknaa) here -- गप करना (gap karnaa) sounds off. 2) The plural form गप्पें (gappen) is far more common in everyday speech than the singular गप (gap); learners who only learn the singular will sound slightly bookish.

Sense 2 : a tall tale, an exaggerated or made-up story
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गप्पबाज़ी (gappbaazii), झूठ (jhooth, stronger, implies deliberate lie)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone a गप्पी (gappii, 'a person who tells tall tales') is usually affectionate teasing, aimed at the friend or uncle who always has an unbelievable story to tell -- it is closer to 'storyteller/exaggerator' than 'liar.'
Common Mistakes: 1) गप (gap) in this sense is milder than झूठ (jhooth) -- it suggests exaggeration or fanciful storytelling for entertainment, not a malicious lie; learners sometimes reach for झूठ (jhooth) when गप (gap) would sound more natural and less accusatory.
