काग़ज़ (kaaghaz)

noun Mainly a concrete noun meaning 'paper' or 'a document/piece of paper'; the document sense is very common in everyday bureaucratic and legal talk.
Sense 1 : paper (the material)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पत्र (patr, literary/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the stationery shop (स्टेशनरी की दुकान, steshanrii kii dukaan) where you ask for काग़ज़ (kaaghaz) by the sheet or the packet -- it is the everyday word a shopkeeper expects, not a bookish term.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say पेपर (pepar) borrowed from English, which Hindi speakers do use casually, but काग़ज़ (kaaghaz) is the native word heard in markets, schools, and offices and sounds more natural in most sentences.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे एक काग़ज़ चाहिए। I need a piece of paper.
(mujhe ek kaaghaz chaahie।)

Sense 2 : document, official paper
noun, masculine Very often used in the plural काग़ज़ात (kaaghzaat) or काग़ज़ (kaaghaz) itself to mean 'papers/documents' collectively, as in identity or property papers.
Synonyms: दस्तावेज़ (dastaavez), प्रपत्र (prapatr, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi government office clerk about काग़ज़ (kaaghaz) and they mean your ID, address proof, and other paperwork -- this sense is deeply tied to India's document-heavy bureaucracy, where 'सारे काग़ज़ लाना' (saare kaaghaz laanaa, 'to bring all the documents') is a constant refrain.
Common Mistakes: 1) In bureaucratic contexts, काग़ज़ (kaaghaz) alone (without the plural आत ending) is still understood as 'documents' in the plural sense from context; learners sometimes over-insist on the plural form when the singular is already doing the job.
Sample Sentences:
सारे काग़ज़ साथ लाना, वरना काम नहीं होगा। Bring all the documents with you, otherwise the work won't get done.
(saare kaaghaz saath laanaa, varnaa kaam nahiin hogaa।)
