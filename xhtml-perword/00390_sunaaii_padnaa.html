सुनाई पड़ना (sunaaii padnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to be audible / to be heard,' built from the noun-ish participle सुनाई (sunaaii) plus पड़ना (padnaa, 'to fall'); closely related to सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa), which means almost the same thing with a slightly different feel.
Sense 1 : to be audible, to be heard (involuntarily)
verb, intransitive Used almost exclusively with a को (ko)-marked experiencer: मुझे आवाज़ सुनाई पड़ी (mujhe aavaaz sunaaii padii, 'I heard a sound') -- the experiencer never takes ने (ne) since there is no agentive action, only perception happening to them.
Synonyms: सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa), सुनना (sunnaa, not a true synonym, means 'to listen/hear' as a deliberate act)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb reflects a very Hindi way of framing perception: sounds and sights are not things you actively grab, they are things that 'fall' upon you (पड़ना, padnaa) or are 'given' to you (देना, denaa), so the experiencer stays in the dative with को (ko) rather than acting as a grammatical subject. It pairs naturally with its visual counterpart, दिखाई पड़ना (dikhaaii padnaa, 'to be visible'), which follows the exact same pattern.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the experiencer with ने (ne) by analogy with other past-tense verbs, but सुनाई पड़ना (sunaaii padnaa) is intransitive and always takes को (ko) for the person perceiving the sound, never ने (ne). 2) Confusing this with सुनना (sunnaa): सुनना (sunnaa) implies active listening (मैंने गाना सुना, mainne gaanaa sunaa, 'I listened to the song'), while सुनाई पड़ना (sunaaii padnaa) implies the sound simply reached your ears on its own. 3) Overusing सुनाई पड़ना (sunaaii padnaa) where सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa) would sound more natural in casual speech -- both are correct but देना (denaa) is somewhat more common in everyday Delhi speech.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे बाहर से कुछ आवाज़ सुनाई पड़ रही है। I can hear some noise from outside.
(mujhe baahar se kuchh aavaaz sunaaii pad rahii hai।)
