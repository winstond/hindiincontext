ख़ाली-हाथ (khaalii-haath)

adjective Literally 'empty-handed'; used both literally (arriving without a gift or without bringing anything) and figuratively (returning from an effort with nothing to show for it, i.e. unsuccessful).
Sense 1 : empty-handed (literal, without bringing anything)
adjective Functions as an invariant adjective/adverbial modifier, typically paired with verbs of coming or going: ख़ाली-हाथ आना (khaalii-haath aanaa, 'to come empty-handed'), ख़ाली-हाथ जाना (khaalii-haath jaanaa, 'to go empty-handed').
Synonyms: बिना कुछ लिए (binaa kuchh lie, without bringing anything)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian households, showing up ख़ाली-हाथ (khaalii-haath) to someone's home, especially for a festival or family visit, is considered mildly impolite -- a small box of mithai or fruit is the norm. This is why hosts and guests alike joke about it, and why the phrase carries a light social judgment beyond its literal meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like an ordinary -आ adjective (e.g. adding gender/number endings); it stays fixed as ख़ाली-हाथ (khaalii-haath) regardless of the subject's gender or number.

Sense 2 : empty-handed, unsuccessful (figurative, having gained nothing)
adjective Common in the pattern ख़ाली-हाथ लौटना (khaalii-haath lautnaa, 'to return empty-handed') meaning an effort, negotiation, or search yielded nothing.
Synonyms: नाकाम (naakaam, unsuccessful), निराश (niraash, disappointed, partial overlap)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense shows up often in news and everyday complaint -- a candidate returning ख़ाली-हाथ (khaalii-haath) from an interview, or a team from a tournament -- extending the physical image of empty hands to empty results.
