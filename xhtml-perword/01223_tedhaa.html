टेढ़ा (tedhaa)

adjective Primarily describes something physically bent, crooked, or slanted; extends naturally to a figurative sense of a person's or situation's tricky, awkward, or difficult nature.
Sense 1 : physically crooked, bent, or slanted
adjective
Synonyms: मुड़ा हुआ (mudaa huaa), झुका हुआ (jhukaa huaa, leaning/bent), तिरछा (tirchhaa, slanted/oblique)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday complaints -- a नाक (naak, nose) that is crooked, a लाइन (laain) that is not straight, a पेड़ (ped, tree) growing bent. The famous phrase टेढ़ी उँगली से घी निकालना (tedhii unglii se ghii nikaalnaa, 'to get ghee out with a bent finger') uses this very adjective to describe using clever, forceful, or roundabout means to get something done -- worth knowing since टेढ़ा shows up in several such idioms about crooked/difficult means.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for तिरछा (tirchhaa) when टेढ़ा (tedhaa) is meant -- तिरछा leans more toward 'diagonal/slanted at an angle' (like a tilted line), while टेढ़ा covers general crookedness or bending, including zigzag or irregular shapes.

(yah tasviir tedhii lagii hai, ise siidhaa kar do।)

Sense 2 : figuratively difficult, tricky, or awkward (of a situation, question, or task)
adjective
Synonyms: मुश्किल (mushkil), पेचीदा (pechiidaa, complicated/convoluted), जटिल (jatil, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indians often describe a stubborn or difficult person's temperament as टेढ़ा (tedhaa) too, as in टेढ़ा आदमी (tedhaa aadmii, 'a difficult/cranky man') -- the same word for a bent stick doubles as a bent personality, which is a nice example of how Hindi maps physical shape onto character.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to मुश्किल (mushkil) for 'difficult' and miss that टेढ़ा (tedhaa) specifically implies a problem that is convoluted or awkward in shape, not just hard -- for example a टेढ़ा सवाल (tedhaa savaal, 'a tricky/loaded question') suggests a question designed to trip you up, not merely a hard one.
