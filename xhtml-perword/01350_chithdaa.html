चिथड़ा (chithdaa)

noun A noun meaning a rag or tattered scrap of cloth, most often met in its plural form describing worn-out, torn clothing; also lives inside a common idiom about poverty.
Sense 1 : rag, tattered piece of cloth
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कपड़ा (kapdaa, scrap of cloth, broader meaning), टुकड़ा (tukdaa, generic piece/scrap)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word most in the set phrase चिथड़े पहनना (chithde pahannaa, 'to wear rags') or चिथड़ों में (chithdon men, 'in rags'), painting a vivid picture of poverty -- much like English 'dressed in rags.' It carries real emotional weight in stories and songs about hardship, so it's a word with color, not just a neutral term for damaged cloth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use कपड़ा (kapdaa) for any old cloth, but चिथड़ा (chithdaa) specifically implies something torn, worn-out, or in shreds -- not just old or used. 2) It is almost always heard in its plural form चिथड़े (chithde) when describing someone's ragged clothes, even when referring to a single garment's state.

Sense 2 : figurative: something torn to shreds, ruined or destroyed (reputation, hope, etc.)
noun, masculine Used almost exclusively in the idiom चिथड़े कर देना/उड़ जाना (chithde kar denaa or chithde ud jaanaa) to mean something abstract -- hope, honor, plans -- has been utterly destroyed.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same image as English 'torn to shreds,' but Hindi leans on it more colorfully: इज़्ज़त के चिथड़े उड़ गए (izzat ke chithde ud gae, 'his honor was torn to shreds') is a phrase you'll hear in dramatic conversation, news commentary, or films whenever someone's reputation is publicly destroyed.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only works with abstract nouns like इज़्ज़त (izzat, honor) or उम्मीद (ummiid, hope) as the thing being destroyed -- it doesn't extend to physical objects other than cloth itself, where you'd stay literal.
