नुक़ता (nuqtaa)

noun Primarily the diacritical dot placed under certain Devanagari consonants (like क़, ख़, ग़, ज़, फ़) to represent sounds borrowed from Persian/Arabic; also has an extended sense meaning 'point' or 'subtlety' in a discussion.
Sense 1 : the dot diacritic under a Devanagari letter marking a Persian/Arabic-derived sound
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बिंदु (bindu, generic dot, not specific to this diacritic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, most people drop the नुक़्ता (nuqtaa) entirely -- ज़रूर (zaroor) becomes जरूर (jaroor) in casual writing and nobody blinks. You'll see it taken seriously mainly in Urdu-influenced print, News Hindi, or when someone is being careful about words of Persian/Arabic origin like क़िला (qilaa) or ख़ुशी (khushii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often ignore the नुक़्ता (nuqtaa) in writing and pronunciation, saying काम (kaam, 'work') for ख़ाम (khaam) type distinctions -- but note ज़रूरत (zaroorat) and जरूरत (jaroorat) are both accepted, so dropping the नुक़्ता (nuqtaa) is common and not always 'wrong', just a style/register choice. 2) The word itself has a variant spelling नुक्ता (nuktaa) without the nuqta on the क़ -- both are seen, even though this is ironic given the word's own meaning.

Sense 2 : a fine point, subtlety, or nuance in an argument or matter
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बात (baat), बारीकी (baariikii, emphasizes fine detail)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in phrases like इस बात में एक नुक़्ता है (is baat men ek nuqtaa hai, 'there is a subtlety in this matter') -- it's the kind of word a sharp debater or a thoughtful uncle at a chai stall uses when pointing out that something isn't as simple as it looks.
