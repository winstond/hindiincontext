कई बार (kaii baar)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'several times' or 'sometimes' -- built from कई (kaii, 'several') plus बार (baar, 'time/occasion').
Sense 1 : sometimes, on occasion
adverb
Synonyms: कभी-कभी (kabhii-kabhii), कभी (kabhii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in casual conversation, e.g. someone explaining a habit: कई बार मैं ऐसा करता हूँ (kaii baar main aisaa kartaa hoon, 'I do this sometimes'). It sits comfortably alongside कभी-कभी (kabhii-kabhii) with almost the same everyday register -- Delhi speakers switch between the two freely.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कभी-कभी (kabhii-kabhii) by default; कई बार (kaii baar) is just as common in speech and carries a slightly stronger sense of 'this has happened a number of times,' not just 'once in a while.' 2) It does not need कई (kaii) to agree with anything -- the phrase is frozen and invariant regardless of what follows.

Sense 2 : many times, repeatedly
adverb
Synonyms: बार-बार (baar-baar), अनेक बार (anek baar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense leans into the literal meaning of कई (kaii, 'several/many') -- useful when someone is venting frustration, like कई बार कहा है (kaii baar kahaa hai, 'I've said it so many times'), a classic parental or exasperated-friend line in Delhi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Context decides whether कई बार (kaii baar) means 'sometimes' or 'many times' -- there is no grammatical marker distinguishing the two, so learners should listen for whether the speaker is emphasizing frequency (this sense) or occasional occurrence (the other sense).
