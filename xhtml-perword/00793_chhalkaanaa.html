छलकाना (chhalkaanaa)

verb The causative of छलकना (chhalaknaa, 'to overflow/spill'), so छलकाना (chhalkaanaa) means to make a liquid spill or overflow -- literally, or figuratively for emotions and eyes brimming over.
Sense 1 : to spill/slosh a liquid (by causing it to overflow)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: गिराना (giraanaa, spilling by dropping/tipping), बहाना (bahaanaa, to make flow)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture carrying a lassi (lassii, 'buttermilk drink') glass filled right to the brim through a crowded market -- if you jostle it, you'll छलकाना (chhalkaanaa) it. It's the everyday word for that small domestic mishap.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this with गिराना (giraanaa): गिराना (giraanaa) is spilling by knocking something over or dropping it, while छलकाना (chhalkaanaa) specifically means liquid brimming over the edge of its container, often just from movement or being too full -- like carrying a too-full glass. 2) The base verb छलकना (chhalaknaa) is intransitive ('the tea overflowed on its own'), while छलकाना (chhalkaanaa) is the transitive causative ('I spilled the tea') -- mixing these up is the most common error.

Sense 2 : to let emotion/tears well up and show (figurative overflow)
verb, transitive Commonly used with आँसू (aansoo, 'tears') or आँखें (aankhen, 'eyes') as the object, e.g. आँसू छलकाना (aansoo chhalkaanaa, 'to let tears well up') -- the eyes or tears are treated as a liquid that brims over, echoing the literal sense.
Synonyms: बहाना (bahaanaa, for tears specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi poetry and film dialogue love this image: आँखों में आँसू छलकाना (aankhon men aansoo chhalkaanaa, 'to let tears well up in the eyes') treats sorrow or joy as a liquid held just below the rim, about to spill. It's a favorite move in emotional scenes and ghazals (ghazal).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for रोना (ronaa, 'to cry') when they mean the subtler image of eyes glistening or brimming without a full cry -- छलकाना (chhalkaanaa) captures that in-between moment. 2) This sense is almost always paired with आँख (aankh) or आँसू (aansoo) as object; using it alone with a person as subject and no liquid-word nearby can sound odd.
