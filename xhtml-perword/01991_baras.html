बरस (baras)

noun/verb बरस (baras) is a noun meaning 'year', used interchangeably with साल (saal) in everyday speech; it also appears as a verb meaning 'to rain' (a poetic/literary variant of बरसना (barasnaa)), which is a separate, much less common sense.
Sense 1 : year
noun, masculine
Synonyms: साल (saal), वर्ष (varsh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear बरस (baras) constantly when people talk about age or how long ago something happened, like पाँच बरस पहले (paanch baras pahle, 'five years ago'). It sits right alongside साल (saal) with no real difference in meaning, just a slightly earthier, more traditional flavor, the kind of word a grandmother or a folk song uses.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to साल (saal) for 'year' and forget बरस (baras) is just as common in speech, especially with ages: दस बरस का (das baras kaa, 'ten years old'). 2) बरस (baras) stays unchanged after numerals the same way साल (saal) does; don't add a plural marker.

Sense 2 : to rain (poetic)
verb, intransitive This is a poetic/archaic-flavored stem form used mainly in songs, verse, and set phrases; in ordinary conversation बरसना (barasnaa) is used instead.
Synonyms: बरसना (barasnaa)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll meet this mostly in monsoon-themed poetry and old film songs, where बादल बरस (baadal baras, 'clouds rain') sounds more lyrical than the everyday बादल बरसना (baadal barasnaa). It's the same image Hindi loves: rain, tears, and blessings all 'raining down' (barasnaa) rather than simply falling.
Sample Sentences:
बादल बरसते हैं और धरती हरी हो जाती है। The clouds rain and the earth turns green.
