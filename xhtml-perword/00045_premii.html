प्रेमी (premii)

noun/adjective Two related senses: as a noun, प्रेमी (premii) means 'boyfriend/lover' (the feminine counterpart is a separate lemma, प्रेमिका premikaa); as an adjective, it's a compound suffix meaning '-loving' or 'fond of', as in संगीत-प्रेमी (sangiit-premii, 'music lover').
Sense 1 : boyfriend, male romantic lover
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आशिक़ (aashiq), माशूक (maashook, literary), बॉयफ्रेंड (boyaphrend, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word that shows up constantly in Bollywood song lyrics and dialogues -- प्रेमी (premii) and प्रेमिका (premikaa) are the classic 'hero and heroine' pairing in romantic storytelling. In real-life conversation among friends, though, English loans like बॉयफ्रेंड (boyaphrend) or गर्लफ्रेंड (garlaphrend) are just as common, especially in urban Delhi speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) प्रेमी (premii) is grammatically masculine and refers to a male partner; for a female partner use the separate word प्रेमिका (premikaa), not a feminine-inflected form of प्रेमी (premii). 2) In everyday casual talk about modern relationships, especially among younger speakers, बॉयफ्रेंड (boyaphrend) is often more natural than प्रेमी (premii), which can sound slightly formal or filmy.

Sense 2 : fond of, having deep affection for (as a compound suffix meaning '-loving')
adjective Used productively as the second half of a compound noun+प्रेमी (premii) combination: पुस्तक-प्रेमी (pustak-premii, 'book lover'), पशु-प्रेमी (pashu-premii, 'animal lover'), संगीत-प्रेमी (sangiit-premii, 'music lover'). It does not stand alone to mean 'fond of' -- it needs the attached noun.
Synonyms: अनुरागी (anuraagii, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this suffix everywhere in Indian civic and media language -- प्रकृति-प्रेमी (prakriti-premii, 'nature lover'), देश-प्रेमी (desh-premii, 'patriot', literally 'country-lover'). It's a handy Sanskrit-style building block for turning almost any noun into 'a lover/enthusiast of ___'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to say 'X प्रेमी है' (X premii hai) to mean 'X is fond of it', but this sense only works glued onto the thing being loved as a compound (e.g. फ़िल्म-प्रेमी film-premii, not just प्रेमी premii on its own with a separate object).
