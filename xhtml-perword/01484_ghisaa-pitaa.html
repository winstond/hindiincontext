घिसा-पिटा (ghisaa-pitaa)

adjective A compound adjective built from घिसना (ghisnaa, 'to wear away/erode') and पीटना (piitnaa, 'to beat/hammer'), together meaning 'worn out, threadbare, cliched' -- used both literally of physical objects and figuratively of stale ideas, phrases, or excuses.
Sense 1 : cliched, stale, hackneyed (of ideas, phrases, excuses)
adjective
Synonyms: पुराना (puraanaa), दोहराया हुआ (dohraayaa huaa, repeated), बासी (baasii, stale)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Indians reach for when mocking Bollywood plot formulas or a politician's stock speech -- घिसी-पिटी बातें (ghisii-pitii baaten, 'the same old lines') is a very common complaint about anything recycled one too many times, from film scripts to wedding-season small talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पुराना (puraanaa) to say an idea is 'stale,' but native speakers reach for घिसा-पिटा (ghisaa-pitaa) specifically when something has been repeated so often it has lost its punch -- a tired joke, a stock excuse, a formulaic dialogue.

(yaar, yah vahii ghisii-pitii kahaanii hai jo har doosrii philm men dikhaate hain।)

Sense 2 : worn out, threadbare (of objects)
adjective
Synonyms: पुराना (puraanaa), फटा-पुराना (phataa-puraanaa), जीर्ण (jiirn, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture an old rickshaw seat or a pair of chappals (chappal) rubbed smooth from years of use -- that physical image of things being scraped and beaten down is exactly what this word literally paints before it ever gets used figuratively.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use पुराना (puraanaa) where घिसा-पिटा (ghisaa-pitaa) is meant, but पुराना (puraanaa) just means 'old' while घिसा-पिटा (ghisaa-pitaa) specifically implies visible wear from use, like a coat or shoe rubbed thin.
