ईमानदार (iimaandaar)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'honest'; related to ईमान (iimaan, faith/integrity) and ईमानदारी (iimaandaarii, honesty, the abstract noun).
Sense 1 : honest, trustworthy
adjective
Synonyms: सच्चा (sachchaa), खरा (kharaa, of a person's character or dealings), निष्ठावान (nishthaavaan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: ईमान (iimaan) originally carries a sense of religious faith and moral conviction (from Arabic via Persian/Urdu), so ईमानदार (iimaandaar) literally frames honesty as 'holding onto one's faith/conviction' -- it's a word with real moral weight, often used for shopkeepers, officials, or employees who resist bribery or cheating, not just casual truth-telling. You'll hear it a lot in the context of सरकारी नौकरी (sarkaarii naukrii, government job) discussions, where an ईमानदार अफ़सर (iimaandaar afsar, honest officer) is practically a folk hero given how rare that reputation is perceived to be.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सच्चा (sachchaa) and ईमानदार (iimaandaar) interchangeably, but सच्चा (sachchaa) leans towards 'truthful/genuine' while ईमानदार (iimaandaar) specifically praises integrity in dealings, money, or duty -- a शख्स (shakhs, person) who doesn't cheat or lie for gain. 2) The abstract noun is ईमानदारी (iimaandaarii, honesty) -- learners often wrongly add a feminine ending to ईमानदार (iimaandaar) itself, but as an adjective it is invariant and does not change for gender or number.
