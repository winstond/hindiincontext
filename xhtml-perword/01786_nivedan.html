निवेदन (nivedan)

noun/verb A formal, polite word meaning 'request/appeal'; used both as a noun and inside the compound verb निवेदन करना (nivedan karnaa, 'to request').
Sense 1 : a formal request or appeal
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रार्थना (praarthnaa, formal), अनुरोध (anurodh), गुज़ारिश (guzaarish)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear निवेदन (nivedan) at the start of formal speeches and written appeals -- 'सभी से निवेदन है कि...' ((sabhii se nivedan hai ki, 'it is requested of everyone that...')) is a classic opener for announcements, invitations, and public notices. It sits a notch above अनुरोध (anurodh) in humility and formality, closer to how an employee might address a boss or a devotee might address god.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अनुरोध (anurodh) in every polite-request context, but निवेदन (nivedan) carries a more humble, almost self-lowering tone -- it is what you say when addressing someone you consider above you, like an official, elder, or audience, rather than a peer. 2) As a noun it is grammatically masculine and takes करना (karnaa) to form the verb, not होना (honaa) -- मेरा निवेदन है ((meraa nivedan hai, 'my request is')) is the natural frame, not a direct 'I request' translation.
Sample Sentences:
सभी से निवेदन है कि अपनी सीट पर बैठे रहें। It is requested that everyone remain seated.
(sabhii se nivedan hai ki apnii siit par baithe rahen।)

Sense 2 : to request, to humbly ask
verb, transitive Formed as the compound निवेदन करना (nivedan karnaa); the person being asked is marked with से (se), as in किसी से निवेदन करना (kisii se nivedan karnaa, 'to request someone').
Synonyms: अनुरोध करना (anurodh karnaa), गुज़ारिश करना (guzaarish karnaa), प्रार्थना करना (praarthnaa karnaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb shows up constantly in formal and written registers -- wedding cards, office memos, radio and TV announcements -- often in the passive-feeling frame '...से निवेदन है कि...' rather than a direct 'मैं निवेदन करता हूँ' (main nivedan kartaa hoon, 'I request'), which would sound oddly self-important in most everyday contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mark the person addressed with को (ko) by analogy with English 'request him,' but the natural marking is with से (se) -- आपसे निवेदन है ((aapse nivedan hai, 'it is requested of you')), not आपको निवेदन है.
