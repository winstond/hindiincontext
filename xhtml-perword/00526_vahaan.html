वहाँ (vahaan)

adverb A basic locative adverb meaning 'there', paired naturally with यहाँ (yahaan, 'here') and used constantly to point to a place already established in the conversation.
Sense 1 : in/at that place (physical location)
adverb
Synonyms: उस जगह (us jagah, 'that place'), उधर (udhar, 'that direction/side')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: वहाँ (vahaan) and यहाँ (yahaan) form the basic 'here/there' pair every beginner learns early, and Delhi speakers use वहाँ (vahaan) constantly in daily direction-giving, like वहाँ रख दो (vahaan rakh do, 'put it there') when pointing at a spot in a room or shop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उधर (udhar) when they mean वहाँ (vahaan): उधर (udhar) points toward a direction or general area ('that way'), while वहाँ (vahaan) names a specific place already mentioned or understood. 2) वहाँ (vahaan) is invariant and never changes form for gender, number, or case, unlike many other words a learner has been drilling.

Sense 2 : in that situation/context (figurative/abstract 'there')
adverb Used to refer back to a topic, situation, or point already under discussion rather than a physical location, e.g. वहाँ मुझे लगता है कि... (vahaan mujhe lagtaa hai ki..., 'there [on that point] I think that...').
Synonyms: उस मामले में (us maamle men, 'in that matter')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is simply the same spatial word वहाँ (vahaan) stretched to cover an abstract 'place' in a conversation or argument, much like English speakers say 'there' when they mean 'on that point' -- the mental leap from physical to abstract space works almost identically in both languages.
