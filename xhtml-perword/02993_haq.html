हक़ (haq)

noun A right or entitlement -- something owed to you legally, morally, or by relationship; also used in the fixed idiom हक़ जताना (haq jataanaa) 'to assert one's claim.' Also spelled हक (without the nuqta), especially in casual writing.
Sense 1 : right, entitlement
noun, masculine Takes पर (par) for the thing the right is over ('मेरा हक़ इस ज़मीन पर है', meraa haq is zamiin par hai, 'I have a right over this land') and का (kaa) for the person/thing it belongs to.
Synonyms: अधिकार (adhikaar), हिस्सा (hissaa, sometimes used for a rightful share)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हक़ (haq) is the word you hear in everyday disputes over inheritance, chores, or affection alike -- a mother might say बच्चों का हक़ है (bachchon kaa haq hai, 'it is the children's right') about food or love, not just property. It comes from Arabic/Urdu and carries a slightly more emotionally loaded, conversational feel than the more bureaucratic-sounding अधिकार (adhikaar), which you'd see on a government form.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अधिकार (adhikaar) by default since it is the textbook word, but हक़ (haq) is what people actually say in daily speech and carries a warmer, more personal sense of 'this is rightfully mine/yours' rather than a formal legal register. 2) The idiom हक़ जताना (haq jataanaa, 'to assert a claim/right') is very common in family and relationship contexts and does not translate literally -- it does not mean simply 'to show a right.'
Sample Sentences:
यह मेरा हक़ है, कोई एहसान नहीं। This is my right, not a favor.
(yah meraa haq hai, koii ehsaan nahiin।)
