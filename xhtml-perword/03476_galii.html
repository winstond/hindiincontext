गली (galii)

noun A single-sense everyday noun: a narrow lane or alley, especially the kind found in old neighborhoods and markets.
Sense 1 : narrow lane, alley, street
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सड़क (sadak, wider road, not a close synonym), कूचा (koochaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गली (galii) is the word for the winding, narrow lanes of old city neighborhoods (think Chandni Chowk or any old mohalla (mohallaa)), often just wide enough for a scooter or a cart, lined with homes and small shops. It shows up constantly in everyday directions -- 'is gali mein' (is galii men, 'in this lane') -- and in the common idiom गली-गली (galii-galii, 'through every lane,' i.e. everywhere), and it also carries a slightly gritty, streetwise connotation in phrases like गली का कुत्ता (galii kaa kuttaa, 'street dog') or गली का लड़का (galii kaa ladkaa, 'a street kid/rough boy').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सड़क (sadak) for any 'street,' but गली (galii) specifically means a narrow, often residential lane -- use सड़क (sadak) for a proper road that takes traffic. 2) गली (galii) is feminine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. तंग गली (tang galii, 'narrow lane'), not the masculine ending a learner might default to.
