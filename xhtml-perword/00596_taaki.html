ताकि (taaki)

conjunction A subordinating conjunction meaning 'so that' -- it introduces a clause of purpose and is almost always paired with a subjunctive verb in the clause it introduces.
Sense 1 : so that, in order that
conjunction Introduces a purpose clause; the verb in the ताकि (taaki) clause is normally in the subjunctive (e.g. ...ताकि वह आ सके, '...so that he can come'), not the indicative.
Synonyms: जिससे (jisse), इसलिए कि (islie ki)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Hindi speakers reach for constantly in everyday advice and instructions -- a mother might say जल्दी सो जाओ ताकि सुबह जल्दी उठ सको (jaldii so jaao taaki subah jaldii uth sako, 'go to sleep early so that you can wake up early in the morning'). It is a great anchor for practicing the subjunctive, since ताकि (taaki) almost demands one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often keep the verb after ताकि (taaki) in the simple present or future instead of the subjunctive -- ताकि वह आए (taaki vah aae, 'so that he may come') is correct, not ताकि वह आएगा (taaki vah aaegaa). 2) ताकि (taaki) is not interchangeable with क्योंकि (kyonki, 'because') -- one gives purpose, the other gives reason, and confusing them reverses the logic of the sentence. 3) The clause introduced by ताकि (taaki) always comes after the main clause it explains; putting it first sounds unnatural.
