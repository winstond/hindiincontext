इश्तहार (ishtahaar)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'advertisement' or 'public notice/poster' -- an Urdu-origin word still common in spoken Hindi, especially for printed notices, posters, and announcements.
Sense 1 : advertisement, public notice, poster
noun, masculine
Synonyms: विज्ञापन (vigyaapan), पोस्टर (postar, for a printed/pasted notice specifically), घोषणा (ghoshnaa, for an official announcement)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear इश्तहार (ishtahaar) used for old-style notices -- a poster for a missing person, a court or police announcement, a wanted notice, or a hand-pasted ad on a wall -- giving it a slightly old-world, print-era flavor compared to the more modern-sounding विज्ञापन (vigyaapan), which dominates in TV and digital advertising contexts. It comes from Urdu/Persian, reflecting the strong Persianate layer of everyday Hindi-Urdu vocabulary in Delhi speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for विज्ञापन (vigyaapan) and miss that इश्तहार (ishtahaar) is used interchangeably in everyday speech, especially for printed notices pasted on walls, missing-person notices, or government announcements, not just commercial ads. 2) The verb usually paired with it is लगाना (lagaanaa, 'to put up') or निकालना (nikaalnaa, 'to issue/put out'), rather than बनाना (banaanaa) which sounds off for the act of publishing or posting it.

(diivaar par ek puraanaa ishtahaar chipkaa thaa।)
