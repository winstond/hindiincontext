सुलाना (sulaanaa)

verb The causative of सोना (sonaa, 'to sleep') -- means 'to put someone to sleep, to lay someone down to rest.'
Sense 1 : to put to sleep, to lull to sleep
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic word for the nightly ritual of putting a child to bed -- बच्चे को सुलाना (bachche ko sulaanaa) is something every Indian parent or grandparent talks about, often paired with गाना (gaanaa, 'song') or लोरी (lorii, 'lullaby'). It is the direct causative partner of सोना (sonaa, 'to sleep'), so if you know that verb, this one follows naturally in form and logic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse सुलाना (sulaanaa) with सोना (sonaa) itself -- सुलाना (sulaanaa) always needs someone else as the object being put to sleep, it cannot mean 'to sleep' on its own. 2) Do not confuse with सुनाना (sunaanaa, 'to tell/narrate'), which looks and sounds similar but is unrelated in meaning.

माँ रोज़ रात को बच्चे को सुलाती है। Mother puts the child to sleep every night.
(maan roz raat ko bachche ko sulaatii hai।)

Sense 2 : to lay down, to make someone lie down (not necessarily to sleep)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: लिटाना (litaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used when the point is simply getting someone horizontal -- a nurse or mother saying मरीज़ को सुलाना (mariiz ko sulaanaa) is about positioning the patient to rest, with actual sleep being incidental. लिटाना (litaanaa) is a close cousin that leans even more toward the physical 'lay flat' sense without any sleep connotation.
