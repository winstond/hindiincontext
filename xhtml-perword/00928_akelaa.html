अकेला (akelaa)

adjective/adverb A common adjective meaning 'alone' or 'single,' with an extended sense meaning 'lonely'; also occasionally used as an adverb meaning 'alone, by oneself.'
Sense 1 : alone, by oneself, unaccompanied
adjective
Synonyms: एकल (ekal, formal), तनहा (tanahaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday life: वह अकेला रहता है (vah akelaa rahtaa hai, 'he lives alone') is a very ordinary sentence, since living alone is often remarked upon in a culture where joint families and shared homes are the norm. The word itself carries a faint echo of that social backdrop -- being अकेला (akelaa) is noteworthy precisely because togetherness is the default expectation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that this adjective agrees in gender and number with the noun it describes, so a woman alone is अकेली (akelii), not अकेला (akelaa). 2) Do not confuse with केवल (keval) or सिर्फ़ (sirf), which mean 'only' -- अकेला (akelaa) describes being physically or situationally unaccompanied, not exclusivity.

(vah akelaa rahtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : lonely, isolated (emotional sense)
adjective This figurative sense extends the physical 'alone' into an emotional register, describing a felt state of loneliness rather than mere physical solitude.
Synonyms: तनहा (tanahaa), उदास (udaas, sad rather than lonely, related mood)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The line between 'alone' and 'lonely' in English is a single lexical split, but in Hindi अकेला (akelaa) carries both, and context or tone of voice usually signals which one is meant -- मुझे अकेला लगता है (mujhe akelaa lagtaa hai, 'I feel lonely') leans clearly emotional thanks to the दात्ive-subject frame with लगना (lagnaa).

Sense 3 : alone, single-handedly, on one's own
adverb Used adverbially to emphasize that an action was done without help or company, often in constructions like अकेला ही (akelaa hii, 'all by himself/alone').
Synonyms: तनहा (tanahaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This adverbial use often shows up in praise or complaint about someone shouldering a task without help -- वह अकेले सब कुछ संभालती है (vah akele sab kuchh sambhaaltii hai, 'she manages everything all by herself') is the kind of line you will hear about a hardworking mother or a struggling shopkeeper.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for a separate adverb when Hindi simply reuses अकेला (akelaa) itself, still agreeing with the subject, e.g. उसने अकेले यह काम किया (usne akele yah kaam kiyaa, 'he did this work alone') where the oblique-like form अकेले (akele) is used adverbially.
