इच्छा करना (ichchhaa karnaa)

verb A conjunct verb built from the noun इच्छा (ichchhaa, 'desire, wish') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'), meaning 'to wish for' or 'to desire' something. Closely related to the more common verb चाहना (chaahnaa), which expresses the same idea more directly and is used far more often in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : to wish, to desire (something)
verb, transitive The desired thing is usually marked with की (kii) as in किसी चीज़ की इच्छा करना (kisii chiiz kii ichchhaa karnaa, 'to wish for something'), rather than taking a direct object the way चाहना (chaahnaa) does.
Synonyms: चाहना (chaahnaa), कामना करना (kaamnaa karnaa, more formal/literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will hear इच्छा (ichchhaa) far more often as a standalone noun in phrases like मेरी इच्छा है (merii ichchhaa hai, 'my wish is...') than paired with करना (karnaa) as a verb phrase; the noun+है (hai) construction feels more natural to most speakers than the conjunct verb form. It has a gentle, almost reflective tone, well suited to talking about hopes, spiritual wishes, or long-term desires rather than immediate wants like hunger or thirst.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use this the way they would चाहना (chaahnaa), putting the desired noun directly before करना (karnaa) without की (kii); native speakers usually say इच्छा है कि (ichchhaa hai ki, 'the wish is that...') or X की इच्छा करना (X kii ichchhaa karnaa) rather than a bare object. 2) In casual conversation, चाहना (chaahnaa) is the default verb for 'want/wish'; इच्छा करना (ichchhaa karnaa) sounds a shade more formal or written, so overusing it in casual speech can sound stiff.
Sample Sentences:
मैं चाहता हूँ कि तुम खुश रहो, यही मेरी इच्छा है। I want you to be happy, this is my wish.
(main chaahtaa hoon ki tum khush raho, yahii merii ichchhaa hai।)
