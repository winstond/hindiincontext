मरम्मत करना (marammat karnaa)

verb A compound built from the noun मरम्मत (marammat, 'repair'), a loanword from Arabic/Persian via Urdu, plus the light verb करना (karnaa, 'to do'). The whole phrase means 'to repair, to fix' -- a single lexical unit even though it's written as two words.
Sense 1 : to repair, to fix (something broken)
verb, transitive The noun मरम्मत (marammat) takes की (kii) before करना (karnaa) when the object is stated: गाड़ी की मरम्मत करना (gaadii kii marammat karnaa, 'to repair the car'). The thing being fixed attaches to मरम्मत (marammat) via का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke), not directly to करना (karnaa).
Synonyms: ठीक करना (thiik karnaa, more everyday/general), सुधारना (sudhaarnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly with mechanics and handymen -- मिस्त्री (mistrii) is the guy who does मरम्मत (marammat) on everything from scooters to ceiling fans. In everyday chatter, though, people often reach for the simpler ठीक करना (thiik karnaa, 'to make right/fix') instead, saving मरम्मत करना (marammat karnaa) for slightly more formal or written contexts like a repair shop's sign or a bill.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to treat मरम्मत (marammat) as directly taking the object with करना (karnaa), saying गाड़ी मरम्मत करना (gaadii marammat karnaa) instead of the correct गाड़ी की मरम्मत करना (gaadii kii marammat karnaa). 2) Because मरम्मत (marammat) is grammatically the object of करना (karnaa) in this compound, past tense takes ने (ne) on the subject: उसने गाड़ी की मरम्मत की (usne gaadii kii marammat kii, 'he repaired the car') -- learners sometimes forget ने (ne) here since 'repair' feels like an intransitive event to them.

(mistrii kal merii ghadii kii marammat karegaa।)
