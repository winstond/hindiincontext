संस्कृत (sanskrit)

noun/adjective Two main uses: as a proper noun, the name of the Sanskrit language (sanskrit); as an adjective, meaning 'refined, cultured, processed/purified' -- the literal sense behind the language's name.
Sense 1 : the Sanskrit language
noun, feminine
Synonyms: देववाणी (devvaanii, literary), संस्कृत भाषा (sanskrit bhaashaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Every Hindi speaker meets संस्कृत (sanskrit) in school as the ancient language of the Vedas, Sanskrit dramas, and countless mantras still recited at weddings and pujas today. It is one of India's 22 scheduled languages and is taught as a compulsory subject in many schools, though almost no one speaks it as a mother tongue anymore -- it lives on mainly in ritual, scholarship, and as the root-stock for a huge share of modern Hindi vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat संस्कृत (sanskrit) as an adjective meaning 'Sanskrit' (as in 'Sanskrit word'), but for that you need the adjectival form संस्कृत का (sanskrit kaa) or संस्कृत-भाषा का (sanskrit-bhaashaa kaa) -- e.g. संस्कृत का शब्द (sanskrit kaa shabd, 'a Sanskrit word'), not a direct compounding.

Sense 2 : refined, cultured, purified
adjective This is the literal, older sense of the word -- 'made complete/refined' -- from which the language's name derives; the language was seen as the 'refined' or 'perfected' speech, in contrast to the natural, unpolished प्राकृत (praakrit, 'natural/vernacular') languages.
Synonyms: परिष्कृत (parishkrit, literary), सुसंस्कृत (susanskrit), शिष्ट (shisht)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Knowing that संस्कृत (sanskrit) literally means 'well-made' or 'refined' unlocks a lot of related vocabulary: संस्कार (sanskaar, 'refinement, upbringing, rite') and संस्कृति (sanskriti, 'culture') all share this root. So when Hindi speakers talk about a person having अच्छे संस्कार (achchhe sanskaar, 'good upbringing/values'), they are drawing on the same idea of being 'shaped and refined' that gives Sanskrit its name.
