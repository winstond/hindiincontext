चुनौती (chunautii)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'challenge,' used both literally (a dare) and abstractly (a difficult task or test one faces).
Sense 1 : challenge, difficult task
noun, feminine Very commonly paired with the verb देना (denaa, 'to give') to mean 'to challenge someone,' and with मिलना (milnaa) or का सामना करना (kaa saamnaa karnaa, 'to face') to mean 'to face a challenge.'
Synonyms: मुश्किल (mushkil, meaning scope: difficulty in general), कठिनाई (kathinaaii, meaning scope: difficulty in general)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word constantly in Indian news and political speech -- देश के सामने बड़ी चुनौती है (desh ke saamne badii chunautii hai, 'there is a big challenge before the country') is a stock phrase in editorials and speeches. It has a slightly formal, earnest ring to it -- in casual chat about everyday annoyances, people are just as likely to say मुश्किल (mushkil) or दिक्कत (dikkat).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मुश्किल (mushkil) or समस्या (samasyaa, 'problem') when they mean चुनौती (chunautii); चुनौती carries a sense of being tested or having to prove oneself, not just a plain difficulty or problem. 2) The common verb pairing is चुनौती देना (chunautii denaa, 'to throw down a challenge') for challenging someone, and चुनौती का सामना करना (chunautii kaa saamnaa karnaa, 'to face a challenge') for confronting one -- mixing these up is a frequent error.

देश के सामने बड़ी चुनौती है। There is a big challenge before the country.
(desh ke saamne badii chunautii hai।)
