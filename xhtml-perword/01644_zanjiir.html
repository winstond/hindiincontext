ज़ंजीर (zanjiir)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'chain' -- the literal metal chain, and by extension a figurative chain/shackle (of habit, duty, or circumstance).
Sense 1 : chain (physical, metal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सिकड़ी (sikdii, less common), साँकल (saankal, often a door-latch chain specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the ज़ंजीर (zanjiir) on a street dog, a bicycle, or an old wooden door bolted with a साँकल (saankal) -- this is the everyday, physical sense a learner will hear first in the market or at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चेन (chen) since it looks like the English loan; ज़ंजीर (zanjiir) is the native word and is far more common for an actual metal chain (dog chain, door chain, bicycle chain), while चेन (chen) tends to show up for necklace-type chains or handbag straps. 2) Don't drop the nuqta sound when speaking -- ज़ (z) is a distinct 'z' sound borrowed from Persian/Urdu, not the plain ज (j) 'j' sound.

Sense 2 : figurative chain, shackle, bond (of habit, duty, or oppression)
noun, feminine Very common in the plural-feel phrase ज़ंजीरों में जकड़ा होना (zanjiiron men jakdaa honaa, 'to be shackled/bound') used metaphorically for being trapped by circumstance, tradition, or addiction.
Synonyms: बंधन (bandhan), जकड़ (jakad, less common, more 'grip/clutch')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the ज़ंजीर (zanjiir) of Bollywood dialogue and freedom-struggle rhetoric -- chains of gulami (slavery), of samaj (society), of purani rivayaton (old customs) -- a word Hindi reaches for whenever English would say 'shackles' or 'bonds' in a speech or song.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use this only literally and miss how naturally it extends to abstract bondage -- e.g. गुलामी की ज़ंजीरें (gulaamii kii zanjiiren, 'the chains of slavery') or आदत की ज़ंजीर (aadat kii zanjiir, 'the chain of habit') -- this figurative use is at least as common as the literal one in speech and writing.
