ख़्वाब देखना (khvaab dekhnaa)

verb A compound phrase meaning 'to dream' -- both literally (while asleep) and figuratively (to aspire, to fantasize). ख़्वाब (khvaab) is a Persian-origin word for 'dream', paired here with देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see') -- Hindi frames dreaming as 'seeing a dream,' not 'having' one.
Sense 1 : to dream, aspire (figurative)
verb, transitive The thing aspired to is typically marked with का (kaa) before ख़्वाब (khvaab), e.g. तरक्की का ख़्वाब देखना (tarakkii kaa khvaab dekhnaa, 'to dream of progress').
Synonyms: सपना देखना (sapnaa dekhnaa), आकांक्षा रखना (aakaankshaa rakhnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative sense is everywhere in motivational speech, career talk, and film dialogue -- ख़्वाब पूरा करना (khvaab pooraa karnaa, 'to fulfill a dream') is its natural completing partner, so listen for the two phrases used together.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'to dream big' word-for-word; the natural Hindi idiom is बड़े ख़्वाब देखना (bade khvaab dekhnaa, 'to dream big dreams'), keeping ख़्वाब (khvaab) as the plural object rather than using an adverb.

Sense 2 : to dream (while asleep)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: सपना देखना (sapnaa dekhnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ख़्वाब (khvaab) has a slightly more poetic, Urdu-flavored feel than its native counterpart सपना (sapnaa); you'll hear it often in Bollywood songs and shayari, but in plain daily speech सपना देखना (sapnaa dekhnaa) is at least as common.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'have a dream' with होना (honaa) or पाना (paanaa); Hindi always uses देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see') here -- you 'see' a dream, you don't 'have' one. 2) The dream content is usually marked with का (kaa) or expressed as a separate clause, not as a direct object needing ने (ne) -- e.g. उसने ख़्वाब देखा कि... (usne khvaab dekhaa ki..., 'he dreamed that...').
