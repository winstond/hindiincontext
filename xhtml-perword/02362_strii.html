स्त्री (strii)

noun A formal/literary-leaning word for 'woman', used especially in written, official, and biological/grammatical contexts; in everyday spoken Hindi, learners will hear औरत (aurat) or महिला (mahilaa) far more often.
Sense 1 : woman (adult female person)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: औरत (aurat), महिला (mahilaa, more formal/polite, common in speech), नारी (naarii, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet स्त्री (strii) constantly on official forms (स्त्री/पुरुष, strii/purush, 'female/male'), in NGO and government language around women's issues (स्त्री शिक्षा, strii shikshaa, 'women's education'), and in literary or feminist writing, where it carries a certain gravity that महिला (mahilaa) lacks. In daily bazaar or family conversation, though, लोग औरत (aurat) या महिला (mahilaa) ही बोलते हैं (log aurat yaa mahilaa hii bolte hain, 'people just say auraat or mahilaa').
Common Mistakes: 1) Using स्त्री (strii) in casual conversation where महिला (mahilaa) or औरत (aurat) would sound far more natural; स्त्री (strii) tends to show up in writing, forms, and set phrases rather than in everyday chit-chat. 2) Learners sometimes treat स्त्री (strii) as rude or blunt like औरत (aurat) can sometimes feel in certain contexts, but स्त्री (strii) actually carries a neutral-to-respectful, almost academic tone.

Sense 2 : feminine gender (grammar)
noun, feminine Used almost exclusively inside the compound स्त्रीलिंग (striiling) or in grammar-book contrasts with पुल्लिंग (pulling, 'masculine'); rarely stands alone with this meaning.
Synonyms: स्त्रीलिंग (striiling, grammar term, 'feminine gender')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Any Hindi grammar textbook you pick up will pair स्त्रीलिंग (striiling) and पुल्लिंग (pulling) to teach noun gender -- this is the one place where स्त्री (strii) shows up in even fairly basic learner materials, even though the standalone word itself is more literary.
