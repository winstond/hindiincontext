दर्शन देना (darshan denaa)

verb A conjunct expression built on दर्शन (darshan, 'sight/viewing') plus देना (denaa, 'to give') -- literally 'to give a viewing (of oneself)'. Has a devotional core sense and a common ironic/social extension for a person who rarely shows up. Related to the standalone noun दर्शन (darshan) and the verb देना (denaa).
Sense 1 : for a deity, saint, or revered figure to grant a sighting/audience of themselves
verb, transitive The one who is seen is the grammatical subject and takes ने in the perfective (e.g. भगवान ने दर्शन दिए, bhagvaan ne darshan die, 'God granted a viewing'); the devotee is usually marked with को.
Synonyms: दिखना (dikhnaa, not honorific)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase sits at the heart of Hindu temple-going: standing in a long line at a temple like the one at Vaishno Devi or even a small neighborhood mandir, people say wonderingly 'आज भगवान के दर्शन हो गए' (aaj bhagvaan ke darshan ho gae, 'today I got to see/be seen by God') -- the darshan is understood as something the deity actively gives, not something the devotee merely takes for themselves.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use this for ordinary people meeting each other in a plain, neutral way; it is reserved for gods, gurus, or greatly revered figures, so using it about a friend sounds either sarcastic or oddly grandiose unless that tone is intended. 2) The direction is easy to reverse: it is the deity/revered person who 'gives' दर्शन (darshan), and the devotee 'gets' or 'has' it (दर्शन पाना, darshan paanaa, or दर्शन होना, darshan honaa) -- saying a devotee 'gives दर्शन (darshan)' to the deity is backwards.

Sense 2 : to finally show up or make an appearance, said teasingly of someone who has been scarce
verb, transitive Built on the same frame as the devotional sense but applied to an ordinary person, which is precisely what generates the joke; almost always used with आजकल (aajkal) or कभी-कभी (kabhii-kabhii) to underline the rarity.
Synonyms: नज़र आना (nazar aanaa, neutral, no irony), दिखना (dikhnaa, neutral, no irony)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A classic line when a friend who has vanished for weeks finally turns up: 'अरे, आज दर्शन दिए आपने!' (are, aaj darshan die aapne, 'oh look, you've finally graced us with your presence today!') -- borrowing the grandeur of the temple sense to poke affectionate fun at someone acting too important or too busy to visit.
Common Mistakes: 1) This use only works as gentle mockery or teasing among friends/family; using it in a genuinely respectful context about an ordinary person (rather than a deity or revered elder) can sound like you are being sarcastic even when you don't mean to be.
