समझना (samajhnaa)

verb समझना (samajhnaa) is the everyday verb for 'to understand' or 'comprehend', and it stretches to mean 'to consider/regard as' when paired with a noun or adjective describing how someone or something is perceived. Its causative partner समझाना (samjhaanaa) means 'to explain, make someone understand' -- a separate lemma, not a form of this word.
Sense 1 : to understand, comprehend
verb, transitive
Synonyms: ग्रहण करना (grahan karnaa, formal), बूझना (boojhnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A Delhi teacher's favorite check-in line is समझे? (samjhe?, 'understood?') tossed at the end of an explanation -- it is casual, warm, and expects a quick nod or हाँ (haan) back. Pair this lemma mentally with समझाना (samjhaanaa): one is 'to get it,' the other is 'to give it' to someone else.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to मैं समझता हूँ (main samajhtaa hoon) for 'I understand,' but in everyday speech the dative construction मुझे समझ में आता है (mujhe samajh men aataa hai, 'it comes into my understanding') is at least as common and often sounds more natural for on-the-spot comprehension. 2) Don't confuse समझना (samajhnaa, to understand) with its causative look-alike समझाना (samjhaanaa, to explain) -- one describes your own grasp of something, the other describes making someone else grasp it.

Sense 2 : to consider, regard as, assume
verb, transitive Takes the frame [object] + [noun/adjective] + समझना, as in उसने मुझे बच्चा समझा (usne mujhe bachchaa samjhaa, 'he considered me a child') -- the second element states what the object is being taken as.
Synonyms: मानना (maannaa), गिनना (ginnaa, less common in this sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in family scoldings and friendly arguments: मुझे बेवकूफ़ मत समझो (mujhe bevkoof mat samjho, 'don't take me for a fool') is a classic retort you'll hear across Delhi households and markets alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) The phrase समझ लो (samajh lo, 'just take it as...') is often used rhetorically to mean 'consider it done' or 'assume that's the case' -- learners sometimes translate it too literally instead of hearing it as a casual verbal shrug.
