तोता (totaa)

noun Primarily the bird name 'parrot,' with a common figurative sense describing someone who repeats things mechanically without understanding, as in a well-known idiom about rote learning.
Sense 1 : the bird
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: तोता (totaa) is a familiar sight and sound in Delhi -- flocks of green parakeets (locally just called तोता (totaa)) screech over parks and terraces at dusk. In folk belief and old stories, a person's जान (jaan, life force) is sometimes imagined as living inside a तोता (totaa), kept safe far away -- this is the famous 'तोते में जान' (tote men jaan, 'life is in the parrot') motif from folk tales, where the villain cannot be killed until his parrot is found and killed.

Sense 2 : someone who repeats things by rote, without understanding
noun, masculine Almost always used inside the fixed idiom तोता रटंत (totaa ratant, 'parrot-style cramming') or in phrases like तोते की तरह रटना (tote kii tarah ratnaa, 'to memorize like a parrot') rather than standing alone as a plain noun for a person.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Indian school culture has a strong running joke about तोता रटंत विद्या (totaa ratant vidyaa, 'parrot-cramming knowledge') -- students who memorize answers without understanding them are teased as behaving like a तोता (totaa). It is a very relatable dig at rote-learning culture that every Indian student has heard at least once from a teacher or parent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use तोता (totaa) alone to mean 'a person who just repeats things,' but native speakers usually need the fuller phrase (तोते की तरह (tote kii tarah, 'like a parrot') + a verb like रटना (ratnaa, to cram) or बोलना (bolnaa, to speak)) to make the comparison clear -- the bare noun by itself reads as just 'parrot,' not the figurative sense.
