गुठली (guthlii)

noun A simple noun with one core meaning: the hard seed/stone found inside fruits like mangoes, plums, and lychees; a figurative extension covers small hard lumps or knots that feel similar.
Sense 1 : the stone/pit of a fruit
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बीज (biij, seed in general, not just the hard stone)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi kid about mango season and they will talk about sucking the आम की गुठली (aam kii guthlii, 'the mango's pit') clean after the fruit is gone -- it is practically a rite of summer. Grandmothers also sometimes save dried गुठली (guthlii) from tamarind or other fruits for small household uses, like folk remedies or games.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बीज (biij) for any fruit seed, but गुठली (guthlii) is specifically the hard, woody stone found in fruits like mango (आम, aam), lychee (लीची, liichii), or plum (आलूबुखारा, aaloobukhaaraa) -- बीज (biij) fits better for small soft seeds like those in tomatoes or watermelon.

Sense 2 : a small hard lump or knot (figurative)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गाँठ (gaanth, knot, used for lumps in cloth or rope rather than flesh)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Sometimes used loosely for a small hard lump under the skin or in food (like an undissolved lump in dough), by comparison to a fruit stone -- the image of something small, hard, and central carries over naturally.
