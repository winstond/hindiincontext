हड्डी (haddii)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'bone' (haddii), with a common figurative extension for someone being extremely thin, and a life in several everyday idioms about hard work and stubbornness.
Sense 1 : bone (body part)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हड्डी तोड़ देना (haddii tod denaa, to break someone's bones) is a common threat/warning heard from angry parents or in Bollywood scuffles, said half in jest more often than literally -- it is the Hindi equivalent of 'I'll break every bone in your body.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat हड्डी (haddii) as invariant like some other body-part-adjacent words, but it inflects as a regular feminine ी-ending noun (हड्डियाँ haddiyaan in the plural, हड्डियों haddiyon in the oblique plural) -- worth watching for since bone counts often come up in the plural (e.g. 'ribs').

Sense 2 : very thin, skin-and-bones (figurative)
noun, feminine Used predicatively in phrases like हड्डी ही हड्डी रह गई है (haddii hii haddii rah gaii hai, 'nothing but bones is left') or किसी का हड्डी-हड्डी होना (haddii-haddii honaa) to describe someone who has become alarmingly thin, often from illness or overwork.
Synonyms: हड्डियों का ढांचा (haddiyon kaa dhaanchaa, 'a frame of bones', descriptive phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A worried Indian grandmother seeing a grandchild return from hostel or a foreign trip might exclaim हड्डी-हड्डी हो गया है (haddii-haddii ho gayaa hai, 'he's become nothing but bones'), a very typical scene of loving over-concern about weight loss.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense almost always appears with reduplication (हड्डी-हड्डी haddii-haddii) or in a fixed phrase rather than as a bare noun -- using हड्डी alone to mean 'thin person' will sound incomplete to a native ear.

Sense 3 : stubbornness, toughness of character (figurative, idiomatic)
noun, feminine Lives mainly in the idiom हड्डियों में जान होना (haddiyon men jaan honaa, 'to have life/strength in one's bones', meaning to have inner grit or stamina despite appearances) and similar bones-as-seat-of-strength expressions.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Hindi frequently locates inner toughness or endurance in the bones rather than the heart or willpower, as English does -- an old laborer might be described as बूढ़ा है पर हड्डियों में अभी जान है (boodhaa hai par haddiyon men abhii jaan hai, 'he's old but there's still life in his bones'), praising quiet physical resilience.
Common Mistakes: 1) This meaning does not stand alone -- it only surfaces inside set phrases about strength or stubbornness residing 'in the bones,' so learners should not try to use हड्डी by itself to mean 'grit.'
Sample Sentences:
