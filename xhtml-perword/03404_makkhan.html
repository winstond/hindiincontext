मक्खन (makkhan)

noun Primarily the dairy product 'butter' (makkhan); also lives in a very common idiom for flattery -- 'applying butter' to someone means buttering them up.
Sense 1 : butter (dairy product)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian kitchens मक्खन (makkhan) usually means the fresh, home-churned white butter (as opposed to salted table butter, which is often called 'butter' in English or amul butter by brand), the kind associated with Krishna as makhan-chor, the 'butter thief', in childhood stories and bhajans.

Sense 2 : flattery (idiomatic, 'buttering up')
noun, masculine Almost always appears inside the fixed expression मक्खन लगाना (makkhan lagaanaa, 'to apply butter') meaning to flatter someone, often to get a favor.
Synonyms: चापलूसी (chaaploosii), चिकनी-चुपड़ी बातें (chiknii-chupdii baaten, flattering talk)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone a मक्खनबाज़ (makkhanbaaz, a smooth flatterer) or saying मत मक्खन लगा (mat makkhan lagaa, 'don't butter me up') is everyday, slightly teasing office and college banter in Delhi-NCR -- it pokes fun at someone being obviously sweet-talky to get something out of you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use मक्खन (makkhan) alone to mean 'flattery' -- the idiomatic sense really only exists inside the verb phrase मक्खन लगाना (makkhan lagaanaa), not as a standalone noun for the concept.
Sample Sentences:
मत मक्खन लगा, मुझे पता है तुझे क्या चाहिए। Don't butter me up, I know what you want.
(mat makkhan lagaa, mujhe pataa hai tujhe kyaa chaahie।)
