धुआँ (dhuaan)

noun A concrete everyday noun meaning 'smoke,' with a common figurative extension used for gossip/scandal or vague rumors that 'spread' the way smoke does.
Sense 1 : smoke (from fire, cooking, exhaust, etc.)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a Delhi winter morning: someone lights a coal sigड़ी (sigdii) or a roadside fire to keep warm, and the धुआँ (dhuaan) stings your eyes before you even see the flame. This is the word you will hear constantly around Diwali firecrackers, tandoors, and the capital's notorious smog conversations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the chandrabindu and write धुआ (dhuaa) instead of धुआँ (dhuaan) -- the nasalization is part of the standard spelling. 2) With verbs like निकलना (nikalnaa, 'to emerge') or उठना (uthnaa, 'to rise'), धुआँ (dhuaan) is the subject, not the object -- learners sometimes try to force a transitive frame ('to make smoke') where a native speaker would just say the smoke itself rose or came out.
Sample Sentences:

(sigdii se dhuaan nikal rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : rumor, scandal, talk that spreads without confirmed substance
noun, masculine Almost always appears in the set phrase धुआँ उठना (dhuaan uthnaa, 'for talk/rumor to arise') or धुआँ फैलना (dhuaan phailnaa, 'for talk to spread'), echoing the literal image of smoke rising and spreading.
Synonyms: अफ़वाह (afvaah, more common word for 'rumor')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: There is an old saying, 'जहाँ आग होती है वहीं धुआँ उठता है' (jahaan aag hotii hai vahiin dhuaan uthtaa hai, 'where there is fire, smoke rises there too') -- the Hindi version of 'there's no smoke without fire,' used when gossip is suspected to have some truth behind it.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is only used for spreading talk or scandal, not for smoke-related idioms about anger or trouble -- do not confuse it with idioms built on आग (aag, 'fire'), which cover a different figurative range (temper, danger).

(unke talaaq kii baat to bas dhuaan hai, abhii kuchh pakkaa nahiin hai।)
