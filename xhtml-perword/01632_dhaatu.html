धातु (dhaatu)

noun Primarily 'metal' in everyday and scientific speech; also carries a technical grammatical sense ('verb root') that learners of Hindi grammar will meet.
Sense 1 : metal (the material)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear धातु (dhaatu) constantly in contexts like जेवर किस धातु का है (jevar kis dhaatu kaa hai, 'what metal is this jewelry made of'), since Delhi's jewelry markets (like Karol Bagh or Chandni Chowk) are full of shoppers asking exactly this. It also shows up in chemistry class as the general term for 'metal' versus अधातु (adhaatu, 'non-metal').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat धातु (dhaatu) as masculine by default since many -तु/-नु ending nouns are masculine; it is actually feminine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. यह धातु मजबूत है (yah dhaatu majboot hai, 'this metal is strong').
Sample Sentences:
यह जेवर किस धातु का है? What metal is this jewelry made of?
(yah jevar kis dhaatu kaa hai?)

Sense 2 : verb root (grammatical term)
noun, feminine Used in grammar instruction to refer to the base form of a verb before endings are attached, e.g. चल (chal) is the धातु (dhaatu) of चलना (chalnaa).
Synonyms: जड़ (jad, meaning scope: root in general, not specifically grammatical)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense belongs to the classroom and grammar-book world rather than daily conversation; you will meet it if you study Hindi verb conjugation formally, where every verb is said to have a धातु (dhaatu) to which suffixes are added.
Sample Sentences:

(hindii men har kriyaa kii ek dhaatu hotii hai।)
