प्रकार (prakaar)

noun/adverb A single-sense word meaning 'type/kind/way' -- used both as a countable noun and inside a fixed set of adverbial phrases meaning 'in this/that way'.
Sense 1 : type, kind, sort
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तरह (tarah), किस्म (kism), भाँति (bhaanti, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: प्रकार (prakaar) is the word you meet on packaging, in school textbooks, and in official Hindi -- 'yeh kitne prakar ke hote hain' is classic classroom phrasing. तरह (tarah) is its everyday spoken twin; reach for प्रकार when the tone is a notch more formal or explanatory.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to तरह (tarah) in speech and forget प्रकार (prakaar) is the go-to word in written, formal, or explanatory Hindi -- news, textbooks, official notices. 2) The counting pattern is 'number + प्रकार + के + noun' (e.g., तीन प्रकार के फल (tiin prakaar ke phal, 'three kinds of fruit')) -- learners sometimes drop के (ke) by analogy with English.
Sample Sentences:
यह फल कितने प्रकार के होते हैं? How many types of this fruit are there?
(yah phal kitne prakaar ke hote hain?)

Sense 2 : in this/that way, manner
adverb Occurs in fixed frames such as इस प्रकार (is prakaar, 'in this way, thus') and किस प्रकार (kis prakaar, 'in what way, how') -- functions as a formal, writerly substitute for ऐसे (aise) or कैसे (kaise).
Synonyms: तरह से (tarah se), भाँति (bhaanti, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear इस प्रकार (is prakaar) constantly in news broadcasts and formal speeches, almost like a written-register cousin of ऐसे (aise) or इस तरह (is tarah) -- it signals 'and so, in this manner' when someone is summing up or explaining a process.
Common Mistakes: 1) इस प्रकार (is prakaar) is common in essays and speeches as a connector meaning 'thus/in this manner' -- learners often reach for इसलिए (islie, 'therefore') instead, but the two aren't interchangeable: इस प्रकार describes manner, इसलिए gives a reason.
