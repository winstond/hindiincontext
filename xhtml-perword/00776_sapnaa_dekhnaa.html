सपना देखना (sapnaa dekhnaa)

verb A compound expression built from सपना (sapnaa, 'dream' as a noun) plus देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see'); Hindi frames dreaming as 'seeing a dream,' not as a verb of its own. Covers both the literal night-time dream and the figurative sense of aspiring or fantasizing.
Sense 1 : to dream (while asleep)
verb, transitive सपना (sapnaa) takes को (ko) marking on the experiencer in the common frame X को सपना आना (X ko sapnaa aanaa, 'X gets a dream') as an alternative construction, but with देखना (dekhnaa) the dreamer is the ने (ne)-marked subject, since देखना (dekhnaa) is transitive.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Where English treats dreaming as its own verb, Hindi treats a dream as something you 'see' with your eyes closed -- it sits in the same family of body-and-perception idioms as नींद आना (niind aanaa, 'sleep arrives') for falling asleep, reinforcing how Hindi often frames internal experiences as things that happen to or in front of you rather than actions you perform outright.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say केवल सपना (keval sapnaa) alone to mean 'I dreamed,' but Hindi needs the verb देखना (dekhnaa) to complete the thought -- सपना (sapnaa) by itself is just the noun 'dream.' 2) Because देखना (dekhnaa) is transitive here, the past tense requires ने (ne) marking on the subject: मैंने सपना देखा (mainne sapnaa dekhaa, 'I saw a dream'), which trips up learners who associate 'dreaming' with an involuntary, subject-only verb like सोना (sonaa).

Sense 2 : to dream of, aspire to, fantasize about (figurative)
verb, transitive Commonly takes an infinitive or possessive-oblique object, as in सफल होने का सपना देखना (saphal hone kaa sapnaa dekhnaa, 'to dream of becoming successful') or किसी चीज़ का सपना देखना (kisii chiiz kaa sapnaa dekhnaa, 'to dream of something'), with का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) linking the goal to सपना (sapnaa).
Synonyms: ख़्वाब देखना (khvaab dekhnaa, more poetic/Urdu-flavored variant), कल्पना करना (kalpnaa karnaa, to imagine, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind big, aspirational Hindi phrases -- parents telling children बड़े सपने देखो (bade sapne dekho, 'dream big') is a very familiar piece of everyday encouragement in Indian households and classrooms, much like its English equivalent but carrying real emotional weight around ambition and family hopes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to attach the aspiration directly to देखना (dekhnaa) with के लिए (ke lie) instead of using the का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) + सपना (sapnaa) frame, producing an unnatural sentence -- the correct pattern is X का सपना देखना (X kaa sapnaa dekhnaa), not X के लिए सपना देखना (X ke lie sapnaa dekhnaa).
