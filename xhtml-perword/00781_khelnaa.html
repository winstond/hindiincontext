खेलना (khelnaa)

verb The everyday verb for 'to play' -- games, sports, instruments in a loose sense, and also cards or gambling; it extends figuratively into 'playing with' something risky or 'being at play' in a situation.
Sense 1 : to play (a game or sport)
verb, transitive Takes the game/sport as a direct object without a postposition: क्रिकेट खेलना (kriket khelnaa, 'to play cricket'), होली खेलना (holii khelnaa, 'to play Holi').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kids on any Delhi street will say चलो खेलते हैं (chalo khelte hain, 'let's play') for everything from गली क्रिकेट (galii kriket, street cricket) to कैरम (kairam, carrom) -- खेलना (khelnaa) is the universal verb that covers sport, board games, and even playful festivals like होली (holii) where you 'play' color rather than 'celebrate' it in the English sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for a postposition like 'खेल में' (khel men) to say 'play a game,' but the game name itself is the direct object: वह फुटबॉल खेलता है (vah phutbol kheltaa hai, 'he plays football'), not 'फुटबॉल में'. 2) With festivals like होली (holii) or पानी (paanii, water fights), खेलना (khelnaa) is used rather than 'celebrate,' which surprises learners expecting मनाना (manaanaa) for every festival.
Sample Sentences:
चलो, आज शाम को क्रिकेट खेलते हैं। Come on, let's play cricket this evening.
(chalo, aaj shaam ko kriket khelte hain।)

Sense 2 : to toy with, risk, or manipulate (figurative)
verb, intransitive Common in fixed figurative phrases like जान से खेलना (jaan se khelnaa, 'to risk one's life') or किसी की भावनाओं से खेलना (kisii kii bhaavnaaon se khelnaa, 'to toy with someone's feelings') -- here the object of concern is marked with से (se), not treated as a direct object the way a game is.
Synonyms: जान से खेलना (jaan se khelnaa, 'to play with one's life', idiomatic set phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in dramatic contexts -- a mother scolding a reckless son with तुम अपनी जान से क्यों खेलते हो (tum apnii jaan se kyon khelte ho, 'why do you play with your own life'), or in soap-opera dialogue about someone playing with another's emotions; the shift from direct object to से (se)-marking signals the leap from literal play to figurative risk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar with the direct-object pattern from the 'play a game' sense may forget that this figurative sense marks its object with से (se): भावनाओं से खेलना (bhaavnaaon se khelnaa), not भावनाओं खेलना.
Sample Sentences:

(tum apnii jaan se kyon khelte ho?)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : to gamble, play cards/dice for stakes
verb, transitive Often paired explicitly with जुआ (juaa, 'gambling') as जुआ खेलना (juaa khelnaa), but ताश खेलना (taash khelnaa, 'to play cards') alone can imply stakes depending on context.
Synonyms: जुआ खेलना (juaa khelnaa, the explicit phrase for gambling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: During Diwali evenings, many Delhi families sit down to ताश खेलना (taash khelnaa) for small stakes as a harmless tradition, which is why this sense sits right next to the innocent 'play cards' meaning rather than feeling purely negative.
Common Mistakes: 1) ताश खेलना (taash khelnaa) by itself just means 'to play cards' -- it does not automatically mean gambling; the gambling sense needs context or the word जुआ (juaa) to be unambiguous.

Sense 4 : to play (an instrument, in casual/folk usage)
verb, transitive Rare and dialectal for instruments; बजाना (bajaanaa) is the verb learners should actually use for instruments -- खेलना (khelnaa) surfaces here mainly in old songs or rural speech.
Synonyms: बजाना (bajaanaa, the standard verb for instruments)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll mostly meet this sense in older folk songs or poetic lines rather than in daily conversation, where बजाना (bajaanaa) has taken over completely for instruments.
