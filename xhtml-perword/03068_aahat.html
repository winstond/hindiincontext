आहट (aahat)

noun A single-sense noun for a faint, often unseen sound -- footsteps, a rustle, a stir -- that signals someone or something's presence before it is confirmed.
Sense 1 : faint sound signaling approach or presence
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आवाज़ (aavaaz, broader: any sound), खटपट (khatpat, clattering noise), सरसराहट (sarasraahat, rustling specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries a lovely suspenseful quality -- think of a mother in the kitchen who hears कदमों की आहट (kadmon kii aahat, 'the sound of footsteps') and knows her child is home before he even calls out. It shows up often in Hindi film songs and poetry to evoke someone's presence being felt just before they arrive, almost like a sixth sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for आवाज़ (aavaaz) by default, but आहट (aahat) is specifically a faint, half-heard sound that tips you off to someone's presence -- a loud noise like a car horn is आवाज़ (aavaaz), not आहट (aahat). 2) It is almost always paired with the verb सुनना (sunnaa, 'to hear') or पाना (paanaa, 'to sense/get') via the common phrase आहट पाना (aahat paanaa, 'to sense/detect [someone approaching]') -- learners sometimes try देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see') instead, which does not fit since आहट (aahat) is heard or sensed, not seen.

दरवाज़े के बाहर किसी की आहट सुनाई दी। A faint sound of someone's presence was heard outside the door.
(darvaaze ke baahar kisii kii aahat sunaaii dii।)
