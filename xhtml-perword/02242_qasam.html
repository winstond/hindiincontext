क़सम (qasam)

noun Means 'oath, swear, vow' -- almost always encountered in the emotionally loaded phrase of swearing on someone or something dear, rather than in legal contexts.
Sense 1 : oath, vow, swear (invoking something precious)
noun, feminine Common frame: [thing/person]-की क़सम (kii qasam) meaning 'I swear on ...', e.g. तुम्हारी क़सम (tumhaarii qasam, 'I swear on you'). Also used with खाना (khaanaa) as in क़सम खाना (qasam khaanaa, 'to take/swear an oath').
Synonyms: सौगंध (saugandh, literary), प्रतिज्ञा (pratigyaa, more formal, closer to 'pledge')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Swearing on a loved one is a very Delhi/North Indian way of expressing sincerity or pleading -- तेरी क़सम (terii qasam, 'I swear on you') or मेरी माँ की क़सम (merii maan kii qasam, 'I swear on my mother') carries real emotional weight in an argument or a promise, much more than a neutral 'I swear' does in English. It also shows up constantly in Bollywood dialogue and everyday drama between friends and couples.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to translate 'I swear!' literally with मैं क़सम खाता हूँ (main qasam khaataa hoon) in casual speech, when native speakers usually just say a short exclamation like तेरी क़सम (terii qasam) or भगवान की क़सम (bhagvaan kii qasam) without a full verb. 2) The नुक़्ता (nuqtaa) dot under क is often dropped in casual typing/writing as कसम (kasam) -- both are seen constantly and understood identically.
Sample Sentences:
तेरी क़सम, मैंने यह बात किसी को नहीं बताई। I swear on you, I haven't told anyone this.
(terii qasam, mainne yah baat kisii ko nahiin bataaii।)
