दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'door' or 'gate' -- covers both the physical door and, by extension, an entrance/gateway opening.
Sense 1 : door, gate, entrance
noun, masculine
Synonyms: द्वार (dvaar, literary), गेट (get, colloquial, for a larger gate or compound entrance), फाटक (phaatak, a large gate, e.g. of a farm or level crossing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian homes the दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa) carries a small social ritual of its own -- knocking, calling out 'कोई है?' (koii hai?, 'is anyone there?'), or the host's instruction 'दरवाज़ा बंद कर दो' (darvaazaa band kar do, 'shut the door') to keep out street dust or stray animals. It also shows up idiomatically, as in 'दरवाज़े पर दस्तक देना' (darvaaze par dastak denaa, 'to knock at the door'), often used figuratively for opportunity knocking.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गेट (get) for every kind of entrance, but दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa) is the default word for an ordinary room or house door -- गेट (get) is better saved for a compound/building gate. 2) The verb pairing matters: a door is opened with खोलना (kholnaa) and closed with बंद करना (band karnaa) -- learners sometimes try to force खुलना (khulnaa) into a transitive sentence like 'मैंने दरवाज़ा खुला' (mainne darvaazaa khulaa), which is wrong; it must be 'मैंने दरवाज़ा खोला' (mainne darvaazaa kholaa, 'I opened the door').

कृपया दरवाज़ा बंद कर दो, बाहर बहुत धूल है। Please shut the door, there is a lot of dust outside.
(kripyaa darvaazaa band kar do, baahar bahut dhool hai।)
