ज़बान (zabaan)

noun Primarily means 'tongue' (the body part) and by extension 'language' or 'speech'; also lives inside common idioms about promises and speaking ability. Closely related to the more formal/Sanskritic जीभ (jiibh) for the body part and भाषा (bhaashaa) for language.
Sense 1 : tongue (body part)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जीभ (jiibh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In the kitchen or at the doctor's, you'll hear ज़बान (zabaan) just as often as जीभ (jiibh) -- someone might say ज़बान जल गई (zabaan jal gaii, 'my tongue got burnt') after biting into hot food straight off the tawa.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जीभ (jiibh) by default since it's the 'dictionary' word for tongue, but ज़बान (zabaan) is at least as common in everyday speech, especially in idioms about taste and speech.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : language, speech
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भाषा (bhaashaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear people affectionately call Hindi or Urdu अपनी ज़बान (apnii zabaan, 'our own tongue/language') -- it carries a warmth that भाषा (bhaashaa) doesn't quite have, similar to how English speakers say 'mother tongue' rather than 'native language' in casual talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) ज़बान (zabaan) in this sense leans slightly more informal/conversational than भाषा (bhaashaa), which is the word you'd expect in a school syllabus or official document.
Sample Sentences:
उर्दू बहुत मीठी ज़बान है। Urdu is a very sweet language.
(urdoo bahut miithii zabaan hai।)

Sense 3 : a promise, one's word (of honor)
noun, feminine Mainly survives in fixed idiomatic phrases such as ज़बान देना (zabaan denaa, 'to give one's word'), ज़बान से फिरना (zabaan se phirnaa, 'to go back on one's word'), and ज़बान का पक्का (zabaan kaa pakkaa, 'a man of his word').
Synonyms: वादा (vaadaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ज़बान का पक्का (zabaan kaa pakkaa, 'firm of word') is a real compliment in North Indian culture -- it praises someone whose spoken word is as binding as a signed contract, reflecting an oral culture of honor around promises.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense doesn't stand alone the way 'promise' does in English -- it almost always appears locked inside one of a handful of set phrases rather than as a free-standing noun meaning 'promise.'
Sample Sentences:
