यथार्थवाद (yathaarthvaad)

noun A single-sense abstract noun meaning 'realism' -- the philosophical/literary/artistic stance of depicting things as they actually are, rather than idealized. Built from यथार्थ (yathaarth, 'real, actual') plus the suffix -वाद (vaad, 'ism/doctrine'), the same pattern seen in words like साम्यवाद (saamyvaad, 'communism').
Sense 1 : realism (philosophical, literary, or artistic doctrine)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वास्तविकतावाद (vaastviktaavaad, rare)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is a classroom and literary-criticism word above all -- you'll meet it discussing Premchand's (premchand) fiction or Hindi cinema's 'new wave,' where critics contrast यथार्थवाद (yathaarthvaad) with रूमानियत (roomaaniyat, 'romanticism') or आदर्शवाद (aadarshvaad, 'idealism'). The -वाद (vaad) suffix is worth knowing on its own since it turns any concept into an '-ism' -- यथार्थ (yathaarth) 'real/actual' becomes यथार्थवाद (yathaarthvaad) 'realism' the same way सत्य (saty) 'truth' underlies सत्याग्रह (satyaagrah).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with वास्तविकता (vaastviktaa, 'reality' as a plain fact or state), which is the everyday noun for 'reality,' while यथार्थवाद (yathaarthvaad) specifically names the '-ism,' the doctrine or artistic approach of depicting reality. 2) This word is used almost exclusively in academic, literary, or critical contexts (discussing novels, cinema, art movements) -- it rarely comes up in casual conversation, so learners should not reach for it when they just mean 'that's realistic' (for which प्रैक्टिकल (praiktikal) or व्यावहारिक (vyaavahaarik) is more natural in speech).
