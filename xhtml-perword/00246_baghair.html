बग़ैर (baghair)

postposition A single-sense postposition meaning 'without'; almost always paired with के (ke) or used before a verb's oblique infinitive to mean 'without doing something'.
Sense 1 : without
postposition Governs a noun or oblique infinitive, typically completed by के (ke): चाय के बग़ैर (chaay ke baghair, 'without tea') or खाए बग़ैर (khaae baghair, 'without eating'). के (ke) is often dropped in fast speech but is the fuller, more careful form.
Synonyms: बिना (binaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बग़ैर (baghair) is a Persian-origin word that sits comfortably in everyday speech alongside its more common cousin बिना (binaa); the two are used interchangeably by most Delhi speakers, so learning one well and recognizing the other is enough. You will hear it constantly in complaints like पैसे के बग़ैर कुछ नहीं होता (paise ke baghair kuchh nahiin hotaa, 'nothing happens without money').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often place बग़ैर (baghair) after the noun without के (ke) and assume this is always optional; in careful or written Hindi, के (ke) is expected, especially after nouns rather than verb stems. 2) Confusing word order: बग़ैर (baghair) can precede the noun too (बग़ैर चाय के (baghair chaay ke, 'without tea')), which surprises learners used to postpositions always following their noun.
Sample Sentences:

(baghair paise ke kuchh nahiin hotaa।)
