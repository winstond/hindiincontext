आगे निकलना (aage nikalnaa)

verb A compound verb combining आगे (aage, 'ahead/forward') with निकलना (nikalnaa, 'to emerge/exit'); literally 'to come out ahead,' it covers both the physical sense of overtaking someone and the figurative sense of surpassing or getting ahead in life, work, or competition.
Sense 1 : to surpass, outdo, get ahead (in life, career, competition)
verb, intransitive The person or standard being surpassed is marked with से (se): वह अपने भाई से आगे निकल गया (vah apne bhaaii se aage nikal gayaa, 'he got ahead of his brother').
Synonyms: बढ़ना (badhnaa, 'to advance/progress'), पार करना (paar karnaa, 'to cross/surpass')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase for the classic competitive comparison every Indian family knows well -- comparing exam ranks, salaries, or promotions among siblings, cousins, or classmates: फ़लाना आगे निकल गया (falaanaa aage nikal gayaa, 'so-and-so got ahead'). It carries a sense of overtaking someone who was previously level with or behind you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बेहतर होना (behtar honaa, 'to be better') when the natural Hindi choice for 'getting ahead of someone' in career or life is आगे निकलना (aage nikalnaa), which has a more dynamic, competitive feel. 2) Remains intransitive even in this figurative use: वह सबसे आगे निकल गई (vah sabse aage nikal gaii, 'she got ahead of everyone'), not उसने.
Sample Sentences:
इस बार राहुल सबसे आगे निकल गया। This time Rahul got ahead of everyone.
(is baar raahul sabse aage nikal gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to overtake, get ahead of (physically)
verb, intransitive The person or thing overtaken is marked with से (se): गाड़ी बस से आगे निकल गई (gaadii bas se aage nikal gaii, 'the car overtook the bus').
Synonyms: पीछे छोड़ना (piichhe chhodnaa, 'to leave behind')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture overtaking traffic on a Delhi road -- this is exactly the phrase used for a car, bike, or runner pulling ahead of another. The verb निकलना (nikalnaa) itself often carries this sense of 'emerging out from' a pack, which is why it pairs so naturally with आगे (aage).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to mark the overtaken party with को (ko) instead of से (se); the correct pattern is 'X से आगे निकलना' (X se aage nikalnaa). 2) निकलना (nikalnaa) is intransitive, so this compound does not take ने (ne) in the past tense even though the action feels active: वह आगे निकल गया (vah aage nikal gayaa, 'he went ahead'), not उसने.
