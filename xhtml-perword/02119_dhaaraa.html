धारा (dhaaraa)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'stream/current/flow' (of water, electricity, or air), extended figuratively to a 'flow' of thought or events, and also used as the legal term for a 'section' or 'clause' of law. Inline Hindi placeholders throughout.
Sense 1 : stream, current (of water)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रवाह (pravaah), बहाव (bahaav)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the Ganga's current when someone says नदी की धारा (nadii kii dhaaraa, 'the river's current') -- Hindi speakers use धारा (dhaaraa) for the moving water itself, distinct from the river as a geographic entity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse धारा (dhaaraa) with नदी (nadii); नदी (nadii) is the river itself, while धारा (dhaaraa) is the current or flow of water within it (or any flowing body).

Sense 2 : flow, trend (figurative -- of thought, events, time)
noun, feminine Used in phrases like समय की धारा (samay kii dhaaraa, 'the flow of time') or विचारों की धारा (vichaaron kii dhaaraa, 'a stream of thoughts').
Synonyms: प्रवाह (pravaah), रुझान (rujhaan, trend/inclination)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense treats abstract things like time or thought as flowing water -- a very natural metaphor in Hindi, so विचारों की धारा (vichaaron kii dhaaraa, 'a stream of thoughts') sounds poetic but not forced.

Sense 3 : current (electrical)
noun, feminine Commonly appears as विद्युत धारा (vidyut dhaaraa) in science contexts, but बिजली की धारा (bijlii kii dhaaraa) is the everyday phrasing.
Synonyms: विद्युत धारा (vidyut dhaaraa, technical/full term)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian schoolkid meets in physics class for 'electric current' -- बिजली की धारा (bijlii kii dhaaraa, 'electric current') is standard textbook phrasing, paralleling how धारा (dhaaraa) describes water's flow.

Sense 4 : section, clause (of a law)
noun, feminine Always used with a number, as in धारा 302 (dhaaraa 302, 'Section 302'), referencing a specific clause of a legal code.
Synonyms: अनुच्छेद (anuchchhed, used for constitutional articles rather than penal code sections)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly on the news -- धारा 144 (dhaaraa 144) lagaa dii gayii, 'Section 144 was imposed', is standard reporting language for curfew-like restrictions. Journalists and lawyers use धारा (dhaaraa) the way English speakers use 'Section' before a number.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often meet this sense first through news or crime dramas without realizing it is the same word as 'river current' -- धारा 144 (dhaaraa 144) is a famous example, referring to a section of law restricting public assembly.
