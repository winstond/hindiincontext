गरमी (garmii)

noun Mainly means 'heat' -- both the physical/weather sense and the summer season -- with a secondary figurative sense of 'anger, heat of temper.' Also spelled गर्मी (garmii) with a nukta-free rendering; both spellings are common.
Sense 1 : heat (temperature)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ताप (taap, formal/scientific), उष्णता (ushntaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Delhi household reaches for from April onward -- गरमी बहुत है (garmii bahut hai, 'it's very hot') is practically a season greeting on the street. It pairs naturally with सर्दी (sardii, 'cold'), and the two words together map the whole North Indian weather cycle that people organize daily life around.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तापमान (taapmaan) when they just mean 'it's hot' -- तापमान (taapmaan) is 'temperature' as a measurable quantity, while गरमी (garmii) is the everyday word for the felt heat itself. 2) The common weather complaint uses a दे body-frame: गरमी लग रही है (garmii lag rahii hai, 'I'm feeling hot') -- learners sometimes try to make themselves the grammatical subject instead of using लगना (lagnaa) with गरमी as the subject.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : summer (the season)
noun, feminine Often used in the plural-feeling phrase गरमी की छुट्टियाँ (garmii kii chhuttiyaan, 'summer vacation'), a set phrase every school-going kid in India knows.
Synonyms: गरमी का मौसम (garmii kaa mausam, 'summer season', fuller phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गरमी की छुट्टियाँ (garmii kii chhuttiyaan, 'summer holidays') are a childhood institution in India -- schools close for weeks in May-June, and the phrase itself carries that same nostalgia English speakers get from 'summer break.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say मौसम गरमी (mausam garmii) instead of the correct possessive order गरमी का मौसम (garmii kaa mausam, 'the season of heat/summer').

Sense 3 : anger, heat of temper (figurative)
noun, feminine Common in the fixed collocation गरमी दिखाना (garmii dikhaanaa, 'to show attitude/temper') and गरमी उतरना (garmii utarnaa, 'for one's temper to cool down').
Synonyms: गुस्सा (gussaa), तेज़ी (tezii, less common in this figurative sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear an elder scold a mouthy youngster with ज़्यादा गरमी मत दिखाओ (zyaadaa garmii mat dikhaao, 'don't show so much attitude') -- the metaphor of temper-as-heat runs the same direction in English ('hot-headed', 'heated argument'), so this sense usually feels intuitive to English speakers.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense overlaps with गुस्सा (gussaa) but leans specifically toward a hot-headed, provocative display of temper rather than plain anger -- learners sometimes use them as flat synonyms in all contexts, which is not quite right.
Sample Sentences:
