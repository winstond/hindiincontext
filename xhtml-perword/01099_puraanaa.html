पुराना (puraanaa)

adjective A common adjective covering 'old' in the sense of age of an object or duration of time (used since long ago), and by extension 'former/ex-' or 'stale/outdated'; all senses share one root idea of 'not new'.
Sense 1 : old (of objects, in age/condition)
adjective
Synonyms: जीर्ण (jiirn, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday word you will hear in a Delhi bazaar for anything from पुरानी किताबें (puraanii kitaaben, 'old books') at Daryaganj's Sunday book market to a पुरानी कार (puraanii kaar, 'old/used car'). Note the contrast with नया (nayaa, 'new') -- the two form the most basic old/new pair a learner will use constantly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बूढ़ा (boodhaa) for any 'old' thing, but बूढ़ा (boodhaa) is reserved for aged people/animals; objects, clothes, houses, and cars take पुराना (puraanaa) instead. 2) For 'old friend' or 'old habit' meaning long-standing rather than aged-in-years, पुराना (puraanaa) is still correct -- e.g. पुराना दोस्त (puraanaa dost, 'old friend') -- English speakers sometimes wrongly search for a separate word.

Sense 2 : old, long-standing (of relationships, time periods, practices)
adjective
Synonyms: पूर्व (poorv, formal/former)
Frequency: high

Sense 3 : stale, outdated, no longer fresh/relevant
adjective
Synonyms: बासी (baasii, for stale food specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in पुरानी सोच (puraanii soch, 'old-fashioned thinking') -- a very common way to criticize outdated attitudes, often contrasted with आधुनिक (aadhunik, 'modern') in family or generational conversations.
Common Mistakes: 1) For stale food specifically, native speakers usually prefer बासी (baasii) over पुराना (puraanaa); पुराना (puraanaa) works but sounds more general, like 'not fresh anymore' rather than 'spoiled.'
