विरुद्ध (viruddh)

postposition/adjective A formal postposition/adjective meaning 'against' or 'opposed to' -- used to mark opposition, contrast, or conflict, often in legal, political, or debate contexts.
Sense 1 : against, in opposition to
postposition Takes a preceding noun in the oblique with के (ke): X के विरुद्ध (ke viruddh, 'against X').
Synonyms: खिलाफ़ (khilaaf), उलटे (ulte, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly in newspaper headlines and courtroom dramas -- 'सरकार के विरुद्ध याचिका' (sarkaar ke viruddh yaachikaa, 'a petition against the government') is the classic register. In daily chit-chat, most Delhi speakers reach for खिलाफ़ (khilaaf) instead, saving विरुद्ध (viruddh) for when they want to sound serious or official.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use सीधे (siidhe) case marking instead of के (ke); remember it is always X के विरुद्ध (ke viruddh), never X विरुद्ध (viruddh) alone. 2) Confusing with खिलाफ़ (khilaaf) -- both mean 'against,' but विरुद्ध (viruddh) feels more formal/written, appropriate for legal notices, official complaints, or news reports rather than casual speech.

Sense 2 : opposite, contrary, adverse
adjective Used predicatively or attributively to describe ideas, opinions, or conditions that are contrary/opposed, e.g. विरुद्ध विचार (viruddh vichaar, 'contrary/opposing views').
Synonyms: प्रतिकूल (pratikool, formal), उलटा (ultaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shares its root with the postpositional use -- both carry the core idea of standing 'against' or 'opposite' something, whether that's a person, a law, or just an idea.
