गूढ़ (goodh)

adjective A literary/formal adjective meaning 'deep, hidden, mysterious' or 'abstruse, hard to fathom' -- used for ideas, meanings, or texts rather than everyday objects.
Sense 1 : hidden, secret, mysterious
adjective
Synonyms: रहस्यमय (rahasymay), गुप्त (gupt), छिपा (chhipaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This word shows up describing a गूढ़ मुस्कान (goodh muskaan, 'an enigmatic smile') or गूढ़ रहस्य (goodh rahasy, 'a deep mystery') -- it carries a slightly bookish, almost poetic flavor rather than everyday chatter. You will meet it more in essays, spiritual writing, or news commentary than in a market conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this word in casual speech where रहस्यमय (rahasymay) or छिपा हुआ (chhipaa huaa) would sound far more natural -- गूढ़ (goodh) belongs to formal or written registers. 2) It describes qualities of things (a mystery, a meaning, a smile) rather than people hiding themselves; for a person who is secretive, learners should use रहस्यमय (rahasymay) instead.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी मुस्कान में कुछ गूढ़ था। There was something mysterious in her smile.
(uskii muskaan men kuchh goodh thaa।)

Sense 2 : abstruse, hard to understand
adjective
Synonyms: जटिल (jatil, complicated), दुर्बोध (durbodh, literary, harder to understand)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Used for things like गूढ़ ज्ञान (goodh gyaan, 'esoteric/profound knowledge') or गूढ़ बातें (goodh baaten, 'cryptic remarks') -- often applied to scriptures, philosophy, or someone's cryptic way of speaking, carrying a hint of respect for depth rather than mere confusion.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is about intellectual difficulty (a text, a philosophy, a riddle), not physical or logistical difficulty -- for a hard task, learners should use कठिन (kathin) or मुश्किल (mushkil) instead.
Sample Sentences:
