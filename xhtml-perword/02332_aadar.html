आदर (aadar)

noun A single core sense: 'respect, regard' shown to elders, teachers, or anyone deserving deference; mostly used as a noun in set phrases like आदर करना (aadar karnaa).
Sense 1 : respect, regard, esteem
noun, masculine Commonly appears in the compound verb frame आदर करना (aadar karnaa, 'to show respect') with the person respected marked by की (kii).
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त (izzat), सम्मान (sammaan), मान (maan, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear in the context of touching an elder's feet or addressing a teacher formally -- it carries a slightly more formal, almost old-school tone compared to इज़्ज़त (izzat), which is more common in everyday speech about honor or dignity. Many Hindi schools still teach the moral line 'बड़ों का आदर करो' (badon kaa aadar karo, 'respect your elders') as a foundational value.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for इज़्ज़त (izzat) in every context, but आदर (aadar) leans toward respect for character, age, or position (elders, teachers, gurus) rather than the more honor/reputation-tinged इज़्ज़त (izzat), so swapping them can sound slightly off in formal or devotional contexts. 2) The verb pairing is आदर करना (aadar karnaa), not आदर देना (aadar denaa) -- the latter is understood but sounds unnatural to native ears compared to the standard collocation.
