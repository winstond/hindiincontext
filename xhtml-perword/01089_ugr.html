उग्र (ugr)

adjective A formal/literary adjective describing intensity that borders on danger -- fierce, violent, or extreme, used both for tempers and moods and for escalating situations (protests, fire, illness).
Sense 1 : violent, intense, escalating (of a situation, protest, fire, mood in a crowd)
adjective
Synonyms: हिंसक (hinsak), भड़का हुआ (bhadkaa huaa), तीव्र (tiivr)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will meet constantly on Hindi news channels -- प्रदर्शन उग्र हो गया (pradarshan ugr ho gayaa, 'the protest turned violent') is a stock headline phrase, so recognizing उग्र (ugr) here is genuinely useful for reading or watching Indian news.
Common Mistakes: 1) उग्र (ugr) describes the escalating intensity of an event (a protest turning violent, a fire spreading), while हिंसक (hinsak) more narrowly means 'violent' in the sense of causing physical harm -- news Hindi often prefers उग्र (ugr) precisely because it captures rising tension, not just violence itself, as in प्रदर्शन उग्र हो गया (pradarshan ugr ho gayaa, 'the protest turned violent/intense').

Sense 2 : fierce, hot-tempered (of a person's nature or mood)
adjective
Synonyms: क्रोधी (krodhii), गुस्सैल (gussail), तेज़-तर्रार (tez-tarraar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word belongs to a slightly formal, almost newsy register -- you will hear it more from a news anchor describing a person's temper than from a friend gossiping about their boss, where गुस्सैल (gussail) feels more natural.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गुस्सा (gussaa, 'anger') when they mean a temperament, but उग्र (ugr) describes the person or mood itself as fierce, not the emotion in the moment -- you would say वह उग्र स्वभाव का है (vah ugr svabhaav kaa hai, 'he has a fierce temperament'), not use उग्र (ugr) to mean 'he got angry.'
