आराम से (aaraam se)

adverb A common adverbial phrase built from आराम (aaraam, 'rest/comfort') plus the instrumental postposition से (se); covers 'comfortably/at ease' and, in speech, 'take it easy, no rush.'
Sense 1 : comfortably, with ease
adverb
Synonyms: इत्मीनान से (itmiinaan se), सुख से (sukh se, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi -- a host telling a guest आराम से बैठिए (aaraam se baithie, 'sit comfortably/make yourself at home'), or someone describing a smooth journey as हम आराम से पहुँच गए (ham aaraam se pahunch gae, 'we got there comfortably/easily'). It carries a warm, hospitable tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat आराम से (aaraam se) as only meaning 'restfully,' missing that it commonly modifies everyday actions like sitting, eating, or traveling to mean 'comfortably' or 'without strain.' 2) It is often placed right before the verb it modifies, e.g. आराम से बैठिए (aaraam se baithie, 'sit comfortably') -- learners sometimes tack it on at the end instead, which sounds less natural.

(aaraam se baithie, chaay abhii aatii hai।)

Sense 2 : slowly, calmly, without hurry
adverb Frequently used as a standalone reassurance rather than modifying a specific verb, e.g. as a one-line response to someone apologizing for being slow or worried.
Synonyms: धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire, focuses on slowness rather than calm), इत्मीनान से (itmiinaan se)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic soothing phrase in Delhi-NCR life -- said to someone rushing for a train, worried about a deadline, or stressed in traffic: आराम से, हो जाएगा (aaraam se, ho jaaegaa, 'relax, it'll get done'). It reflects a very Indian conversational instinct to calm someone down before addressing the actual problem.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a standalone phrase, आराम से (aaraam se) functions like 'take your time' or 'relax' -- learners often reach for धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire) instead, which stresses slowness of pace rather than the calm, unhurried attitude आराम से (aaraam se) conveys.
