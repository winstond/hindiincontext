ओर (or)

noun Mainly a directional noun meaning 'side' or 'direction', almost always used inside postposition-like phrases such as की ओर (kii or, 'towards'); also has a figurative extension for attention/inclination.
Sense 1 : direction, side
noun, feminine Almost always appears as की ओर (kii or) after a noun or pronoun in the oblique, functioning like 'towards' -- e.g. घर की ओर (ghar kii or, 'towards home').
Synonyms: दिशा (dishaa), तरफ़ (taraf)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: तर॥ ओर (tar॥ or) is the everyday choice in casual Delhi speech, while की ओर (kii or) feels a touch more neutral or written -- you will see ओर (or) often in news reports and formal announcements giving directions or describing trends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use ओर (or) alone as if it meant 'direction' in isolation, but in natural speech it almost always needs की (kii) before it and a reference point before that -- bare ओर (or) sounds incomplete. 2) Confusing की ओर (kii or) with के तरह (ke tarah, 'like'/'in the manner of') -- they look structurally similar but mean very different things.
Sample Sentences:
वह घर की ओर चला गया। He walked off toward home.
(vah ghar kii or chalaa gayaa।)

Sense 2 : figurative: attention, inclination, regard
noun, feminine Used in phrases like इस ओर ध्यान देना (is or dhyaan denaa, 'to pay attention to this') where ओर (or) points figuratively rather than literally in space.
Synonyms: ध्यान (dhyaan, attention)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use extends the same spatial logic English speakers know from 'turning towards someone' -- Hindi just keeps ओर (or) doing double duty for both literal direction and mental/emotional orientation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'side' when it actually means 'attention' or 'regard' -- e.g. सब की ओर देखना (sab kii or dekhnaa, 'to look after everyone') is about care, not literal glancing sideways.
