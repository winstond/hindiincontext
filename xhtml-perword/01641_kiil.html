कील (kiil)

noun Mainly a concrete noun meaning a nail/peg/pin; also has an extended use for a pimple or small painful lump on the skin.
Sense 1 : nail, peg, pin (metal or wooden spike)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कांटा (kaantaa, can overlap for a pointed peg), पिन (pin, small metal pin)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a carpenter's shop or a roadside cobbler hammering a कील (kiil) into a wall to hang a calendar or a shoe sole -- it's a very tactile, everyday household word, often heard as दीवार में कील (diivaar men kiil, 'a nail in the wall').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse कील (kiil) with कीला (kiilaa) or with चाबी (chaabii, 'key'), which is unrelated despite the similar sound. 2) It refers to the physical spike itself, not the act of hammering -- that's ठोकना (thoknaa) or गाड़ना (gaadnaa).
Sample Sentences:

(diivaar men ek kiil thok do।)

Sense 2 : pimple, small hard lump on skin
noun, feminine This sense usually appears in the plural or in set phrases about skin, e.g. कील-मुंहासे (kiil-munhaase, 'pimples and blackheads').
Synonyms: मुहासा (muhaasaa, more common for facial pimples/acne)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in everyday beauty-product ads and family chats about skin care -- कील-मुंहासे (kiil-munhaase) is the standard paired phrase, much like 'pimples and blackheads' rolls off the tongue together in English.
