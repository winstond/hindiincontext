धुएँ (dhuen)

noun This is the oblique/plural form of धुआँ (dhuaan, 'smoke'), used before postpositions (as in धुएँ से, dhuen se, 'from the smoke') or to refer to multiple instances of smoke/fumes.
Sense 1 : smoke, fumes (oblique/plural form)
noun, masculine This is not an independent lemma with its own meaning distinct from धुआँ (dhuaan) -- it is the same word in its oblique case form, required whenever a postposition follows (धुएँ में, dhuen men, 'in the smoke') or when referring to smoke in a plural/generic sense (कई तरह के धुएँ, kaii tarah ke dhuen, 'many kinds of smoke').
Synonyms: धूम्र (dhoomr, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this form constantly in everyday phrases about pollution, cooking fires, or cigarettes -- धुएँ से भरा कमरा (dhuen se bharaa kamraa, 'a room full of smoke') or धुएँ की वजह से (dhuen kii vajah se, 'because of the smoke') are common ways Delhi folks talk about the city's smog, especially around Diwali and in winter.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use धुआँ (dhuaan) before a postposition out of habit -- but the oblique form धुएँ (dhuen) is required, e.g. धुएँ से आँखें जल रही हैं (dhuen se aankhen jal rahii hain, 'eyes are burning from the smoke'), not धुआँ से (dhuaan se). 2) Because धुआँ (dhuaan) is usually treated as a mass noun in English ('smoke'), learners are sometimes unsure it even has an oblique/plural form -- but Hindi nouns of this class regularly take one.
