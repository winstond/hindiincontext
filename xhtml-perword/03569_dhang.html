ढंग (dhang)

noun/adverb Mainly means 'way/manner (of doing something)' and, in a related everyday use, 'proper/decent manner' as in behaving or dressing ढंग से (dhang se, properly).
Sense 1 : way, manner, method
noun, masculine Very commonly used with का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) to link to the thing being done, e.g. बात करने का ढंग (baat karne kaa dhang, 'a way of talking').
Synonyms: तरीका (tariikaa), तरीक़ा (tariiqaa, variant form), रीति (riiti, literary), शैली (shailii, style)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in feedback and complaints: बात करने का ढंग नहीं है (baat karne kaa dhang nahiin hai, 'you don't know how to talk properly') is a classic scolding line from a parent, teacher, or boss. It's less about the abstract 'method' and more about *how* something is done, often with an implied judgment about whether it was done well.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तरीका (tariikaa) by default; ढंग (dhang) is just as common and interchangeable in most everyday contexts, so don't treat it as rare or bookish. 2) ढंग (dhang) is grammatically masculine even though it often refers to something abstract like a habit or style; learners sometimes mismark agreement expecting a feminine noun for abstract qualities.

(tumhen baat karne kaa dhang nahiin hai।)

Sense 2 : properly, decently, in the right way
adverb Occurs fixed as ढंग से (dhang se) modifying a verb, usually in negative or corrective contexts: ढंग से बैठो (dhang se baitho, 'sit properly').
Synonyms: ठीक से (thiik se), सही तरीके से (sahii tariike se)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday scolding register: a mother telling a child ढंग से खाओ (dhang se khaao, 'eat properly') or a boss saying ढंग से काम करो (dhang se kaam karo, 'do the work properly') is one of the most common corrective phrases in Hindi households and offices alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is the से-marked adverbial use of the noun sense, not a separate word to memorize from scratch, but learners often fail to recognize ढंग से (dhang se) as a fixed chunk meaning 'properly' and instead try to translate it literally as 'by manner.' 2) It's frequently used negatively -- ढंग से काम नहीं करता (dhang se kaam nahiin kartaa, 'doesn't work properly') -- so learners should expect it in complaints more than in praise.
