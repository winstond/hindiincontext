दुबला-पतला (dublaa-patlaa)

adjective A single-sense descriptive adjective meaning 'thin/lean/skinny,' used for people's physique. It is a reduplicative-style pair of near-synonyms (दुबला (dublaa) + पतला (patlaa)) joined for emphasis, a very common pattern in Hindi descriptive adjectives.
Sense 1 : thin, lean, skinny (of a person's build)
adjective
Synonyms: पतला (patlaa), कमज़ोर (kamzor, often implies weak/frail rather than just slim), छरहरा (chharahraa, slim in a fit, attractive way)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic phrase a Delhi aunty uses when fussing over a guest: 'तुम तो बहुत दुबले-पतले हो गए हो, खाना ठीक से खाते हो या नहीं?' (tum to bahut duble-patle ho gae ho, khaanaa thiik se khaate ho yaa nahiin, 'You've become so thin, are you even eating properly?'). Pairing two close synonyms like this (दुबला (dublaa) + पतला (patlaa)) is a common Hindi habit for emphasis, similar in spirit to English 'thin and lanky.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use it for thin objects like paper or rope, but दुबला-पतला (dublaa-patlaa) is reserved for describing a person's or animal's body -- for a thin object, use पतला (patlaa) alone. 2) It is usually neutral-to-sympathetic in tone, not necessarily negative like 'skinny' can sound in English; calling someone this is not normally rude.
