दस्त लगना (dast lagnaa)

verb A common idiomatic verb phrase meaning 'to have diarrhea' -- literally 'loose motions to strike/apply to (someone)'. Uses a dative-subject construction with को (ko).
Sense 1 : to have diarrhea, to have loose motions
verb, intransitive Takes a dative subject with को (ko): मुझे दस्त लगे हैं (mujhe dast lage hain, 'I have diarrhea'). दस्त (dast) is grammatically plural/countable in usage even for a single bout, so लगना (lagnaa) typically agrees in the plural (लगे, lage) rather than singular.
Synonyms: पेट ख़राब होना (pet kharaab honaa, milder, more general phrase for 'upset stomach')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the everyday way to talk about an upset stomach in Hindi, especially useful when traveling in India as a first bout of 'Delhi belly' is almost a rite of passage for visitors. Families often reach straight for home remedies like दही-चावल (dahii-chaaval, 'yogurt-rice') or ORS packets before considering a doctor, and asking 'क्या हुआ, दस्त लगे हैं क्या?' (kyaa huaa, dast lage hain kyaa?, 'What happened, do you have loose motions?') is a very normal, unembarrassed question between friends or family in India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person the subject with ने (ne) as in a normal transitive sentence, but this phrase always uses को (ko) -- you don't 'do' दस्त, they 'strike' you. 2) Because दस्त (dast) behaves like a plural noun here, learners sometimes wrongly use singular agreement (दस्त लगा है, dast lagaa hai) when the more natural everyday form is plural (दस्त लगे हैं, dast lage hain), though both are heard.
Sample Sentences:
उसे कल रात से दस्त लगे हैं। He has had diarrhea since last night.
(use kal raat se dast lage hain।)
