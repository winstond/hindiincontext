फैलना (phailnaa)

verb One core intransitive verb meaning 'to spread' or 'to expand' -- covers physical spreading (fire, water, an odor), bodily/spatial stretching out, and the figurative spread of things like news, disease, or rumors.
Sense 1 : to spread, to extend (physically)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: फैल जाना (phail jaanaa, with completive फैल जाना), विस्तृत होना (vistrit honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture spilled chai on a tablecloth or smoke drifting through a room -- फैलना (phailnaa) is exactly that visual of something spreading outward from a point. It very often pairs with जाना (jaanaa) as फैल जाना (phail jaanaa) to mark the completed spread, e.g. after a monsoon puddle has covered a whole street.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बढ़ना (badhnaa) here, but बढ़ना (badhnaa) means to increase/grow in degree or amount, while फैलना (phailnaa) means to spread out over an area or surface -- पानी फैल गया (paanii phail gayaa, 'the water spread out') versus पानी बढ़ गया (paanii badh gayaa, 'the water level rose').

Sense 2 : to spread (news, disease, rumor -- figurative)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: फैल जाना (phail jaanaa), प्रसारित होना (prasaarit honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb you hear constantly in news broadcasts and family gossip alike -- अफ़वाह फैल गई (afvaah phail gaii, 'the rumor spread') or बीमारी फैल रही है (biimaarii phail rahii hai, 'the disease is spreading'). It treats news and illness the same way it treats water or fire: as something that expands outward on its own, without anyone actively 'doing' the spreading -- a nice example of Hindi's love of intransitive, agentless framing.
Common Mistakes: 1) With abstract subjects like खबर (khabar, 'news') or बीमारी (biimaarii, 'disease'), फैलना (phailnaa) is the natural native choice; learners sometimes overuse बताना (bataanaa) or a passive construction where Hindi just uses this one intransitive verb.

Sense 3 : to stretch out, sprawl (limbs, body)
verb, intransitive Commonly used with body parts as subject: हाथ फैलाना (haath phailaanaa) is the causative counterpart 'to stretch out one's arms/hands', while पैर फैलाकर सोना (pair phailaakar sonaa, 'to sleep with legs sprawled out') shows this intransitive sense in a common idiom.
Synonyms: पसरना (pasarnaa, less common), फैल जाना (phail jaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of someone flopping onto a charpai after a long day and letting their arms and legs go loose and wide -- that relaxed, unfolding motion is what फैलना (phailnaa) captures here. It is the intransitive twin of the causative फैलाना (phailaanaa, 'to spread/stretch something out'), which a learner will meet as its own separate lemma.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only know the 'spread of news' sense and are surprised फैलना (phailnaa) also describes a person's body relaxing or sprawling across a bed or sofa -- टाँगें फैलाकर बैठना (taangen phailaakar baithnaa, 'to sit with legs stretched out') is a very ordinary everyday image.
