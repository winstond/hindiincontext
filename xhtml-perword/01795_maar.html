मार (maar)

noun Primarily a noun meaning a hit/blow or beating, and by extension the harmful force or 'punch' of something (heat, cold, market forces); also survives as the stem inside many compound verbs and idioms built on मारना (maarnaa, 'to hit').
Sense 1 : a hit, blow, or beating
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पिटाई (pitaaii), चोट (chot, can also mean 'injury')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मार पड़ेगी (maar padegii) is a classic line of household discipline in Indian families -- said by a parent, grandparent, or elder sibling half as threat, half as ritual, often without any real beating ever following. It captures how मार (maar) sits in the emotional register of upbringing more than in actual violence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पिटाई (pitaaii) by default, but मार (maar) is the more everyday word a parent or friend actually uses when threatening or describing a beating, e.g. मार पड़ेगी (maar padegii, 'you'll get a beating'). 2) मार (maar) pairs naturally with पड़ना (padnaa) rather than होना (honaa) -- the beating 'falls' on you, it doesn't simply 'happen.'
Sample Sentences:
अगर तुमने झूठ बोला तो मार पड़ेगी। If you lie, you'll get a beating.
(agar tumne jhooth bolaa to maar padegii।)

Sense 2 : harsh or damaging force/impact (of heat, cold, market, etc.)
noun, feminine Used with धूप (dhoop), गर्मी (garmii), सर्दी (sardii), मंदी (mandii) and similar nouns to describe their harsh, almost physically felt impact.
Synonyms: असर (asar, broader: 'effect'), प्रकोप (prakop, literary, stronger: 'wrath/fury')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday complaints about Delhi's climate and economy alike: गर्मी की मार (garmii kii maar, 'the brutal heat') in June, or महंगाई की मार (mahangaaii kii maar, 'the crush of inflation') at the sabzi mandi. It's a very Hindi way of framing hardship as an external force landing blows on you, rather than something you simply 'experience.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate this sense too literally as 'hitting' -- but धूप की मार (dhoop kii maar, 'the brunt of the sun/heat') describes suffering under harsh weather or conditions, not any physical strike. 2) This sense almost always appears in the genitive frame X की मार (kii maar), not as a free-standing noun.
