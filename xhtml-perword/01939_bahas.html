बहस (bahas)

noun/verb A feminine noun meaning an argument, debate, or dispute; it also appears heavily in a compound-verb pairing (बहस करना (bahas karnaa)) which carries the main verbal sense. Nearly all everyday use revolves around this one core idea, split here between the noun and its most common verbal use.
Sense 1 : argument, debate, dispute (verbal conflict or discussion)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: तकरार (takraar, quarrel/squabble), विवाद (vivaad, more formal, dispute), झगड़ा (jhagdaa, fight/quarrel, stronger)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear बहस (bahas) constantly in the context of Indian news debates and political shows -- टीवी बहस (tiivii bahas, 'TV debate') is practically a genre of its own, often loud and combative. In a family setting, elders might gently scold with बहस मत करो (bahas mat karo, 'don't argue') when a child talks back.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for झगड़ा (jhagdaa) when they mean a heated verbal exchange, but बहस (bahas) is specifically about arguing points or positions (spoken debate), while झगड़ा (jhagdaa) implies a more personal fight or falling-out. 2) बहस (bahas) does not by itself imply anger or hostility -- a parliamentary or classroom बहस (bahas) can be perfectly civil, unlike झगड़ा (jhagdaa).

Sense 2 : to argue, to debate (with someone)
verb, transitive Formed as a compound verb with करना (karnaa): बहस करना (bahas karnaa). The person argued with takes से (se): उससे बहस करना (usse bahas karnaa, 'to argue with him/her').
Synonyms: झगड़ना (jhagadnaa, to quarrel/fight, stronger), तर्क करना (tark karnaa, to reason/argue logically, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बहस करना (bahas karnaa) is what elders often warn against with the phrase बड़ों से बहस मत करो (badon se bahas mat karo, 'don't argue with your elders') -- a very familiar refrain in Indian households, tied to the cultural value placed on respecting elders.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mark the person argued with using के साथ (ke saath) by analogy with English 'argue with,' but से (se) is the natural choice here: मुझसे बहस मत करो (mujhse bahas mat karo, 'don't argue with me').
