द्रव (drav)

noun/adjective A technical/scientific word for 'liquid' or 'fluid', mostly encountered in chemistry, physics, or medical contexts rather than casual kitchen talk (Hindi speakers usually just say पानी (paanii) or use the English word 'liquid' in daily life).
Sense 1 : liquid, fluid
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तरल (taral)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word most on medicine bottles (द्रव औषधि, drav aushdhi, 'liquid medicine') or in school science textbooks describing states of matter -- ठोस (thos, solid), द्रव (drav, liquid), and गैस (gais, gas). It has the same formal flavor as saying 'fluid' instead of 'liquid' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for द्रव (drav) in everyday speech where a native speaker would just say पानी (paanii) or use the borrowed English word 'liquid'; द्रव (drav) sounds textbook-technical, like something from a chemistry class or a medicine label, not kitchen conversation.

Sense 2 : liquid, fluid (as a descriptor)
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. द्रव रूप (drav roop, 'liquid form').
Synonyms: तरल (taral)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use pairs closely with its noun sense above -- both come from the same Sanskrit root meaning 'to melt or flow', which is why द्रव (drav) conceptually links liquid states to the idea of something that has 'become fluid'.
