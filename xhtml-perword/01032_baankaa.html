बाँका (baankaa)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective describing a dashing, rakish, jaunty look or bearing, especially of a man; also survives as a noun for such a dashing man. It carries a stylish, slightly swaggering flavor rather than plain 'handsome'.
Sense 1 : dashing, rakishly stylish, jauntily tilted
adjective Often used with nouns like टोपी (topii, 'cap') or अंदाज़ (andaaz, 'style') to describe something worn or carried at a rakish angle or with swagger, e.g. बाँकी टोपी (baankii topii, 'a cap worn at a jaunty tilt').
Synonyms: छैला (chhailaa), रंगीला (rangiilaa), फबीला (phabiilaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture an old-style Lucknavi or Delhi dandy with his टोपी (topii) cocked just so and a swing in his walk -- that's the image बाँका (baankaa) conjures. It has an old-fashioned, almost folk-song flavor, showing up often in Bollywood lyrics and older Hindi literature describing a devil-may-care young hero.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सुंदर (sundar) or हैंडसम (haindsam) when they mean 'handsome,' but बाँका (baankaa) specifically evokes swagger and a stylish tilt, not just good looks -- a plain, well-groomed man is not बाँका (baankaa). 2) This word is heard far more often describing a man's bearing than a woman's; for a woman, learners should reach for other terms.

Sense 2 : a dashing young man, a dandy or gallant
noun, masculine Used as a standalone noun referring to the man himself, e.g. गाँव का बाँका (gaanv kaa baankaa, 'the village's dashing young man').
Synonyms: छैला (chhailaa), रंगीला (rangiilaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This noun sense pairs naturally with the adjective sense above -- the बाँका (baankaa) is simply the man who carries himself in that jaunty, swaggering way. It has a slightly rustic, storytelling ring to it, the kind of word you'd meet in a folk tale or an old film song rather than in office chat.
