हाथ (haath)

noun Primarily a body-part noun for 'hand/arm,' but it also appears constantly in idioms about control, help, and involvement, and as a measure of length or of cards in a game.
Sense 1 : hand/arm (body part)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the anchor sense that all the idiomatic uses below grow out of -- once you have हाथ (haath) = hand firmly in mind, expressions like हाथ लगाना (haath lagaanaa) or हाथ बँटाना (haath bantaanaa) become easy to remember because they are literally pictures of hands doing things.
Common Mistakes: 1) हाथ (haath) covers both 'hand' and 'arm/forearm' -- Hindi does not routinely distinguish them the way English does, so learners sometimes hunt for a separate word for 'arm' when हाथ (haath) already covers it (बाँह (baanh) exists for 'arm' specifically but is far less common in speech).
Sample Sentences:

(meraa haath dard kar rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : control, possession, influence ('in one's hands')
noun, masculine Common frame: X किसी के हाथ में है (X kisii ke haath men hai, 'X is in someone's hands/control').
Synonyms: क़ब्ज़ा (qabzaa, more physical/legal possession), बस (bas, control, as in किसी के बस में (kisii ke bas men))
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image English uses when it says something is 'in someone's hands' -- Hindi and English overlap nicely here, which makes this sense an easy, low-risk one to adopt early.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate 'it's in my hands' too literally with पास (paas, 'near/with') when the idiomatic sense of control or decision-making specifically wants हाथ में (haath men), not पास (paas).

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : help, assistance (as in lending a hand)
noun, masculine Appears in fixed collocations like हाथ बँटाना (haath bantaanaa, 'to share the work/help out') and हाथ लगाना (haath lagaanaa, 'to lend a hand' or, contextually, 'to touch').
Synonyms: मदद (madad), सहायता (sahaaytaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this at home constantly: a mother telling her kids रसोई में हाथ बँटाओ (rasoii men haath bantaao, 'help out in the kitchen') -- it carries a warm, family-labor connotation rather than formal assistance.
Common Mistakes: 1) हाथ बँटाना (haath bantaanaa) specifically means to share/divide the workload (help with a task), not just any kind of help -- for general assistance मदद करना (madad karnaa) is the more neutral choice.
Sample Sentences:
माँ ने कहा, "रसोई में हाथ बँटाओ।" Mother said, "Help out in the kitchen."

Sense 4 : a unit of measure (a hand's length, roughly a cubit) or a round/turn in a game
noun, masculine Used in ताश का एक हाथ (taash kaa ek haath, 'a hand/round of cards') and in older measurements of cloth or distance.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense mirrors English 'a hand of cards' almost exactly -- if you play ताश (taash, cards) with friends in India, you will hear हाथ (haath) used this way very naturally.
