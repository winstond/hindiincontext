मज़बूत (mazboot)

adjective A common adjective meaning 'strong' or 'sturdy', used for physical strength, durability, and figuratively for relationships, arguments, or economic strength.
Sense 1 : physically strong, sturdy, durable
adjective
Synonyms: ताक़तवर (taaqatvar), मज़बूती से बना (mazbootii se banaa, descriptive phrase), पक्का (pakkaa, of construction/material), दृढ़ (dridh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any shopkeeper in a Delhi bazaar about a lock, a bag, or a suitcase, and 'मज़बूत है?' (mazboot hai?, 'is it sturdy?') is the first question a buyer asks -- durability sells. It's the default word for anything built to last, from furniture to a friendship.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for ताक़तवर (taaqatvar) when describing an object (a table, a rope, a lock), where मज़बूत (mazboot) is the natural choice; ताक़तवर (taaqatvar) fits better for people and animals with physical power. 2) For a strong-tasting substance (chai, spice) मज़बूत (mazboot) is not used; कड़क (kadak) or तेज़ (tez) are used instead.

Sense 3 : strong, solid, firm (figurative: relationships, arguments, positions, economy)
adjective
Synonyms: पुख़्ता (pukhtaa, especially for arguments/plans), ठोस (thos, especially for evidence/grounds), दृढ़ (dridh, literary, especially for resolve)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly on the news -- मज़बूत सरकार (mazboot sarkaar, 'strong government'), मज़बूत रिश्ता (mazboot rishtaa, 'strong relationship'), मज़बूत इरादे (mazboot iraade, 'strong resolve') -- it's the go-to word whenever Hindi wants to praise stability or resilience in something abstract.
Common Mistakes: 1) In news and everyday talk about the economy, market, or currency, मज़बूत (mazboot) is the standard word (अर्थव्यवस्था मज़बूत है (arthavyvasthaa mazboot hai, 'the economy is strong')) -- learners often default to ताक़तवर (taaqatvar) here, which sounds odd for abstract systems.
