मिस्त्री (mistrii)

noun A loanword (from Portuguese via mestre/mistri) meaning a skilled manual tradesman -- mason, carpenter, plumber, or mechanic depending on context. One core sense.
Sense 1 : skilled tradesman / craftsman (mason, carpenter, mechanic, etc.)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कारीगर (kaariigar, craftsman, broader term), बढ़ई (badhaii, carpenter specifically), राज मिस्त्री (raaj mistrii, mason specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every neighborhood in Delhi-NCR has its go-to मिस्त्री (mistrii) -- the guy you call when the tap leaks, the fan stops, or the scooter won't start; it is a term of practical respect for hands-on skill, not a formal job title. You will also hear राज मिस्त्री (raaj mistrii) specifically for a mason who builds walls, showing how the base word combines with a qualifier to narrow the trade.
Common Mistakes: 1) मिस्त्री (mistrii) is a general term that shifts meaning with context -- a plumbing job makes it 'plumber,' a car problem makes it 'mechanic,' a wall makes it 'mason.' Learners sometimes assume it always means one fixed trade, but Hindi speakers let the situation supply the specialty. 2) For a specifically car/machine mechanic, speakers often just say मिस्त्री (mistrii) or the more specific मोटर मिस्त्री (motar mistrii) rather than a separate borrowed word.
