तौर (taur)

noun A noun meaning 'way' or 'manner', met by learners almost entirely inside fixed frames like तौर पर (taur par) and तौर तरीके (taur tariike) rather than used freely on its own.
Sense 1 : way, manner (in the postpositional frame X तौर पर)
noun, masculine Almost always appears as [adjective/noun] + तौर पर (taur par) to form adverbial phrases, e.g. आम तौर पर (aam taur par, 'generally'), खास तौर पर (khaas taur par, 'especially'), कानूनी तौर पर (kaanoonii taur par, 'legally').
Synonyms: तरीका (tariikaa), ढंग (dhang), रूप (roop)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear आम तौर पर (aam taur par) constantly in everyday Delhi speech -- it is the go-to way to say 'generally speaking' before making a broad statement, much like English speakers reach for 'generally' or 'usually' to soften a claim.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use तौर (taur) alone the way English uses 'way', but it almost never stands without पर (par); the meaning lives in the combination तौर पर (taur par), not in the bare noun. 2) Confusing तौर पर (taur par) with तरह से (tarah se) -- both translate as 'in a ... way', but तौर पर (taur par) leans toward describing a category, capacity, or official/formal manner (कानूनी तौर पर (kaanoonii taur par, 'legally')), while तरह से (tarah se) leans toward describing the style or method of an action.
Sample Sentences:
आम तौर पर वह समय पर आता है। Generally speaking, he comes on time.
(aam taur par vah samay par aataa hai।)

Sense 2 : manner, style, method (as in तौर तरीके)
noun, masculine Chiefly seen in the fixed plural compound तौर तरीके (taur tariike), referring to someone's overall manners, habits, or way of doing things -- rarely used as a standalone singular noun outside the तौर पर (taur par) frame.
Synonyms: तरीका (tariikaa), अंदाज़ (andaaz)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: तौर तरीके (taur tariike) is what an elder might criticize in a young person -- 'उसके तौर तरीके ठीक नहीं हैं' (uske taur tariike thiik nahiin hain, 'his manners/ways are not right') -- capturing both etiquette and general conduct in one phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use तौर (taur) alone to mean 'manners' or 'style', but native speakers reach for the paired form तौर तरीके (taur tariike) for that meaning; the bare singular sounds incomplete in this sense.
