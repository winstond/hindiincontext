गति (gati)

noun Primarily means 'speed/pace' in everyday use, but carries a rich secondary life as 'motion/movement' (physics-style), and as 'fate/condition' or 'course/way' in more reflective or literary-tinged speech, e.g. talking about someone's life circumstances or the afterlife.
Sense 1 : speed, pace
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रफ़्तार (raftaar), चाल (chaal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly on Indian road signs: गति सीमा (gati siimaa, 'speed limit'). It is also the default word in any scientific or technical register for velocity, so it bridges casual and formal contexts easily.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्पीड (spiid) from English, which is common in casual speech, but गति (gati) is the word used in writing, instructions, and anything remotely formal (traffic signs, news reports, exam questions). 2) गति (gati) usually takes धीमी (dhiimii, 'slow') or तेज़ (tez, 'fast') as its adjective, not the verb-based descriptions an English speaker might default to.
Sample Sentences:
गाड़ी की गति बहुत तेज़ थी। The car's speed was very fast.
(gaadii kii gati bahut tez thii।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
यहाँ गति सीमा साठ किलोमीटर प्रति घंटा है। The speed limit here is sixty kilometers per hour.
(yahaan gati siimaa saath kilomiitar prati ghantaa hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
कृपया अपनी गति कम करें, आगे स्कूल है। Please reduce your speed, there's a school ahead.

Register: Formal -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

Sense 2 : motion, movement
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चाल (chaal), हरकत (harkat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense you meet in a school physics class -- गति के नियम (gati ke niyam, 'laws of motion') -- and also in more philosophical or devotional writing about the constant movement of time, life, or the universe.
Sample Sentences:
न्यूटन ने गति के नियम दिए। Newton gave the laws of motion.
(nyootan ne gati ke niyam die।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
हर वस्तु में गति का एक निश्चित नियम होता है। Every object has a fixed law governing its motion.
(har vastu men gati kaa ek nishchit niyam hotaa hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
पृथ्वी की गति के कारण दिन और रात होते हैं। Day and night happen because of the earth's motion.
(prithvii kii gati ke kaaran din aur raat hote hain।)

जीवन में गति बनी रहनी चाहिए, रुकना ठीक नहीं। There should always be motion in life; stopping isn't good.

Sense 3 : fate, condition, course of life
noun, feminine Often appears with a possessive: किसी की गति (kisii kii gati, 'someone's fate/condition').
Synonyms: दशा (dashaa), हालत (haalat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in the common expression क्या गति होगी (kyaa gati hogii, 'what will become of [him/her]'), often said with real worry about someone's future or well-being. It also appears in a devotional register, where a soul's गति (gati) after death (its path or destination) is a serious theological topic.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense can sound heavier or more literary than 'condition' feels in English -- it is often used sympathetically or gravely, as in describing someone's sorry state, rather than in a neutral, everyday way. 2) Do not confuse with गति (gati) meaning speed; context (usually a possessive plus a tone of pity or concern) signals this sense.
Sample Sentences:
अगर उसने पढ़ाई नहीं की, तो उसकी क्या गति होगी? If he doesn't study, what will become of him?
(agar usne padhaaii nahiin kii, to uskii kyaa gati hogii?)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Future -- Voice: Active
बेचारे की बड़ी बुरी गति हुई है। The poor fellow has come to a very bad state.
(bechaare kii badii burii gati huii hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
