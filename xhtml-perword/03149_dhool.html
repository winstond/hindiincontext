धूल (dhool)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'dust,' with a common figurative extension in idioms about worthlessness or humility.
Sense 1 : dust; fine dry particles of dirt
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गर्द (gard), रज (raj, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a country with dusty roads and open windows, धूल (dhool) is a constant fact of household life -- swept off doorsteps every morning and wiped from furniture in a running battle. It also carries a strong figurative charge: धूल फाँकना (dhool phaanknaa, 'to eat dust,' i.e. to struggle or waste effort) and धूल चटाना (dhool chataanaa, 'to make someone eat dust,' i.e. to defeat them badly) both use dust as a stand-in for defeat and hardship, much like the English 'bite the dust.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse धूल (dhool) with धुआँ (dhuaan, 'smoke') because they sound similar -- धूल is the solid grit that settles on surfaces, not the airborne haze from fire. 2) मिट्टी (mittii, 'soil/earth/clay') is often reached for instead of धूल when describing dusty roads or shoes, but मिट्टी is the substance itself while धूल is specifically its fine, airborne or settled form.
