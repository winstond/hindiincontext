लटकाना (latkaanaa)

verb The causative of लटकना (lataknaa, 'to hang/dangle') -- means 'to hang (something) up' literally, and also 'to keep someone hanging' or delay a matter figuratively.
Sense 1 : to hang, suspend (something)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: टांगना (taangnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture hanging a coat on a hook -- कोट को खूँटी पर लटकाना (kot ko khoontii par latkaanaa, 'to hang the coat on the peg') -- or garlands and lights during a festival. This is the everyday, concrete sense you will hear most often around the house.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use लटकना (lataknaa) when they mean the transitive act of hanging something up -- लटकना (lataknaa) describes the coat dangling on its own, while लटकाना (latkaanaa) describes you putting it there.
Sample Sentences:
माँ ने कोट को खूँटी पर लटकाया। Mother hung the coat on the peg.
(maan ne kot ko khoontii par latkaayaa।)

Sense 2 : to keep someone waiting, delay/stall (a person or matter)
verb, transitive Often used with a person as object plus a time expression, e.g. महीनों लटकाना (mahiinon latkaanaa, 'to string along for months'), describing an unresolved case, application, or promise.
Synonyms: टालना (taalnaa, to postpone/put off), लटकाए रखना (latkaae rakhnaa, to keep dangling, phrase variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly about government offices and paperwork: फ़ाइल को महीनों से लटका रखा है (faail ko mahiinon se latkaa rakhaa hai, 'they've kept the file hanging for months'). It captures that very Indian bureaucratic frustration of a case that just never gets closed.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is about people or pending matters, not physical objects -- don't confuse it with sense 1's literal hanging. 2) It carries a mildly negative, frustrated tone, as if someone is being unfairly made to wait; it's not neutral like 'postpone.'
