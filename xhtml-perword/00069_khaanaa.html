खाना (khaanaa)

verb/noun Covers two closely linked things: the verb 'to eat' and the noun 'food/meal' derived from it. Not to be confused with ख़ाना (khaanaa), a distinct Persian-derived word meaning 'compartment, box' (as in कारख़ाना (kaarkhaanaa, 'factory')).
Sense 1 : to eat
verb, transitive
Synonyms: भोजन करना (bhojan karnaa, formal), जीमना (jiimnaa, regional/rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: खाना (khaanaa) is one of the first verbs every learner meets, and it shows up constantly in the everyday question खाना खाया? (khaanaa khaayaa?, 'have you eaten?') -- a genuine greeting of care in Delhi households, not just small talk. It also extends metaphorically, as in मार खाना (maar khaanaa, 'to take a beating') or धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to be deceived'), where 'eating' means 'receiving/suffering' something.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget खाना (khaanaa) is transitive and take ने (ne) in the perfective -- मैंने खाना खाया (mainne khaanaa khaayaa, 'I ate food') is correct, not मैं खाया (main khaayaa). 2) The noun खाना (khaanaa, 'food') and the verb खाना (khaanaa, 'to eat') look identical, which can cause confusion in phrases like खाना खाना (khaanaa khaanaa, 'to eat food') -- this doubling is normal, not a mistake.

(main roz subah andaa khaataa hoon।)

Sense 2 : to receive/suffer (something negative), figurative extension
verb, transitive Occurs in fixed collocations like मार खाना (maar khaanaa, 'to get beaten'), धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to be cheated'), कसम खाना (kasam khaanaa, 'to take an oath'), ठोकर खाना (thokar khaanaa, 'to stumble'). The object is fixed within each idiom rather than freely chosen.
Synonyms: सहना (sahnaa, 'to endure', broader meaning), झेलना (jhelnaa, 'to bear/undergo')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same खाना (khaanaa) as 'to eat', extended metaphorically: in Hindi you 'eat' a beating, a betrayal, or a stumble, framing misfortune as something absorbed into the body. Once you notice this pattern, you'll spot it everywhere -- धक्का खाना (dhakkaa khaanaa, 'to get jolted/shoved') is another common one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to translate these idioms word-for-word from English ('to receive a beating') instead of learning the खाना (khaanaa) collocation as a fixed chunk. 2) कसम खाना (kasam khaanaa, 'to swear an oath') can confuse learners since 'eating an oath' has no English logic -- it simply must be memorized as a set phrase.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : food, meal
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भोजन (bhojan, formal), आहार (aahaar, formal/dietary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: खाना (khaanaa) is the everyday word for a meal, as in खाना बनाना (khaanaa banaanaa, 'to cook/make food') -- a phrase you'll hear in nearly every Indian household multiple times a day. It's less formal than भोजन (bhojan), which shows up more on menus or in written/polite contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) खाना (khaanaa) as a noun is masculine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take masculine forms -- खाना अच्छा है (khaanaa achchhaa hai, 'the food is good'), not अच्छी (achchhii).
