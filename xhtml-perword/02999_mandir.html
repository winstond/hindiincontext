मंदिर (mandir)

noun A concrete noun for a Hindu temple; it has one primary sense but a common figurative extension for a place treated with temple-like reverence.
Sense 1 : Hindu temple, a place of worship
noun, masculine
Synonyms: देवालय (devaalay, literary), देवस्थान (devasthaan, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Almost every neighborhood in Delhi-NCR has a small local मंदिर (mandir), often just a single room or even a roadside shrine under a tree, and people casually drop in for a few seconds on their way to work. The word also anchors common compounds like मंदिर जाना (mandir jaanaa, 'to go to the temple'), which is a routine weekly or daily habit for many families rather than a special occasion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मंदिर (mandir) for any place of worship regardless of religion; in everyday Hindi it specifically means a Hindu temple. For a mosque, church, or gurdwara, native speakers use मस्जिद (masjid), गिरजाघर (girjaaghar), and गुरुद्वारा (gurudvaaraa) respectively.

Sense 2 : figurative: a place or institution regarded with reverence
noun, masculine Used in fixed honorific phrases like विद्या का मंदिर (vidyaa kaa mandir, 'temple of learning') to elevate an institution, especially schools.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in school mottos and speeches, where a school is called विद्या का मंदिर (vidyaa kaa mandir) to stress that education deserves the same respect as worship. It's a formal, slightly old-fashioned flourish rather than something said in casual conversation.
