जुटना (jutnaa)

verb An intransitive verb with two related everyday senses: (1) people or things coming together/assembling in one place, and (2) getting down to work or applying oneself busily to a task. Closely paired with its transitive counterpart जुटाना (jutaanaa, 'to gather/arrange/procure') -- जुटना is what happens on its own, जुटाना is making it happen.
Sense 1 : to gather, assemble (of people or a crowd)
verb, intransitive Typically used with a collective subject like भीड़ (bhiid, 'crowd') or लोग (log, 'people'); the place is usually marked with पर (par) or में (men).
Synonyms: इकट्ठा होना (ikatthaa honaa), जमा होना (jamaa honaa), एकत्र होना (ekatr honaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in news reports: भीड़ जुट गई (bhiid jut gaii, 'a crowd gathered') after an accident, a cricket match, or a political rally -- it's the standard verb for describing a crowd forming on its own, without anyone having organized it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मिलना (milnaa) here, but मिलना (milnaa) is about people meeting each other by arrangement, while जुटना (jutnaa) describes a crowd or mass forming, often spontaneously, at a spot.
Sample Sentences:
दुर्घटना के बाद वहाँ भीड़ जुट गई। A crowd gathered there after the accident.
(durghatnaa ke baad vahaan bhiid jut gaii।)

Sense 2 : to get down to, apply oneself busily (to a task)
verb, intransitive Takes the task with में (men) -- काम में जुटना (kaam men jutnaa, 'to get down to work') -- and is often used in the perfective to show someone has thrown themselves into an activity.
Synonyms: लग जाना (lag jaanaa), जुड़ जाना (jud jaanaa, less common in this sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the verb you'll hear about someone before an exam or deadline: वह तैयारी में जुट गया (vah taiyaarii men jut gayaa, 'he got down to preparing') -- it carries a sense of focused, almost head-down busyness, like rolling up your sleeves.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is easy to confuse with the crowd-gathering sense since the verb form is identical; context (a single person plus a task marked with में (men), versus a crowd at a place) is what tells them apart.
