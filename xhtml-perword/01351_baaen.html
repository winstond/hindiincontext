बाएँ (baaen)

adverb/adjective The oblique/adverbial form of बायाँ (baayaan, 'left'), used mainly as the direction word 'left/to the left' and in the fixed pair बाएँ-दाएँ (baaen-daaen, 'left and right').
Sense 1 : in the leftward direction
adverb Often paired with a postposition: बाएँ हाथ पर (baaen haath par, 'on the left hand side'), or used bare as a direction command: बाएँ मुड़िए (baaen mudie, 'turn left').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear from every auto-rickshaw driver and every well-meaning aunty giving directions in the market -- बाएँ से दूसरी गली (baaen se doosrii galii, 'the second lane from the left') is classic Delhi direction-giving. It pairs naturally with दाएँ (daaen, 'right') and सीधे (siidhe, 'straight') as the three basic direction words every learner needs early on.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say दाएँ (daaen) and बाएँ (baaen) in the wrong order out of habit from English 'left and right' -- Hindi speakers conventionally say बाएँ-दाएँ (baaen-daaen) with left first, same as English, so this is usually fine, but the fixed pairing itself is worth noting as an idiom rather than two separate words used ad hoc. 2) This form is used adverbially and does not change for gender or number the way बायाँ (baayaan) does when modifying a noun directly -- learners sometimes wrongly try to inflect बाएँ (baaen) itself.

Sense 2 : left (side), oblique form used before a postposition
adjective This is the oblique form of बायाँ (baayaan) required before a following postposition, as in बाएँ हाथ से (baaen haath se, 'with the left hand').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This oblique form links directly back to बायाँ (baayaan); learners who know that लड़का (ladkaa) becomes लड़के (ladke) before postpositions will recognize the same pattern here.
