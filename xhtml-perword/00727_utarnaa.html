उतरना (utarnaa)

verb An intransitive verb built around the idea of 'coming down' -- physically descending, disembarking, and by extension fading, wearing off, or being resolved. Its causative partner उतारना (utaarnaa) means 'to take down / bring down' and is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to descend, get down/off
verb, intransitive Takes से (se) for the place descended from: बस से उतरना (bas se utarnaa, 'to get off the bus').
Synonyms: नीचे आना (niiche aanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday word for getting off a bus, auto, or train -- you will hear conductors shout अगला स्टॉप, उतर जाओ (aglaa stop, utar jaao, 'next stop, get off') across Delhi buses. It also describes getting off a horse, elephant, or even a high perch, keeping the physical sense of 'coming down' at its core.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for नीचे जाना (niiche jaanaa) when getting off a vehicle, but उतरना (utarnaa) is the natural verb for disembarking from a bus, train, plane, or horse. 2) The place you get down from is marked with से (se), not से पहले (se pahle) or किनारे (kinaare) -- सीढ़ी से उतरना (siidhii se utarnaa, 'to come down the stairs'), not सीढ़ी में।
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to go down, drop, or subside (of levels, water, prices, swelling, etc.)
verb, intransitive Used for water levels, floods, swelling, fever, and prices going down: बुखार उतर गया (bukhaar utar gayaa, 'the fever went down').
Synonyms: घटना (ghatnaa, for prices/quantities), कम होना (kam honaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi treats fever, swelling, and flood water as things that 'descend' rather than simply 'decrease' -- बाढ़ का पानी उतर गया (baadh kaa paanii utar gayaa, 'the floodwater has gone down') and बुखार उतरना (bukhaar utarnaa) both borrow the same downward image from the core physical sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) For a fever or swelling going down, learners often default to कम होना (kam honaa), but उतरना (utarnaa) is the more natural, almost physical way to describe it subsiding, as if it is 'descending' out of the body.

Sense 3 : to fade, wear off (of color, intoxication, effect)
verb, intransitive Common with रंग (rang, 'color'), नशा (nashaa, 'intoxication'), and असर (asar, 'effect') as subjects.
Synonyms: फीका पड़ना (phiikaa padnaa, for color), ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa, less vivid)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as one 'descends' from a height, colors, dyes, and intoxication are imagined as 'coming down' off a person -- रंग उतर गया (rang utar gayaa, 'the color has faded') and उसका नशा उतर गया (uskaa nashaa utar gayaa, 'his intoxication wore off') both use this same everyday metaphor.
Common Mistakes: 1) नशा उतरना (nashaa utarnaa, 'to sober up') is a set phrase; learners sometimes translate 'sober up' literally instead of reaching for this idiom, which native speakers use very naturally after a night out or a festival.
