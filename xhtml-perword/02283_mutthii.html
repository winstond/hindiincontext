मुट्ठी (mutthii)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'fist' or 'handful' -- the shape of a closed hand and the quantity it can hold; also lives inside common idioms about control and secrecy.
Sense 1 : fist (the closed hand)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear मुट्ठी बाँधना (mutthii baandhnaa, 'to clench the fist') used literally for a child making a fist, and also figuratively for gritting through hardship -- the fist as a small store of resolve.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for हाथ (haath, hand) when they mean a closed fist specifically -- मुट्ठी (mutthii) is the clenched shape, not the open hand or arm.

Sense 2 : handful (the quantity a closed hand holds)
noun, feminine Used with भर (bhar) as मुट्ठी भर (mutthii bhar, 'a handful of') to quantify small amounts, e.g. of grain, rice, or coins.
Synonyms: चुटकी (chutkii, a pinch, smaller quantity)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: मुट्ठी भर चावल (mutthii bhar chaaval, 'a handful of rice') is the kind of phrase you hear in kitchens and also in stories about frugality -- a handful is the classic unit of 'just enough' or 'barely anything' in Hindi storytelling.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : figurative: control, grip, or something kept secret/hidden
noun, feminine Common in fixed expressions: किसी को मुट्ठी में रखना (kisii ko mutthii men rakhnaa, 'to keep someone in one's fist/under one's control'); मुट्ठी में होना (mutthii men honaa, 'to be in one's grip/control'); बात मुट्ठी में रखना (baat mutthii men rakhnaa, 'to keep a matter close/secret').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The image is very physical: power in Hindi is often something you hold shut in your hand rather than something you 'have' abstractly, so a boss who 'has everyone in his मुट्ठी (mutthii)' is picturing a closed fist, not a chart of authority.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only appears inside these fixed idiomatic frames -- मुट्ठी (mutthii) alone does not mean 'control' outside of them, so learners should not use it as a free-standing abstract noun for power.
