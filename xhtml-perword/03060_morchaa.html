मोरचा (morchaa)

noun Primarily a battlefield/political term meaning a front-line position or defensive stand (morchaa), which extends into a common political sense of a united protest front or coalition; also carries a rarer, more literal sense of rust.
Sense 1 : a defensive front, position, or stand (literal or figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मोर्चा (morchaa, variant spelling), मुहाज़ (muhaaz)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear मोरचा संभालना (morchaa sambhaalnaa) used well beyond the battlefield -- a manager 'holding the front' at work, a mother 'taking charge' of a household crisis. It carries the flavor of stepping up to defend or manage a difficult situation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only learn the political-rally sense (मोरचा निकालना (morchaa nikaalnaa)) and miss this more basic military/defensive sense, e.g. मोरचा संभालना (morchaa sambhaalnaa, 'to hold the front/take charge of the defense'). 2) The verb पार करना (paar karnaa) is not used here; the fixed collocation is मोरचा संभालना (morchaa sambhaalnaa) or मोरचा लेना (morchaa lenaa).

Sense 2 : a political front or coalition, especially one formed in protest
noun, masculine Common in the fixed phrase मोरचा निकालना (morchaa nikaalnaa, 'to take out a protest march') and in party names like संयुक्त मोरचा (sanyukt morchaa, 'United Front').
Synonyms: गठबंधन (gathbandhan, coalition, broader use), मंच (manch, platform/front)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Open any Hindi newspaper during election season and you will see this word in headlines -- students, farmers, or opposition parties 'taking out a मोरचा (morchaa)' to press their demands. It is the everyday word for a political or protest alliance in Indian public life.
Common Mistakes: 1) मोरचा निकालना (morchaa nikaalnaa) specifically means organizing a protest march/demonstration, not just 'forming' a coalition -- learners sometimes use निकालना (nikaalnaa) loosely for 'to form,' which sounds off here.

Sense 3 : rust (on metal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ज़ंग (zang)
Frequency: low
Common Mistakes: 1) ज़ंग (zang) is by far the more common everyday word for rust; मोरचा (morchaa) in this sense is heard less often in casual speech and can sound slightly old-fashioned or regional to some listeners.
