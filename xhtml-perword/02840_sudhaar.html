सुधार (sudhaar)

noun A single core meaning cluster around 'improvement/reform': the noun names the act or result of fixing, correcting, or improving something, from a small correction to a large social/political reform.
Sense 1 : improvement, correction, reform
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुधार (sudhaar), बेहतरी (behatrii), संशोधन (sanshodhan, more formal, esp. of texts/rules), सुधाई (sudhaaii, colloquial, of objects/repairs)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear सुधार (sudhaar) constantly in Indian news and politics -- आर्थिक सुधार (aarthik sudhaar, 'economic reforms'), शिक्षा में सुधार (shikshaa men sudhaar, 'improvement in education') -- it's the standard word for policy-level 'reform,' not just personal betterment. It shares its root with the verb सुधारना (sudhaarnaa, 'to improve/fix, transitive') and the intransitive सुधरना (sudharnaa, 'to improve, get better') -- all three are worth learning together as a family.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सुधार (sudhaar) where सुधारना (sudhaarnaa, the verb 'to improve/correct') is needed -- सुधार is the noun naming the result or process, not the action itself, so a sentence like 'मैं अपनी हिंदी सुधार करूँगा' sounds off; the natural verb is either सुधारना (sudhaarnaa) directly ('हिंदी सुधारूँगा') or the phrase सुधार करना (sudhaar karnaa) with सुधार as the object. 2) In the sense of 'reform' (social, political, economic), सुधार is almost always used with a following genitive or compound, e.g. आर्थिक सुधार (aarthik sudhaar, 'economic reform'), not as a free-standing abstract noun the way 'reform' works in English.
