भक्ति (bhakti)

noun Primarily a noun meaning devotion or worship, most often in a religious/spiritual sense; also used more loosely for deep devotion to a person, cause, or ideal.
Sense 1 : religious devotion, worship
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पूजा (poojaa, worship, ritual-focused), श्रद्धा (shraddhaa, reverence/faith), उपासना (upaasnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: भक्ति (bhakti) is the word behind the Bhakti movement, the centuries-long devotional tradition (Kabir, Meera, Tulsidas) that emphasized a personal, loving relationship with God over ritual or caste hierarchy. When Hindus talk about भक्ति भाव (bhakti bhaav, 'a devotional mood/feeling'), they mean that same heart-centered surrender, distinct from mere religious duty.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use भक्ति (bhakti) interchangeably with पूजा (poojaa), but भक्ति (bhakti) is the inner devotional feeling/state, while पूजा (poojaa) is the outward ritual act (lighting a lamp, offering flowers, etc.).

Sense 2 : deep devotion or loyalty to a person, cause, or ideal (secular extension)
noun, feminine Takes की (kii) to mark the object of devotion, e.g. देश की भक्ति (desh kii bhakti, 'devotion to the country').
Synonyms: समर्पण (samarpan, surrender/dedication), निष्ठा (nishthaa, loyalty, formal), लगन (lagan, passionate dedication)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word stretched beyond temple and God -- you'll hear देशभक्ति (deshbhakti, 'patriotism', literally 'devotion to the country') on Republic Day, or guru भक्ति (guru bhakti, devotion to one's teacher). It signals a totality of commitment, not just liking or respecting something.
