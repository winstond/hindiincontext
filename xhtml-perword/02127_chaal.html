चाल (chaal)

noun Covers physical movement (gait, speed, a move in a game) and, by extension, cunning or trickery -- the same root that gives you चलना (chalnaa, 'to move/go').
Sense 1 : gait, way of walking or moving
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चलने का ढंग (chalne kaa dhang, descriptive phrase), गति (gati, more about speed than style)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in phrases like हाथी की चाल (haathii kii chaal, 'elephant's gait') describing a slow, stately walk, or मस्त चाल (mast chaal, 'a carefree, swaggering walk') -- Hindi loves describing personality through how someone moves.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse चाल (chaal) with चलन (chalan), which means 'custom, practice, currency of use' rather than a person's manner of walking. 2) चाल (chaal) describes the *style* of movement (a horse's gait, someone's swagger), not the act of moving itself, which is चलना (chalnaa).

Sense 2 : trick, ploy, scheme (often deceptive)
noun, feminine Very commonly used with चलना (chalnaa) in the fixed expression चाल चलना (chaal chalnaa, 'to play a trick/make a move') and with the verb समझना (samajhnaa) in चाल समझ जाना (chaal samajh jaanaa, 'to see through someone's trick').
Synonyms: चालबाज़ी (chaalbaazii, more elaborate scheming), धोखा (dhokhaa, emphasizes the betrayal), षड्यंत्र (shadyantr, formal, 'conspiracy')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense is everywhere in political commentary and family dramas alike -- नेता की चाल (netaa kii chaal, 'the politician's ploy') or सास की चाल (saas kii chaal, 'the mother-in-law's scheme') are stock phrases in Hindi soap operas and news debates, where everyone is always suspicious of everyone else's चाल (chaal).
Common Mistakes: 1) चाल (chaal) alone usually implies a scheme or ploy already in motion, whereas धोखा (dhokhaa) is the act or feeling of betrayal itself -- they overlap but are not interchangeable. 2) The set phrase is चाल चलना (chaal chalnaa), literally 'to move a move' -- learners sometimes try चाल करना (chaal karnaa) by analogy with other noun+करना constructions, but this sounds unnatural.

Sense 3 : speed, pace (of a vehicle, process, or the economy)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गति (gati), रफ़्तार (raftaar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this used for the pace of the economy or the market too -- बाज़ार की चाल (baazaar kii chaal, 'the market's movement/trend') -- extending 'speed of walking' into 'the way something is trending.'
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense चाल (chaal) often appears with बढ़ना (badhnaa, 'to increase') or धीमी होना (dhiimii honaa, 'to slow down') -- e.g. गाड़ी की चाल (gaadii kii chaal, 'the speed of the vehicle') -- and learners sometimes reach for गति (gati) or रफ़्तार (raftaar) instead, which is fine, but चाल (chaal) is the more colloquial, everyday choice.
Sample Sentences:
गाड़ी की चाल धीरे-धीरे बढ़ रही थी। The car's speed was gradually increasing.

Sense 4 : a move (in a game like chess)
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear at any chess ({{शतरंज}}) table in a park -- अच्छी चाल (achchhii chaal, 'a good move') is high praise. It bridges naturally into the 'trick, ploy' sense below, since a clever move on the board and a clever move in life are conceptually the same word.
