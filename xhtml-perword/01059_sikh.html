सिख (sikh)

noun/adjective A noun referring to a follower of Sikhism, also used adjectivally to describe things associated with the Sikh community. Not to be confused with सीख (siikh, 'advice/lesson') or सीख (siikh, 'skewer'), which are spelled with a long ी and are different words entirely.
Sense 1 : a Sikh person
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi has one of the largest Sikh populations outside Punjab, and you will hear सिख (sikh) constantly in everyday conversation here -- from neighborhood gurdwaras to references to सिख भाई-बहन (sikh bhaaii-bahan, 'Sikh brothers and sisters') in civic speech. The word comes from the same root as सीखना (siikhnaa, 'to learn') and सिख्खी (sikhkhii) or शिष्य (shishy, 'disciple'), reflecting the core idea of a Sikh as a 'disciple' of the Guru.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this word in writing with सीख (siikh), which means 'advice' or a 'skewer' -- the vowel length (short इ vs long ी) is the only difference and changes the meaning completely. 2) The word is used both as a noun ('a Sikh') and as an adjective before another noun, e.g. सिख धर्म (sikh dharm, 'the Sikh religion'), without changing form.

Sense 2 : relating to Sikhism or Sikh people
adjective Used invariantly before a noun, e.g. सिख धर्म (sikh dharm, 'Sikh religion'), सिख इतिहास (sikh itihaas, 'Sikh history').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this adjectival use in phrases like सिख गुरुद्वारा (sikh gurudvaaraa, 'Sikh gurdwara') or सिख परंपरा (sikh parampraa, 'Sikh tradition') -- it simply attaches to the noun it modifies without any change in form.
