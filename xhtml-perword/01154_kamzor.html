कमज़ोर (kamzor)

adjective A common adjective meaning 'weak' -- covers physical weakness, weak health, weak arguments/relationships, and by extension a fondness or soft spot for someone/something. Also spelled कमजोर (kamjor) without the nuqta; both are widely seen.
Sense 1 : physically weak, frail
adjective
Synonyms: दुर्बल (durbal, formal), निर्बल (nirbal, literary), कमज़ोर-सा (kamzor-saa, somewhat weak)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around health talk in Delhi -- a grandmother saying किसी का शरीर कमज़ोर है (kisii kaa shariir kamzor hai, 'someone's body is weak') to mean they need more ghee, milk, or rest, not that they're ill. It's the default word for 'run down' in everyday family concern.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बीमार (biimaar, 'sick') when they mean कमज़ोर (kamzor) -- a person can be कमज़ोर (kamzor) (weak, low on strength) without currently being sick; the two overlap but aren't the same claim.

Sense 2 : weak, unconvincing (of an argument, plan, case, etc.)
adjective
Synonyms: लचर (lachar, somewhat rare), सतही (satahii, shallow/weak)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used just like English 'weak' in this abstract sense -- a कमज़ोर दलील (kamzor daliil, 'a weak argument') or कमज़ोर योजना (kamzor yojnaa, 'a weak plan') is instantly understood the same way a Delhi lawyer or office colleague would use 'weak' in English.

Sense 3 : financially weak, poor
adjective
Synonyms: गरीब (gariib), निर्धन (nirdhan, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In government and news contexts you constantly hear कमज़ोर वर्ग (kamzor varg, 'weaker section') -- the official-ish term for economically disadvantaged groups, used in policy talk about reservations and welfare schemes.
