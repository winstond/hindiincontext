पतंगा (patangaa)

noun A moth or large flying insect drawn to light; closely related to पतंग (patang), which means 'kite' (the flying toy) but can in some regions also refer to this same insect -- the two words overlap and are easily confused.
Sense 1 : moth (insect attracted to flame/light)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कीट (kiit, general term for insect)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The image of a पतंगा (patangaa) circling a diya or candle flame until it burns itself is a stock image in Hindi poetry and film lyrics, symbolizing reckless, self-destructive love or devotion -- shama and parvana (the candle and the moth) is a whole poetic motif borrowed from Urdu tradition. This gives rise to the extended sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse with पतंग (patang), which most commonly means 'kite' (the flying toy) in the Delhi-NCR region -- पतंगा (patangaa) with the extra आ is the safer, unambiguous word for the insect. 2) The moth-to-flame image is used figuratively in Hindi much like in English, so learners sometimes miss the figurative sense entirely and only learn the literal insect meaning.

Sense 2 : figurative: one who is helplessly drawn to something/someone, often to their own ruin
noun, masculine Almost always appears inside the fixed comparison शमा का पतंगा (shamaa kaa patangaa, 'moth of the candle flame') or a similar phrase pairing पतंगा (patangaa) with शमा (shamaa, flame/candle) or आग (aag, fire) -- it is rarely used as a free-standing metaphor without that pairing.
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll meet this mostly in older Hindi film songs and ghazal-influenced lyrics, where a lover is called a पतंगा (patangaa) helplessly circling the beloved's flame -- a direct borrowing of the Urdu shama-parvana (candle-moth) motif into Hindi film poetry.
