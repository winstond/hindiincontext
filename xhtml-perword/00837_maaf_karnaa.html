माफ़ करना (maaf karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the Persian-origin word माफ़ (maaf, 'forgiven/excused') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means 'to forgive' or 'to excuse' and is one of the most everyday polite expressions in Hindi, used both for genuine apologies and for small courtesy phrases like 'excuse me.'
Sense 1 : to forgive, to pardon
verb, transitive The person forgiven is marked with को (ko): मुझे माफ़ करो (mujhe maaf karo, 'forgive me') -- literally 'to me, do forgiveness.'
Synonyms: क्षमा करना (kshamaa karnaa, formal), माफ़ी देना (maafii denaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind the common phrase मुझे माफ़ कर दो (mujhe maaf kar do, 'please forgive me'), often heard in heartfelt apologies between friends and family, and it carries more emotional weight than the polite 'excuse me' sense below -- it is used when something real needs mending, not just a bump on the metro.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say माफ़ करना (maaf karnaa) as if the object is the wrong done, but the object taken with को (ko) is the *person* being forgiven, not the offense: मुझे माफ़ कर दो (mujhe maaf kar do, 'forgive me'), not the mistake itself. 2) The offense, if mentioned, needs its own postposition, often के लिए (ke lie): इस गलती के लिए मुझे माफ़ कर दो (is galtii ke lie mujhe maaf kar do, 'forgive me for this mistake').
Sample Sentences:
मुझे माफ़ कर दो, मुझसे बहुत बड़ी गलती हो गई। Forgive me, I made a really big mistake.
(mujhe maaf kar do, mujhse bahut badii galtii ho gaii।)

Sense 2 : excuse me / sorry (polite formula)
verb, transitive Used as a fixed imperative formula -- माफ़ कीजिए (maaf kiijie) or माफ़ करना (maaf karnaa) itself -- to preface a request, interruption, or minor apology, much like English 'excuse me' or 'sorry.'
Synonyms: माफ़ कीजिए (maaf kiijie, fixed polite form), सॉरी (sorii, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear माफ़ कीजिए (maaf kiijie) from a shopkeeper stepping past you in a crowded Delhi market, or from someone politely interrupting to ask directions -- it softens an intrusion the way 'excuse me' does in English, without necessarily implying real wrongdoing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for सॉरी (sorii) in every context out of habit, but माफ़ कीजिए (maaf kiijie) or ज़रा सुनिए (zaraa sunie) sound more natural when politely interrupting a stranger, reserving सॉरी (sorii) for lighter everyday bumps.
Sample Sentences:
माफ़ कीजिए, क्या यह सीट खाली है? Excuse me, is this seat free?
(maaf kiijie, kyaa yah siit khaalii hai?)
