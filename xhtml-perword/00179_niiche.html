नीचे (niiche)

adverb/postposition/adjective Mainly used as an adverb/postposition meaning 'below, down, underneath'; also functions as an adjective in a few fixed phrases. All senses share the core idea of a lower position.
Sense 1 : downward direction or lower position (no object of comparison)
adverb Used alone (without a following noun + की/के) to mean 'down' or 'downstairs' in a general sense, e.g. नीचे आओ (niiche aao, 'come down').
Synonyms: तले (tale, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Indian homes and apartment buildings, नीचे (niiche) is the everyday word for 'downstairs' -- a mother calling out नीचे आ जाओ (niiche aa jaao, 'come downstairs') is a very common household scene.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add का (kaa) or की (kii) even when नीचे (niiche) is used alone as 'downstairs/down' -- no possessive marker is needed here since there is no object being related to, e.g. नीचे आओ (niiche aao, 'come down'), not नीचे का आओ.
Sample Sentences:

(niiche aa jaao, khaanaa taiyaar hai।)

Sense 2 : below, under, beneath (relative to a stated noun)
postposition Takes the oblique with के (ke): X के नीचे (ke niiche, 'under X'), as in मेज़ के नीचे (mez ke niiche, 'under the table').
Synonyms: तले (tale, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the go-to phrase for physical position -- बिस्तर के नीचे (bistar ke niiche, 'under the bed') is the classic place kids hide things (or where parents look for lost slippers).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop के (ke) and say मेज़ नीचे (mez niiche) instead of मेज़ के नीचे (mez ke niiche) -- as a compound postposition, नीचे (niiche) needs के (ke) linking it to the noun it governs.

Sense 3 : lower, downstairs (attributive, invariant)
adjective Used before a noun to mean 'lower/downstairs', e.g. नीचे वाला कमरा (niiche vaalaa kamraa, 'the downstairs room') -- typically needs वाला (vaalaa) to attach directly to a noun.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in phrases like नीचे की मंज़िल (niiche kii manzil, 'the lower/ground floor') when people describe multi-story homes so common in Delhi-NCR colonies.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 4 : figuratively lower in status, rank, or level
adverb Used metaphorically for hierarchy or scale, e.g. उससे नीचे (usse niiche, 'below/lower than him/her') in rank or ability.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English says someone is 'beneath' another in rank, Hindi reuses the same physical-space word नीचे (niiche) for social or professional hierarchy -- a very natural metaphor that Hindi and English share.
Sample Sentences:
इस दफ़्तर में मेरा पद उससे नीचे है। In this office, my rank is lower than his.
