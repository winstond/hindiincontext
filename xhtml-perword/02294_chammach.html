चम्मच (chammach)

noun A simple, everyday noun meaning 'spoon' -- both the physical utensil and, informally, a person who flatters or sucks up to someone.
Sense 1 : spoon (utensil)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear चाय का चम्मच (chaay kaa chammach, 'teaspoon') and बड़ा चम्मच (badaa chammach, 'tablespoon/serving spoon') constantly in kitchen talk -- Hindi doesn't usually bother with separate single words for teaspoon versus tablespoon the way English does, it just qualifies चम्मच (chammach) with size.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for छुरी (chhurii) or काँटा (kaantaa) when they mean चम्मच (chammach) -- remember छुरी (chhurii) is 'knife' and काँटा (kaantaa) is 'fork', while चम्मच (chammach) alone is 'spoon'.

Sense 2 : flatterer, yes-man (informal, figurative)
noun, masculine Used almost always for a person, often in phrases like किसी का चम्मच होना (kisii kaa chammach honaa, 'to be someone's yes-man').
Synonyms: चमचा (chamchaa, more common in this figurative sense), भाई-चारा (bhaaii-chaaraa, not a true synonym, avoid), चापलूस (chaaploos)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image as English 'brown-noser' but built on a spoon rather than a nose -- picture someone always 'serving' and agreeing with their boss, ladling out praise. In everyday Delhi speech you will hear this sense attached to चमचा (chamchaa) much more often than to चम्मच (chammach) itself, so treat this as the less frequent variant of a lively colloquial idea.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is far more commonly expressed with the related word चमचा (chamchaa) rather than चम्मच (chammach) itself -- if you hear someone called a 'spoon' in this insulting sense, it is more likely चमचा (chamchaa).
