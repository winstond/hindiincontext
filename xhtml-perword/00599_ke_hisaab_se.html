के हिसाब से (ke hisaab se)

postposition A common postposition-like phrase meaning 'according to' or 'as per,' built from हिसाब (hisaab, 'calculation/account') plus से (se); it attaches to an oblique-marked noun via के (ke).
Sense 1 : according to, as per (a standard, opinion, or rate)
postposition Governs a noun in the oblique with के (ke) before हिसाब से (hisaab se); with pronouns this becomes मेरे हिसाब से (mere hisaab se, 'in my opinion/according to me') etc.
Synonyms: के अनुसार (ke anusaar, more formal/written), के मुताबिक (ke mutaabik)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मेरे हिसाब से (mere hisaab se) is the everyday Hindi equivalent of 'in my opinion' or 'the way I see it,' and you will hear it constantly in casual debates over chai about cricket, politics, or which route beats Delhi traffic best. It also works very literally for rates and measures, like पाँच रुपये किलो के हिसाब से (paanch rupye kilo ke hisaab se, 'at the rate of five rupees per kilo'), echoing हिसाब's (hisaab) core sense of 'accounting/reckoning.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मेरा हिसाब से (meraa hisaab se) instead of मेरे हिसाब से (mere hisaab se) -- हिसाब (hisaab) triggers the oblique on the preceding possessive, so मेरा (meraa) must become मेरे (mere) even though हिसाब (hisaab) itself is singular. 2) Confusing this with के बारे में (ke baare men, 'about') -- के हिसाब से (ke hisaab se) means 'according to/based on' a standard or view, not 'on the topic of'.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे हिसाब से यह फ़िल्म बहुत अच्छी थी। In my opinion, this movie was very good.
(mere hisaab se yah film bahut achchhii thii।)

Sense 2 : in proportion to, depending on, in keeping with
postposition Used to mark a scaling or matching relationship, e.g. काम के हिसाब से पैसे (kaam ke hisaab se paise, 'money in proportion to the work'), distinct from the opinion sense above.
Synonyms: के अनुसार (ke anusaar, more formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in very practical Delhi contexts: a tailor sizing कपड़े नाप के हिसाब से (kapde naap ke hisaab se, 'clothes according to measurement'), or a boss saying काम के हिसाब से तनख्वाह (kaam ke hisaab se tanakhvaah, 'salary in proportion to the work'). It captures a very Hindi instinct for framing fairness as proportionality rather than fixed rules.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for के अनुसार (ke anusaar) by default, but in spoken contexts about proportion or fit -- like clothes, pay, or effort -- के हिसाब से (ke hisaab se) sounds far more natural.
