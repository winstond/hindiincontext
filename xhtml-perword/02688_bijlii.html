बिजली (bijlii)

noun Mainly means 'electricity' or 'power (as in electric supply)', with a common extended sense of 'lightning'; both meanings are everyday and closely related in the learner's mind since Hindi uses one word for both.
Sense 1 : electricity, electric power/supply
noun, feminine
Synonyms: विद्युत (vidyut, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Talking about बिजली (bijlii) going and coming is a huge part of daily conversation in most Indian towns -- बिजली चली गई (bijlii chalii gaii, 'the power's gone') and बिजली आ गई (bijlii aa gaii, 'the power's back') are practically weather-talk. The electricity bill itself is called बिजली का बिल (bijlii kaa bil, 'electricity bill').
Common Mistakes: 1) बिजली (bijlii) is used both for the abstract concept of electricity and for the household supply -- बिजली चली गई (bijlii chalii gaii, 'the power went out') is about supply cutting off, not the substance disappearing. 2) Learners often reach for विद्युत (vidyut) thinking it is the everyday word; in speech बिजली (bijlii) is what people actually say.

Sense 2 : lightning
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The verb that pairs naturally with this sense is चमकना (chamaknaa, 'to flash/shine') -- बिजली चमकी (bijlii chamkii, 'lightning flashed') -- rather than any verb of falling, since Hindi frames lightning as a flash of light. The same word for lightning and electricity is a nice reminder that electricity itself was once called 'captured lightning' in a lot of world languages.

Sense 3 : figurative: something/someone dazzling, sudden, or lightning-fast
noun, feminine Used attributively or in comparisons to describe speed, shock, or striking beauty, e.g. बिजली की तरह (bijlii kii tarah, 'like lightning', meaning very fast) or बिजली गिरना (bijlii girnaa, 'for a bolt to fall', meaning a sudden shock/disaster strikes).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use shows up constantly in Bollywood-style hyperbole and everyday exaggeration -- calling a fast-moving person or a stunning-looking woman बिजली (bijlii) is a common, almost filmy, compliment or comment.
Common Mistakes: 1) बिजली गिरना (bijlii girnaa) does not literally mean lightning striking the ground in most everyday use -- it is commonly used for sudden bad news or disaster falling on someone, e.g. उस पर बिजली गिर गई (us par bijlii gir gaii, 'disaster struck him/misfortune befell him').
