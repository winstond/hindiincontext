सकना (saknaa)

verb A grammatical auxiliary verb meaning 'to be able to, can' -- it attaches to the verb stem of another verb and carries no independent lexical meaning of its own.
Sense 1 : ability/capability modal ('can, to be able to')
verb Attaches directly to the bare verb stem of the main verb: stem + सकना (saknaa), e.g. जा सकना (jaa saknaa, 'to be able to go'), कर सकना (kar saknaa, 'to be able to do'). It then conjugates fully for tense, aspect, and gender/number agreement just like any ordinary verb -- सकता हूँ (saktaa hoon), सकेगी (sakegii), सका (sakaa), etc.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: सकना (saknaa) is how Hindi builds its everyday 'can' -- but unlike the fixed English modal, it is a real, fully conjugating verb, so you'll hear it shift shape constantly: कर सकता हूँ (kar saktaa hoon, 'I can do'), कर सकती थी (kar saktii thii, 'she could have done'), कर सकेंगे (kar sakenge, 'we will be able to'). Because it is purely grammatical, it never stands alone -- you'll never hear सकना (saknaa) by itself the way you might hear a standalone verb like जाना (jaanaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes attach सकना (saknaa) to the infinitive instead of the bare stem -- जाना सकता हूँ (jaanaa saktaa hoon) is wrong; it must be जा सकता हूँ (jaa saktaa hoon). 2) For negation, नहीं (nahiin) goes before सकना's conjugated form, not before the main verb: नहीं जा सकता (nahiin jaa saktaa, 'cannot go'), not जा नहीं सकता (jaa nahiin saktaa) -- though the latter is also heard and not wrong, learners often place नहीं (nahiin) awkwardly elsewhere. 3) सकना (saknaa) expresses ability/possibility only; it is easy to confuse with चाहिए (chaahie) (should/ought) or पड़ना (padnaa) (have to, forced necessity), which express different flavors of modality entirely.
