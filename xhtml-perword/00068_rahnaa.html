रहना (rahnaa)

verb Core meaning is 'to live/reside' and 'to stay/remain'; this single root also powers several very common compound-verb constructions covered as separate senses below: perfect-participle + रहना (rahnaa) (a resulting state persists), imperfect-participle + रहना (rahnaa) (an action continues), and adjective + रहना (rahnaa) (a state persists).
Sense 1 : to live, reside
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: बसना (basnaa, to settle/live), निवास करना (nivaas karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the go-to answer to आप कहाँ रहते हैं (aap kahaan rahte hain, 'where do you live') in everyday small talk, whether you mean your home city or your current flat -- Hindi doesn't bother distinguishing 'live' from 'reside' the way English sometimes does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बसना (basnaa) when they simply mean 'to live somewhere'; बसना (basnaa) implies settling or making a home, whereas रहना (rahnaa) is the neutral everyday word for where you currently live. 2) With this sense, the location takes में (men) or पर (par), not पास (paas) -- मैं दिल्ली में रहता हूँ (main dillii men rahtaa hoon, 'I live in Delhi'), not मेरे पास (mere paas).

Sense 3 : compound verb: adjective + रहना -- to remain/stay in a state
verb, intransitive Frame: [adjective] + रहना (rahnaa), e.g. चुप रहना (chup rahnaa, 'to stay quiet'), खुश रहना (khush rahnaa, 'to stay happy'), स्वस्थ रहना (svasth rahnaa, 'to stay healthy'). रहना (rahnaa) conjugates for tense/aspect; the adjective itself stays invariant here.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the frame behind classic parental commands like चुप रहो (chup raho, 'be quiet/stay quiet') and everyday well-wishes like खुश रहो (khush raho, 'stay happy') -- Hindi treats maintaining a state as something you actively 'stay' in, not just 'are'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert होना (honaa) where रहना (rahnaa) is idiomatic -- चुप रहो (chup raho, 'stay quiet') is the natural command, not चुप होओ (chup hoo), which sounds like 'become quiet' rather than 'stay quiet'.

Sense 4 : compound verb: perfect participle + रहना -- a completed action's resulting state persists
verb, intransitive Frame: [verb's perfect participle, -आ/-ई/-ए agreeing with subject] + रहना (rahnaa), e.g. सोया रहना (soyaa rahnaa, 'to remain/stay asleep', from सोना (sonaa)), पड़ी रहना (padii rahnaa, 'to stay lying somewhere', from पड़ना (padnaa)), बनी रहना (banii rahnaa, 'to remain/stay intact', from बनना (bannaa)). This describes the lingering result of a finished action, not an action still in progress.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear बनी रहना (banii rahnaa) often in warm wishes about relationships -- दोस्ती बनी रहे (dostii banii rahe, 'may the friendship stay intact') -- and पड़ा रहना (padaa rahnaa) about things (or people!) left lying around idly, sometimes with a scolding edge, as in वह दिन भर पड़ा रहता है (vah din bhar padaa rahtaa hai, 'he just lies around all day').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this with the imperfect-participle + रहना (rahnaa) construction (e.g. बोलते रहना (bolte rahnaa), 'to keep talking'); सोया रहना (soyaa rahnaa) means 'to remain in the state of having fallen asleep', while a form like सोते रहना (sote rahnaa) would suggest repeated or ongoing sleeping, which is not how this construction is used in practice -- the perfect-participle version is about a persisting result, not a repeated action. 2) The participle must agree in gender/number with the subject, just as it would in a simple past tense -- किताब मेज़ पर पड़ी रही (kitaab mez par padii rahii, 'the book stayed lying on the table'), feminine to match किताब (kitaab).

Sense 5 : compound verb: imperfect participle + रहना -- an action continues or keeps happening
verb, intransitive Frame: [verb's imperfect participle, -ता/-ती/-ते agreeing with subject] + रहना (rahnaa), e.g. बोलते रहना (bolte rahnaa, 'to keep on talking'), चलते रहना (chalte rahnaa, 'to keep walking/going'), सोचती रहना (sochtii rahnaa, 'to keep thinking', feminine subject). This marks an ongoing, habitual, or repeated continuation of the action itself, in contrast to the perfect-participle sense above which marks a persisting result.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the natural way to say someone 'kept on' doing something -- वह हँसता रहा (vah hanstaa rahaa, 'he kept laughing') -- and it pairs especially well with habitual advice or complaints, like तुम खाते रहते हो (tum khaate rahte ho, 'you keep eating/you're always eating').
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not swap in the perfect participle here -- रोता रहा (rotaa rahaa, 'kept crying') describes ongoing crying, while रोया रहा (royaa rahaa) is not idiomatic in the same way, since crying doesn't leave a lingering 'completed' state the way sleeping or lying down does. 2) The participle agrees with the subject in gender/number, but रहना (rahnaa) itself carries the tense -- वह बोलती रहती है (vah boltii rahtii hai, 'she keeps talking' as a habitual trait) versus वह बोलती रही (vah boltii rahii, 'she kept talking' in that one instance).

Sense 6 : to be left over, remain (of a quantity or remainder)
verb, intransitive Often used with a को-marked or possessive-marked experiencer to express what someone 'has left' -- मेरे पास कुछ पैसे रह गए (mere paas kuchh paise rah gae, 'I had some money left').
Synonyms: बचना (bachnaa, to be saved/left over)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Common in everyday accounting of leftovers, time, or unfinished business -- कितना काम रह गया है (kitnaa kaam rah gayaa hai, 'how much work is left') is a classic question at the end of a busy day.
Common Mistakes: 1) बचना (bachnaa) and रहना (rahnaa) overlap here, but रह जाना (rah jaanaa) (with जाना (jaanaa)) is far more common in speech for 'ended up remaining' than plain रहना (rahnaa) alone -- learners should expect to hear this compounded form more often than the bare verb in this sense.
