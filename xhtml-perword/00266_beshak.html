बेशक (beshak)

adverb/conjunction A confidence adverb meaning 'undoubtedly, certainly' -- and, used at the start of a sentence in concessive contexts, 'granted, sure' (as in 'sure, but...'). One core sense, two flavors of use.
Sense 1 : certainly, undoubtedly
adverb
Synonyms: निश्चित रूप से (nishchit roop se), ज़रूर (zaroor), निःसंदेह (nihsandeh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear बेशक (beshak) a lot in debates, news discussions, and opinionated conversation -- it is the word Hindi speakers reach for to plant a flag before making their real point, much like English 'granted' or 'no doubt.' It has a slightly more formal, considered flavor than the everyday ज़रूर (zaroor).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ज़रूर (zaroor) in every 'certainly/sure' context, but बेशक (beshak) leans more toward affirming a fact or truth ('undoubtedly this is true') while ज़रूर (zaroor) leans toward willingness or promise ('sure, I'll do it'). Swapping them is understandable but not quite native. 2) It sits comfortably at the start of a sentence, unlike many adverbs learners have met so far -- it doesn't need to hug the verb.
Sample Sentences:

(beshak vah mehantii hai।)

Sense 2 : granted...but (concessive)
conjunction Used clause-initially in a two-part frame: बेशक (beshak) X, लेकिन/पर (lekin/par) Y -- concedes X before pivoting to the speaker's real point in the second clause.
Synonyms: हालांकि (haalaanki), भले ही (bhale hii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the 'granted, but...' move you'll hear in Hindi arguments and op-eds alike: बेशक वह अमीर है, पर खुश नहीं है (beshak vah amiir hai, par khush nahiin hai, 'granted he's rich, but he isn't happy'). It's a favorite structure for sounding reasonable while still getting your jab in.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense needs its पर (par) or लेकिन (lekin) partner in the second clause to land properly -- dropping it can leave the concession feeling unfinished to a native ear. 2) Learners sometimes treat this as a simple synonym for भले ही (bhale hii), but बेशक (beshak) carries a firmer, more assertive tone -- it concedes a point almost as if daring the listener to disagree.
