महबूब (mahboob)

noun/adjective A warm, romantic word borrowed from Urdu/Persian meaning 'beloved' -- used both as a noun (the beloved person) and as an adjective (dear, beloved). Heard constantly in Hindi film songs and ghazals.
Sense 1 : beloved person, sweetheart
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रेमी (premii), प्रियतम (priytam, literary), जानम (jaanam, term of endearment), माशूक (maashook, variant form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries the perfume of Urdu shayari and Bollywood romance -- you will hear it in countless film songs where a lover addresses their महबूब (mahboob) with longing. It sits at the more poetic, filmy end of the spectrum compared to everyday words like प्रेमी (premii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat महबूब (mahboob) as gender-fixed masculine only, but it is used for a female beloved too, especially in songs addressed to a woman -- the feminine form महबूबा (mahboobaa) exists separately and is more specific to a female beloved. 2) It is stronger and more poetic than दोस्त (dost) or यार (yaar) -- do not use it casually for a friend.

Sense 2 : beloved, dear
adjective Used attributively before a noun, as in मेरे महबूब शहर (mere mahboob shahar, 'my beloved city') -- it modifies people or places one holds dear, not just romantic partners.
Synonyms: प्रिय (priy), प्यारा (pyaaraa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This adjectival use extends beyond romance -- a poet might call their hometown or homeland महबूब (mahboob), showing how the word's warmth stretches to any deeply cherished thing, not only a lover.
Common Mistakes: 1) As an adjective it is invariant -- learners familiar with -आ adjectives like अच्छा (achchhaa) sometimes wrongly try to inflect it to महबूबी or महबूबे; it stays महबूब (mahboob) regardless of the noun's gender or number.
