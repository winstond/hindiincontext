इतने में (itne men)

conjunction/adverb A narrative connector used mainly in storytelling: it marks 'just at that moment' / 'meanwhile,' introducing a sudden interruption or a new event into an ongoing scene.
Sense 1 : just then, at that very moment (narrative interruption)
conjunction Typically opens a clause describing an event that breaks into an ongoing action -- the preceding clause is often in the imperfective ('was doing X') and इतने में introduces what happened next.
Synonyms: तभी (tabhii), इतने में ही (itne men hii, emphatic variant), उसी वक़्त (usii vaqt)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic 'just then...' hinge in Hindi storytelling -- picture someone narrating a bazaar mishap: 'मैं चाय पी रहा था, इतने में बारिश शुरू हो गई' (main chaay pii rahaa thaa, itne men baarish shuroo ho gaii, 'I was drinking tea, and just then it started raining'). Grandmothers telling bedtime stories lean on it constantly to keep the plot moving.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with इतने में meaning 'in this much (money/time),' which is a literal quantity phrase, not the narrative connector -- context (a story in progress vs. talk of price or duration) is the only way to tell them apart. 2) It is not used to start a conversation or a formal written argument -- it belongs to spoken/narrated anecdotes and storytelling registers, so overusing it in essays sounds odd.

Sense 2 : in this much (quantity, money, or time)
adverb Literal use of इतना (itnaa, 'this much') plus में (men, 'in'), referring back to an amount of money, time, or resources just mentioned -- e.g. 'इतने में तुम्हारा काम हो जाएगा' (itne men tumhaaraa kaam ho jaaegaa, 'this much will be enough to get your work done').
Synonyms: इस कीमत में (is kiimat men, money-specific), इस दौरान (is dauraan, time-specific)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly while bargaining in an Indian market -- a shopkeeper might say 'इतने में नहीं होगा, थोड़ा और दो' (itne men nahiin hogaa, thodaa aur do, 'it won't happen for this much, give a bit more'), tying the phrase directly to haggling culture.
Common Mistakes: 1) This literal sense is easy to mix up with the storytelling connector above -- if the sentence is about a price, budget, or duration rather than an interrupted scene, it is this sense. 2) इतने (itne) here is simply the oblique form of इतना (itnaa) agreeing with में (men), not a separate word to memorize -- the phrase is compositional, not idiomatic.
