सागर (saagar)

noun A literary/elevated word for 'ocean, sea'; the everyday word learners typically reach for first is समुद्र (samudr).
Sense 1 : ocean, sea
noun, masculine
Synonyms: समुद्र (samudr), सिंधु (sindhu, literary), जलधि (jaldhi, literary, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet सागर (saagar) most in songs, poems, and city names -- like Bhopal's नर्मदा सागर (narmdaa saagar) or the town Sagar (saagar) itself in Madhya Pradesh -- rather than in someone's casual weekend chat about the beach, where समुद्र (samudr) rules.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat सागर (saagar) as the default everyday word for 'sea' -- in casual speech समुद्र (samudr) is far more common; सागर (saagar) shows up more in poetry, place names, and formal or descriptive writing. 2) It is masculine, so adjectives and verb agreement follow the masculine pattern -- गहरा सागर (gahraa saagar), not गहरी सागर (gahrii saagar).

Sense 2 : a vast quantity or abundance of something (figurative)
noun, masculine Used in genitive constructions like दुखों का सागर (dukhon kaa saagar, 'an ocean of sorrows') to express overwhelming abundance, much like English 'a sea of.'
Synonyms: समुद्र (samudr)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same metaphor English uses -- 'a sea of troubles,' 'an ocean of love' -- so it tends to feel intuitive to English speakers. You will hear it in devotional and romantic contexts alike, such as प्रेम का सागर (prem kaa saagar, 'an ocean of love').
