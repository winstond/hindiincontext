शिक्षित (shikshit)

adjective A learned/borrowed-from-Sanskrit adjective meaning 'educated'; also has a narrower verb-like sense of 'trained' in specific skill contexts. Related to शिक्षा (shikshaa, 'education').
Sense 1 : educated, literate
adjective
Synonyms: पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa), साक्षर (saakshar, formal, focuses on literacy specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet शिक्षित (shikshit) constantly in newspaper headlines and government discourse -- शिक्षित बेरोज़गार (shikshit berozgaar, 'educated unemployed') is a stock phrase describing India's degree-holders who can't find matching jobs. In daily conversation about a person, though, most Delhi families will simply say वह पढ़ा-लिखा है (vah padhaa-likhaa hai, 'he is educated') rather than reaching for this more formal word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शिक्षित (shikshit) in casual speech where a native speaker would say पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa) instead -- शिक्षित (shikshit) tends to show up in writing, news, or formal discussion about education levels, literacy rates, and social issues. 2) Do not confuse with शिक्षक (shikshak, 'teacher') -- similar spelling, unrelated role.

Sense 2 : trained, instructed (in a specific skill)
adjective Often paired with a domain noun to specify the skill: संगीत में शिक्षित (sangiit men shikshit, 'trained in music').
Synonyms: प्रशिक्षित (prashikshit, more specific to formal/professional training)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with प्रशिक्षित (prashikshit), which is the word you'll hear more often in professional or technical contexts like job training programs; शिक्षित (shikshit) in this sense leans slightly more literary or general, as in describing someone's upbringing or classical education (for instance in music or dance).
