दोहराना (dohraanaa)

verb दोहराना (dohraanaa) is a transitive verb built on the root दो- (do, 'two') plus हराना, giving the core sense 'to do a second time' -- it covers both literal repetition of words/actions and the extended sense of revising lessons.
Sense 1 : to repeat (words, an action, a statement)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: दुहराना (duhraanaa, variant spelling), पुनरावृत्ति करना (punraavritti karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in classrooms and language drills -- a teacher says मेरे बाद दोहराओ (mere baad dohraao, 'repeat after me') before the class echoes a word or sentence back in chorus. It is also the polite way to ask someone to repeat themselves: क्या आप दोहरा सकते हैं? (kyaa aap dohraa sakte hain, 'could you repeat that?').
Common Mistakes: 1) Because दोहराना (dohraanaa) already builds in the idea of 'once more,' learners often pile on दुबारा (dubaaraa) as well, producing the redundant दुबारा दोहराना (dubaaraa dohraanaa, 'to repeat again') -- native speakers just say दोहराना (dohraanaa) alone. 2) It describes repeating a specific thing said or done, not habitual repeated action over time -- for 'he keeps repeating this mistake' speakers usually reach for बार-बार (baar-baar, 'again and again') plus a plain verb rather than दोहराना (dohraanaa) alone.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे बाद दोहराओ। Repeat after me.
(mere baad dohraao।)

Sense 2 : to revise, go over again (study material)
verb, transitive Very commonly paired with पाठ (paath, 'lesson') or सबक (sabak, 'lesson') as in अपना पाठ दोहराना (apnaa paath dohraanaa, 'to revise one's lesson').
Synonyms: रिवीज़न करना (riviizan karnaa, colloquial loanword), याद करना (yaad karnaa, meaning scope: memorize)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian student hears before exams -- a parent or teacher saying अपने नोट्स दोहरा लो (apne nots dohraa lo, 'go over your notes') rather than पढ़ना (padhnaa, 'to study/read'), because the emphasis is on going back over material already learned, not learning it fresh.
