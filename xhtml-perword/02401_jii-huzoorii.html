जी-हुज़ूरी (jii-huzoorii)

noun A single noun meaning excessive, servile flattery of a superior -- toadying or sycophancy. Built from जी-हुज़ूर (jii-huzoor, 'yes sir', a servile form of address) plus the abstract-noun suffix -ई (ii).
Sense 1 : sycophancy, servile flattery toward a superior
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चापलूसी (chaaploosii), खुशामद (khushaamad), तलवे चाटना (talve chaatnaa, idiom, 'to lick soles')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जी-हुज़ूर (jii-huzoor) itself echoes the old Mughal-court habit of addressing a noble or officer with exaggerated deference, so जी-हुज़ूरी (jii-huzoorii) carries a whiff of the durbar -- the underling bowing and scraping before the boss. In office gossip today, people use it for the colleague who always agrees with the boss just to stay in favor: 'बॉस की जी-हुज़ूरी करता है' (bos kii jii-huzoorii kartaa hai, 'he does yes-sir-ing to the boss'). It pairs naturally with चापलूसी (chaaploosii), which is more general flattery, while जी-हुज़ूरी (jii-huzoorii) specifically evokes obsequious yes-manship toward someone in authority.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat जी-हुज़ूरी (jii-huzoorii) as a neutral synonym for 'respect' or 'politeness' -- it is always negative, implying servility and self-interest, not genuine courtesy. 2) It is almost always used to describe someone else's behavior critically; using it about your own conduct sounds oddly self-deprecating unless you intend that effect.

(vah hameshaa bos kii jii-huzoorii kartaa hai।)
