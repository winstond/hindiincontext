दिलाना (dilaanaa)

verb The causative of देना (denaa, 'to give'): it means to make/have someone give something, or to have something procured/secured for someone via an intermediary. A second, distinct sense uses it for making someone recall or reminding them of something (linked to दिलाना's role as causative of the 'remembering' sense embedded in याद (yaad) constructions).
Sense 1 : to cause to be given / to get (something) for someone
verb, transitive Takes a three-way frame: X ne Y ko Z dilaayaa ('X had Z given to Y'), where X is the causer/agent-through-another-person, e.g. पिता ने बेटे को नौकरी दिलाई (pitaa ne bete ko naukrii dilaaii, 'the father got the son a job [through his connections/efforts]').
Synonyms: मुहैया कराना (muhaiyaa karaanaa, formal), उपलब्ध कराना (uplabdh karaanaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic 'getting something done through someone' verb -- very alive in sentences about parents pulling strings for a job (नौकरी दिलाना, naukrii dilaanaa), lawyers securing justice (इंसाफ़ दिलाना, insaaf dilaanaa), or a shopkeeper being persuaded to give a discount. It reflects how Hindi often frames outcomes as achieved via an intermediary rather than directly by the subject.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse दिलाना (dilaanaa) with सीधे देना (siidhe denaa, 'to give directly') -- दिलाना implies the subject arranged for or facilitated the giving through effort or influence, not that they handed it over themselves. 2) The person who actually benefits is marked with को (ko), and this is easy to drop or misplace: दोस्त ने मुझे किताब दिलाई (dost ne mujhe kitaab dilaaii, 'my friend got me the book [from someone else]').

Sense 2 : to remind someone (of something)
verb, transitive Almost always appears as the fixed collocation याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa), literally 'to cause memory,' with the frame X ne Y ko Z ki yaad dilaayii.
Synonyms: याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa, fixed collocation), स्मरण कराना (smaran karaanaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: याद दिलाना (yaad dilaanaa) is what you say when someone jogs your memory -- a mother reminding a child to call a relative, or a friend reminding you of an old debt. It sits alongside याद आना (yaad aanaa, 'to come to mind on its own') and याद रखना (yaad rakhnaa, 'to keep in mind'), forming a small family of memory expressions built around याद (yaad) as a noun that things are 'given,' 'come,' or are 'kept.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use दिलाना (dilaanaa) alone to mean 'remind,' but it almost never appears without याद (yaad) in this sense -- the two words form a single functional unit. 2) The thing being remembered takes की/का/के (kii/kaa/ke) before याद, not को: उसने मुझे मेरे वादे की याद दिलाई (usne mujhe mere vaade kii yaad dilaaii, 'he reminded me of my promise').
