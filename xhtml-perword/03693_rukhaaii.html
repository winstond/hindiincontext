रुखाई (rukhaaii)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'dryness' or 'curtness' -- used both for physical dryness of skin/texture and, more commonly in everyday speech, for a cold or brusque manner in someone's behavior or tone. Related to the adjective रूखा (rookhaa, 'dry, curt').
Sense 1 : coldness or curtness in manner/tone
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रूखापन (rookhaapan), बेरुखी (berukhii, more about indifference/aloofness), कठोरता (kathortaa, harshness, broader sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word when someone describes a person's tone on the phone or in a conversation as having रुखाई (rukhaaii) -- think of a shopkeeper or official who answers questions without any warmth, just flat and businesslike. It often carries a note of hurt: 'usne rukhaai se jawaab diya' implies the speaker felt slighted by the coldness, not just that the reply was brief.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ठंडापन (thandaapan) to mean 'coldness of manner,' but that word is used almost exclusively for literal temperature; रुखाई (rukhaaii) is the natural word for emotional/tonal coldness. 2) Note the आई (aaii) suffix pattern -- this is an abstract noun formed from the adjective रूखा (rookhaa), similar to how अच्छाई (achchhaaii) comes from अच्छा (achchhaa).

Sense 2 : physical dryness (of skin, hair, texture)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सूखापन (sookhaapan)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in everyday contexts like winter skincare talk -- 'sardiyon mein twacha mein rukhaai aa jaati hai' ('skin gets dry in winter'). It is less common in casual speech than the emotional sense above, which tends to dominate in conversation.
