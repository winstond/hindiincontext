फ़र्क़ (farq)

noun/verb A common loanword meaning 'difference' or 'distinction' -- mostly used as a noun (फ़र्क़ (farq)), often in the fixed phrase फ़र्क़ पड़ना (farq padnaa, 'to make a difference/matter').
Sense 1 : difference, distinction
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अंतर (antar), भेद (bhed, formal/literary), फ़ासला (faaslaa, distance/gap, figurative)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a Persian-origin word that has fully settled into everyday Hindi -- you will hear it constantly in Delhi markets and homes, e.g. asking क्या फ़र्क़ है? (kyaa farq hai, 'what's the difference?') when comparing two things, two brands, two people.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mix up फ़र्क़ (farq) with अंतर (antar); both work fine in casual speech, but फ़र्क़ (farq) feels more conversational and अंतर (antar) slightly more formal/written. 2) The nuqta under फ़ is often dropped in casual writing (फर्क (phark)), which is fine and widely seen, but say it with the 'f' sound (not 'ph').

Sense 2 : to matter, to make a difference (used impersonally with को)
verb, intransitive Occurs only in the fixed collocation फ़र्क़ पड़ना (farq padnaa), with the person affected marked by को: मुझे फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (mujhe farq nahiin padtaa, 'it doesn't matter to me').
Synonyms: मतलब होना (matlab honaa, 'to matter, to have relevance')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मुझे कोई फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (mujhe koii farq nahiin padtaa, 'it makes no difference to me') is an extremely common way to shrug off a choice in daily conversation -- a classic dative-subject construction like लगना (lagnaa), where the feeling 'happens to' the person rather than being an action they perform.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to say 'it doesn't matter' with कोई बात नहीं (koii baat nahiin) where फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (farq nahiin padtaa) is more natural when comparing outcomes or options, e.g. deciding between two choices. 2) Remember the subject of concern takes को, not से or पर.
