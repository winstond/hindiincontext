दूध (doodh)

noun A single, concrete-noun sense: milk, in both its literal and everyday figurative kitchen-table uses.
Sense 1 : milk (the drink/liquid)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Milk is a daily-life staple in North Indian households -- the doodhwaala (doodhvaalaa, 'milkman') delivering fresh दूध every morning is a scene most Delhi-NCR families grew up with, even if today it is more often a packet from the corner store. दूध is also central to hospitality and ritual: warm दूध before bed is classic parental advice, and it is offered in poojas and used to make sweets like खीर (khiir) and पनीर (paniir).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मुझे दूध पसंद है (mujhe doodh pasand hai, 'I like milk') correctly, but stumble on quantity phrasing -- Hindi speakers usually say एक गिलास दूध (ek gilaas doodh, 'a glass of milk') rather than trying to pluralize दूध itself, since it is a mass noun with no natural plural. 2) दूध is grammatically masculine, so watch agreement: दूध गरम है (doodh garam hai, 'the milk is hot'), not the feminine form.

Sense 2 : milk-like sap/liquid (of plants, etc.)
noun, masculine Used for the milky sap of certain plants, e.g. आक का दूध (aak kaa doodh, 'milkweed sap').
Synonyms: रस (ras, broader term for sap/juice)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is a natural extension of the primary meaning -- any white, milk-like fluid oozing from a plant or fruit (like a green papaya or certain cacti) gets called दूध, the same way English might stretch 'milk' to coconut milk or milkweed.
