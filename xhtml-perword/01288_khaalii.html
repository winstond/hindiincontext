ख़ाली (khaalii)

adjective/adverb Mainly an adjective meaning 'empty' or 'vacant,' with a very common extended sense of 'free' (time, a person available) and an adverbial use meaning 'only, just.' Also spelled without the nuqta as खाली (khaalii).
Sense 1 : empty, vacant
adjective
Synonyms: रिक्त (rikt, literary), सूना (soonaa, evokes emptiness/desolation)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly on the street: ख़ाली सीट (khaalii siit, 'empty seat') on a bus, or a shopkeeper saying बोतल ख़ाली है (botal khaalii hai, 'the bottle is empty'). It is one of those everyday, high-mileage adjectives every learner should nail early.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for रिक्त (rikt) in everyday speech; that word belongs to formal or written registers, while ख़ाली (khaalii) is what people actually say for an empty glass, room, or seat.
Sample Sentences:

(yah botal khaalii hai।)

Sense 2 : free, available (of time or a person)
adjective
Synonyms: फ़ारिग़ (faarigh, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the natural way to ask a friend or shopkeeper if they have time for you: क्या तुम आज शाम को ख़ाली हो? (kyaa tum aaj shaam ko khaalii ho, 'are you free this evening?'). It extends the physical 'empty' sense to a person's schedule -- a very natural metaphor once you notice it.
Common Mistakes: 1) When asking if someone is free (not busy), ख़ाली (khaalii) is used with होना (honaa) plus the person marked in the nominative, e.g. आप ख़ाली हैं? (aap khaalii hain, 'are you free?') -- learners sometimes overreach and add को (ko) as if it needed dative marking, but it does not.

Sense 3 : just, only, merely
adverb Used before a noun or phrase to mean 'just/only,' as in ख़ाली बातें (khaalii baaten, 'just talk, empty words').
Synonyms: सिर्फ़ (sirf), केवल (keval, somewhat formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear ख़ाली बातें (khaalii baaten, 'just talk / empty words') used dismissively, e.g. when someone makes promises with no substance -- a nice bridge between the literal 'empty' sense and this figurative 'merely' sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this adverbial sense ख़ाली (khaalii) sits right before the word it limits, much like सिर्फ़ (sirf) -- learners sometimes place it as if it were still the 'empty' adjective modifying a noun elsewhere in the sentence, which changes the meaning.
Sample Sentences:
मैं ख़ाली मज़ाक कर रहा था। I was just joking.
(main khaalii mazaak kar rahaa thaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Continuous -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
