दिक़्क़त (diqqat)

noun A single everyday sense: 'trouble' or 'difficulty', usually the small practical kind rather than a deep crisis.
Sense 1 : trouble, difficulty, problem
noun, feminine Very often paired with the dative-subject frame: X को दिक़्क़त होना/होना (X ko diqqat honaa) 'X has/faces a difficulty', mirroring the same construction as लगना (lagnaa) and पसंद होना (pasand honaa).
Synonyms: परेशानी (pareshaanii), मुश्किल (mushkil), तकलीफ़ (takliif), समस्या (samasyaa, more formal, often for bigger/abstract problems)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly in shops, autos, and offices in Delhi when something minor goes wrong -- a plug that does not fit, a form that is missing a signature. कोई दिक़्क़त नहीं (koii diqqat nahiin, 'no problem at all') is practically a national reflex of politeness, said to smooth over any small hiccup.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मुझे यह समस्या है (mujhe yah samasyaa hai) when दिक़्क़त (diqqat) would sound more natural for a small, everyday inconvenience; समस्या (samasyaa) feels heavier, almost like 'issue' in a report. 2) The retroflex/nuqta-heavy spelling causes learners to write दिकत or दिक्कत (dikkat, common simplified spelling without nuqtas) -- both simplified spellings are widely seen in casual writing and are fine to recognize, but दिक़्क़त (diqqat) is the fuller written form. 3) कोई दिक़्क़त नहीं (koii diqqat nahiin, 'no problem') is a fixed, extremely common reassurance phrase -- learners sometimes translate 'no problem' word-for-word with समस्या (samasyaa) instead, which sounds stiffer.

(mujhe saans lene men diqqat ho rahii hai।)
