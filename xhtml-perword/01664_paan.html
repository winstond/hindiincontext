पान (paan)

noun Mainly the betel-leaf preparation chewed as a mouth-freshener/digestif after meals; also has an older, less common sense of 'drink/beverage' surviving mostly in compound words.
Sense 1 : betel leaf preparation (chewed with areca nut, lime, etc.)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पान (paan) is a fixture of Delhi street life -- you'll see a पानवाला (paanvaalaa, 'paan seller') at a small stall folding leaves with lime paste, katha, areca nut, and sometimes sweet gulkand or tobacco, then wrapping them into a neat triangular packet. It's traditionally eaten right after a meal or a wedding feast, believed to aid digestion and freshen the mouth, and offering पान (paan) to guests is itself a small gesture of hospitality.
Common Mistakes: 1) पान (paan) is not 'betel leaf' alone -- the plain leaf itself is पान का पत्ता (paan kaa pattaa, 'betel leaf'); पान (paan) usually refers to the whole folded, filled preparation ready to chew. 2) Don't confuse with पानी (paanii, 'water') -- learners sometimes mishear or misspell the two, but they are unrelated in meaning.

Sense 2 : drink, beverage (mainly in compounds)
noun, masculine Rarely stands alone with this meaning in everyday speech -- mostly survives inside compound words like जलपान (jalpaan, 'light refreshment/snack').
Synonyms: पेय (pey, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This older sense of पान (paan) as 'drink' is the same root you meet in जलपान (jalpaan) at a wedding or office reception, where you're offered tea, snacks, and cold drinks -- the word literally breaks down as जल (jal, 'water') plus पान (paan, 'drinking').
