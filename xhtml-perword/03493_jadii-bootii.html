जड़ी-बूटी (jadii-bootii)

noun A compound noun meaning 'medicinal herbs/plants' -- literally combining जड़ी (jadii, root/herb) and बूटी (bootii, herb/plant), used together as one set phrase for herbal remedies.
Sense 1 : medicinal herbs, herbal remedies
noun, feminine
Synonyms: औषधि (aushdhi, formal), वनस्पति (vanaspti, broader term for plants/flora)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries the whole world of desi home remedies -- think of a grandmother reaching for तुलसी (tulsii, holy basil) or अदरक (adrak, ginger) and calling it घर की जड़ी-बूटी (ghar kii jadii-bootii, 'home herbal remedy'). It is also the standard term for the herbs used in आयुर्वेद (aayurved, Ayurveda), so you will see it on packaging for herbal teas, oils, and supplements.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat जड़ी-बूटी (jadii-bootii) as two separate countable nouns; in practice it functions as a single collective term for 'herbs/herbal remedies' and is most natural in the plural or as a mass noun -- जड़ी-बूटियाँ (jadii-bootiyaan) for specific varieties. 2) Do not confuse with सब्ज़ी (sabzii, vegetable/curry) -- जड़ी-बूटी refers specifically to plants used for medicinal or magical purposes, not cooking vegetables.

Sense 2 : magical/mystical herb (folklore sense)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: संजीवनी (sanjiivnii, life-restoring herb, mythological)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In folk tales and old Hindi films, a जड़ी-बूटी (jadii-bootii) is often a rare magical plant that can cure poison, revive the dead, or grant special powers -- much like a fairy-tale 'potion herb.' This sense overlaps with the mythological संजीवनी बूटी (sanjiivnii bootii, the life-restoring herb from the Ramayana) that Hanuman fetches to save Lakshman.
