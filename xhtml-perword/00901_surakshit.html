सुरक्षित (surakshit)

adjective An adjective meaning 'safe/secure' or 'protected', also commonly used to mean 'reserved' (e.g. a reserved seat or booking).
Sense 1 : safe, secure, protected
adjective
Synonyms: सुरक्षित (surakshit), महफूज़ (mahphooz, colloquial), बचा हुआ (bachaa huaa, safe from harm)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word everywhere in Delhi -- on ATM signs, in safety announcements on the Metro ('अपना सामान सुरक्षित रखें', apnaa saamaan surakshit rakhen, 'keep your belongings safe'), and in parental advice about being careful. It has a slightly more formal, official feel than ठीक (thiik) or बचा हुआ (bachaa huaa) when talking about physical safety.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use असुरक्षित (asurakshit) and सुरक्षित (surakshit) carelessly -- remember असुरक्षित (asurakshit) is the direct opposite, 'unsafe', not a weaker form of सुरक्षित (surakshit). 2) The verb used with this adjective is usually होना (honaa) or रखना (rakhnaa) ('to keep safe'), not बनना (bannaa) -- साइट सुरक्षित है (saait surakshit hai, 'the site is safe') sounds natural, साइट सुरक्षित बनती है (saait surakshit bantii hai) does not.

Sense 2 : reserved, booked (of a seat, ticket, etc.)
adjective Used with सीट (siit) or टिकट (tikat) to mean the seat/ticket has been set aside for someone; overlaps with आरक्षित (aarakshit) but सुरक्षित (surakshit) is the more everyday word for this in speech.
Synonyms: रिज़र्व (rizarv, colloquial, from English), आरक्षित (aarakshit, formal/official)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: On Indian Railways, you will hear सीट सुरक्षित है (siit surakshit hai, 'the seat is reserved') from a conductor or fellow passenger -- a small everyday extension of 'safe' into 'set aside/protected for someone'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for आरक्षित (aarakshit) by default because it is the 'official' word (used for caste-based reservation categories, आरक्षण aarakshan) -- but for a simply booked train or restaurant seat, सुरक्षित (surakshit) or बुक (buk) is what people actually say.
