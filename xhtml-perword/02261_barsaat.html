बरसात (barsaat)

noun Means 'rain, rainfall' as a season/event; also used figuratively for a 'shower' or 'downpour' of something abstract like tears or blessings.
Sense 1 : the rainy season, or rain/rainfall itself
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बारिश (baarish), वर्षा (varshaa, formal), मॉनसून (monsoon, monsoon season specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North India, बरसात (barsaat) evokes the whole monsoon season, not just water falling from the sky -- think wet earth, mango season winding down, and the relief after brutal Delhi summer heat. You will often hear it in phrases like बरसात का मौसम (barsaat kaa mausam, 'the rainy season') or बरसात के दिन (barsaat ke din, 'the rainy days/monsoon days').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat बरसात (barsaat) and बारिश (baarish) as fully interchangeable in every sentence; बरसात (barsaat) leans toward naming the season or a spell of rain, while बारिश (baarish) is more the everyday word for an individual rain event ('it's raining' is usually बारिश हो रही है (baarish ho rahii hai, 'it is raining'), not बरसात हो रही है).

Sense 2 : a figurative shower or outpouring (of tears, blessings, gifts, etc.)
noun, feminine Used with genitive constructions like आँसुओं की बरसात (aansuon kii barsaat, 'a shower of tears') or खुशियों की बरसात (khushiyon kii barsaat, 'a shower of joys').
Synonyms: बौछार (bauchhaar, often used for a sudden burst/spray)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use borrows the imagery of relentless monsoon rain to describe something coming down abundantly and continuously -- Hindi loves this move of turning weather into emotion, so आँसुओं की बरसात (aansuon kii barsaat, 'a shower of tears') feels natural and poetic rather than odd, the way it might in English.
