परे (pare)

postposition/adverb Mainly a postposition/adverb meaning 'beyond' or 'away, aside', often paired with से (se) as से परे (se pare, 'beyond, past'); also used in the fixed expression टाल-परे in some idiomatic push-away senses. Related in feel to दूर (door) but more literary/abstract.
Sense 1 : beyond, past (a limit or point)
postposition Almost always occurs as से परे (se pare) governing the noun in the oblique with से (se): समझ से परे (samajh se pare, 'beyond understanding').
Synonyms: दूर (door), उस पार (us paar, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this constantly in the set phrase समझ से परे (samajh se pare, 'beyond comprehension') -- a very common way to say something makes no sense, almost like shrugging and saying 'it's beyond me.' It has a slightly more literary or reflective flavor than the everyday दूर (door).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop से (se) and say just X परे (pare) when they mean 'beyond X' -- the से is required to mark what is being gone beyond. 2) परे (pare) is more abstract/formal than दूर (door); for plain physical distance ('the shop is far') दूर (door) is the natural choice, not परे (pare).
Sample Sentences:

(uskaa vyavahaar merii samajh se pare hai।)

Sense 2 : aside, away, off to a distance
adverb Used with verbs of moving or pushing, e.g. परे हटना (pare hatnaa, 'to move aside') or परे करना (pare karnaa, 'to push aside').
Synonyms: दूर (door), अलग (alag, 'apart, separate')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: परे हटो (pare hato, 'move aside!') is the kind of sharp, everyday command you'll hear in a crowded bazaar or from someone clearing a path -- it has a bit of an edge to it, less polite than ज़रा हटिए (zaraa hatie).
Common Mistakes: 1) परे हटो (pare hato) is a common, slightly blunt way to say 'move aside/get out of the way' -- learners sometimes reach for this in polite contexts where a softer phrase would fit better.
