दो (do)

numeral/noun दो (do) is the number 'two,' used as a numeral before nouns; it also appears in a handful of fixed idiomatic phrases built on the idea of 'two.'
Sense 1 : the number two
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear दो (do) constantly in daily transactions -- ordering दो चाय (do chaay, 'two teas') at a stall or asking for दो किलो (do kilo, 'two kilos') of something at the sabzi mandi. It also shows up in the common phrase दो-चार (do-chaar, 'a few, several'), where pairing small numbers together gives a vague, approximate quantity -- a very Hindi way of saying 'a handful' without being exact.
Common Mistakes: 1) दो (do) does not change form before masculine, feminine, or plural nouns -- unlike एक (ek), which has its own quirks, दो (do) is simply invariant, so learners sometimes hunt for agreement that never happens. 2) When counting people informally, native speakers often say दो जने (do jane, 'two people/folks') or दो लोग (do log) rather than a bare दो (do) standing alone as a pronoun for 'two people' -- dropping the noun sounds abrupt in careful speech.

Sense 2 : the numeral 'two' as a written figure or abstract unit (e.g., in math, dice, cards)
noun, masculine Used when referring to the digit or unit itself rather than counting nouns, e.g. in dice, playing cards, or simple arithmetic.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense pops up in everyday games -- rolling a दो (do) on the dice in Ludo, or naming cards while playing rammy (rammii) with family on a lazy afternoon. It is the same word as the numeral, just used as a standalone noun rather than modifying something.
