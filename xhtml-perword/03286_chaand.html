चाँद (chaand)

noun The moon, both as the everyday sky object and as a common term of endearment in Hindi poetry and speech.
Sense 1 : the moon (celestial body)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चंद्रमा (chandrmaa, formal), चंद्र (chandr, literary), माह (maah, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चाँद (chaand) is the word you will hear in everyday conversation and in film songs alike, whereas चंद्रमा (chandrmaa) sounds a bit more bookish or ceremonial, the kind of word used in a school essay about the solar system. Watching the moon together, especially on festivals like करवा चौथ (karvaa chauth), is a genuine cultural touchpoint in North Indian households.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes write चाँद (chaand) without the chandrabindu as चांद (chaand) with just an anusvara -- both are seen in print and both are read the same way, so this is not a serious error, but the nasalized vowel is the correct underlying sound. 2) Do not confuse with चाँदी (chaandii, 'silver'), which is a completely different word despite the visual and aural similarity.

Sense 2 : a beautiful or beloved face/person (term of endearment)
noun, masculine Used metaphorically, often in phrases comparing a beloved's face to the moon, e.g. चाँद सा चेहरा (chaand saa chehraa, 'a moon-like face').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the moon of Bollywood lyrics and Urdu-Hindi poetry -- calling someone's face चाँद (chaand) or saying चाँद का टुकड़ा (chaand kaa tukdaa, 'a piece of the moon') is a classic, almost old-fashioned compliment, the kind an admirer might say with a hand over the heart. It is affectionate and a little theatrical, not something you would say in a plain, everyday context.
