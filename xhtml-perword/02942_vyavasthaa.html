व्यवस्था (vyavasthaa)

noun A formal, Sanskrit-derived noun covering the idea of 'arrangement/system' -- it ranges from a concrete arrangement someone has made for you (a room, a ride) to an abstract system or order (traffic system, law and order, political system).
Sense 1 : arrangement, provision made for something
noun, feminine Commonly used with करना (karnaa, 'to make/do') as व्यवस्था करना (vyavasthaa karnaa, 'to make arrangements') and with होना (honaa) to say an arrangement exists or is in place.
Synonyms: इंतज़ाम (intzaam), बंदोबस्त (bandobast)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word everywhere in India in its official register: सुरक्षा व्यवस्था (surakshaa vyavasthaa, 'security arrangements') outside a wedding hall, यातायात व्यवस्था (yaataayaat vyavasthaa, 'traffic arrangement') on a news ticker, or खाने-पीने की व्यवस्था (khaane-piine kii vyavasthaa, 'arrangements for food and drink') at a family function. It carries a slightly more formal, organized flavor than इंतज़ाम (intzaam), which feels more everyday and improvised.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for इंतज़ाम (intzaam) as a default and forget व्यवस्था (vyavasthaa) works just as well, and sounds slightly more formal/official -- useful in written notices, hotel or travel contexts, or office speech. 2) The phrase व्यवस्था करना (vyavasthaa karnaa) takes an oblique-marked purpose, e.g. रहने की व्यवस्था (rahne kii vyavasthaa, 'arrangement for staying'), which learners sometimes phrase clumsily by dropping की (kii).

शादी के लिए खाने-पीने की व्यवस्था हो गई है। The arrangements for food and drink have been made for the wedding.

Sense 2 : system, order (abstract structure governing something)
noun, feminine Used for institutional or societal systems: शिक्षा व्यवस्था (shikshaa vyavasthaa, 'education system'), राजनीतिक व्यवस्था (raajniitik vyavasthaa, 'political system').
Synonyms: प्रणाली (pranaalii, formal), तंत्र (tantr, formal/technical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up heavily in news and political speech -- कानून-व्यवस्था (kaanoon-vyavasthaa, 'law and order') is a fixed pairing you'll hear constantly on Hindi news channels discussing crime or policing. It signals the word's more abstract, structural register compared to the concrete-arrangement sense above.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'system' with सिस्टम (sistam, a common English loanword) even in contexts where native speakers would prefer the native व्यवस्था (vyavasthaa), especially in political, legal, or social discussion -- सिस्टम (sistam) leans more toward mechanical or bureaucratic gripes ('the system is broken').
