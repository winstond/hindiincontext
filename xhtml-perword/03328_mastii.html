मस्ती (mastii)

noun Covers playful fun/frolic (masti mastii karnaa, 'to have fun/mess around'), a state of intoxicated or blissful abandon, and by extension a mood of carefree high spirits.
Sense 1 : fun, playfulness, frolic
noun, feminine Very commonly paired with the verb करना (karnaa) in the phrase मस्ती करना (mastii karnaa, 'to mess around/have fun'), which behaves as its own light expression rather than a literal 'doing' of fun.
Synonyms: मज़ा (mazaa), तफ़री (tafrii, colloquial), उछल-कूद (uchhal-kood, boisterous physical play)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear kids and college students say चलो, मस्ती करते हैं (chalo, mastii karte hain, 'come on, let's have some fun') constantly in Delhi -- it is the default word for lighthearted goofing off, whether it's splashing in the rain or teasing a friend. It sits at the fun end of a spectrum that runs toward मज़ा (mazaa, enjoyment/pleasure) on one side and नौटंकी (nautankii, dramatics/antics) on the more theatrical side.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate मस्ती करना (mastii karnaa) too literally as 'to do fun' -- it is better rendered as 'to horse around' or 'to have fun,' and can carry a mild undertone of mischief depending on context. 2) मस्ती is not marked with ने (ne) as an object here; करना (karnaa) is intransitive-feeling in this idiom and behaves like normal light-verb usage, so beginners sometimes wrongly add ने (ne) to the subject when there is no true transitive object relationship intended.

Sense 2 : intoxicated bliss, heady abandon (as in bhang/drink or spiritual ecstasy)
noun, feminine Used for the woozy, carefree feeling brought on by an intoxicant (especially भांग bhaang on Holi) or, in devotional/Sufi contexts, by spiritual rapture.
Synonyms: नशा (nashaa, intoxication/high), सुरूर (suroor, literary, dreamy intoxication)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense comes alive around Holi, when people describe the loose-limbed, laughing haze after drinking भांग (bhaang) as मस्ती (mastii) -- and it shows up in Sufi and Kabir-style poetry too, where the devotee is 'drunk' on divine love rather than any real drink. It is the same underlying idea as the fun-sense above: a loss of ordinary restraint, just tilted toward chemical or spiritual causes instead of simple play.
