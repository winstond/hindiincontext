होशियारी (hoshiyaarii)

noun A feminine noun built from the adjective होशियार (hoshiyaar, 'clever/smart') plus the abstract-noun suffix -ई; it covers both the positive sense of cleverness/skill and the negative sense of cunning or being 'too clever by half'.
Sense 1 : cleverness, intelligence, skillfulness
noun, feminine
Synonyms: समझदारी (samajhdaarii), चतुराई (chaturaaii), सूझबूझ (soojhboojh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word a proud parent uses about a child who handled something tricky well -- 'देखो कितनी होशियारी से किया' (dekho kitnii hoshiyaarii se kiyaa, 'look how cleverly he did it'). It praises the doing, not just the having, of intelligence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बुद्धि (buddhi) when they mean this word, but बुद्धि (buddhi) is more about raw intellect or reasoning power, while होशियारी (hoshiyaarii) is about practical smartness or presence of mind in a situation.

Sense 2 : cunning, sly cleverness (negative)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चालाकी (chaalaakii), धूर्तता (dhoorttaa, stronger/more negative)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in the same breath as a scolding -- a shopkeeper or elder saying 'होशियारी मत करो' (hoshiyaarii mat karo, 'don't try any tricks') when they suspect someone is trying to outsmart or shortchange them. The line between admirable smarts and shady scheming is thin, and this sense lives right on it.
Common Mistakes: 1) The same word can flip from a compliment to an accusation depending on tone and context -- 'ज़्यादा होशियारी मत दिखाओ' (zyaadaa hoshiyaarii mat dikhaao, 'don't try to be too clever') is a warning, not praise, so learners should listen for tone rather than assume the word is always positive.
