नमी (namii)

noun A single-sense noun meaning dampness or humidity, used both for weather/climate and for physical wetness on surfaces or in materials.
Sense 1 : moisture, dampness, humidity
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आर्द्रता (aardrtaa, formal/technical), सीलन (siilan, dampness that causes mustiness, e.g. on walls)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear नमी (namii) constantly in Delhi's weather chat, especially in the monsoon and pre-monsoon months when people complain about उमस (umas, sticky humidity) -- a related word describing the uncomfortable *feeling* caused by high नमी (namii). It also shows up on skincare and hair-product packaging (त्वचा में नमी (tvachaa men namii, 'moisture in the skin')), so the word bridges everyday weather talk and beauty/health vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पानी (paanii) when they mean a light dampness -- नमी (namii) is the right word for humidity in the air or slight wetness in soil, cloth, or walls, not for standing water or liquid itself. 2) सीलन (siilan) is close but carries a negative, musty connotation (as in a damp wall or monsoon-affected room), while नमी (namii) is neutral and can even be desirable, as in skin care or soil for plants.
