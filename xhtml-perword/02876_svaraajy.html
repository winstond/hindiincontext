स्वराज्य (svaraajy)

noun A weighty compound noun meaning 'self-rule' or 'self-governance' -- combining स्व (sv, self/own) with राज्य (raajy, kingdom/rule). It carries strong historical and political resonance, especially tied to India's independence movement.
Sense 1 : self-rule, self-governance (political/historical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वशासन (svashaasan), आत्मशासन (aatmshaasan, literary), स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa, broader sense, independence)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word is inseparable from India's freedom struggle -- Tilak's famous call 'स्वराज्य मेरा जन्मसिद्ध अधिकार है' (svaraajy meraa janmsiddh adhikaar hai, 'Self-rule is my birthright') is a cornerstone of nationalist history taught in Indian schools. Note that स्वराज (svaraaj), without the added य (y), is the more commonly seen short form in modern political speech (as in Gandhi's vision of स्वराज), while स्वराज्य (svaraajy) is the fuller, slightly more formal or historical variant, often associated with Maratha history too -- Shivaji's kingdom is called हिंदवी स्वराज्य (hindvii svaraajy, 'Hindu self-rule/kingdom').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa), which means 'independence' or 'freedom' more generally; स्वराज्य (svaraajy) specifically emphasizes self-governance -- ruling oneself -- rather than just being free from external control. 2) It is a noun, not a verb or slogan by itself; it needs to be embedded in a sentence or phrase to function grammatically, unlike the standalone exclamatory use of स्वराज (svaraaj).

(tilak ne kahaa thaa, svaraajy meraa janmsiddh adhikaar hai।)
