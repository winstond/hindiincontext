गरम (garam)

adjective Means 'hot' or 'warm' -- of temperature (food, water, weather) and, figuratively, of temper or mood. Also spelled गर्म (garm); both forms are common.
Sense 1 : hot / warm (temperature)
adjective
Synonyms: गर्म (garm, variant form), तपता (taptaa, hot/scorching)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Chai and food temperature come up constantly in daily life -- चाय गरम है (chaay garam hai, 'the tea is hot') or खाना गरम कर दो (khaanaa garam kar do, 'heat up the food') are things you will hear in almost any Delhi kitchen. Street vendors also shout गरम गरम (garam garam, 'hot hot') to advertise fresh, hot snacks like samosas straight out of the oil.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say ठंडा-गरम (thandaa-garam) pairing to describe weather or drinks going from cold to hot, or vice versa -- this fixed pairing order is what native speakers use, not गरम-ठंडा (garam-thandaa). 2) For fever/body heat, speakers say शरीर गरम है (shariir garam hai, 'the body is hot') or बुखार है (bukhaar hai, 'there is fever') rather than describing the person as गरम directly.
Sample Sentences:

(chaay garam hai, jaldii pii lo।)

Sense 2 : hot-tempered, heated, intense (figurative)
adjective Used for temper, arguments, or a tense atmosphere: गरम मिज़ाज (garam mizaaj, 'hot temperament') or बहस गरम हो गई (bahas garam ho gaii, 'the debate got heated').
Synonyms: तेज़ (tez, sharp/quick-tempered), उग्र (ugr, formal, fierce)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will often hear उसका दिमाग गरम है (uskaa dimaag garam hai, 'his head is hot,' i.e., he loses his temper easily) -- the same heat metaphor as English 'hot-headed,' which makes this one intuitive to pick up.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'hot-tempered' with गुस्सैल (gussail) only, missing that गरम is equally natural and often used with मिज़ाज (mizaaj, temperament) or दिमाग (dimaag, mind/head) as a set phrase.
Sample Sentences:
