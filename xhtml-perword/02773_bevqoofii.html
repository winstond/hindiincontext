बेवक़ूफ़ी (bevqoofii)

noun The abstract noun 'foolishness, stupidity' derived from बेवक़ूफ़ (bevqoof, 'foolish/fool') by adding the abstract-noun suffix -ई (ii).
Sense 1 : foolishness, stupidity (the quality)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मूर्खता (moorkhtaa, formal), बेवकूफी (bevkoophii, variant spelling without nuqta), नासमझी (naasamjhii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in scoldings at home: यह क्या बेवक़ूफ़ी है (yah kyaa bevqoofii hai, 'what foolishness is this') is a classic mother-to-child or friend-to-friend line in Delhi households. Note the nuqta under फ़ (f) reflects the word's Persian/Urdu origin (वक़ूफ़, understanding/sense) -- dropping the nuqta to write बेवकूफी (bevkoophii) is extremely common in casual writing and perfectly acceptable.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as an adjective describing a person directly; it is a noun naming the quality itself, so to call a person foolish you still need बेवक़ूफ़ (bevqoof) + है (hai), while बेवक़ूफ़ी (bevqoofii) is what you commit or show, as in बेवक़ूफ़ी करना (bevqoofii karnaa, 'to act foolishly').

Sense 2 : a foolish act or mistake (a specific instance)
noun, feminine Used countably in this sense, often with एक (ek) or in the plural बेवक़ूफ़ियाँ (bevqoofiyaan) to mean 'foolish acts,' distinct from the abstract-quality sense above.
Synonyms: गलती (galtii, overlaps only when the foolish act is also a plain mistake), भूल (bhool, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind a common self-critical or regretful line like मुझसे बड़ी बेवक़ूफ़ी हो गई (mujhse badii bevqoofii ho gaii, 'I made a big blunder') -- notice the दे-मार्किंग-free से (se) construction typical of involuntary-mistake phrasing in Hindi, where the blame lands softly on the person rather than making them the grammatical agent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because the word looks abstract, learners hesitate to pluralize or count it; but native speakers freely say एक बेवक़ूफ़ी (ek bevqoofii, 'one foolish act') or कई बेवक़ूफ़ियाँ (kaii bevqoofiyaan, 'several foolish acts') when referring to specific incidents rather than the general trait.
