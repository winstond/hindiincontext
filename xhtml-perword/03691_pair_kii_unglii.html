पैर की उँगली (pair kii unglii)

noun A compound noun meaning 'toe' -- literally 'finger/digit of the foot' (pair kii unglii). One straightforward sense.
Sense 1 : toe (digit of the foot)
noun, feminine Built from उँगली (unglii, 'finger/digit') plus पैर की (pair kii, 'of the foot'); the same word उँगली (unglii) covers both fingers and toes in Hindi, so the possessive phrase पैर की (pair kii) is what specifies 'toe' rather than 'finger.'
Synonyms: पैर की अंगुली (pair kii angulii, spelling variant), पंजे की उँगली (panje kii unglii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi doesn't bother with separate root words for 'finger' and 'toe' the way English does -- both are just उँगली (unglii), and you specify which one you mean by saying 'of the hand' (हाथ की haath kii) or 'of the foot' (पैर की pair kii). This reflects a broader pattern in Hindi body vocabulary where fewer base nouns cover more ground than in English, relying on possessive phrases to narrow things down.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to say 'toe' with just उँगली (unglii) alone, but without पैर की (pair kii) a listener will default to hearing 'finger' -- the qualifier is not optional if the foot isn't already clear from context. 2) Word order matters: पैर की उँगली (pair kii unglii) is correct, not उँगली का पैर (unglii kaa pair), which would nonsensically mean 'the foot of the finger.'
