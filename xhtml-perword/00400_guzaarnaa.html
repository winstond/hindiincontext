गुज़ारना (guzaarnaa)

verb Mainly means 'to spend/pass' time, and by extension 'to live through' a period of life; related to गुज़रना (guzarnaa, 'to pass/go by'), which is the intransitive counterpart.
Sense 1 : to spend or pass (time)
verb, transitive Takes a time expression as its direct object, e.g. वक़्त गुज़ारना (vaqt guzaarnaa, 'to spend time').
Synonyms: बिताना (bitaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in phrases like ज़िंदगी गुज़ारना (zindgii guzaarnaa, 'to live out one's life') or रात गुज़ारना (raat guzaarnaa, 'to spend the night') -- it carries a gentle sense of enduring or getting through time, not just clocking it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with गुज़रना (guzarnaa); गुज़ारना (guzaarnaa) is transitive (you spend time), while गुज़रना (guzarnaa) is intransitive (time passes on its own) -- 'साल गुज़र गया' (saal guzar gayaa, 'the year passed') versus 'मैंने साल गुज़ारा' (mainne saal guzaaraa, 'I spent the year'). 2) With this transitive sense, ने-marking on the subject in the perfective is required, unlike with its intransitive cousin.

(hamne poorii raat jaagkar guzaarii।)

Sense 2 : to make ends meet, get by (on limited means)
verb, transitive Often appears with गुज़र-बसर (guzar-basar) as गुज़र-बसर करना (guzar-basar karnaa), the more common way to say 'to make a living/get by' -- गुज़ारना (guzaarnaa) alone in this sense is somewhat less frequent than that fixed phrase.
Synonyms: काटना (kaatnaa, 'to get through', informal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up when people talk about tough financial times: थोड़े में गुज़ारना (thode men guzaarnaa, 'to make do with little') is a very Indian-household way of praising frugality and resilience.
