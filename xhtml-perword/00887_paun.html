पौन (paun)

numeral A number word meaning 'three-quarters' or 'minus one-quarter,' used mainly to talk about time and quantities, e.g. पौन घंटा (paun ghantaa, 'three-quarters of an hour').
Sense 1 : three-quarters (of a unit)
numeral Used before a unit noun (hour, kilogram, dozen, etc.) to mean 'that unit minus a quarter' -- पौन किलो (paun kilo, 'three-quarters of a kilogram') -- much like how सवा (savaa) means 'plus a quarter' and साढ़े (saadhe) means 'plus a half.'
Synonyms: तीन चौथाई (tiin chauthaaii, more literal/explicit)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पौन (paun) belongs to a small family of quantity-words -- सवा (savaa, 'plus a quarter'), साढ़े (saadhe, 'plus a half'), and पौन (paun, 'minus a quarter') -- that Hindi speakers use constantly for clock time and market weights, so mastering all three together pays off fast. You'll hear it most in phrases like पौन घंटा (paun ghantaa, 'forty-five minutes') or पौने दस बजे (paune das baje, 'at a quarter to ten') -- note the changed form पौने (paune) before बजे (baje), which the morphology table will cover.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तीन चौथाई (tiin chauthaaii) by default since it mirrors the English calculation, but पौन (paun) is what people actually say in daily speech, especially with time and weight. 2) पौन (paun) is always used as a modifier directly before the noun, not as a standalone fraction the way you might say 'three-quarters' alone in English.
