चार (chaar)

numeral/adjective The number 'four' -- used both as a counting numeral and, idiomatically, in a handful of set phrases meaning 'a few' or 'several'.
Sense 1 : the number four
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear चार (chaar) constantly in daily bargaining and directions -- चार सौ (chaar sau, 'four hundred') at the sabzi mandi, or चार बज गए (chaar baj gae, 'it's already four o'clock') when someone is running late.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect चार (chaar) like an adjective before a noun -- it stays invariant regardless of the noun's gender or case: चार लड़के (chaar ladke, four boys), चार लड़कियाँ (chaar ladkiyaan, four girls).

Sense 2 : a few, several (approximate small number)
adjective Used loosely, not as an exact count -- चार बातें (chaar baaten, 'a few words/things to say') doesn't mean literally four.
Synonyms: कुछ (kuchh), दो-चार (do-chaar, variant emphasizing 'a couple to a few')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the चार (chaar) of everyday idiom: चार लोग (chaar log, 'a few people,' i.e. 'what will people think') is a classic line Indian parents use to invoke social judgment -- चार लोग क्या कहेंगे (chaar log kyaa kahenge, 'what will people say').
Common Mistakes: 1) This 'a few' sense is easy to miss because it looks identical to the literal numeral -- चार दिन (chaar din) in casual speech often just means 'a few days,' not exactly four.
