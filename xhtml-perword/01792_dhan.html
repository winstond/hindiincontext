धन (dhan)

noun Mainly a noun meaning wealth or money in a somewhat formal/literary register; it also appears as a grammar/science term for 'positive' (as in positive charge or positive numbers).
Sense 1 : wealth, riches, money (formal register)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पैसा (paisaa), दौलत (daulat), संपत्ति (sampatti, formal, often 'property/assets'), माल (maal, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: धन (dhan) carries a certain gravity -- it is the word you hear in phrases about prosperity and good fortune, like धन-दौलत (dhan-daulat, 'wealth and riches') or the goddess Lakshmi being called धन की देवी (dhan kii devii, 'goddess of wealth'). It sits a notch above पैसा (paisaa) in formality, closer to English 'wealth' than 'cash.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for धन (dhan) as a everyday word for 'money,' but in casual speech पैसा (paisaa) is what people actually say at the shop or with friends; धन (dhan) shows up more in proverbs, financial contexts, or slightly elevated speech (savings, prosperity, wealth in a moral or philosophical sense).

Sense 2 : positive (as in positive charge, positive number, or grammatical/astrological technical sense)
noun, masculine Used almost exclusively as part of set technical terms such as धन आवेश (dhan aavesh, 'positive charge') or धन चिन्ह (dhan chinh, 'plus sign'), rather than as a freestanding descriptive word.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same धन (dhan) you will meet in a school science textbook -- positive charge is धन आवेश (dhan aavesh) and negative is ऋण आवेश (rin aavesh). It is a specialized, low-frequency use for a conversational learner, but worth recognizing if you ever read Hindi science material.
