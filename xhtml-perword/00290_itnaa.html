इतना (itnaa)

adjective/adverb/pronoun A demonstrative quantifier meaning 'this much/so much', inflecting for gender/number (itnii, itne); it also does duty as an intensifier ('so') before adjectives and adverbs. Pairs naturally with जितना (jitnaa, 'as much as') in correlative constructions.
Sense 1 : this much, so much (quantity)
adjective Agrees in gender/number with the noun it quantifies: इतना पैसा (itnaa paisaa, 'this much money'), इतनी चीज़ें (itnii chiizen, 'this many things').
Synonyms: इस कदर (is kadar, more literary/emphatic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the kitchen or market: इतना नमक मत डालो (itnaa namak mat daalo, 'don't put in so much salt') or इतने पैसे नहीं हैं (itne paise nahiin hain, 'I don't have this much money'). It is the everyday partner of जितना (jitnaa) in the classic 'as much as this much' correlative frame that Hindi uses constantly where English restructures the sentence entirely.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget to inflect इतना (itnaa) for gender/number with the noun it modifies, leaving it stuck in the masculine singular form even before feminine or plural nouns. 2) इतना (itnaa) is often paired with जितना (jitnaa) in 'as much as' comparisons (जितना...इतना...), and learners sometimes use सिर्फ (sirph) or दूसरा (doosraa) constructions instead of this natural correlative pattern.

Sense 2 : so, to this extent (intensifier before adjectives/adverbs)
adverb Invariant in this use, placed directly before the adjective or adverb it intensifies: इतना अच्छा (itnaa achchhaa, 'so good'), इतना जल्दी (itnaa jaldii, 'so quickly').
Synonyms: इस कदर (is kadar, literary/emphatic), इतना ज़्यादा (itnaa zyaadaa, emphatic variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word behind exasperated lines like तुम इतना क्यों बोलते हो? (tum itnaa kyon bolte ho?, 'why do you talk so much?') -- a very Delhi-household kind of complaint, usually from a mother to a chatty child.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mistakenly inflect इतना (itnaa) here as if it were agreeing with the following adjective, but as an intensifying adverb before an adjective it stays invariant while the adjective itself takes the agreement, e.g. इतनी अच्छी लड़की (itnii achchhii ladkii, 'such a good girl') -- here इतनी (itnii) agrees with लड़की (ladkii), not the reverse.

Sense 3 : this much (standing alone for a quantity)
pronoun Used without a following noun when the quantity is understood from context: इतना काफ़ी है (itnaa kaafii hai, 'this much is enough').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear a shopkeeper or host say बस, इतना ही (bas, itnaa hii, 'that's all, just this much') as a natural way to close off a transaction or offer -- a compact, very common phrase pattern.
