रौनक (raunak)

noun A single warm, atmospheric noun meaning the liveliness, glow, or bustling charm of a place or gathering -- one sense only, but a favorite word for describing markets, festivals, and homes full of people.
Sense 1 : liveliness, brightness, festive bustle of a place or occasion
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चहल-पहल (chahal-pahal, bustle of activity), बहार (bahaar, literary), जगमगाहट (jagamgaahat, sparkle/brightness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi shopkeeper about Diwali and they'll say 'bazaar mein khoob raunak hai' (baazaar men khoob raunak hai, 'the market is really lively') -- this word is the go-to for describing lights, crowds, and festive energy together, and it's just as often used for a home full of guests as for a lit-up market street. It also gets used affectionately about a face: 'चेहरे पर रौनक आ गई' (chehre par raunak aa gaii, 'his face lit up') shows the same glow-metaphor applied to a person.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for 'liveliness' only in the sense of a person's energy, but रौनक (raunak) describes the atmosphere of a place, market, event, or even a face -- not an individual's personality. 2) It is very commonly used with आना (aanaa) and बढ़ना (badhnaa) -- 'बाज़ार में रौनक आ गई' (baazaar men raunak aa gaii, 'the market came alive') -- treating रौनक (raunak) as something that arrives or grows, not something you simply 'have.'
