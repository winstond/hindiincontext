चिमटा (chimtaa)

noun A common kitchen and household item, primarily 'tongs,' with a related fire-tool sense.
Sense 1 : kitchen tongs
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi household about चिमटा (chimtaa) and they'll picture the metal tongs hanging by the stove, used to flip रोटी (rotii) on the तवा (tavaa, 'griddle') or to pull embers in and out of the चूल्हा (choolhaa, 'traditional stove'). It's one of those everyday kitchen nouns every learner should know early, right alongside चम्मच (chammach, 'spoon') and कड़ाही (kadaahii, 'wok/pan').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with चिमटी (chimtii, 'tweezers/small clip'), which is a smaller, different tool -- चिमटा (chimtaa) is specifically the larger kitchen tongs used for handling hot रोटी (rotii, 'flatbread') or coals.

Sense 2 : fire tongs / iron pincers (also associated with ascetics)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word extended to the heavier iron tongs used for handling fire or coals -- and famously, the metal चिमटा (chimtaa) carried by wandering साधु (saadhu, 'ascetics') and sometimes struck like a percussion instrument during भजन (bhajan, 'devotional songs'). If you've seen a सन्यासी (sanyaasii, 'renunciant') on the street with a metal rod jangling with rings, that's often called a चिमटा (chimtaa) too.
