भीख (bhiikh)

noun A noun meaning 'alms, begging' -- almost always seen in the fixed phrase भीख माँगना (bhiikh maangnaa, 'to beg'), which carries both a literal and a strong figurative sense of humiliation.
Sense 1 : alms, charity given to a beggar
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दान (daan, charitable giving, broader sense), खैरात (khairaat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word constantly at traffic signals and outside temples in Delhi, where बच्चे भीख माँगते हैं (bachche bhiikh maangte hain, 'children beg for alms') is a common, sobering sight. Because of this, भीख माँगना (bhiikh maangnaa) has taken on a heavy figurative weight too -- see the next sense -- so Hindi speakers reach for it whenever they want to evoke the shame of having to plead for something that should be given freely.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use भीख (bhiikh) for any kind of charity or donation; it specifically means what is given to a beggar on the street, not a temple donation or organized charity, which is दान (daan) or चंदा (chandaa). 2) The verb almost always paired with this noun is माँगना (maangnaa, 'to ask for') for the beggar's action, and देना (denaa, 'to give') for the giver's action -- मुझसे भीख मत माँगो (mujhse bhiikh mat maango, 'don't beg from me') is far more natural than trying to build other verb combinations.

(laal battii par ek bachchaa gaadii kii khidkii par bhiikh maangne aayaa।)

Sense 2 : figurative: a humiliating, undeserved plea for something
noun, feminine Used almost exclusively inside the phrase भीख माँगना (bhiikh maangnaa) or भीख में मिलना (bhiikh men milnaa, 'to get as a handout'), applied to respect, love, favors, or justice rather than money.
Synonyms: गुहार (guhaar, a desperate plea, not quite the same shame connotation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the common rebuke भीख में मिली इज़्ज़त नहीं चाहिए (bhiikh men milii izzat nahiin chaahie, 'I don't want respect given like alms') -- the idea that anything handed over out of pity rather than earned is not worth having is a deeply felt cultural value, tied closely to notions of इज़्ज़त (izzat, 'honor/dignity').
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative use is much stronger than English 'begging' -- मैं तुमसे प्यार की भीख नहीं माँगूँगा (main tumse pyaar kii bhiikh nahiin maangoongaa, 'I will not beg you for love') signals wounded pride, not a neutral request, so learners should reserve it for emotionally charged moments, not casual asking (for which माँगना (maangnaa) alone or please-type requests are used).

मुझे भीख में मिली इज़्ज़त नहीं चाहिए। I don't want respect that's given to me like alms.
