दस्त (dast)

noun Mainly a medical/colloquial word for 'loose motions, diarrhea'; also survives in a couple of set idioms meaning 'hand' (from Persian), which are literary/fixed rather than everyday vocabulary.
Sense 1 : diarrhea, loose motions
noun, masculine Almost always used with लगना (lagnaa) or होना (honaa), e.g. दस्त लगना (dast lagnaa, 'to have loose motions') -- the person affected is marked with को (ko), not ने (ne).
Synonyms: पेचिश (pechish, dysentery, narrower medical sense), लूज़ मोशन (looz moshan, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will actually hear at a Delhi chemist's counter or from a worried mother describing a child's upset stomach -- it is direct and commonly used, not a euphemism like the English 'upset stomach.' Doctors and lay people alike say it without embarrassment.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat दस्त (dast) as a verb-like action they 'do'; in real usage it 'happens to' someone -- मुझे दस्त लगे हैं (mujhe dast lage hain, 'I have loose motions'), not a construction with ने (ne). 2) दस्त (dast) is plural-agreeing in most everyday usage (दस्त लगे हैं, dast lage hain) even though it refers to one ongoing condition -- learners often default to singular agreement.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे कल रात से दस्त लगे हैं। I've had diarrhea since last night.
(mujhe kal raat se dast lage hain।)

Sense 2 : hand (literary, from Persian)
noun, masculine Survives mainly inside fixed idioms rather than as a freestanding word for 'hand' in speech.
Synonyms: हाथ (haath)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense almost only inside the idiom दस्त-ओ-गिरेबान होना (dast-o-girebaan honaa, to grapple/come to blows) or in Urdu-influenced poetry -- not something to reach for in daily conversation, where हाथ (haath) is the normal word.
