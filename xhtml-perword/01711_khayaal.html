ख़याल (khayaal)

noun A word borrowed from Persian/Arabic that lives right at the center of Hindi-Urdu emotional and mental vocabulary: it covers 'thought/idea', 'opinion', and 'care/concern' (as in ख़याल रखना (khayaal rakhnaa)). Also commonly spelled without the nuqta as खयाल (khayaal).
Sense 1 : thought, idea
noun, masculine
Synonyms: विचार (vichaar), सोच (soch)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Thoughts in Hindi typically 'come' to you rather than being something you 'have': मुझे एक ख़याल आया (mujhe ek khayaal aayaa, 'a thought came to me') is the natural frame, echoing the same dative-subject logic learners already know from लगना (lagnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for विचार (vichaar) by default, but ख़याल (khayaal) is the word people actually use in casual speech for a passing thought or notion -- विचार (vichaar) leans slightly more formal/deliberate. 2) The verb पड़ना (padnaa) with ख़याल (khayaal) -- ख़याल पड़ना (khayaal padnaa) -- is far less common than ख़याल आना (khayaal aanaa, 'a thought occurs to me'); learners sometimes force पड़ना (padnaa) by analogy with other words.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे एक ख़याल आया। A thought came to me.

Sense 2 : opinion, view
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राय (raay), नज़रिया (nazriyaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मेरे ख़याल से (mere khayaal se) is one of the most common openers in everyday Hindi conversation, doing the job of English 'I think' or 'in my view' -- you will hear it constantly in debates, dinner-table chats, and news interviews alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) मेरे ख़याल से (mere khayaal se, 'in my opinion') is the fixed way to preface an opinion -- learners sometimes mistranslate it word-for-word as 'from my thought' instead of recognizing it as the idiomatic equivalent of 'I think that...'.

Sense 3 : care, concern, attention (toward someone/something)
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively in the compound expression ख़याल रखना (khayaal rakhnaa, 'to take care of') with का (kaa) marking the person or thing cared for.
Synonyms: देखभाल (dekhbhaal), फ़िक्र (fikr)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: अपना ख़याल रखना (apnaa khayaal rakhnaa, 'take care of yourself') is the classic parting line in Hindi -- said at railway platforms, airport goodbyes, and at the end of nearly every phone call home, much like English 'take care'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say ख़याल करना (khayaal karnaa) when they mean 'take care of', but the natural verb here is रखना (rakhnaa) -- अपना ख़याल रखना (apnaa khayaal rakhnaa, 'take care of yourself') is the standard phrase, not ख़याल करना (khayaal karnaa). 2) The person/thing being cared for takes का/की/के (kaa), not को (ko): उसका ख़याल रखो (uskaa khayaal rakho, 'take care of him/her'), not उसे (use).
