हटना (hatnaa)

verb An intransitive verb meaning 'to move away/aside' or 'to withdraw'; its causative partner हटाना (hataanaa, 'to remove, move something') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to move aside, get out of the way
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: खिसकना (khisaknaa, to shift/scoot over), दूर होना (door honaa, to get away/distant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear हटो हटो (hato hato, 'move, move!') shouted constantly in crowded bazaars and by rickshaw-wallahs trying to get through traffic -- it's the everyday Delhi soundtrack of someone clearing a path.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for हटाना (hataanaa) when they mean हटना (hatnaa) -- हटना (hatnaa) is what you do yourself (move aside), हटाना (hataanaa) is what you do to something else (remove it). 2) As a command, हटो (hato, 'move!') or हट जाओ (hat jaao) is what you shout at someone blocking your path -- it can sound rude/abrupt in a way 'excuse me' doesn't, so tone matters.
Sample Sentences:
हटो, रास्ता दो! Move aside, give way!
(hato, raastaa do!)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active
रिक्शेवाला चिल्ला रहा था, "हटो हटो!" The rickshaw-wallah was shouting, 'Move, move!'

Sense 2 : to withdraw, back down (from a stance, decision, or position)
verb, intransitive Often paired with से (se) to mark what is being withdrawn from, e.g. अपनी बात से हटना (apnii baat se hatnaa, 'to go back on one's word').
Synonyms: पीछे हटना (piichhe hatnaa, compound-verb form, 'to step back/retreat'), मुकरना (mukarnaa, to renege/go back on one's word)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense extends the physical 'moving aside' into the world of arguments and commitments -- अपनी ज़िद से नहीं हटा (apnii zid se nahiin hataa, 'he didn't budge from his stubbornness') is a very common way to describe someone who won't compromise, a trait often discussed with a mix of exasperation and admiration in Indian families.
