दुर्लभ (durlabh)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'rare, hard to obtain' -- used for scarce objects, opportunities, or qualities.
Sense 1 : rare, scarce, hard to find/obtain
adjective
Synonyms: विरल (viral, less common in speech), अनोखा (anokhaa, unique/one-of-a-kind rather than scarce), बेशकीमती (beshkiimtii, emphasizes value more than scarcity)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet दुर्लभ (durlabh) constantly in news headlines, museum plaques, and nature documentaries -- दुर्लभ प्रजाति (durlabh prajaati, 'rare species') and दुर्लभ अवसर (durlabh avsar, 'rare opportunity') are stock phrases. It carries a slightly bookish, respectful weight, a bit like saying 'exceedingly rare' rather than just 'rare' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अनोखा (anokhaa) when they mean दुर्लभ (durlabh) -- अनोखा stresses being unusual or one-of-a-kind, while दुर्लभ (durlabh) stresses that something is scarce or hard to obtain; a two-headed coin is अनोखा (anokhaa), a rare disease is दुर्लभ (durlabh). 2) This word sits at a slightly formal/written register -- in casual speech, people often just say कम मिलता है (kam miltaa hai, 'it's rarely found') instead of reaching for दुर्लभ (durlabh).

(yah ek durlabh prajaati hai, jo ab sirph in janglon men paaii jaatii hai।)
