ग़ायब (ghaayab)

adjective An adjective meaning 'absent, disappeared, missing' -- used constantly in everyday speech about people, objects, and even abstract things like signal or electricity going away.
Sense 1 : disappeared, vanished, missing
adjective Almost always used with होना (honaa) or हो जाना (ho jaanaa) -- 'ग़ायब होना' (ghaayab honaa, 'to disappear') is the default construction; used with कर देना (kar denaa) it becomes causative, 'to make disappear.'
Synonyms: लापता (laaptaa, used mainly for missing people), अदृश्य (adrishy, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is a great example of everyday Urdu vocabulary fully absorbed into spoken Hindi -- you will hear ग़ायब (ghaayab) in Delhi markets, homes, and offices just as often as in Bollywood dialogue, usually with a note of mild exasperation: 'बिजली फिर ग़ायब हो गई' (bijlii phir ghaayab ho gaii, 'the power's gone again'). It pairs naturally with जादू से ग़ायब होना (jaadoo se ghaayab honaa, 'to disappear as if by magic'), the phrase kids use for magic tricks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अदृश्य (adrishy) to say something 'disappeared,' but that word is bookish and describes invisibility as a state, not the everyday act of vanishing -- ग़ायब (ghaayab) is what people actually say when keys, electricity, or a friend go missing. 2) The nuqta on ग़ (gh) is often dropped in casual writing (गायब), which is common and acceptable, but the correct pronunciation has a voiced velar fricative, closer to Urdu 'gh' than a plain 'g'.
