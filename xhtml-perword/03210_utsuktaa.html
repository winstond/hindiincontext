उत्सुकता (utsuktaa)

noun A single-sense abstract noun meaning 'eagerness/curiosity' -- the feeling of wanting to know or do something. Related to the adjective उत्सुक (utsuk, 'eager, curious').
Sense 1 : eagerness, curiosity, keenness
noun, feminine Formed from the adjective उत्सुक (utsuk, 'eager') plus the abstract-noun suffix -ता (taa), a common pattern for turning adjectives into nouns of quality.
Synonyms: जिज्ञासा (jigyaasaa, more about intellectual curiosity), उत्कंठा (utkanthaa, literary), बेचैनी (bechainii, restlessness/impatience, not pure curiosity)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word a lot in slightly more thoughtful or written speech -- news anchors, teachers, and writers reach for उत्सुकता (utsuktaa) when describing the public's or a character's eagerness to know an outcome, like exam results or election news. In everyday chatty speech, people often just say बेसब्री से इंतज़ार (besabrii se intzaar, 'impatient waiting') instead, so this word has a slightly more measured, literate feel without being stiff or archaic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use it where English 'excitement' is meant -- उत्सुकता (utsuktaa) is specifically the eager-to-know/eager-to-see feeling, not general excitement or joy, for which उत्साह (utsaah) or खुशी (khushii) fit better. 2) It is typically paired with की (kii) plus a noun or verbal noun to say what one is eager about, e.g. उससे मिलने की उत्सुकता (usse milne kii utsuktaa, 'eagerness to meet him/her') -- learners often try to attach से (se) instead of building the की-phrase.
