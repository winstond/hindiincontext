धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire)

adverb A reduplicated adverb meaning 'slowly, gradually' -- the doubling of धीरे (dhiire) intensifies the sense of a gradual, unhurried process. One core meaning, used constantly in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : slowly, gradually
adverb
Synonyms: आहिस्ता आहिस्ता (aahistaa aahistaa), क्रमशः (kramshah, formal, used for sequential/gradual progression rather than physical slowness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This reduplication pattern -- saying a word twice for emphasis or continuity -- is everywhere in Hindi (धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire), थोड़ा थोड़ा (thodaa thodaa), एक एक (ek ek)), and it's one of the most native-sounding habits a learner can pick up. You'll hear धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire) constantly in contexts like learning a language, healing from an illness, or a relationship developing -- it carries a gentle, reassuring undertone, almost like telling someone 'don't rush, it'll happen in its own time.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use सिर्फ़ धीरे (dhiire) when they mean a gradual process over time (like improving at something or a habit fading), but the reduplicated धीरे धीरे (dhiire dhiire) is what native speakers reach for to convey 'over time, bit by bit' -- a single धीरे (dhiire) leans more toward 'slowly' in manner or speed (e.g. driving slowly). 2) It describes the pace of an action, not politeness or gentleness of tone -- learners sometimes conflate it with 'softly' when talking about voice or touch, where चुपके से (chupke se) or आहिस्ता से (aahistaa se) may fit better depending on context.
