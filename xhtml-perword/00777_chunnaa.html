चुनना (chunnaa)

verb A transitive verb with two everyday senses: 'to choose/select' (elections, options, decisions) and 'to pick/pluck' (physically gathering things like fruit, flowers, or stones one by one).
Sense 1 : to choose, select, elect
verb, transitive
Synonyms: चयन करना (chayan karnaa, formal), छाँटना (chhaantnaa, to select by sorting/picking out)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind चुनाव (chunaav, 'election') -- so every election season, you will hear चुनना (chunnaa) constantly on the news: जनता ने उन्हें चुना (jantaa ne unhen chunaa, 'the public chose him'). It is also the everyday word for choosing anything from a menu, a shop shelf, or a career path.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for छाँटना (chhaantnaa) when they mean a simple choice; छाँटना (chhaantnaa) leans toward sorting through options and picking out the best, while चुनना (chunnaa) is the neutral, all-purpose word for choosing, including voting in elections. 2) In the political sense ('to elect'), the object is the person elected, not the voters -- वे उसे नेता चुनते हैं (ve use netaa chunte hain, 'they choose him as leader') -- learners sometimes mismap the roles from English 'elect someone.'
Sample Sentences:

(jantaa ne use apnaa netaa chunaa।)

Sense 2 : to pick, pluck, gather (one by one)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: तोड़ना (todnaa, to pluck by breaking off, e.g. flowers/fruit), बीनना (biinnaa, to pick up/glean small items like grain or stones)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in the kitchen a lot: घर की महिलाएँ अक्सर दाल या चावल चुनती हैं (ghar kii mahilaaen aksar daal yaa chaaval chuntii hain, 'women at home often pick over lentils or rice') to remove small stones or husks before cooking -- a very concrete, everyday image tied to this sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to तोड़ना (todnaa) for all picking, but चुनना (chunnaa) specifically suggests a careful, one-by-one selection process -- picking the good ones out from a pile, like चावल चुनना (chaaval chunnaa, 'to pick over rice for stones/husks') -- rather than just detaching something from a plant.
