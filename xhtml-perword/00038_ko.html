को (ko)

postposition को (ko) is one of the workhorse postpositions of Hindi: it marks indirect objects ('to'), certain definite direct objects, the experiencer in dative-subject constructions (with words like लगना (lagnaa) and चाहिए (chaahie)), and points in time ('on/at').
Sense 1 : to/for (indirect object marker)
postposition Marks the recipient or goal of verbs like देना (denaa, 'to give'), बताना (bataanaa, 'to tell'), and भेजना (bhejnaa, 'to send'): राम को किताब दो (raam ko kitaab do, 'give the book to Ram').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the core 'goal' sense of को (ko) -- something moves, literally or figuratively, toward the person marked by it. The other senses of को (ko) (marking objects, experiencers, even points in time) all extend from this same idea of something being 'directed at' a target.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for के लिए (ke lie) here, but के लिए marks purpose or benefit ('for the sake of'), while को marks the actual recipient of the action -- राम को दो (raam ko do, 'give [it] to Ram') is different from राम के लिए लाओ (raam ke lie laao, 'bring [it] for Ram').

Sense 2 : marks a specific/definite direct object
postposition Appears on a direct object, especially a specific person, when the speaker wants to single it out: राम को बुलाओ (raam ko bulaao, 'call Ram [specifically]') versus बच्चों को खाना दो (bachchon ko khaanaa do, 'give the children food').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers use को (ko) here the way English uses stress or word order to flag 'this particular one' -- a bare direct object feels generic, but adding को (ko) picks out a definite, known individual, almost always when that object is a person or named animate being.
Common Mistakes: 1) Dropping को on a definite human object makes a sentence sound odd or incomplete to a native ear -- राम बुलाओ (raam bulaao) reads as clipped or unnatural next to the correct राम को बुलाओ (raam ko bulaao). 2) Conversely, adding को to an indefinite object is wrong: मुझे एक किताब चाहिए (mujhe ek kitaab chaahie, 'I need a book') never takes को before किताब (kitaab) because the book isn't a specific, known one.

Sense 3 : marks the experiencer in dative-subject constructions
postposition Used with verbs and constructions of feeling, necessity, or perception -- लगना (lagnaa), पसंद आना (pasand aanaa), चाहिए (chaahie), दर्द होना (dard honaa) -- where the person affected is marked with को (ko) rather than behaving as a grammatical subject: मुझको ठंड लगती है (mujhko thand lagtii hai, 'I feel cold').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi treats feelings, needs, and sensations as things that happen *to* a person rather than things a person actively does -- so 'I like it' becomes मुझे पसंद है (mujhe pasand hai, literally 'to me it is pleasing'). This को-as-experiencer (ko) pattern is one of the biggest conceptual shifts for an English speaker learning Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners who know को (ko) mainly as 'to' sometimes drop it here and treat the experiencer like an English subject: मैं पानी चाहिए (main paanii chaahie) instead of the correct मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paanii chaahie, 'I need water').

Sense 4 : on/at (marks a point in time)
postposition Attaches to days of the week and specific time expressions: सोमवार को (somvaar ko, 'on Monday'), सुबह को (subah ko, 'in the morning').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The same 'pointing at a target' logic behind को (ko) as 'to' also lets it single out a point in time, much like English 'on' before a day -- 'on Monday' and सोमवार को (somvaar ko) both aim the sentence at one specific slot.
Common Mistakes: 1) को (ko) is used with days of the week and general time-of-day words but tends to drop out with more elaborate calendar dates, which can confuse learners about when it's required versus optional.
