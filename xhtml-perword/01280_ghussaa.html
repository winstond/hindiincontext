ग़ुस्सा (ghussaa)

noun/adjective Mainly a noun meaning 'anger', almost always paired with the verb आना (aanaa) in the dative-subject frame 'X को ग़ुस्सा आना' (anger comes to X); also has a common adjective-like predicate use meaning 'angry'.
Sense 1 : anger, temper
noun, masculine Almost always appears with आना (aanaa) in the pattern 'X को ग़ुस्सा आना' -- literally 'anger comes to X' -- rather than any verb meaning 'to feel'.
Synonyms: क्रोध (krodh, formal), नाराज़गी (naaraazgii, mild/displeasure)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This को + noun + आना frame -- the same one used for नींद (niind, sleep) or भूख (bhookh, hunger) -- treats anger as something that visits or overtakes you rather than something you actively feel, which is a very Hindi way of framing emotions as involuntary events. You will hear mothers across Delhi say 'मुझे बहुत ग़ुस्सा आता है तुम पर' (mujhe bahut ghussaa aataa hai tum par, 'I get so angry at you') constantly in daily family life.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say 'मैं ग़ुस्सा हूँ' (main ghussaa hoon) by analogy with English 'I am angry,' but the natural construction uses को: 'मुझे ग़ुस्सा आता है' (mujhe ghussaa aataa hai, 'I get angry') or 'मुझे ग़ुस्सा आया' (mujhe ghussaa aayaa, 'I got angry'). 2) The verb of choice is आना (aanaa), not होना (honaa) -- ग़ुस्सा होना (ghussaa honaa) is heard but sounds less idiomatic than ग़ुस्सा आना (ghussaa aanaa) for the onset of anger.

Sense 2 : angry (predicate use)
adjective Used predicatively with होना (honaa), e.g. 'वह ग़ुस्सा है' (vah ghussaa hai, 'he/she is angry'); functions here as an invariant descriptive word rather than the noun taking आना (aanaa).
Synonyms: नाराज़ (naaraaz, displeased/upset), क्रोधित (krodhit, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This adjectival use overlaps closely with नाराज़ (naaraaz), which leans more toward 'upset/displeased' (as with a sulking friend), while ग़ुस्सा (ghussaa) as an adjective leans toward the hotter, more visible anger.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because it looks identical to the noun, learners sometimes force the को...आना (ko...aanaa) pattern even when a simple 'है' (hai) predicate would sound more natural, and vice versa -- both exist, but they are not interchangeable in every sentence; listening to native usage sentence by sentence is the best guide.
