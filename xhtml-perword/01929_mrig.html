मृग (mrig)

noun A literary/classical word for 'deer' or 'antelope', met mostly in poetry, mythology, and set expressions (like मृगतृष्णा (mrigtrishnaa)) rather than everyday speech about animals.
Sense 1 : deer, antelope (literary/classical term)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हिरन (hiran, everyday word), बारहसिंगा (baarahsingaa, specifically a large antlered deer)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This word lives on mainly in classical and devotional registers -- think of Sanskrit-flavored poetry, the Ramayana's golden deer (the माया मृग (maayaa mrig, illusory/magical deer) that lures Sita), or the zodiac sign मृग (mrig/मृग राशि, Capricorn). A Delhi kid pointing at a deer in the zoo will say हिरन (hiran), not मृग (mrig).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use मृग (mrig) as their everyday word for 'deer' after meeting it in a poem or in the zodiac sign मृग (mrig, Capricorn) -- in ordinary conversation हिरन (hiran) is what people actually say. 2) मृग (mrig) is grammatically masculine even when referring to a doe; there is no separate feminine form in common use.
