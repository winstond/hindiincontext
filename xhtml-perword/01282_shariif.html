शरीफ़ (shariif)

adjective A borrowed adjective (from Arabic-Persian via Urdu) describing a person as decent, respectable, and well-behaved -- one sense covers this everyday usage.
Sense 1 : decent, well-behaved, respectable (of a person)
adjective
Synonyms: सीधा (siidhaa, simple/mild-mannered), भला (bhalaa), नेक (nek, formal/literary), सज्जन (sajjan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the phrase शरीफ़ आदमी (shariif aadmii, 'a decent/respectable man') or शरीफ़ लड़की (shariif ladkii, 'a respectable/well-behaved girl') -- it is the go-to word Delhi parents and neighbors use to vouch for someone's character, especially in the context of arranged marriage talk or vetting a new tenant or son-in-law. Note the nuqta under फ़ (borrowed from Persian/Arabic) -- dropping it (शरीफ) is common in casual writing and still understood.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use शरीफ़ (shariif) to mean 'clever' or 'good at something' -- it only describes moral/behavioral decency, not skill or intelligence. 2) It carries a slight connotation of being harmless or even a bit naive/pushover-ish in some contexts (e.g. शरीफ़ आदमी (shariif aadmii, 'decent/harmless man')) -- tone in context matters, it is not purely a compliment like 'virtuous.'

(vah bahut shariif aadmii hai।)
