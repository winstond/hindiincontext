छत (chhat)

noun A common household noun with two closely linked senses: the flat roof/rooftop of a house (where Delhi families sit, dry pickles, fly kites) and, by extension, the ceiling of a room.
Sense 1 : rooftop, terrace
noun, feminine
Synonyms: छज्जा (chhajjaa, overhang/ledge, meaning scope), टैरेस (taires, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian homes, छत (chhat) is a genuine living space, not just a structural top -- families sit out there in the evenings, kids fly kites off it during Independence Day and Makar Sankranti, and in summer people even sleep on the छत (chhat) under the open sky. Asking 'छत पर चलें?' (chhat par chalen?, 'shall we go up to the roof?') is a very normal, everyday invitation, not an unusual one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume छत (chhat) means only 'ceiling' as in English and miss its far more common sense of a flat, usable rooftop terrace -- Delhi houses are typically built with flat roofs meant for sitting, drying clothes, and sleeping in summer. 2) छत पर (chhat par, 'on the roof') is the fixed way to say 'upstairs on the roof' -- learners sometimes reach for ऊपर (oopar) alone, which is vaguer and just means 'up/upstairs' without specifying the rooftop.

Sense 2 : ceiling
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word simply viewed from inside the room rather than from above -- Hindi doesn't split 'roof' and 'ceiling' into separate words the way English does, so छत (chhat) does double duty for both, and context (are you inside looking up, or outside standing on it) tells you which is meant.
