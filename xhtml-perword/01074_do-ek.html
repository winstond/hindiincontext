दो-एक (do-ek)

adjective A fixed compound of दो (do, 'two') and एक (ek, 'one') meaning 'a couple of' or 'a few' -- used to give a vague small quantity, not an exact count.
Sense 1 : a couple of, a few (approximate small number)
adjective Placed before the noun it quantifies, like a regular numeral: दो-एक दिन (do-ek din, 'a couple of days').
Synonyms: एक-दो (ek-do, reversed order, equally common), कुछ (kuchh, less specific), चंद (chand, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic Hindi way of being deliberately vague about small numbers -- similar to how English speakers say 'a couple of' without meaning exactly two. You will hear it constantly in daily chit-chat: दो-एक मिनट रुको (do-ek minat ruko, 'wait a couple of minutes').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat it as an exact number ('two-one') rather than a vague approximation like 'a couple/few'. 2) It is often confused with एक-दो (ek-do) -- both are correct and interchangeable, so there is no real error in swapping them, just a stylistic habit some speakers have of preferring one order over the other.
Sample Sentences:
दो-एक मिनट रुको, मैं अभी आता हूँ। Wait a couple of minutes, I'm coming right now.
(do-ek minat ruko, main abhii aataa hoon।)
