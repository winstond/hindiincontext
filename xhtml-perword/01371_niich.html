नीच (niich)

adjective/noun An adjective (and derived noun use) meaning 'low, base, vile' -- almost always about a person's moral character or social standing, not physical height.
Sense 1 : morally base, vile, despicable
adjective
Synonyms: कमीना (kamiinaa, colloquial), घटिया (ghatiyaa), दुष्ट (dusht, wicked), नीचा (niichaa, related but distinct: usually 'low/inferior' rather than 'vile')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: नीच (niich) carries real social weight in India -- historically it was used to demean people by caste or class, so calling someone नीच (niich) today still echoes that ugly hierarchy, not just a personal insult about bad behavior. A Delhi parent scolding a child for cheating might say उसकी सोच बहुत नीच है (uskii soch bahut niich hai, 'his thinking is very base'), keeping it about character rather than status, which is the safer, more common modern usage.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse नीच (niich) with नीचा (niichaa) -- नीच (niich) is a strong moral insult about character, while नीचा (niichaa) is a regular variable adjective meaning 'low' (height, position, sound) with a milder, more literal sense. 2) This is a harsh, insulting word -- do not use it lightly the way English speakers might toss around 'lowly'; in Hindi it can genuinely wound or provoke.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी सोच बहुत नीच है। His thinking is very base.
(uskii soch bahut niich hai।)

Sense 2 : a base/vile person (used as an epithet)
noun, masculine Used as a standalone noun of address or reference, e.g. वह नीच है (vah niich hai, 'he is a scoundrel'), rather than modifying another noun.
Synonyms: कमीना (kamiinaa, colloquial), दुष्ट (dusht)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in old Hindi films and mythological dramas hurled at villains -- it has a slightly dramatic, stagey flavor in everyday speech, so using it casually among friends can sound almost theatrical or old-fashioned.
Common Mistakes: 1) This noun use is essentially the adjective standing alone as a label for a person -- learners should treat it as a strong personal insult, not a neutral descriptor.
