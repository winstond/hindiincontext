सवार (savaar)

adjective/noun/verb Mainly means someone riding or mounted on something (a vehicle, animal); the related noun and compound-verb senses build on this same idea of 'being aboard/mounted.'
Sense 1 : riding, mounted, aboard
adjective Used predicatively with होना (honaa) or बैठना (baithnaa): घोड़े पर सवार (ghode par savaar, 'mounted on a horse') -- takes पर (par) to mark what is being ridden.
Synonyms: आरूढ़ (aaroodh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear सवार (savaar) constantly around Delhi bus stops and train platforms in announcements like यात्री सवार हो रहे हैं (yaatrii savaar ho rahe hain, 'passengers are boarding') -- it carries a slightly more formal, announcement-style flavor than the everyday चढ़ना (chadhnaa, 'to climb/board').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use में (men) instead of पर (par) for the thing being ridden -- it is बस पर सवार (bas par savaar, 'aboard the bus'), not बस में सवार (bas men savaar), even though बस में बैठना (bas men baithnaa, 'to sit in the bus') is fine with में.

Sense 2 : rider, horseman
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: सवार्नी (savaarnii, rare, feminine rider), घुड़सवार (ghudasvaar, specifically horseman)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In old Delhi and in Mughal-era history lessons, सवार (savaar) evokes cavalry riders and royal processions -- it still shows up in words like घुड़सवार (ghudasvaar, 'horse-rider/cavalryman') and घुड़सवारी (ghudasvaarii, 'horse-riding' as a sport).

Sense 3 : to mount, board, get on (compound verb: सवार होना)
verb, intransitive Functions as a compound verb सवार होना (savaar honaa) with पर (par) marking the vehicle/animal: वह ट्रेन पर सवार हुआ (vah tren par savaar huaa, 'he boarded the train').
Synonyms: चढ़ना (chadhnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: There is also a vivid figurative use, भूत सवार होना (bhoot savaar honaa, 'to be possessed/obsessed, as if a ghost has mounted someone') -- Delhi speakers use this playfully for an obsession, like उस पर पढ़ाई का भूत सवार है (us par padhaaii kaa bhoot savaar hai, 'he's obsessed with studying').
Common Mistakes: 1) सवार (savaar) itself never conjugates -- only होना (honaa) in सवार होना (savaar honaa) carries tense/agreement, so learners should not try to inflect सवार (savaar) directly.
