हाँ (haan)

interjection/particle The basic word for 'yes' in Hindi, also used as a filler/attention word in conversation to mean 'right' or 'so' -- covers both senses here.
Sense 1 : affirmative response
interjection
Synonyms: जी हाँ (jii haan, more polite/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हाँ (haan) is said constantly in daily life, often doubled for warmth or emphasis, as in हाँ हाँ (haan haan, 'yes yes'), which softens an answer and can mean anything from enthusiastic agreement to a slightly impatient 'yes, yes, I heard you.' Tone carries a lot of the meaning here, much more than in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often skip हाँ (haan) and just repeat the verb to affirm, which is also correct, but they should know हाँ (haan) alone is a complete and natural answer to a yes/no question -- no need to build a full sentence. 2) जी हाँ (jii haan) is not simply hाँ plus a filler word; adding जी (jii) before or after हाँ (haan) is the standard way to make the 'yes' more respectful when speaking to elders or strangers.

Sense 2 : conversational filler / discourse marker
particle Used mid-sentence or at the start of a sentence to resume a thought, seek agreement, or add mild emphasis, without functioning as a direct answer to a question.
Synonyms: अच्छा (achchhaa, overlaps as a filler)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This filler use is very much a spoken-register habit -- you will hear it constantly in real conversation, stitching sentences together or checking that the listener is still on board, but it rarely shows up this way in formal writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners tend to only recognize हाँ (haan) as 'yes' and miss it when it appears mid-conversation as a filler, for example हाँ, तो फिर क्या हुआ (haan, to phir kyaa huaa, 'so, then what happened') -- here it is not answering anything, just picking the thread back up. 2) Placed at the end of a statement, हाँ (haan) can function like a tag-question seeking confirmation, similar to 'right?' in English, e.g. तुम आ रहे हो, हाँ (tum aa rahe ho, haan, 'you're coming, right').
