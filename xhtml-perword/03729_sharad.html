शरद (sharad)

noun Refers to the autumn season in the traditional Indian six-season calendar, and is also widely used as a personal name.
Sense 1 : autumn (the Indian season, roughly September-October)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पतझड़ (patjhad, the season of leaf-fall, more everyday), शरद ऋतु (sharad ritu, fuller form specifying 'season')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: शरद (sharad) is one of the six ritu (ritu) or seasons in the classical Indian calendar, sitting between the monsoon and winter, and is associated with clear skies and the full moon of शरद पूर्णिमा (sharad poornimaa), a festival night linked to Krishna's raas leela and considered especially auspicious for moonlight. You will meet this word far more in poetry, festival names, and calendars than in casual weather chat, where पतझड़ (patjhad) is more common.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often equate शरद (sharad) directly with English 'autumn/fall' as if the seasons line up exactly, but the traditional Hindu calendar's शरद (sharad) falls around September-October, not the Western autumn months. 2) It is frequently used in the compound शरद ऋतु (sharad ritu) or शरद पूर्णिमा (sharad poornimaa); learners sometimes miss that शरद alone can sound slightly literary in casual speech compared to पतझड़ (patjhad).

Sense 2 : a common Hindu personal name (masculine)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: शरद (sharad) is a popular given name for men across North India, often chosen for children born around the शरद (sharad) season; you will meet it as a first name well before you meet it as the season-word itself in daily conversation.
