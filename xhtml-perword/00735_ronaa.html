रोना (ronaa)

verb/noun Mainly the everyday verb 'to cry, weep'; it also has a common noun sense (a complaint, a sob story), especially in the fixed phrase रोना-धोना (ronaa-dhonaa).
Sense 1 : to cry, weep
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: रुदन करना (rudan karnaa, literary), सिसकना (sisaknaa, to sob/whimper), बिलखना (bilakhnaa, to wail/cry helplessly)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers often soften an admission of crying with a smile, saying things like मैं तो रो पड़ा (main to ro padaa, 'I just burst into tears') -- the compound रो पड़ना (ro padnaa) is how most real crying-in-a-story gets described, not bare रोना (ronaa). It also appears in the everyday scolding रोना बंद करो (ronaa band karo, 'stop crying'), a line most kids in Delhi-NCR have heard from a parent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for रोना (ronaa) even when सिसकना (sisaknaa) or बिलखना (bilakhnaa) would better capture a quieter sob or a helpless wail -- रोना (ronaa) itself is the neutral, all-purpose 'cry.' 2) The compound रो पड़ना (ro padnaa) ('to suddenly burst into tears') is extremely common in narration; learners tend to default to plain रोना (ronaa) even when describing a sudden onset, where रो पड़ना (ro padnaa) sounds more native.

Sense 2 : complaint, grievance, sob-story
noun, masculine Almost always heard in the compound रोना-धोना (ronaa-dhonaa, 'complaining and moaning about troubles'), or in phrases like अपना रोना रोना (apnaa ronaa ronaa, 'to go on about one's own troubles').
Synonyms: शिकायत (shikaayat, complaint), गिला (gilaa, grievance)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a lovely case of a Hindi infinitive doing double duty as a noun: रोना (ronaa) 'to cry' becomes रोना (ronaa) 'a crying/complaint' the same way English turns 'to cry' into 'a cry.' The idiom रोना-धोना (ronaa-dhonaa) is the classic way to describe a whole session of family or friends venting about their troubles over chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) रोना (ronaa) as a noun means griping about one's troubles, not literal tears -- a sentence like उसका रोना सुनो (uskaa ronaa suno, 'listen to his complaining') has nothing to do with actual crying. 2) Learners sometimes miss that this noun sense is masculine and behaves like a normal -आ noun, distinct from the infinitive verb form it looks identical to.
