जौहरी (jauhrii)

noun A jeweler or gem merchant/appraiser -- one straightforward sense, used both literally and figuratively for someone who can spot genuine quality or talent.
Sense 1 : jeweler; gem merchant or appraiser
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुनार (sunaar, specifically a goldsmith/gold-worker), रत्न व्यापारी (ratn vyaapaarii, formal, 'gem trader')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In old market lanes like Chandni Chowk's Dariba Kalan or Jaipur's Johari Bazaar (literally 'jewelers' market'), जौहरी (jauhrii) is a respected, traditional title passed down through family businesses. Figuratively, Hindi speakers love to say a good judge of talent -- a director spotting a future star, a teacher spotting a gifted student -- 'has the eye of a जौहरी (jauhrii)', since a real jeweler can tell a genuine gem from a fake at a glance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse जौहरी (jauhrii) with सुनार (sunaar) -- सुनार (sunaar) is a goldsmith who works metal, while जौहरी (jauhrii) specifically deals in and appraises precious stones/jewels, though in everyday speech people often use them loosely for anyone in the jewelry trade.
