बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg)

noun/adjective A respectful word for an elderly person, used both as a noun ('elder, senior citizen') and as an adjective ('elderly, senior').
Sense 1 : an elderly/senior person, especially a respected elder
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: बड़े (bade, often used for family elders), वृद्ध (vriddh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Indian families, बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg) carries real social weight -- elders are consulted before big decisions, and touching their feet (pair chhoonaa) is a common gesture of respect. Calling someone बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg) rather than बूढ़ा (boodhaa) signals deference, not just age.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg) as gender-marked like other nouns, but it stays the same form for men and women -- only the verb agreement or accompanying adjective changes. 2) It is used respectfully, not clinically -- for a neutral, purely biological sense of 'old person' without the respect connotation, बूढ़ा (boodhaa) or बूढ़ी (boodhii) is more common, though those can sound blunt or even rude depending on tone.

Sense 2 : elderly, senior, aged
adjective
Synonyms: वृद्ध (vriddh, formal), बूढ़ा (boodhaa, can sound blunt)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in respectful phrases like बुज़ुर्ग लोग (buzurg log, 'elderly people') in news reports, public announcements, or family conversations -- it is the polite, socially preferred way to describe someone as old.
Common Mistakes: 1) As an adjective, बुज़ुर्ग (buzurg) is invariant -- it does not change form before masculine or feminine nouns, unlike बूढ़ा (boodhaa) / बूढ़ी (boodhii), which learners often expect by analogy.
