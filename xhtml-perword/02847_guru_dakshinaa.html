गुरु दक्षिणा (guru dakshinaa)

noun A compound noun meaning the fee, gift, or tribute given to a teacher/mentor by a student, traditionally after completing their education. Rooted in गुरु (guru, 'teacher') plus दक्षिणा (dakshinaa, 'ritual gift/fee').
Sense 1 : the traditional gift or fee given to a teacher/mentor
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase comes straight out of the old गुरुकुल (gurukul) system, where a student lived with and learned from a guru for years, then offered दक्षिणा (dakshinaa) not as a bill but as heartfelt thanks -- sometimes money, sometimes a specific task the guru requested (the Mahabharata's Ekalavya story is the most famous, if sobering, example). Today Indians still invoke गुरु दक्षिणा (guru dakshinaa) loosely for any gesture of gratitude toward a mentor, teacher, or coach, even outside formal education -- for instance, a successful student donating to their old school.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as just a synonym for 'tuition fee' ( फीस (phiis) ), but गुरु दक्षिणा (guru dakshinaa) carries a sense of voluntary, respectful tribute rather than a fixed, contractual payment -- using it interchangeably with फीस (phiis) in a modern school context can sound off. 2) It is grammatically feminine ( दक्षिणा (dakshinaa) ), so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. गुरु दक्षिणा दी (guru dakshinaa dii, 'gave [feminine] the guru's fee'), which learners sometimes get wrong by defaulting to masculine agreement.
