जी (jii)

particle/interjection/noun Covers the respectful suffix/particle जी (jii) attached to names and titles or used alone as a polite 'yes/sir-ma'am', the standalone affirmative/attentive particle, and the separate noun जी (jii) meaning 'heart, mind, spirit' seen in expressions like जी करना (jii karnaa).
Sense 1 : respectful honorific suffix/address term
particle Attaches directly after a name, title, or kinship term (e.g. मोहन जी (mohan jii), पापा जी (paapaa jii)) to mark respect; also stands alone as a polite way to address or answer someone.
Synonyms: साहब (saahab, used for men), साहिबा (saahibaa, formal, used for women)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Adding जी (jii) after someone's name is one of the first things you learn living in Delhi -- rickshaw drivers, shopkeepers, and neighbors all use it constantly. It softens speech and signals respect without the stiffness of a full title, so 'सुनिए जी' (sunie jii, 'listen, sir/ma'am') feels warm rather than formal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often skip जी (jii) entirely when speaking to elders or strangers, which can sound curt or disrespectful even if the sentence is grammatically fine. 2) जी (jii) is gender-neutral and gender-invariant -- it does not change form for men or women, unlike साहिबा (saahibaa).

Sense 2 : polite yes / attentive response
interjection Used alone as a one-word polite affirmation or acknowledgment, often in response to being called or asked something.
Synonyms: हाँ (haan), जी हाँ (jii haan, emphatic polite yes)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: When a teacher or elder calls your name and you respond just with 'जी' (jii, 'yes?'), you're being polite and attentive at once -- it's the everyday equivalent of a respectful 'yes?' in English, heard constantly in shops, offices, and homes across Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) जी (jii) alone can mean either 'yes' or simply 'I'm listening' depending on context and tone -- learners sometimes render it only as 'yes' and miss the acknowledgment sense.

Sense 3 : heart, mind, inner feeling (as the seat of desire/emotion)
noun, masculine Mainly survives in fixed expressions such as जी करना (jii karnaa, 'to feel like doing something') and जी भर (jii bhar, 'to one's heart's content'), where जी (jii) is treated grammatically like मन (man).
Synonyms: मन (man), दिल (dil)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same जी (jii) as in जी भर के खाना (jii bhar ke khaanaa, 'to eat to one's heart's content') -- a word Delhi grandmothers use liberally at the dinner table, urging you to eat until your जी (jii) is satisfied, not just your stomach.
Common Mistakes: 1) In जी करना (jii karnaa), जी (jii) takes को (ko) on the experiencer, as in मेरा जी नहीं कर रहा (meraa jii nahiin kar rahaa, 'I don't feel like it') or मुझे जी करता है (mujhe jii kartaa hai) -- learners sometimes try to make जी (jii) itself the subject of a normal verb instead of following this dative-experiencer pattern shared with मन करना (man karnaa).
