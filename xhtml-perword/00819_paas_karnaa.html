पास करना (paas karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from पास (paas, 'near/pass') plus करना (karnaa); its two everyday senses are 'to pass' (an exam, a bill) and, more colloquially, 'to pass/hand over' something to someone.
Sense 1 : to pass (an exam, test, bill, proposal)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: उत्तीर्ण होना (uttiirn honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday talk about school and college, both पास करना (paas karnaa) and पास होना (paas honaa) are used constantly, and Delhi students switch between them depending on whether they want to foreground their own effort ('main ne pass kiya') or the outcome ('main pass ho gaya'). The same verb extends naturally to formal contexts like a bill or resolution being passed in a legislature or committee.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say परीक्षा पास होना (pariikshaa paas honaa) instead of परीक्षा पास करना (pariikshaa paas karnaa) -- the exam is the object you 'pass' (transitive, with करना (karnaa)), not something that happens to you; the intransitive पास होना (paas honaa, 'to be/get passed') is a distinct, equally common construction where the exam itself is the subject, e.g. मैं पास हो गया (main paas ho gayaa, 'I passed/got through'). 2) With ने-marking in the past, remember करना (karnaa) is transitive, so मैंने परीक्षा पास की (mainne pariikshaa paas kii, 'I passed the exam') takes ने (ne) and agrees with परीक्षा (pariikshaa), not with मैं (main).

Sense 2 : to pass/hand something to someone
verb, transitive Used with the recipient marked by को (ko): नमक मुझे पास करो (namak mujhe paas karo, 'pass me the salt') -- though many speakers prefer बढ़ाना (badhaanaa) or देना (denaa) here, and पास करना (paas karnaa) in this sense feels slightly influenced by English 'pass'.
Synonyms: देना (denaa), बढ़ाना (badhaanaa, hand over/pass, colloquial in this sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this at a dinner table when someone wants the water jug or bread passed down, especially among younger, more English-exposed speakers in Delhi-NCR; older or more traditional speakers usually reach for बढ़ाना (badhaanaa) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is less universally used than the exam sense; some native speakers find नमक पास करो (namak paas karo) a bit odd or English-influenced and would default to नमक बढ़ाओ (namak badhaao) or नमक दो (namak do) instead -- learners should recognize it but lean on बढ़ाना (badhaanaa)/देना (denaa) for natural speech.
