देहात (dehaat)

noun Means the countryside/rural areas, as opposed to the city (शहर, shahar); mostly used in one straightforward sense, though it carries a distinct cultural flavor.
Sense 1 : the countryside, rural areas
noun, masculine Almost always used with the postposition में (men) -- देहात में (dehaat men, 'in the countryside') -- to mean 'out in the rural areas', often collectively covering the villages around a town or region rather than any single village.
Synonyms: गांव (gaanv, refers to a single village rather than the countryside in general), ग्रामीण क्षेत्र (graamiin kshetr, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: देहात (dehaat) is the word a city person uses when talking about 'going out to the countryside' -- it carries a mild outsider's-eye-view, the way an office-goer in Delhi might say वो तो देहात में रहते हैं (vo to dehaat men rahte hain, 'they live out in the countryside'). It pairs naturally with शहर (shahar, 'city') as the classic city-versus-countryside contrast in everyday conversation, news, and politics.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use देहात (dehaat) and गांव (gaanv) interchangeably, but गांव (gaanv) names one specific village while देहात (dehaat) refers to rural territory in general, often several villages' worth. 2) The adjective form देहाती (dehaatii) is very common in speech (meaning 'rustic' or, informally, 'unsophisticated') -- learners should recognize it as related but should not use देहात (dehaat) itself as an adjective.
