हथकड़ी (hathakdii)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'handcuffs'; it also extends figuratively to mean any restraint or shackle on one's freedom.
Sense 1 : handcuffs
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a scene straight out of a Hindi crime drama or the evening news: a police constable clamping हथकड़ी (hathakdii) onto an accused's wrists outside the थाना (thaanaa, 'police station'). The word is built from हाथ (haath, 'hand') plus कड़ी (kadii, 'link/ring'), literally 'hand-ring' -- a nice window into how Hindi builds concrete compound nouns.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use the singular हथकड़ी (hathakdii) when talking about a pair being put on someone, which is correct in Hindi -- unlike English 'handcuffs,' this word is grammatically singular for one set (हथकड़ी लगाना, hathakdii lagaanaa, 'to handcuff'), so do not add a plural marker out of habit from the English plural.
Sample Sentences:
पुलिस ने चोर को हथकड़ी लगा दी। The police handcuffed the thief.
(pulis ne chor ko hathakdii lagaa dii।)

Sense 2 : figurative restraint, shackle on freedom
noun, feminine Used metaphorically for anything that limits one's freedom, often in phrases like कानून की हथकड़ी (kaanoon kii hathakdii, 'the shackles of the law').
Synonyms: बंदिश (bandish, restriction), बंधन (bandhan, bond/tie)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will hear this in a more emotional register too -- a person might say कि यह नौकरी तो हथकड़ी बन गई है (ki yah naukrii to hathakdii ban gaii hai, 'this job has become like handcuffs for me'), meaning it has trapped them. It works the same way English speakers say someone feels 'shackled' by duty or circumstance.
Sample Sentences:
यह नौकरी तो हथकड़ी बन गई है। This job has become like a shackle for me.
(yah naukrii to hathakdii ban gaii hai।)
