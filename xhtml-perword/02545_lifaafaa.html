लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'envelope' or, by extension, a 'packet/bag' used for wrapping or carrying things; also carries a well-known idiomatic sense pointing at outward show versus inner substance.
Sense 1 : envelope
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa) most often at weddings and festivals, where relatives hand over cash tucked inside a decorated envelope as a gift -- this practice is itself sometimes just called 'देना लिफ़ाफ़ा (denaa lifaafaa, to give an envelope [of money])' in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa) with पैकेट (paiket); लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa) is specifically the flat paper kind used for letters, cards, and cash gifts, while पैकेट (paiket) is a broader packet or pouch.

Sense 2 : bag/packet (for carrying or wrapping small items)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: थैली (thailii), पैकेट (paiket)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday shopping talk, especially at a neighborhood kirana store, लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa) can simply mean the plastic or paper bag your groceries or snacks are handed to you in -- a shopkeeper might casually ask 'लिफ़ाफ़े में दे दूँ? (lifaafe men de doon?, shall I put it in a bag?)'.

Sense 3 : outward show hiding true nature (idiomatic)
noun, masculine Used mainly in the idiom लिफ़ाफ़ा देख कर मत खत्म करो किताब का अंदाज़ा (lifaafaa dekh kar mat khatm karo kitaab kaa andaazaa, don't judge the book by its envelope/cover), often shortened in casual speech to just criticizing someone as 'सिर्फ़ लिफ़ाफ़ा है (sirf lifaafaa hai, it's just an envelope/facade)'.
Synonyms: दिखावा (dikhaavaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense rides on a popular Hindi couplet-style saying that plays on 'envelope' versus 'letter inside' to mean 'don't judge by appearances' -- a lovely image, since in India an envelope's outer decoration (especially on wedding or festival envelopes) often says nothing about what is actually inside.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may not realize this figurative sense exists outside the fixed idiom -- on its own, लिफ़ाफ़ा (lifaafaa) does not automatically mean 'fake' the way 'facade' does in English; it only carries that sense inside this idiomatic frame.
Sample Sentences:
लिफ़ाफ़ा देखकर मत जाओ, अंदर क्या है ये देखो। Don't go by the outward show, look at what's actually inside.
(lifaafaa dekhkar mat jaao, andar kyaa hai ye dekho।)
