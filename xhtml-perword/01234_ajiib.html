अजीब (ajiib)

adjective An invariant adjective meaning 'strange, odd, weird' -- used constantly in everyday speech about people, situations, feelings, and behavior.
Sense 1 : strange, odd, unusual
adjective
Synonyms: अनोखा (anokhaa), विचित्र (vichitr, formal/literary), निराला (niraalaa, literary), अटपटा (ataptaa, awkward/clumsy-feeling)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: अजीब (ajiib) is one of those words Delhi-wallahs sprinkle into almost any conversation about something that felt 'off' -- अजीब लग रहा है (ajiib lag rahaa hai, 'it feels strange') is a very natural way to voice mild unease or discomfort without being dramatic about it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अजीब (ajiib) when they mean 'surprising' -- but अजीब (ajiib) leans toward 'odd/off' rather than 'amazing,' so अजीब बात है (ajiib baat hai, 'that's a strange thing') carries a slightly uneasy or puzzled tone, not delight. 2) It is invariant -- it does not change to अजीबा (ajiibaa) or अजीबी (ajiibii) for gender or number, unlike the -आ/-ई adjectives learners have already met.

Sense 2 : weird, eccentric (of a person's nature or behavior)
adjective
Synonyms: सनकी (sankii, eccentric/quirky, sharper edge), विचित्र (vichitr, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Said of a person -- अजीब आदमी है (ajiib aadmii hai, 'he's a weird guy') -- this is a common, mildly judgmental way to describe someone whose habits or manner don't fit the expected norm, often said half-affectionately about a quirky relative or colleague.

Sense 3 : used in the fixed expression अजीब सा (a bit odd, a strange sort of)
adjective Pairs with सा (saa) to soften the description -- अजीब सा (ajiib saa) means 'a bit strange' or 'a strange kind of' rather than flatly 'strange,' often used with feelings: अजीब सा एहसास (ajiib saa ehsaas, 'a strange sort of feeling').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: अजीब सा (ajiib saa) is the classic phrase people use to describe a vague, hard-to-pin-down feeling -- a mix of nervousness, sadness, or unease -- exactly the kind of emotional shorthand you'll hear in casual conversation or song lyrics.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop सा (saa) and lose the softened, groping-for-words nuance that native speakers reach for when describing a feeling they can't quite name.
