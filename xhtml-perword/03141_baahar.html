बाहर (baahar)

adverb/adjective Mainly an adverb/postposition-like word meaning 'outside/out', with a related noun-ish use in fixed phrases; one word, one core spatial idea extended in a few directions.
Sense 1 : outside, out (location or direction)
adverb Often combines with से (se) 'from outside' or takes a following noun + की/के (kii/ke) to mean 'outside of X', e.g. घर के बाहर (ghar ke baahar, 'outside the house').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बाहर (baahar) and अंदर (andar) are a natural pair every learner should learn together -- think of a Delhi household where mothers call बाहर मत जाओ (baahar mat jaao, 'don't go outside') to kids playing in the gali. The word carries a strong sense of 'away from the safety/familiarity of home', not just neutral geography.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say घर बाहर (ghar baahar) instead of the correct घर के बाहर (ghar ke baahar) -- बाहर needs के (ke) linking it to the noun it follows, similar to अंदर (andar). 2) Confusing बाहर (static/directional 'outside') with निकलना (nikalnaa, 'to exit/emerge') -- बाहर is where you are or go, not the act of leaving.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : out of town, away (from one's usual place)
adverb Used with होना (honaa) or जाना (jaanaa) to describe someone being away from their home city, e.g. वो बाहर है (vo baahar hai, 'he/she is out of town').
Synonyms: बाहर गया हुआ (baahar gayaa huaa, phrase variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a very everyday use -- when a Dilliwaala says मैं बाहर हूँ (main baahar hoon, 'I'm out of town'), it usually means away from Delhi-NCR, not literally standing outside a building. It is the standard way to explain absence on a phone call.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes default to शहर से बाहर (shahar se baahar) when just बाहर (baahar) alone is idiomatic and sufficient in casual speech to mean 'out of town' or 'traveling', with the city understood from context.

Sense 3 : outside, external, outer (as a modifier before a noun)
adjective As a bare modifier बाहर (baahar) is less common than its derived adjective बाहरी (baahrii); बाहर itself appears attributively mainly in fixed expressions like बाहर वाला (baahar vaalaa, 'the one outside/from outside').
Synonyms: बाहरी (baahrii, adjective form used before nouns)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense is a bridge to its cousin बाहरी (baahrii), which is the true adjective 'external/outsider'. Knowing this link helps explain why people from outside a community are sometimes called बाहरी लोग (baahrii log, 'outsiders').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बाहर (baahar) as a direct adjective before a noun (e.g. incorrectly बाहर आदमी) when बाहरी (baahrii) or a बाहर + वाला (vaalaa) construction is needed instead.
