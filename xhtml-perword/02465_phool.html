फूल (phool)

noun Primarily 'flower', with a common figurative extension to something delicate/precious; a separate, unrelated homophone sense means 'swollen/puffed up' from the verb फूलना (phoolnaa).
Sense 1 : flower, blossom
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पुष्प (pushp, literary), कुसुम (kusum, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the marigold and rose garlands sold outside every Delhi mandir (mandir) -- फूल (phool) is the everyday word you'd use buying them from a thelewaala (thelevaalaa), while पुष्प (pushp) shows up in bhajans and formal writing, not in the market.
Sample Sentences:
बगीचे में बहुत सारे फूल खिले हैं। Many flowers have bloomed in the garden.
(bagiiche men bahut saare phool khile hain।)

Sense 2 : something delicate or precious (figurative, often of a child)
noun, masculine Common in fixed expressions like फूल सा बच्चा (phool saa bachchaa, 'a child as delicate as a flower') and फूलों जैसा (phoolon jaisaa, 'flower-like, tender').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Parents and grandparents love calling a small child मेरा फूल (meraa phool, 'my little flower') -- it carries the same tenderness as English 'sweetheart' but leans specifically on softness and fragility rather than sweetness.
