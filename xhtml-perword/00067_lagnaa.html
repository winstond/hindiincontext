लगना (lagnaa)

verb A hugely versatile intransitive verb built around the core idea of 'contact/attachment' or 'application' -- it branches into senses like to seem/appear, to feel (physical/emotional sensations), to be affixed/installed/posted, to take time or cost, to be struck/hit, and as an auxiliary marking the start of an action (lag gayaa).
Sense 1 : to seem, to appear, to be liked/felt as a subjective impression
verb, intransitive Takes a को-marked experiencer with the thing being judged as grammatical subject: मुझे यह फ़िल्म अच्छी लगी (mujhe yah film achchhii lagii, 'I liked this film' / 'this film seemed good to me').
Synonyms: प्रतीत होना (pratiit honaa, literary/formal), जान पड़ना (jaan padnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This सा (lagnaa) is the verb behind some of the most common small talk in Hindi -- कैसा लगा? (kaisaa lagaa?, 'how did you like it/how did it seem?') is asked constantly after a meal, a movie, a trip. It reflects a broader Hindi habit of framing personal reactions as impressions that arrive at you, rather than opinions you actively hold.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate this too literally as 'to seem' and miss that it is the natural, most common way to say 'I like/liked' something -- मुझे अच्छा लगा (mujhe achchhaa lagaa) is far more idiomatic than a literal 'to like' verb in many contexts. 2) The adjective describing the impression agrees with the subject noun, not with the को-marked experiencer -- learners sometimes wrongly try to make it agree with the person.

(mujhe yah philm bahut achchhii lagii।)

Sense 2 : to feel a physical or emotional sensation (hunger, thirst, cold, fear, etc.)
verb, intransitive The experiencer takes को, and a sensation noun (भूख, ठंड, डर, प्यास) is the grammatical subject: मुझे भूख लगती है (mujhe bhookh lagtii hai, 'I feel hungry'), उसे ठंड लग रही है (use thand lag rahii hai, 'she is feeling cold'), बच्चे को डर लगता है (bachche ko dar lagtaa hai, 'the child feels scared').
Synonyms: महसूस होना (mahsoos honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi treats hunger, cold, and fear as things that 'arrive' or 'attach themselves' to you rather than states you actively enter -- so a mother might ask a child तुम्हें भूख लगी है? (tumhen bhookh lagii hai?, 'are you hungry?', literally 'has hunger attached to you?'). This is the same dative-experiencer logic as लगना's 'seem' sense, but the two families of sentences look and feel completely different in practice.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make the person the subject as in English ('I feel hungry') and forget the को-marking with the sensation noun as the real subject. 2) This sense is easily confused with the separate 'to seem/to be liked' sense (मुझे अच्छा लगता है, mujhe achchhaa lagtaa hai) since both use को...लगना -- but here the subject is always a specific sensation noun (भूख, प्यास, ठंड, डर, गर्मी), not a general adjective of quality.

Sense 3 : to be affixed, applied, installed, put up, or posted/appointed
verb, intransitive Covers things being physically attached or put in place -- ताला लगा है (taalaa lagaa hai, 'a lock is on/attached'), पोस्टर लगा है (postar lagaa hai, 'a poster has been put up'), पेड़ लगा है (ped lagaa hai, 'a tree has been planted') -- and by extension a person being placed into a job or post: उसकी नौकरी लग गई (uskii naukrii lag gaii, 'she got a job/was appointed').
Synonyms: जड़ना (jadnaa, more about embedding/studding), स्थापित होना (sthaapit honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same root idea as आग लगना (aag lagnaa, 'to catch fire') -- fire is imagined as 'attaching itself' to something, which is why that expression uses लगना rather than a verb for burning. You will also hear this sense in everyday phrases like मेरा मन लग गया (meraa man lag gayaa, 'I've settled in/started enjoying it'), where one's mind is described as having 'attached' to a place or activity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for लगाना (lagaanaa) (the transitive 'to affix/apply') when they actually want the intransitive result-state लगना, e.g. describing a poster as already up (पोस्टर लगा है) rather than the act of putting it up. 2) नौकरी लगना (naukrii lagnaa) surprises learners expecting a separate verb for 'getting a job' -- it is simply this same placement sense extended to careers/postings.

Sense 4 : to take (time, money, effort) -- as a cost or requirement
verb, intransitive The quantity (time, money) is the grammatical subject and the person incurring it is को-marked: मुझे दो घंटे लगे (mujhe do ghante lage, 'it took me two hours'), कितना पैसा लगेगा? (kitnaa paisaa lagegaa?, 'how much money will it take/cost?').
Synonyms: खर्च होना (kharch honaa, for money specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Asking कितना लगेगा? (kitnaa lagegaa?, 'how much will it take/cost?') is a everyday bargaining phrase you will hear in autos and markets across Delhi, where लगेगा stands in comfortably for both money and time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use लेना (lenaa) by analogy with 'it takes' but लगना is what natives actually use for durations and costs. 2) The verb agrees with the time/money noun (often plural, e.g. घंटे लगे) not with the person, which can trip up learners used to English word order.

Sense 5 : to be struck, hit, or sustain an injury/impact
verb, intransitive Used both for a blow physically landing (गेंद उसे लगी, gend use lagii, 'the ball hit him') and, as a fixed collocation, चोट लगना (chot lagnaa, 'to get hurt/injured') -- the को-marked person is the one struck or injured.
Synonyms: टकराना (takraanaa, more about collision), चोट लगना (chot lagnaa, fixed phrase for 'to be injured')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will often hear concerned questions like तुम्हें चोट तो नहीं लगी? (tumhen chot to nahiin lagii?, 'you didn't get hurt, did you?') after any small stumble -- a very natural, caring register of everyday Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with the transitive मारना (maarnaa) ('to hit someone') -- लगना describes the impact landing on someone, not the act of striking them. 2) चोट लगना (chot lagnaa) is so fixed a phrase that learners should learn it as a unit ('to get injured') rather than building it word by word.

Sense 6 : auxiliary marking the onset/start of an action (with another verb's infinitive stem)
verb, intransitive Combines with the oblique infinitive of another verb to show an action beginning: बारिश होने लगी (baarish hone lagii, 'it started raining'), वह रोने लगा (vah rone lagaa, 'he started crying').
Synonyms: शुरू होना (shuroo honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This construction is everywhere in storytelling and everyday narration -- अचानक बारिश होने लगी (achaanak baarish hone lagii, 'suddenly it started to rain') is the kind of line you will hear in casual accounts of a day out gone slightly wrong.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa) in every context, not realizing V-ने लगना (lagnaa) is the far more natural, idiomatic way to express 'started doing X' for spontaneous or involuntary onsets. 2) The subject and main verb still follow normal agreement rules for लगना itself (intransitive, no ने), which can confuse learners who expect the ने-construction to appear here since the embedded verb feels 'active'.
Sample Sentences:

(achaanak baarish hone lagii।)
