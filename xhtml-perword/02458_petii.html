पेटी (petii)

noun Mainly a small box/case/trunk (as used for storage or luggage), and by extension a colloquial slang term for 'one lakh rupees' in money-talk. A related but distinct sense refers to the bellows-box of a harmonium.
Sense 1 : box, case, trunk
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बक्सा (baksaa), संदूक (sandook)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the metal or wooden पेटी (petii) that families keep on the topmost shelf of an almirah, packed with old letters, jewelry, or winter quilts -- it carries a slightly old-fashioned, household warmth that 'box' in English doesn't quite have.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पेटी (petii) with पेट (pet, 'stomach') because of the shared root -- पेटी (petii) is unrelated in everyday meaning and refers to a container, not the body part.

Sense 2 : one lakh rupees (slang)
noun, feminine Used in casual money-talk, often in business, real estate, or bargaining contexts: दो पेटी (do petii, 'two lakh rupees').
Synonyms: लाख (laakh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in markets, property deals, or over chai when someone is talking price in hushed, insider tones -- it's the kind of number-slang that signals you're 'in the know,' much like saying 'a grand' for a thousand in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) This slang sense only applies to money contexts -- calling an actual box a पेटी (petii) is completely normal and unrelated to the money slang, so learners shouldn't read financial meaning into every use.

Sense 3 : bellows of a harmonium
noun, feminine
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: If you've ever watched someone play a harmonium at a bhajan session or in a music class, the हाथ (haath, 'hand') pumping the side is working the पेटी (petii) -- musicians sometimes even call the whole harmonium पेटी (petii) informally, the way English speakers might say 'the box' for a guitar case.
