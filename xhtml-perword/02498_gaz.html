गज़ (gaz)

noun A unit of length (roughly a yard, used in cloth and land measurement) and, separately, the word for the elephant in some poetic/formal registers. The two are unrelated in origin and meaning.
Sense 1 : unit of length (yard)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask for cloth in a Delhi market and the tailor or shopkeeper will still often quote in गज़ (gaz) rather than meters, especially for things like pardaa fabric or sari lengths -- it is a unit that has outlived the official switch to the metric system. You will also meet it in older land-deed language, where plot sizes are sometimes given in square gaz.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners assume गज़ (gaz) is metric and equals a meter -- it is closer to a yard (about 0.9 meters), a holdover from British-era measurement still used by cloth sellers and in older property documents. 2) In everyday shopping for fabric, you will hear सवा गज़ (savaa gaz, 'one and a quarter yards') and डेढ़ गज़ (dedh gaz, 'one and a half yards') as fixed quantity phrases, not built compositionally.

Sense 2 : elephant (literary/poetic)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हाथी (haathii), हस्ती (hastii, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense of गज़ (gaz) comes from Sanskrit gaja and survives mainly in formal or literary contexts -- compound names, place names, or old-style writing -- rather than in everyday conversation, where हाथी (haathii) is what people actually say.
