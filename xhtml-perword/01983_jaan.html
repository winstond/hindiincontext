जान (jaan)

noun Primarily 'life, life-force' (जान (jaan)) and, by warm extension, a term of endearment for a beloved person; also appears widely inside idioms about vitality, effort, and danger.
Sense 1 : life, life-force, vitality
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जीवन (jiivan, more formal/literary), प्राण (praan, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear जान है तो जहान है (jaan hai to jahaan hai, 'if there is life, there is the world') as a common piece of folk wisdom about prioritizing health over everything else. जान (jaan) and ज़िंदगी (zindgii) overlap a lot in casual speech, but जान (jaan) leans toward the raw, physical 'life-force' sense, which is why it shows up in so many idioms about danger and effort.
Common Mistakes: 1) जान (jaan) means life as vital force/existence, not 'life' in the sense of a biography or lifetime story -- that's ज़िंदगी (zindgii) or जीवन (jiivan) in most everyday contexts, though the two overlap heavily and are often interchangeable. 2) Learners sometimes miss that जान (jaan) is feminine, which affects adjective and verb agreement.
Sample Sentences:
जान है तो जहान है। If there is life, there is the world.
(jaan hai to jahaan hai।)

Sense 2 : term of endearment (darling, my love, my life)
noun, feminine Used vocatively, often attached to a name or on its own, addressing someone the speaker loves -- romantic partners, but also affectionately with children.
Synonyms: जानम (jaanam, more intimate/poetic), जानू (jaanoo, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मेरी जान (merii jaan, 'my darling') is a classic term of endearment you'll hear constantly in Hindi film songs and dialogues between lovers, and also from parents to small children. It literally frames the beloved as one's own life -- a nice example of Hindi's tendency toward intense, embodied metaphors of affection.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is quite intimate -- using जान (jaan) with a casual acquaintance or colleague would sound odd or presumptuous, unlike English 'dear' which is more neutral. 2) मेरी जान (merii jaan, 'my life/darling') is the most common frame; learners sometimes drop मेरी (merii) and lose some of the warmth.

Sense 3 : effort, energy (as in 'putting one's life into something')
noun, feminine Occurs mainly inside set phrases like जान लगाना (jaan lagaanaa, 'to put one's life/all effort into something') and जान लड़ाना (jaan ladaanaa, 'to strive with everything one has').
Synonyms: मेहनत (mehnat, effort/hard work, broader meaning), ज़ोर (zor, force/strength)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: जान लगा दी (jaan lagaa dii, 'gave it my all') is what a student might say after an exam, or a worker after finishing a big task -- it dramatizes ordinary effort as something life-and-death, which is very characteristic of how Hindi talks about hard work.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a figurative extension of the 'life' sense, but it's so idiom-bound that learners should learn it as part of fixed phrases (जान लगाना (jaan lagaanaa), जान लड़ाना (jaan ladaanaa)) rather than trying to use जान (jaan) alone to mean 'effort.'
