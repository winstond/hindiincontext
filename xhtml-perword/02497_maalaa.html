माला (maalaa)

noun Mainly a string of beads or flowers -- a garland or a rosary -- with a natural extension to any 'string/chain' of similar items, and a name element.
Sense 1 : garland (flowers)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: हार (haar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see माला (maalaa) everywhere in Delhi-NCR life: garlands of marigold (gende kaa phool) for a puja (poojaa), a groom garlanded at his wedding, or a VIP welcomed with a maala at the airport. Politicians famously get garlanded on stage, and 'phoolon ki maala' (phoolon kii maalaa, 'a garland of flowers') is a very common phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse माला (maalaa) with हार (haar) -- माला (maalaa) is used for the flower garlands offered to gods, guests, and at weddings/felicitations, while हार (haar) leans toward jewelry-style neck ornaments (gold, pearl, etc.), though the two overlap in everyday speech.

Sense 2 : rosary, string of beads (for prayer)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जाप माला (jaap maalaa, specific to chanting)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the string of beads used for जाप (jaap, repeated chanting of a mantra or God's name), often made of तुलसी (tulsii, holy basil) or rudraksha seeds. You'll see elders quietly counting beads on a माला (maalaa) while murmuring a mantra -- it is Hindi's equivalent of a rosary or mala beads in yoga/meditation contexts.

Sense 3 : string, chain, series (of similar items)
noun, feminine Used in compounds like शब्दमाला (shabdmaalaa, 'a string/garland of words') or पर्वतमाला (parvatmaalaa, 'mountain range') where माला (maalaa) extends metaphorically to any linear chain of similar things.
Synonyms: श्रृंखला (shrrinkhlaa, formal, used for abstract series/chains)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same 'garland' image stretched to describe any chain of linked things -- a mountain range is literally a 'garland of mountains' (parvatmaalaa) in Hindi, showing how naturally this word extends from flowers to a visual chain of anything similar.
Sample Sentences:
