समझदार (samajhdaar)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'sensible, wise, understanding' -- describing a person's good judgment or maturity.
Sense 1 : sensible, wise, mature in judgment
adjective
Synonyms: अक्लमंद (aklmand), समझदारी वाला (samajhdaarii vaalaa, less common phrasing), बुद्धिमान (buddhimaan, formal), सूझबूझ वाला (soojhboojh vaalaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a word every Indian parent uses constantly -- praising an older sibling for being समझदार (samajhdaar) usually means they behaved responsibly, shared with a younger sibling, or made a mature decision without being told. It carries a warm, approving tone, almost like saying someone 'gets it' or 'has their head on straight.' The root समझ (samajh, understanding) plus दार (daar, a Persian-origin suffix meaning 'possessing') literally builds the word as 'one who possesses understanding.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this where English would say 'smart' in the sense of clever or quick-witted; समझदार (samajhdaar) is about good judgment and maturity, not raw intelligence or cleverness -- for that, चालाक (chaalaak, often has a sly/cunning edge) or तेज़ (tez, sharp/quick) fits better. 2) It is very commonly used to praise children who behave maturely for their age, e.g. calling a child 'the sensible one' -- this usage feels natural in Hindi but can sound oddly formal if translated literally into English.
