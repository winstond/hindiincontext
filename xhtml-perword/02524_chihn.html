चिह्न (chihn)

noun A written/formal word for a mark, sign, or symbol -- something visible that stands for or points to something else, from a small punctuation mark to a national emblem.
Sense 1 : mark, sign, symbol
noun, masculine
Synonyms: निशान (nishaan), संकेत (sanket), प्रतीक (pratiik)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in compounds: विराम चिह्न (viraam chihn, punctuation mark), प्रश्न चिह्न (prashn chihn, question mark), राष्ट्रीय चिह्न (raashtriiy chihn, national emblem). It has a slightly formal, textbook-ish flavor compared to the everyday निशान (nishaan), which is what you'd use for a scar, stain, or a mark left on skin or cloth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for निशान (nishaan) in speech and चिह्न (chihn) in writing without realizing the two overlap heavily -- चिह्न (chihn) leans more formal/written, so it shows up in textbooks, official documents, and compound words rather than casual chat. 2) The स is written with a chandrabindu-less nasal here -- स्पेलिंग is often mis-typed as चिन्ह (chinh); both spellings circulate, but चिह्न (chihn) is the standard form taught in schools.

Sense 2 : symbol/emblem (official or symbolic identity mark)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रतीक (pratiik), निशान (nishaan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is what shows up on election ballots and party flags -- हर पार्टी का अपना चुनाव चिह्न होता है (har paartii kaa apnaa chunaav chihn hotaa hai, 'every party has its own election symbol'). Indian elections are famous for these visual party symbols (a lotus, a hand, a broom), precisely because चिह्न (chihn) lets illiterate voters identify a party without reading its name.
