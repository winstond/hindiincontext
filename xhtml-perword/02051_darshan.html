दर्शन (darshan)

noun Covers two main strands: the everyday/devotional sense of 'seeing' or 'having a sighting of' something revered (a deity, a holy person, a celebrity), and the more formal, learned sense of 'philosophy' as an intellectual discipline. Both trace to the same root, देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see').
Sense 1 : the act of respectfully seeing/viewing a deity, holy person, or revered figure
noun, masculine Very commonly used in the fixed frame X के दर्शन करना (ke darshan karnaa, 'to have/take darshan of X'), where X takes the genitive.
Synonyms: झलक (jhalak, glimpse, less formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word captures something English lacks a single term for: the belief that seeing a deity's image, or a revered guru, is itself a reciprocal, blessing-conferring act -- you see the murti (moorti) and the murti 'sees' you back. You'll hear it constantly outside temples: 'भगवान के दर्शन हो गए' (bhagvaan ke darshan ho gae, 'we got to see/have darshan of God'), and also loosely for a beloved elder or even a star sighting, e.g. 'नेता जी के दर्शन हो गए' (netaa jii ke darshan ho gae, 'we got a glimpse of the leader').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'दर्शन देखना' (darshan dekhnaa) by analogy with 'to see,' but the natural collocation is दर्शन करना (darshan karnaa) or दर्शन होना (darshan honaa), not देखना (dekhnaa). 2) दर्शन (darshan) is grammatically plural/invariant in form even for a single act of seeing -- it is not counted as 'one darshan, two darshans' the way English might.

Sense 2 : philosophy, a system of philosophical thought
noun, masculine Used both for the academic discipline (जीवन दर्शन (jiivan darshan, 'philosophy of life')) and for named schools of Indian philosophy, e.g. वेदांत दर्शन (vedaant darshan, 'the Vedanta school of philosophy').
Synonyms: फ़िलॉसफ़ी (filosfii, English loanword, informal/academic), विचारधारा (vichaardhaaraa, ideology, broader scope)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense in academic or reflective registers: college students talk about पढ़ना दर्शनशास्त्र (darshanshaastr, 'the study of philosophy'), and people describe someone's outlook on life as उसका दर्शन (uskaa darshan, 'his/her philosophy'). It's a more formal, thoughtful register than everyday chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume this sense is interchangeable with the devotional 'sighting' sense just because the spelling is identical -- context (a philosophy classroom vs. a temple) always disambiguates for native speakers, so pay attention to the surrounding words rather than translating word-by-word.

(mainne kolej men darshanshaastr padhaa thaa।)
