अहंकार (ahankaar)

noun A noun meaning ego or arrogance/pride, used both in the everyday sense of an inflated sense of self and in the more philosophical Hindu sense of the ego-principle that separates the self from the divine.
Sense 1 : arrogance, excessive pride
noun, masculine
Synonyms: घमंड (ghamand), गुरूर (guroor), अभिमान (abhimaan)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear a mother or a friend use to scold someone: उसे अपने पैसे का अहंकार है (use apne paise kaa ahankaar hai, 'he is arrogant about his money'). घमंड (ghamand) is its everyday twin and is used almost interchangeably in casual speech, while अहंकार (ahankaar) leans slightly more introspective or moralizing, the kind of word a grandmother or a spiritual guru might use.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use अहंकार (ahankaar) for simple self-confidence; it always carries a negative, critical tone in everyday speech -- it means pride that has gone too far, not healthy self-respect. 2) Don't confuse with आत्मविश्वास (aatmvishvaas), which is the neutral/positive word for 'self-confidence'.

Sense 2 : the ego (as a philosophical/spiritual concept)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अहम् (aham, literary/philosophical), मैं-भाव (main-bhaav, the 'I-feeling')
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: In yogic and Vedantic thought, अहंकार (ahankaar) names the psychological faculty that makes a person feel like a separate 'I' -- the very thing spiritual practice tries to dissolve. You'll meet this sense in discourse on the Bhagavad Gita or in a guru's sermon, less so in daily chit-chat, where the first, more critical sense dominates.
