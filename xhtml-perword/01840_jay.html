जय (jay)

noun/interjection Primarily a noun meaning 'victory,' but its everyday life is as the first half of greeting/hailing formulas like जय हिंद (jay hind, 'victory to India') and as a very common boy's given name.
Sense 1 : victory, triumph
noun, feminine
Synonyms: विजय (vijay), जीत (jiit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जय (jay) rarely stands alone in speech -- it almost always launches a hailing phrase: जय हिंद (jay hind, 'Victory to India' -- a patriotic salutation), जय श्री राम (jay shrii raam, a religious greeting/chant), or जय हो (jay ho, 'may there be victory/glory,' also a well-known song title). Think of it less as a noun you use in daily sentences and more as the opening word of a fixed cheer or blessing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जय (jay) as a general everyday word for 'winning' (as in winning a game or an argument); in ordinary speech जीत (jiit) is what people actually use for that, while जय (jay) stays reserved for slogans, salutations, and elevated/patriotic or devotional contexts.

Sense 2 : hail!, glory to...! (in fixed salutation formulas)
interjection Functions as the fixed first element of a slogan/salutation frame: जय + [name/place] (e.g. जय माता दी (jay maataa dii, 'Glory to the Mother Goddess')).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You hear this pattern everywhere in Delhi-NCR life: political rallies close with जय हिंद (jay hind), temple visits and greetings among the devout use जय श्री कृष्णा (jay shrii krishnaa) or जय माता दी (jay maataa dii), and Sikh greetings use जो बोले सो निहाल, सत श्री अकाल (jo bole so nihaal, sat shrii akaal) with a related spirit though a different word. The formula is a call-and-response of respect, not literal conversation.

Sense 3 : male given name
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: जय (jay) is a hugely popular Hindu boy's name precisely because it means 'victory' -- parents are naming their son 'triumph.' It is also famous as one of the two lead characters (Jai and Veeru) in the classic Bollywood film Sholay (shole), so the name carries a warm, familiar ring for anyone who grew up watching Hindi cinema.
