एक (ek)

numeral/adjective/adverb Primarily the numeral 'one', but एक (ek) also works as an indefinite article ('a/an/a certain'), as an adjective meaning 'single/same', and inside several fixed phrases and conjunctive constructions. The senses below map these uses.
Sense 1 : the number one
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the basic counting word every learner meets on day one, right alongside दो (do) and तीन (tiin) -- it is also the building block for compound numbers like इक्कीस (ikkiis, 'twenty-one'), so getting comfortable with it early pays off down the line.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that एक (ek) itself never takes gender agreement -- it looks the same before masculine and feminine nouns (एक लड़का ek ladkaa, एक लड़की ek ladkii); any agreement happens on the noun/adjective, not on एक (ek) itself.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे पास सिर्फ़ एक रुपया है। I have only one rupee.
(mere paas sirf ek rupyaa hai।)

Sense 2 : a, a certain, some (indefinite article)
adjective Placed directly before the noun, much like English 'a': एक आदमी (ek aadmii, 'a man') or एक दिन (ek din, 'one day/a certain day').
Synonyms: कोई (koii, used for 'some/a certain' with more emphasis on unspecified identity)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind classic story-openers like एक था राजा (ek thaa raajaa, 'once there was a king') -- एक (ek) here functions exactly like English 'a', framing an unspecified but particular person or thing.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers sometimes skip एक (ek) entirely because Hindi doesn't strictly require an article, but leaving it out can lose the 'a certain/some' nuance -- एक दिन (ek din, 'one day, once upon a time') feels noticeably different from just दिन (din, 'day').

एक था राजा। Once there was a king.

Sense 3 : same, single, one and the same
adjective Used attributively to mean 'the same' -- एक बात (ek baat, 'the same/one thing') or हम एक हैं (ham ek hain, 'we are one/united').
Synonyms: समान (samaan, formal), एक जैसा (ek jaisaa, 'alike, similar')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense in emotionally charged lines like हम एक हैं (ham ek hain, 'we are one'), where एक (ek) shifts from counting to expressing unity or sameness -- a natural extension a learner can feel intuitively, much like English 'we are one.'

Sense 4 : once, one time
adverb Often shortened from एक बार (ek baar) in casual speech, especially in requests: एक सुन लो (ek sun lo, 'just listen once').
Synonyms: एक बार (ek baar, more explicit and common phrase form), एक दफ़ा (ek dafaa, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday requests, Delhi speakers often drop बार (baar) and just say एक (ek) -- as in ज़रा एक देख लो (zaraa ek dekh lo, 'just take a look') -- giving the sentence a softer, more casual, cajoling tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may not realize एक (ek) alone can substitute for एक बार (ek baar) in casual imperative requests -- ज़रा एक बोल दो (zaraa ek bol do, 'just say it once') sounds natural to native ears but can seem incomplete to a learner expecting बार (baar) every time.
Sample Sentences:
ज़रा एक देख लो, शायद बात बन जाए। Just take a look once, maybe it'll work out.
(zaraa ek dekh lo, shaayad baat ban jaae।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
