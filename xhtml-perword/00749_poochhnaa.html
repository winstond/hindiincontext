पूछना (poochhnaa)

verb A single core meaning: 'to ask' (a question, or after someone/something) -- the sense splits below only by grammatical frame and by a common idiomatic extension.
Sense 1 : to ask (a question, or ask about/after someone)
verb, transitive Takes से (se) for the person asked: राम से पूछना (raam se poochhnaa, 'to ask Ram'), not को (ko) -- a frequent learner slip since English 'ask him' suggests a direct object.
Synonyms: सवाल करना (savaal karnaa), प्रश्न करना (prashn karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi households, हाल पूछना (haal poochhnaa, 'to ask after someone's well-being') is a small ritual of politeness -- elders often expect you to पूछना before anything else when you visit, and skipping straight to business can seem abrupt.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the person asked with को (ko) on the English model of 'ask him,' but Hindi wants से (se): राम से पूछो (raam se poochho, 'ask Ram'), not राम को पूछो। 2) पूछना (poochhnaa) means 'to ask a question,' not 'to ask for/request' something -- that's माँगना (maangnaa); mixing the two is a common error.

Sense 2 : to care about, pay heed to (usually in negative constructions)
verb, transitive Almost always appears negated: कोई नहीं पूछता (koii nahiin poochhtaa, 'nobody bothers/cares') -- the literal 'ask' sense shades into 'take notice of, value.'
Synonyms: ध्यान देना (dhyaan denaa), फ़िक्र करना (fikr karnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in complaints about being ignored or undervalued: कोई भाव नहीं पूछता (koii bhaav nahiin poochhtaa, 'nobody gives [me/it] any regard') -- a classic Hindi way of expressing wounded pride or neglect, especially about status or attention.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only really surfaces in the negative; learners sometimes try to use it affirmatively to mean 'to care,' which sounds odd -- affirmative 'to care' is better served by ख्याल रखना (khyaal rakhnaa) or फ़िक्र करना (fikr karnaa).
