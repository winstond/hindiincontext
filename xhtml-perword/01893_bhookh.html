भूख (bhookh)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'hunger,' used both for the physical need for food and, figuratively, for a strong craving or drive (e.g. hunger for success).
Sense 1 : physical hunger, appetite for food
noun, feminine Commonly paired with लगना (lagnaa) in the dative-subject frame -- मुझे भूख लगी है (mujhe bhookh lagii hai, 'I am hungry') -- treating hunger as something that arrives rather than a state you are in.
Synonyms: क्षुधा (kshudhaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi mother and she'll tell you भूख (bhookh) is never something you 'have' -- it's something that 'comes' or 'strikes' (लगना (lagnaa)), much like sleep (नींद (niind)) or fear (डर (dar)). This body-as-recipient framing runs through a whole family of Hindi expressions and is one of the first conceptual shifts learners need to make.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I am hungry' with होना (honaa) alone, as in a direct adjective translation, when the natural frame uses भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa) with a को-marked subject. 2) भूखा (bhookhaa) is the adjective 'hungry'; भूख (bhookh) itself is the noun 'hunger' and cannot directly modify a person the way an adjective does.
Sample Sentences:

(mujhe bahut bhookh lagii hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative craving, drive, or hunger for something (success, power, knowledge)
noun, feminine Used with की (kii) to specify the object of the craving, e.g. सत्ता की भूख (sattaa kii bhookh, 'hunger for power').
Synonyms: लालसा (laalsaa), चाह (chaah)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This extended sense shows up constantly in political commentary and film dialogue -- सत्ता की भूख (sattaa kii bhookh) or पैसे की भूख (paise kii bhookh) are stock phrases for describing someone's insatiable ambition, echoing the same 'hunger as a physical drive' logic as the literal sense.
