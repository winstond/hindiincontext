दिल लगना (dil lagnaa)

verb A compound verb built from दिल (dil, 'heart') and लगना (lagnaa, 'to attach/feel'); covers (1) to feel emotionally engaged or interested in something (like a place, activity, or routine), and (2) to fall in love with someone. Both senses treat the heart as attaching itself involuntarily, not as something you control.
Sense 1 : to feel settled/engaged/interested (in a place, activity, or routine)
verb, intransitive Takes a दात-marked (को) experiencer subject: kisii kaa dil kisii jagah/kaam men lagnaa -- 'someone's heart to attach in some place/activity.' The thing one is engaged with is marked with में (men).
Synonyms: मन लगना (man lagnaa), जी लगना (jii lagnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase captures a very Hindi way of talking about mood and belonging: your heart 'attaches' or 'doesn't attach' somewhere, as if it has its own will separate from yours. You'll hear homesick students or new employees say यहाँ दिल नहीं लगता (yahaan dil nahiin lagtaa, 'I don't feel settled here') to a concerned relative on the phone -- it is the standard way to express that vague restless unhappiness of not fitting into a new place.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person the grammatical subject with ने or a plain subject; the experiencer takes का (kaa) possessive marking on दिल, and the verb agrees with दिल, not the person: मेरा दिल नहीं लगता (meraa dil nahiin lagtaa, 'my heart doesn't feel settled'), not मैं दिल नहीं लगता। 2) Confusing this with दिल लगाना (dil lagaanaa), the transitive causative counterpart meaning 'to apply one's heart/attention (to something)' -- लगना here is intransitive and involuntary, not a deliberate act.

Sense 2 : to fall in love (with someone)
verb, intransitive Frame: kisii kaa dil kisii par lagnaa -- the person one falls for is marked with पर (par), while the one falling in love is marked with का (kaa) on दिल, again with दिल as grammatical subject.
Synonyms: प्यार हो जाना (pyaar ho jaanaa), मोहब्बत हो जाना (mohabbat ho jaanaa, slightly more poetic/filmy)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in Bollywood dialogue and lyrics, where दिल (dil) is treated almost as a separate character with its own desires -- लगना here softly says the heart 'got attached' rather than the person 'chose' to love, which fits the broader Hindi pattern of describing emotions as things that happen to you (compare प्यार होना (pyaar honaa) and नींद आना (niind aanaa)).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use को where पर is expected for the person one falls for -- the standard frame is दिल किसी पर लगना (dil kisii par lagnaa), not दिल किसी को लगना. 2) This phrase implies a growing, often one-sided attraction, distinct from the more instantaneous और sudden feel of किसी को किसी से प्यार हो जाना (kisii ko kisii se pyaar ho jaanaa, 'someone to fall in love with someone') -- they overlap but are not perfectly interchangeable in every context.
