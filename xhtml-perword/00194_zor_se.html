ज़ोर से (zor se)

adverb A common adverbial phrase built from ज़ोर (zor, 'force/strength') plus the postposition से (se); covers 'loudly' and 'forcefully/hard', two closely related physical-intensity senses.
Sense 1 : loudly
adverb
Synonyms: ऊँची आवाज़ में (oonchii aavaaz men, 'in a loud voice'), चिल्लाकर (chillaakar, 'by shouting')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly at home and school: बोलो ज़ोर से! (bolo zor se, 'speak up!') is what a teacher or grandparent says to a mumbling kid. It is the default way to tell someone to raise their voice, whether calling across a market or scolding a child.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say तेज़ से (tez se) for 'loudly', but तेज़ (tez) alone (without से (se)) is the natural way to say 'fast/loud' in many contexts -- ज़ोर से (zor se) is the safer, more universally correct choice for volume. 2) Do not confuse with ज़ोर देना (zor denaa, 'to emphasize'), which uses a different construction entirely.
Sample Sentences:
बोलो ज़ोर से, मुझे सुनाई नहीं दे रहा। Speak up loudly, I can't hear you.
(bolo zor se, mujhe sunaaii nahiin de rahaa।)

Sense 2 : forcefully, hard (physical force)
adverb
Synonyms: पूरी ताकत से (poorii taakat se, 'with full strength'), बलपूर्वक (balpoorvak, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase for describing physical impact: a hard push, a slammed door, a tight hug. दरवाज़ा ज़ोर से बंद करो (darvaazaa zor se band karo, 'shut the door hard/firmly') or ज़ोर से पकड़ो (zor se pakdo, 'hold on tight') are everyday parental instructions.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बहुत (bahut, 'very/a lot') where ज़ोर से (zor se) is needed for physical force -- ज़ोर से धक्का दिया (zor se dhakkaa diyaa, 'pushed hard') cannot be replaced with बहुत धक्का दिया (bahut dhakkaa diyaa).
