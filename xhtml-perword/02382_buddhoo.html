बुद्धू (buddhoo)

noun/adjective An affectionate-to-mildly-mocking word for someone acting foolish or silly, most often used as a noun or a direct address.
Sense 1 : a foolish or silly person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), मूर्ख (moorkh, more formal), उल्लू (ulloo, colloquial, 'owl' used to mean fool), गधा (gadhaa, colloquial, 'donkey' used to mean fool)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic word Indian mothers, older siblings, and friends use with a mix of exasperation and affection -- think of someone forgetting their keys for the third time and getting called बुद्धू (buddhoo) with a smile rather than anger. It pairs naturally with a gentle tap on the head or a laugh, unlike sharper insults.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat बुद्धू (buddhoo) as a harsh insult; in most family or friendly contexts it lands closer to 'silly goose' than to a genuine slur, so tone and relationship matter more than the dictionary meaning. 2) As a form of direct address it is often reduplicated or softened with ओ (o buddhoo) -- dropping this can make it sound blunter than intended.

Sense 2 : foolish, silly, dim-witted
adjective
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), मूर्ख (moorkh, more formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used adjectivally it still keeps its warm edge, as in बुद्धू लड़का (buddhoo ladkaa, 'silly boy') -- said the way an aunt might describe a nephew who tripped over his own shoelaces, not as a real condemnation of intelligence.
