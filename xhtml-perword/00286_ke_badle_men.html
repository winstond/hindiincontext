के बदले में (ke badle men)

postposition A postpositional phrase meaning 'in exchange for' or 'instead of'; built from बदला (badlaa, 'exchange/return') plus में (men).
Sense 1 : in exchange for, in return for
postposition Attaches to the oblique form of the preceding noun/pronoun, e.g. उसके बदले में (uske badle men, 'in exchange for that').
Synonyms: एवज़ में (evaz men), की जगह (kii jagah)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in bargaining at a market: पैसों के बदले में सामान (paison ke badle men saamaan, 'goods in exchange for money'). It carries the flavor of a fair trade or transaction, so it fits naturally in contexts of barter, favors, or reciprocity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop में (men) and just say के बदले (ke badle) -- both are correct and common, so this is not an error, but the full form is what beginners are usually taught first. 2) Confusing this with की जगह (kii jagah, 'in place of') -- के बदले में (ke badle men) carries a stronger sense of a trade or transaction, while की जगह (kii jagah) is more neutral substitution.

Sense 2 : instead of, rather than
postposition Used to contrast one action or thing with another, often followed by a clause: पढ़ने के बदले में खेलना (padhne ke badle men khelnaa, 'playing instead of studying').
Synonyms: की जगह (kii jagah), बजाय (bajaay)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shares its root with the 'exchange' sense above -- Hindi treats 'doing X instead of Y' as a kind of conceptual trade, which is a nice window into how the language frames choices as exchanges.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners overuse this for simple substitution where बजाय (bajaay) or की जगह (kii jagah) would sound more natural -- के बदले में (ke badle men) still faintly implies a trade-off or comparison, not pure replacement.
