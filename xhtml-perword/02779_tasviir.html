तस्वीर (tasviir)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'picture' -- covers photographs, drawings, and paintings, plus a figurative sense of an overall 'picture' or 'situation.'
Sense 1 : photograph, picture, image
noun, feminine
Synonyms: फ़ोटो (foto), चित्र (chitr, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, तस्वीर (tasviir) covers everything from a phone photo to a framed painting on the wall -- English forces you to pick 'photo' vs 'picture' vs 'painting,' but Hindi is happy to stay general unless you need to specify with फ़ोटो (foto) or पेंटिंग (penting).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for फ़ोटो (foto) when speaking; तस्वीर (tasviir) is just as common and works for both photos and drawings/paintings, so overusing फ़ोटो (foto) can sound narrower than a native speaker would be. 2) The verb of choice is खींचना (khiinchnaa, to pull/draw) for 'to take a picture' -- तस्वीर लेना (tasviir lenaa) is understood but तस्वीर खींचना (tasviir khiinchnaa) is the more native phrasing.
Sample Sentences:

(yah tasviir bahut puraanii hai।)

Sense 2 : overall picture, situation, state of affairs (figurative)
noun, feminine Used the way English says 'the picture is clear now' or 'paints a picture' -- तस्वीर साफ़ होना (tasviir saaf honaa, for the picture/situation to become clear) is a very natural fixed pairing.
Synonyms: स्थिति (sthiti), हालत (haalat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use mirrors English closely -- 'the picture is becoming clear' -- so it is an easy sense for learners to pick up and start using confidently in discussions about news, politics, or personal situations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes default to स्थिति (sthiti) or हालत (haalat) for 'situation' and miss that तस्वीर (tasviir) is the more vivid, idiomatic choice when describing how a situation looks or is emerging, e.g. in news or conversation about events unfolding.
