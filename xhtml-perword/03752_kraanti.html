क्रांति (kraanti)

noun A single core meaning -- 'revolution' -- used both literally (political upheaval) and figuratively (a sweeping transformation in any field).
Sense 1 : political/social revolution
noun, feminine
Synonyms: विद्रोह (vidroh, rebellion/revolt, narrower), बगावत (bagaavat, uprising, narrower), उलटफेर (ulatpher, upheaval)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Hindi textbooks use for big historical turning points -- फ्रांसीसी क्रांति (phraansiisii kraanti, 'French Revolution'), भारतीय स्वतंत्रता संग्राम (bhaartiiy svatantrtaa sangraam, 'Indian independence movement')-era rhetoric, and slogans like इंकलाब ज़िंदाबाद (inklaab zindaabaad, 'long live the revolution') -- इंकलाब (inklaab) is the more street/political-slogan synonym, while क्रांति (kraanti) is the standard, textbook-neutral term.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use क्रांति (kraanti) where विद्रोह (vidroh) or बगावत (bagaavat) fits better -- क्रांति (kraanti) implies a large-scale, often successful, structural change, not just an act of defiance or a single revolt.

Sense 2 : a sweeping transformation or breakthrough (figurative)
noun, feminine Commonly used in compound noun phrases naming a specific field of change, e.g. हरित क्रांति (harit kraanti, 'Green Revolution'), औद्योगिक क्रांति (audyogik kraanti, 'Industrial Revolution'), डिजिटल क्रांति (dijital kraanti, 'digital revolution').
Synonyms: परिवर्तन (parivartan, transformation, broader), बदलाव (badlaav, change, broader and more neutral)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indians grow up hearing हरित क्रांति (harit kraanti) in school -- the 1960s-70s agricultural transformation that made India food-self-sufficient -- so this figurative sense feels every bit as concrete and real to a Hindi speaker as the political one, not a watered-down metaphor.
