रोग (rog)

noun A noun meaning disease or illness in a fairly formal/medical register, plus a common figurative extension where रोग (rog) means a bad habit or obsession someone is 'afflicted' with.
Sense 1 : disease, illness (medical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बीमारी (biimaarii), व्याधि (vyaadhi, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: रोग (rog) has a slightly more formal, almost textbook flavor compared to बीमारी (biimaarii) -- you'll see it constantly in hospital signage, health campaigns, and Ayurveda contexts (रोग और उपचार, rog aur upchaar, 'disease and treatment' is a classic pairing), while बीमारी (biimaarii) is what you say casually about being sick at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for बीमारी (biimaarii) in speech; रोग (rog) is the word you will meet on medicine labels, in doctors' speech, and in compound words like हृदय रोग (hriday rog, 'heart disease'), so recognizing it passively matters even if बीमारी (biimaarii) is your default active choice.
Sample Sentences:
हृदय रोग आज के समय की सबसे बड़ी समस्याओं में से एक है। Heart disease is one of the biggest problems of today.
(hriday rog aaj ke samay kii sabse badii samasyaaon men se ek hai।)

Sense 2 : a bad habit, obsession, or compulsive fixation (figurative)
noun, masculine Used with होना (honaa) or लगना (lagnaa) to describe someone being 'afflicted' with a habit, e.g. उसे किताबें जमा करने का रोग है (use kitaaben jamaa karne kaa rog hai, 'he has an obsession with collecting books').
Synonyms: लत (lat, more common for addiction specifically), आदत (aadat, neutral habit, weaker sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same conceptual move as calling something 'a disease' in English slang, but it's more natural and common in Hindi -- you'll hear people jokingly say things like उसे मोबाइल का रोग लग गया है (use mobaail kaa rog lag gayaa hai, 'he's gotten addicted to his phone') without any real medical implication, just gentle teasing.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense frames a habit as something that happens to a person, not something they choose -- so it pairs naturally with को (ko) constructions like उसे ... का रोग है (use ... kaa rog hai) rather than वह ... करता है (vah ... kartaa hai), mirroring how illness itself is talked about in Hindi.
