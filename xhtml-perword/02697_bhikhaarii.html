भिखारी (bhikhaarii)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'beggar'; related to the verb माँगना (maangnaa, 'to ask/beg for') and the abstract noun भीख (bhiikh, 'alms, begging').
Sense 1 : beggar
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: फ़कीर (fakiir, can also mean 'mendicant/ascetic'), मंगता (mangtaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word constantly at traffic signals and outside temples in Delhi-NCR, often paired with the common sight of someone tapping on a car window; भीख माँगना (bhiikh maangnaa, 'to beg for alms') is the standard verb phrase built from the same root. Calling someone भिखारी (bhikhaarii) can also be used loosely and a bit harshly as an insult implying someone is being cheap or shameless, e.g. in an argument -- so tone matters.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse भिखारी (bhikhaarii) with फ़कीर (fakiir) -- फ़कीर (fakiir) often carries a spiritual or Sufi-ascetic connotation and isn't always a pure synonym for a street beggar, even though the two overlap in casual speech. 2) The related verb is माँगना (maangnaa, 'to ask for/beg'), not भीखना -- there is no common verb भीखना in everyday use, so learners shouldn't try to back-form one from भिखारी (bhikhaarii).
