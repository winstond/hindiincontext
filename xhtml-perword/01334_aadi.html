आदि (aadi)

adjective/noun Mainly a written/spoken-formal word meaning 'etcetera, and so on' used after a list; also has a literary noun sense meaning 'beginning, origin.'
Sense 1 : and so on, etcetera
adjective Placed directly after the last item in a list, with no comma needed before it: सेब, केला आदि (seb, kelaa aadi, 'apples, bananas, etc.').
Synonyms: वगैरह (vagairah), इत्यादि (ityaadi, one-word variant combining इत्+आदि)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see आदि (aadi) constantly in written Hindi, forms, notices, and news, e.g. फल, सब्ज़ी आदि (phal, sabzii aadi, 'fruits, vegetables, etc.'); in casual speech, वगैरह (vagairah) is what people actually say more often at the sabzi mandi or dinner table.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often add एवं (evan) or और (aur) before आदि (aadi), but native speakers just tack it on directly after the last list item with no connector. 2) आदि (aadi) suggests the list could continue in a similar vein, whereas वगैरह (vagairah) can sound slightly more casual or dismissive -- the two are close but not perfectly interchangeable in tone.

(baazaar men phal, sabzii aadi sab kuchh mil jaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : beginning, origin
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शुरुआत (shuruaat), प्रारंभ (praarambh, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This is the older, more literary sense behind the etc.-usage: आदि (aadi) originally means 'the first, the origin,' as in the phrase आदि से अंत तक (aadi se ant tak, 'from beginning to end'). You will meet it in religious or philosophical contexts, like आदि पुरुष (aadi purush, 'the primal/original being'), more than in daily chatter.
