साक्षात्कार (saakshaatkaar)

noun A formal word covering two related meanings: a job/media 'interview' and, in a more literary sense, a direct personal 'encounter' or 'realization' (as in a face-to-face meeting or spiritual insight).
Sense 1 : interview (job, media, etc.)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: इंटरव्यू (intravyoo, common in casual speech)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this word plastered on college notice boards and government job advertisements -- साक्षात्कार की तारीख़ (saakshaatkaar kii taariikh, 'interview date') is a very common official phrase. In everyday chatter among friends, though, most people just say इंटरव्यू (intravyoo).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to the English loanword इंटरव्यू (intravyoo) in speech, which is perfectly natural, but साक्षात्कार (saakshaatkaar) is the word you'll see in newspapers, official notices, and formal writing -- knowing both lets you read as well as speak. 2) The verb usually paired with this noun is लेना (lenaa) for the interviewer ('to take an interview') and देना (denaa) for the candidate ('to give an interview'), not करना (karnaa).

Sense 2 : direct encounter, face-to-face realization
noun, masculine Often appears with करना (karnaa) in the sense 'to come face to face with' or 'to directly perceive/realize' something -- ईश्वर का साक्षात्कार (iishvar kaa saakshaatkaar, 'direct realization of God') is a set phrase in spiritual/philosophical writing.
Synonyms: अनुभव (anubhav, broader meaning, everyday experience), दर्शन (darshan, literary, especially spiritual)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense grows from the same root idea as the interview meaning -- सा-क्षात् (saakshaat) means 'in person, right before one's eyes' -- so both senses share the core notion of a direct, face-to-face confrontation with something, whether it's a hiring panel or a deep truth. You'll meet this usage mostly in spiritual discourses, philosophy texts, or serious essays rather than daily conversation.
