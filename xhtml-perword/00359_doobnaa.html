डूबना (doobnaa)

verb A single-root verb built around 'sinking/drowning', extending naturally from literal immersion to being financially wiped out to the sun/moon 'setting'. Related causative: डुबाना (dubaanaa).
Sense 1 : to drown, to sink
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: गोता खाना (gotaa khaanaa, to take a dip/plunge)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you hear in monsoon-season news reports about नाव (naav, boats) or इलाके (ilaake, neighborhoods) डूबना (doobnaa) during floods in Delhi-NCR when the यमुना (yamunaa, Yamuna river) rises every year.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बूड़ना (boodnaa) or confuse it with तैरना (tairnaa, 'to swim') since both involve water -- डूबना (doobnaa) specifically means going under, the opposite of staying afloat. 2) The thing that sinks takes नहीं/is the subject directly -- there is no ने (ne) marking since डूबना (doobnaa) is intransitive, e.g. नाव डूब गई (naav doob gaii, 'the boat sank').

(har saal barsaat men yamunaa kaa paanii badhtaa hai aur kaii ilaake doob jaate hain।)

Sense 2 : to be financially ruined, to be lost (money, investment)
verb, intransitive Common with पैसा (paisaa), पैसे (paise), or रुपया (rupyaa) as subject: पैसा डूब गया (paisaa doob gayaa, 'the money was lost/sunk').
Synonyms: बर्बाद होना (barbaad honaa, to be ruined), स्वाहा होना (svaahaa honaa, colloquial, 'to go up in smoke')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Very natural in everyday talk about business or investments -- a shopkeeper might say मेरे पैसे डूब गए (mere paise doob gae, 'my money went down the drain') the same way an English speaker says money was 'sunk' into something, showing the metaphor overlaps nicely between the two languages.

Sense 3 : to set (of sun, moon, stars)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: अस्त होना (ast honaa, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Said constantly and casually -- सूरज डूब रहा है (sooraj doob rahaa hai, 'the sun is setting') is how most people actually talk about evening, saved for poetry only in its अस्त (ast) form.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar with English 'the sun sets' may not expect the same 'sinking' verb used for water to apply here -- सूरज डूब गया (sooraj doob gayaa) is the everyday way to say 'the sun set', while अस्त होना (ast honaa) sounds more bookish.
Sample Sentences:
सूरज डूब रहा है, चलो छत पर चलते हैं। The sun is setting, let's go to the roof.

Sense 4 : to be absorbed/lost (in thought, sorrow, work)
verb, intransitive Takes में (men): ख्यालों में डूबना (khyaalon men doobnaa, 'to be lost in thoughts').
Synonyms: खोया रहना (khoyaa rahnaa, to remain lost in), मगन होना (magan honaa, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same body-and-mind imagery English uses ('drowning in sorrow', 'lost in thought'), so it tends to feel intuitive -- you will hear ग़म में डूबना (gham men doobnaa, 'to be drowned in grief') often in songs and everyday sympathetic talk alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is figurative, so learners may try to translate 'drown in thought' literally with a different verb; डूबना (doobnaa) itself carries that exact metaphor naturally in Hindi too.
