मायने (maayne)

noun A single word meaning 'meaning, significance' -- used almost always in the plural-looking form मायने (maayne) even when referring to one meaning, most commonly in the fixed phrase मायने रखना (maayne rakhnaa, 'to matter').
Sense 1 : meaning, significance, sense
noun, masculine Grammatically plural in form and agreement (takes plural verb/adjective agreement) even when referring to the meaning of a single word or statement; there is no commonly used singular counterpart in this sense.
Synonyms: मतलब (matlab), अर्थ (arth)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the phrase इसके मायने हैं (iske maayne hain, 'this means'), often used when someone is drawing out a deeper implication rather than just defining a word -- it has a slightly more reflective, almost philosophical flavor than मतलब (matlab).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat मायने (maayne) as singular and pair it with singular verb forms; native speakers keep it plural: इसके क्या मायने हैं (iske kyaa maayne hain, 'what does this mean'), not इसका क्या मायना है (iskaa kyaa maaynaa hai). 2) मायने (maayne) asks 'what is the significance/sense of this' and overlaps with मतलब (matlab), but मायने (maayne) leans toward deeper or figurative significance rather than plain dictionary meaning.
Sample Sentences:
इसके मायने क्या हैं? What does this mean?
(iske maayne kyaa hain?)

Sense 2 : importance, value, worth (as in 'to matter')
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively as the object of रखना (rakhnaa) in the fixed expression मायने रखना (maayne rakhnaa, 'to matter, to hold significance'), with the subject marked directly (no ने) since रखना (rakhnaa) here behaves idiomatically.
Synonyms: अहमियत (ahmiyat), महत्व (mahatv)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मायने रखना (maayne rakhnaa) is the everyday way to say 'it matters' -- you will hear it in pep talks and arguments alike, e.g. दिल से किया गया काम मायने रखता है (dil se kiyaa gayaa kaam maayne rakhtaa hai, 'work done with heart matters'). It pairs naturally with कोई (koii) in the negative: कोई मायने नहीं रखता (koii maayne nahiin rakhtaa, 'doesn't matter at all').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'it doesn't matter' word-for-word instead of using the set phrase; the natural Hindi is इससे कोई मायने नहीं रखता (isse koii maayne nahiin rakhtaa, 'it doesn't matter') or यह कोई मायने नहीं रखता (yah koii maayne nahiin rakhtaa). 2) The verb here is always रखना (rakhnaa), not होना (honaa) -- 'X मायने है' is not idiomatic in this sense.
