चमकना (chamaknaa)

verb Basic intransitive verb meaning 'to shine, glow, or flash'; also extends figuratively to a person 'shining' or rising to prominence.
Sense 1 : to shine, glow, sparkle (light, stars, metal, etc.)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: जगमगाना (jagamgaanaa, of lights, more sparkly/festive), दमकना (damaknaa, of skin or face, glowing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind बिजली चमकना (bijlii chamaknaa, 'lightning flashes') -- Hindi treats lightning as something that shines/flashes rather than 'strikes', so बादल गरजते हैं और बिजली चमकती है (baadal garajte hain aur bijlii chamaktii hai, 'clouds thunder and lightning flashes') is the natural way to describe a monsoon storm.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for जलना (jalnaa, 'to burn') when they mean 'to shine', but जलना (jalnaa) describes a flame burning or a light being switched on, not the shining quality itself. 2) चमकना (chamaknaa) is intransitive, so despite describing an active-feeling process (a star shining brightly), it never takes ने (ne) in the past tense -- सूरज चमका (sooraj chamkaa, 'the sun shone'), not सूरज ने चमका।

Sense 2 : to shine (figuratively) -- to become famous, do brilliantly, stand out
verb, intransitive Used for people, especially in contexts like exams, sports, or the entertainment industry: फ़िल्म में चमकना (film men chamaknaa, 'to shine in a film').
Synonyms: छा जाना (chhaa jaanaa, to dominate/take over a scene), नाम कमाना (naam kamaanaa, to earn a name/reputation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in the context of Bollywood and cricket -- a commentator might say वह मैच में चमका (vah maich men chamkaa, 'he shone in the match'), praising a standout performance. It mirrors the English idiom of a 'star' shining.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is about visible success or prominence, not general happiness or goodness -- it would sound odd applied to describe someone's character rather than their achievement or public performance.
