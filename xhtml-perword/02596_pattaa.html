पत्ता (pattaa)

noun Mainly 'leaf' as a plant part, but also used for a thin flat sheet-like piece (like a playing card) and in a couple of common idioms about someone disappearing or someone's fortune/game being decided.
Sense 1 : leaf (of a plant or tree)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पत्ती (pattii, more common for small/tea leaves)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture the neem or peepal tree outside a Delhi house shedding पत्ते (patte, 'leaves') all over the courtyard in winter -- this is the everyday image behind the word. It also shows up in common expressions like पत्ता खड़कना (pattaa khadaknaa, 'a leaf to rustle'), used for the slightest sound or movement.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पत्ता (pattaa) with पत्ती (pattii) -- पत्ता (pattaa) is used for a whole leaf on a tree, while पत्ती (pattii) is common for small leaves like tea leaves (चाय की पत्ती (chaay kii pattii, 'tea leaves')) or leafy bits in cooking.

Sense 2 : playing card
noun, masculine Used especially in the plural, पत्ते (patte), for a deck or hand of cards in games like तीन पत्ती (tiin pattii) or रम्मी (rammii).
Synonyms: कार्ड (kaard)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Card games are a huge part of festival gatherings, especially पत्ते खेलना (patte khelnaa, 'to play cards') during Diwali nights with family. The game तीन पत्ती (tiin pattii, 'three cards') literally takes its name from this sense of the word.

Sense 3 : whereabouts, trace (idiomatic, usually with negation)
noun, masculine Almost always appears in the fixed idiom पत्ता न होना (pattaa n honaa, 'to have no trace/clue of') or पत्ता नहीं (pattaa nahiin, 'no idea/no sign'), often about a person's whereabouts.
Synonyms: निशान (nishaan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this when someone vanishes without a word -- a friend says उसका कोई पत्ता नहीं है (uskaa koii pattaa nahiin hai, 'there's no trace of him'), the same everyday frustration as saying someone 'dropped off the map' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) This idiomatic sense only works in negative constructions like किसी का पत्ता न होना (kisii kaa pattaa n honaa, 'to have no trace of someone') -- learners sometimes try to use पत्ता (pattaa) alone to mean 'trace' in a positive sentence, which doesn't sound natural.

Sense 4 : clue, address/contact information
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पता (pataa, often confused but distinct word for 'address/information')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with the separate word पता (pataa), and in fast casual speech the vowel length distinction can blur -- but in writing and careful speech they stay distinct, so it's worth training your ear early.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not confuse पत्ता (pattaa, 'leaf/card') with the very similar-sounding पता (pataa, 'address, information') -- they are different words with different spellings and meanings, though beginners often mix them up in speech.
