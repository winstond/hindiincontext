चूहा (choohaa)

noun A single core meaning: 'mouse' or 'rat', the common rodent. Also extended informally to the computer mouse.
Sense 1 : rodent (mouse/rat)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मूषक (mooshak, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चूहा (choohaa) is inescapable in Indian households and folklore -- from kitchen pest-control talk to the story of the greedy चूहा (choohaa) in Panchatantra tales. It is also the vehicle of Ganesha (ganesh) in Hindu iconography, so you will hear it in temple contexts too, not just as a pest.
Common Mistakes: 1) Hindi does not distinguish 'mouse' from 'rat' the way English does -- चूहा (choohaa) covers both; a bigger one might be called बड़ा चूहा (badaa choohaa, 'big rat') rather than switching words. 2) The feminine form चुहिया (chuhiyaa) is used for a female or a small/young one, not as a free variant -- do not use चूहा (choohaa) and चुहिया (chuhiyaa) interchangeably.

Sense 2 : computer mouse
noun, masculine
Synonyms: माउस (maaus, common English loanword)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is a playful metonymic extension of the animal sense -- the same shape-based logic English uses. You will see चूहा (choohaa) in pure-Hindi computer terminology (textbooks, government forms) but hear माउस (maaus) in ordinary conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday tech speech, the English loanword माउस (maaus) is actually more common than चूहा (choohaa) for the computer device -- using चूहा (choohaa) here can sound like a deliberate, slightly cute Hindi-ification rather than the natural word choice.
