परहेज़ (parahez)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'abstention/restraint,' especially dietary or health-related self-restriction; also used in a common verbal-phrase pattern with करना (karnaa) meaning 'to abstain from.'
Sense 1 : abstention, restriction (esp. dietary)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: संयम (sanyam, self-restraint, broader sense), परहेज़गारी (parahezgaarii, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word comes up constantly at the doctor's or with elders talking about health -- a Delhi grandmother might say ठंडी चीज़ों से परहेज़ करो (thandii chiizon se parahez karo, 'avoid cold things'). It carries a slightly moralized, disciplined flavor, like dietary or lifestyle self-control rather than a neutral 'diet plan.'
Common Mistakes: 1) परहेज़ (parahez) almost always pairs with से (se) for the thing being avoided -- तेल से परहेज़ (tel se parahez, 'abstention from oil') -- learners sometimes try को (ko) here by analogy with other constructions. 2) It is not a general word for 'diet' (the food plan itself is डाइट (daait) or खुराक (khuraak)); परहेज़ (parahez) specifically names what you are avoiding or restricting.
Sample Sentences:
डॉक्टर ने कहा है कि मुझे मीठे से परहेज़ करना चाहिए। The doctor has said that I should abstain from sweets.
(doktar ne kahaa hai ki mujhe miithe se parahez karnaa chaahie।)

Sense 2 : avoidance, keeping away from something (general/behavioral)
noun, masculine Used beyond food/health for general caution or avoidance of a habit, person, or activity, still typically with से (se) and often with करना (karnaa).
Synonyms: दूरी (doorii, distance/keeping away, broader sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this extended sense when someone talks about avoiding bad company or bad habits -- बुरी आदतों से परहेज़ (burii aadton se parahez, 'avoidance of bad habits') -- showing how a word rooted in dietary caution naturally stretches to moral or behavioral self-control.
