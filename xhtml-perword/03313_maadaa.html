मादा (maadaa)

adjective/noun A biological term for 'female' used for animals and plants, contrasted with नर (nar, 'male'); it is not used for humans.
Sense 1 : female (of animals/plants)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear मादा (maadaa) most often at a zoo, on a nature documentary, or from a vet or farmer talking about livestock -- मादा हाथी (maadaa haathii, 'female elephant') or मादा बकरी (maadaa bakrii, 'female goat'). Its pairing word नर (nar) works the same way, so the two are best learned together.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मादा (maadaa) to describe a human woman, which sounds clinical or even rude -- for people, use औरत (aurat) or महिला (mahilaa) instead. 2) मादा (maadaa) is an invariant adjective -- it does not change form for the gender or number of the noun it describes, unlike most Hindi adjectives ending in आ (aa).

Sense 2 : a female (animal)
noun, feminine Used on its own as a noun when the species is already clear from context, e.g. pointing at an animal and asking यह मादा है या नर (yah maadaa hai yaa nar, 'is this a female or a male')?
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This noun use is common in scientific, veterinary, or wildlife contexts -- a museum sign or nature show might simply say मादा (maadaa) under a picture of an animal, letting the label stand in for 'the female one.'
