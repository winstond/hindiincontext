गिरफ़्तार करना (giraftaar karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the Persian-derived adjective गिरफ़्तार (giraftaar, 'arrested/captured') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means 'to arrest.' Only one sense is in everyday use.
Sense 1 : to arrest (someone)
verb, transitive Takes the person arrested as a direct object, usually marked with को (ko) when definite: पुलिस ने चोर को गिरफ़्तार किया (pulis ne chor ko giraftaar kiyaa, 'the police arrested the thief').
Synonyms: पकड़ना (pakadnaa, everyday/informal, 'to catch/nab'), हिरासत में लेना (hiraasat men lenaa, formal/legal phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the standard word you will hear on every Hindi news channel when reporting an arrest -- गिरफ़्तार किया गया (giraftaar kiyaa gayaa, 'was arrested') is practically a fixed news formula. In everyday chatty speech, though, people often reach for the more casual पकड़ना (pakadnaa, 'to catch') instead, so गिरफ़्तार करना (giraftaar karnaa) has a slightly formal, official flavor, like 'to apprehend' next to 'to catch' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat गिरफ़्तार (giraftaar) alone as a verb and try to conjugate it directly -- it is an invariant adjective, and only करना (karnaa) carries the conjugation. 2) In news and legal contexts the passive-feeling गिरफ़्तार किया गया (giraftaar kiyaa gayaa, 'was arrested') is far more common than the active voice, since reports focus on the person arrested rather than the police -- learners often default to active constructions when a passive would sound more natural.
Sample Sentences:

(pulis ne chor ko giraftaar kar liyaa।)
