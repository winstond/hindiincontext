ग़लत (ghalat)

adjective/adverb/interjection A single core sense meaning 'wrong/incorrect,' used as an adjective for facts, actions, and choices, and also very common as a standalone exclamation or short reply.
Sense 1 : wrong, incorrect, mistaken
adjective
Synonyms: ग़लत (ghalat) खराब (kharaab), sometimes used loosely for 'wrong/bad', अशुद्ध (ashuddh, formal, used for language/pronunciation errors)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear यह ग़लत है (yah ghalat hai, 'this is wrong') constantly in Delhi -- from a teacher marking homework to a friend correcting your Hindi to an auto driver disputing a fare. It is a mild, everyday word, not a harsh judgment; calling something ग़लत (ghalat) is much gentler in tone than calling a person बुरा (buraa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बुरा (buraa, 'bad') when they mean 'incorrect' -- ग़लत (ghalat) is about correctness/accuracy, बुरा (buraa) is about moral or emotional quality; मत बताओ ग़लत बात (mat bataao ghalat baat, 'don't say something wrong/incorrect') versus बुरी बात (burii baat, 'a bad/improper thing to say'). 2) Note the nuqta: ग़लत (ghalat) with the dot under ग is standard; dropping it to गलत (galat) is extremely common in casual writing and still perfectly understood.

Sense 2 : wrongly, incorrectly, mistakenly
adverb Functions adverbially in fixed phrases like ग़लत समझना (ghalat samajhnaa, 'to misunderstand') and ग़लत बोलना (ghalat bolnaa, 'to say/pronounce incorrectly'), where it modifies the verb rather than a noun.
Synonyms: ग़लती से (ghaltii se, 'by mistake', often preferred in this adverbial sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आपने मुझे ग़लत समझा (aapne mujhe ghalat samjhaa, 'you misunderstood me') is a classic line in arguments and dramatic Hindi TV moments alike -- ग़लत समझना (ghalat samajhnaa) is the standard, go-to way to say 'to misunderstand' in everyday speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert से (se) after ग़लत (ghalat) by analogy with ग़लती से (ghaltii se), producing the unnatural ग़लत से (ghalat se); in this adverbial sense ग़लत (ghalat) is simply placed right before the verb with no postposition.

Sense 3 : wrong! (as an exclamatory correction or reply)
interjection Used alone as a one-word reaction, often in quiz-like or corrective contexts, similar to shouting 'wrong!' in English.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a game show host or a teacher snapping back ग़लत! (ghalat!) the instant a wrong answer is given -- short, punchy, and instantly understood without needing a full sentence.
