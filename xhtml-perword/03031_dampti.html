दंपति (dampti)

noun A formal, respectful word for 'married couple' or 'husband and wife' -- used in invitations, news reports, and polite address, not in casual chat.
Sense 1 : married couple, husband and wife (as a unit)
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: पति-पत्नी (pati-patnii, everyday), जोड़ा (jodaa, general pair, not necessarily married)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see दंपति (dampti) constantly on wedding invitations, in temple donor lists, and in news headlines like 'दंपति ने बचाई जान' (dampti ne bachaaii jaan, 'the couple saved a life') -- it has a formal, almost official flavor, the kind of word you'd use introducing a couple on stage at a function, not describing your neighbors over chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat दंपति (dampti) like an ordinary countable noun and try to pluralize or gender-mark it the way they would पति (pati) or पत्नी (patnii) individually -- but दंपति (dampti) already refers to the pair as one unit, so it takes verbs and adjectives in ways that can feel unfamiliar until you see it in context (covered in the morphology notes). 2) It is easy to overuse this in casual speech; native speakers reach for पति-पत्नी (pati-patnii) when just chatting about 'the husband and wife next door.'
Sample Sentences:
यह दंपति पिछले तीस साल से इसी मोहल्ले में रहता है। This couple has been living in this neighborhood for the last thirty years.
(yah dampti pichhle tiis saal se isii mohalle men rahtaa hai।)
