ज़ंग (zang)

noun Means 'rust' (metal corrosion), used literally and in a few common figurative extensions about things going idle or stale.
Sense 1 : rust (corrosion on metal)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture an old iron gate or a bicycle chain left out in Delhi's monsoon -- ज़ंग (zang) is exactly that reddish-brown coating. It's a loanword from Persian, common in everyday speech about tools, vehicles, pipes, anything metal.
Common Mistakes: 1) The verb pairing is ज़ंग लगना (zang lagnaa, 'to rust', literally 'for rust to attach') -- learners sometimes reach for होना (honaa) instead, but लगना (lagnaa) is the natural collocation here, just like with भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa) or प्यास लगना (pyaas lagnaa).
Sample Sentences:

(baarish men saaikil kii chen par zang lag gayaa।)

Sense 2 : figurative decay or staleness from disuse
noun, feminine Used in phrases like दिमाग़ पर ज़ंग लगना (dimaagh par zang lagnaa, 'for the mind to grow rusty/stale') -- the same लगना (lagnaa) construction extends metaphorically to skills, relationships, or the mind going idle.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this when someone hasn't spoken English in years and jokes उनकी अंग्रेज़ी पर ज़ंग लग गई है (unkii angrezii par zang lag gaii hai, 'their English has gone rusty') -- the same physical image of metal seizing up from neglect, applied to a skill going stale.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense always needs a body-part or faculty (दिमाग़ (dimaagh, mind), ज़बान (zabaan, tongue/language), रिश्ता (rishtaa, relationship)) as the thing rusting -- ज़ंग (zang) alone doesn't carry the metaphor without that anchor.

(barson se angrezii nahiin bolii, to us par zang lag gaii hai।)
