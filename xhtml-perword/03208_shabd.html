शब्द (shabd)

noun The basic Hindi word for 'word' or 'sound' -- one main sense in everyday use, with a secondary literary/philosophical shading toward 'sound' itself.
Sense 1 : word (a unit of language)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: लफ़्ज़ (lafz, everyday, common in speech), वाक्यांश (vaakyaansh, phrase, narrower)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet शब्द (shabd) constantly in school contexts -- शब्दकोश (shabdkosh, 'dictionary,' literally 'treasury of words') and शब्दावली (shabdaavlii, 'vocabulary') both build on it, so learning this root pays off across many compound nouns.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use शब्द (shabd) where बात (baat) is meant -- शब्द (shabd) is a single lexical word, while बात (baat) covers a remark, matter, or thing said; 'don't say such things' is ऐसी बातें मत कहो (aisii baaten mat kaho, 'don't say such things'), not ऐसे शब्द (aise shabd).

Sense 2 : sound, noise (literary/philosophical sense)
noun, masculine This older sense of शब्द (shabd) as 'sound' survives mainly in classical/philosophical registers (e.g. Sanskrit-derived discussions of sound as a sense-object) and in some fixed compounds; in ordinary conversation आवाज़ (aavaaz) is what people actually say for 'sound' or 'noise.'
Synonyms: आवाज़ (aavaaz, everyday word for sound/noise), ध्वनि (dhvani, more formal/technical)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: In Indian philosophy, शब्द (shabd) is one of the five sense-objects (alongside sight, touch, taste, smell) tied to the ear -- you might encounter this in a yoga or Ayurveda class rather than in daily chat.
