देना (denaa)

verb देना (denaa) is one of Hindi's most essential verbs: at its core it means 'to give', but it also functions as a permissive auxiliary ('to let'), as a completive/benefactive vector verb attached to other verb stems, and in fixed collocations for undergoing exams, interviews, and auditions.
Sense 1 : to give
verb, transitive Extends constantly beyond physical objects into fixed noun+देना collocations: जवाब देना (javaab denaa, 'to answer'), सलाह देना (salaah denaa, 'to advise'), ध्यान देना (dhyaan denaa, 'to pay attention'), इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa, 'to give permission').
Synonyms: सौंपना (saumpnaa, to hand over/entrust), प्रदान करना (pradaan karnaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: देना pairs constantly with abstract nouns in everyday speech, and its reciprocal pairing with लेना (lenaa) shows up in the idiom लेना-देना (lenaa-denaa) -- as in इससे मेरा कोई लेना-देना नहीं (isse meraa koii lenaa-denaa nahiin, 'I have nothing to do with this'), one of the most common ways to disclaim involvement.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse देना (denaa, direction away from the subject) with मिलना (milnaa, to receive/get) -- मुझे किताब मिली (mujhe kitaab milii, 'I got the book') is not the same event described from the other side as मैंने किताब दी (mainne kitaab dii, 'I gave the book'). 2) With abstract objects, learners often try to translate word-for-word instead of learning the collocation -- सलाह देना (salaah denaa) is simply 'to advise,' not literally 'to give a consultation.'

Sense 2 : completive/benefactive vector verb (V-stem + देना)
verb, transitive Attaches to the stem of a main verb (V + देना) to add a sense of finality or completion, often directed outward or performed for someone else's benefit -- कह देना (kah denaa, 'to just say it / say it once and for all'), बता देना (bataa denaa, 'to inform someone fully'), लिख देना (likh denaa, 'to write it out for someone').
Synonyms: डालना (daalnaa, similar completive vector, but implies abruptness or force)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers reach for V+देना constantly to signal an action is complete and often oriented toward another person -- पापा को बता देना (paapaa ko bataa denaa, 'be sure to tell dad') -- which contrasts with the self-oriented V+लेना vector (ले लेना (le lenaa)); noticing this direction is a big step toward native-sounding speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often skip this vector entirely, saying कहा (kahaa) where a native speaker would say कह दिया (kah diyaa), losing the sense of finality the narration expects. 2) The vector frequently implies the action was done for someone else or to get it settled -- बता देना (bataa denaa) suggests informing someone fully/for their sake, not merely 'telling' in passing.

Sense 3 : to let, to allow (permissive construction)
verb, transitive Built on the frame [Verb-ने + देना]: the person being permitted appears as a को-marked object, and the verb to be permitted takes the oblique infinitive -ने -- मुझे जाने दो (mujhe jaane do, 'let me go'), उसे बोलने दो (use bolne do, 'let him speak').
Synonyms: इजाज़त देना (ijaazat denaa, more formal-sounding phrase for the same idea)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This permissive frame shows up constantly in daily negotiation and parenting. जाने दो (jaane do) is also used idiomatically on its own to mean 'never mind, let it go/drop it,' well beyond its literal sense of physically letting someone go.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the -ने and say मुझे जाना दो (mujhe jaanaa do), which is ungrammatical -- the permissive frame always needs the oblique infinitive, जाने (jaane) not जाना (jaanaa). 2) The को-marking here (मुझे, उसे) marks the person granted permission, which learners can confuse with को as a physical recipient in the plain 'to give' sense.

Sense 4 : to undergo/submit to a formal assessment or presentation
verb, transitive Used with nouns naming a formal assessment or presentation the subject submits to -- परीक्षा देना (pariikshaa denaa, 'to take an exam'), इंटरव्यू देना (intravyoo denaa, 'to attend/give an interview'), ऑडिशन देना (odishan denaa, 'to audition'). Hindi consistently uses देना here even where English says 'take.'
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In India's exam-heavy school culture, परीक्षा देना (pariikshaa denaa) is heard everywhere each exam season; the same grammatical logic -- the subject submits, देना marks it -- carries naturally into adult life with इंटरव्यू देना (intravyoo denaa) during job-hunting season.
Common Mistakes: 1) English speakers instinctively reach for लेना (lenaa, 'to take') for exams, but Hindi uses देना (denaa) from the test-taker's side -- परीक्षा देना (pariikshaa denaa), not परीक्षा लेना (pariikshaa lenaa) (the latter means the examiner administers the exam). 2) This pattern is not limited to exams -- इंटरव्यू देना (intravyoo denaa) and ऑडिशन देना (odishan denaa) follow the identical logic, so it should be learned as a productive pattern, not a single fixed idiom.
