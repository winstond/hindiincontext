शागिर्द (shaagird)

noun A single sense: a disciple or apprentice learning a craft, trade, or art under a master. Persian loanword, common in traditional guru-shagird contexts.
Sense 1 : disciple, apprentice, pupil (of a craft/art/trade master)
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: चेला (chelaa), शिष्य (shishy, formal), विद्यार्थी (vidyaarthii, scope: student more generally)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word comes straight from Persian and carries the flavor of the ustaad-shagird (ustaad-shaagird) tradition -- the old apprenticeship model in Hindustani classical music, dance, cooking, and crafts, where a student lives and trains closely under one master for years. It sits alongside गुरु-शिष्य (guru-shishy), the more Sanskritic/spiritual version of the same idea; शागिर्द (shaagird) leans toward the Mughal-courtly, artisanal, and musical world rather than temple or ashram settings.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use शागिर्द (shaagird) interchangeably with छात्र (chhaatr, 'student') -- but शागिर्द (shaagird) is specifically for someone learning a skill, craft, or art hands-on under a master (ustaad, ustaad), like music, cooking, or a trade, not a school pupil. 2) The relationship word उस्ताद-शागिर्द (ustaad-shaagird, 'master-disciple') is a fixed pairing; using शागिर्द (shaagird) alone without this master-apprentice frame in mind can sound oddly formal or old-fashioned in casual speech.
