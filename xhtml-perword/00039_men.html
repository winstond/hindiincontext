में (men)

postposition में (men) is Hindi's default word for 'in': it marks physical location, time-until-an-event, and membership 'among' a group -- three related but distinct jobs covered as separate senses below.
Sense 1 : in, inside (location or containment)
postposition
Synonyms: के अंदर (ke andar), के भीतर (ke bhiitar, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi treats the mind and heart as containers just like a room, so you'll hear मन में (man men, 'in the mind') and दिल में (dil men, 'in the heart') constantly for feelings and thoughts -- 'in my heart, I felt sad' rather than 'I felt sad'. Everyday location talk leans almost entirely on this one small word: घर में (ghar men, 'in the house'), दिल्ली में (dillii men, 'in Delhi').
Common Mistakes: 1) Confusing में (men) with पर (par): में (men) is 'in/inside' a container or space, पर (par) is 'on' a surface -- मेज़ पर (mez par, 'on the table') vs मेज़ के अंदर (mez ke andar, 'inside/under the table'). 2) में (men) alone is the unmarked, everyday way to say 'in'; के अंदर (ke andar) is used only when you want to stress the physical interior, not as a default swap.
Sample Sentences:
किताब मेज़ पर नहीं, दराज़ में है। The book is not on the table, it's in the drawer.
(kitaab mez par nahiin, daraaz men hai।)

Sense 2 : in (a span of time, marking 'within/after this much time')
postposition Attaches to a time expression to mean 'after this much time has passed': दो घंटे में (do ghante men, 'in two hours') means the event happens once two hours elapse.
Synonyms: के भीतर (ke bhiitar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the में (men) you'll hear in every Delhi auto-rickshaw or chai-stall promise: पाँच मिनट में आ जाऊँगा (paanch minat men aa jaaoongaa, 'I'll be there in five minutes') -- famously elastic, since 'five minutes' is a Delhi-wide unit of indeterminate length.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners reach for के लिए (ke lie) when they mean में (men): के लिए (ke lie) marks the duration something lasts ('for two hours'), while में (men) marks how soon it will happen ('in two hours') -- वह दो घंटे में आएगा (vah do ghante men aaegaa, 'he will come in two hours') vs वह दो घंटे के लिए रुकेगा (vah do ghante ke lie rukegaa, 'he will stay for two hours').
Sample Sentences:
मैं पाँच मिनट में आ जाऊँगा। I'll be there in five minutes.
(main paanch minat men aa jaaoongaa।)

Sense 3 : among, out of (a group)
postposition Often paired with से (se) to pick an item out of a set: इनमें से एक (inmen se ek, 'one among these').
Synonyms: के बीच (ke biich, used for 'between' two parties or physically 'in the midst of')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in everyday group talk -- हम दोस्तों में (ham doston men, 'among us friends') -- framing a group as a shared space that individuals sit 'inside', which is the same container logic as sense 1 stretched to people rather than places.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to बीच (biich) for 'among', but बीच (biich) suggests a physical middle or a relation between two specific parties, while में (men) (often with से (se)) is the natural choice for picking from or belonging to a larger group -- हम में से कोई (ham men se koii, 'someone among us') sounds far more natural than a बीच (biich) version here.
Sample Sentences:
हम दोस्तों में वही सबसे ज़्यादा बोलता है। Among us friends, he's the one who talks the most.
(ham doston men vahii sabse zyaadaa boltaa hai।)
