रियाज़ (riyaaz)

noun Practice or rehearsal, most often of music, singing, or a skill that requires daily discipline; also used more loosely for any regular practice routine.
Sense 1 : musical/vocal practice, daily rehearsal
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अभ्यास (abhyaas), साधना (saadhnaa, suggests devotional/spiritual intensity)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask any Hindustani classical music student in Delhi and they will tell you about their guru's insistence on subah ka riyaaz (subah kaa riyaaz, 'morning practice') -- there's a whole ethos in Indian classical training that riyaaz done in the early morning hours, when the voice and mind are fresh, is especially valuable. The word carries a sense of devotion and discipline, not just casual practice, so it's often used with real respect: 'saalon ka riyaaz' (saalon kaa riyaaz, 'years of practice') implies hard-earned mastery.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अभ्यास (abhyaas) for all kinds of practice, but रियाज़ (riyaaz) is specifically the word used for music, singing, or instrument practice -- a tabla player or a singer does रियाज़ (riyaaz), not अभ्यास (abhyaas), even though both are grammatically fine. 2) The common verb pairing is रियाज़ करना (riyaaz karnaa, 'to practice'), not रियाज़ होना (riyaaz honaa) -- you do the riyaaz, it doesn't happen to you.

Sense 2 : practice or regular exercise of any skill (extended sense)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अभ्यास (abhyaas)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Beyond music, you'll hear riyaaz used for other disciplines that need daily upkeep, like language or calligraphy -- 'Hindi bolne ka riyaaz' (hindii bolne kaa riyaaz, 'practice speaking Hindi') is a completely natural thing for a teacher to say to a learner like you. It still carries that flavor of devoted, repeated effort rather than a one-off drill.
