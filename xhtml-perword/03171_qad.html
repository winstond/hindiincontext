क़द (qad)

noun A single-sense noun meaning a person's height or stature, used both literally (physical height) and figuratively (standing, caliber).
Sense 1 : physical height/stature of a person
noun, masculine
Synonyms: लंबाई (lambaaii), डील-डौल (diil-daul, build/physique, broader than just height)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Height is a frequent topic of family commentary in India, especially about children -- relatives love to remark on how much someone's क़द (qad) has grown since they last saw them. You'll also hear क़द निकलना (qad nikalnaa, 'for height to shoot up') used affectionately about teenagers going through a growth spurt.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for लंबाई (lambaaii) by default, but क़द (qad) is the more natural word specifically for a person's bodily height and is what you'll hear in everyday remarks like 'उसका क़द अच्छा है' (uskaa qad achchhaa hai, 'he/she has good height'). 2) Note the nuqta under क़ (q) -- it's pronounced with a slightly harder 'q' sound (from Persian/Urdu), though many speakers say it closer to 'kad' in casual speech; the spelling without the dot (कद, kad) is also seen and accepted.

Sense 2 : figurative standing, stature, or caliber of a person
noun, masculine This figurative sense typically appears in the set phrase क़द बढ़ना (qad badhnaa, 'stature to increase') or क़द के बराबर (qad ke baraabar, 'equal in stature'), extending the physical-height image to reputation or importance.
Synonyms: रुतबा (rutbaa, social standing/prestige), हैसियत (haisiyat, status/capacity)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the literal 'height' sense, just stretched metaphorically -- much like English 'stature' does double duty for physical height and reputation. You'll meet it often in news headlines about a leader's or celebrity's growing importance.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative use is less common in casual speech than in formal writing, journalism, or political commentary -- e.g. 'उनका क़द बहुत बढ़ गया है' (unkaa qad bahut badh gayaa hai, 'his stature has grown a lot'); don't overuse it in ordinary conversation where रुतबा (rutbaa) or हैसियत (haisiyat) might sound more natural.
