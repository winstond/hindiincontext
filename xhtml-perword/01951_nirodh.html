निरोध (nirodh)

noun Primarily the clinical/formal word for a condom, and also, in a more literary/formal register, a general noun meaning restraint or prevention. Learners will meet it almost entirely in the first sense.
Sense 1 : condom
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कंडोम (kandom)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: निरोध (nirodh) was the name given to India's state-subsidized condom brand in national family-planning campaigns from the 1960s onward, so for many older Indians the word itself evokes those government posters and radio jingles rather than everyday bedroom talk. Younger, urban speakers overwhelmingly prefer the borrowed English word कंडोम (kandom) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) निरोध (nirodh) is the older, clinical/government-campaign term (associated with public health posters and family-planning ads); in ordinary conversation today most speakers just say कंडोम (kandom). Using निरोध (nirodh) in casual speech can sound stiff or dated, almost like reading from a pamphlet. 2) Because of its association with public-health messaging, the word can land as slightly clinical or even mildly comic in a casual context, rather than crude or taboo.

Sense 2 : restraint, prevention, containment
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रोक (rok), नियंत्रण (niyantran), प्रतिबंध (pratibandh, formal/legal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the literal sense the word comes from (नि + रोध (ni + rodh), 'holding back'), and it survives today mainly in formal, bureaucratic, or medical writing -- for instance, 'population control' as जनसंख्या निरोध (jansankhyaa nirodh, 'population control'). In everyday speech, learners are far more likely to hear रोक (rok) or नियंत्रण (niyantran) for this idea.
