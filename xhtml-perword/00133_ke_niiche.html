के नीचे (ke niiche)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'under, below, beneath' -- built from के (ke, the oblique genitive linker) plus नीचे (niiche, 'down/below'). Functions as a single unit governing the noun before it.
Sense 1 : physical location: under, beneath
postposition Takes the oblique form of the preceding noun, as with all compound postpositions built on के (ke): मेज़ के नीचे (mez ke niiche, 'under the table').
Synonyms: तले (tale, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in daily life: बिस्तर के नीचे (bistar ke niiche, 'under the bed') for lost slippers, कुर्सी के नीचे (kursii ke niiche, 'under the chair') for a dropped pen. It pairs naturally with ऊपर (oopar, 'above/on top') as its opposite.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use में (men) or पर (par) instead, since English 'under' can blur with 'inside' or 'on the ground near' -- के नीचे (ke niiche) specifically means directly beneath something, not just nearby or low down. 2) For feminine or plural nouns, only के (ke) is used here, never की (kii) or के (ke) variants agreeing with the noun -- के नीचे (ke niiche) never changes form.

Sense 2 : figurative: under someone's authority, influence, or burden
postposition Common with abstract nouns like दबाव (dabaav, 'pressure'), नियंत्रण (niyantran, 'control'), or किसी के आदेश (kisii ke aadesh, 'someone's orders') to express being subject to or governed by something.
Synonyms: अधीन (adhiin, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative extension mirrors English quite closely -- 'under pressure,' 'under someone's thumb' -- so it tends to feel intuitive once a learner already has the literal spatial sense down.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अधीन (adhiin) by default for 'under someone's control,' but in casual speech के नीचे (ke niiche) is far more natural and commonly heard, e.g. उसके दबाव के नीचे (uske dabaav ke niiche, 'under his pressure').
