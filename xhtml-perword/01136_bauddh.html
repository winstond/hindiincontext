बौद्ध (bauddh)

adjective/noun This word covers both the adjective 'Buddhist' and the noun 'a Buddhist (person)'; it derives from बुद्ध (buddh, the Buddha) plus a vriddhi-derivation pattern common in Sanskrit-based words meaning 'relating to.'
Sense 1 : Buddhist (relating to Buddhism)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly around Delhi-NCR in contexts like बौद्ध धर्म (bauddh dharm, 'Buddhism') or बौद्ध विहार (bauddh vihaar, 'Buddhist monastery') -- the latter is a real place name category, since Delhi has several, including one near Mehrauli. The root बुद्ध (buddh) itself means 'the awakened one,' so बौद्ध (bauddh) literally means 'of/belonging to the awakened one.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse बौद्ध (bauddh, 'Buddhist') with बुद्ध (buddh, 'the Buddha') because of the near-identical spelling; the vowel-length/diphthong on the first syllable is the only difference and it changes the meaning from the person to the religion/adjective. 2) Do not confuse with बुद्धि (buddhi, 'intellect'), which shares the same root but means something unrelated in everyday use.

Sense 2 : a Buddhist person
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used just like English 'a Buddhist' -- वह बौद्ध है (vah bauddh hai, 'he/she is a Buddhist'). In India this word is also tied to modern history: many Dalits who followed B. R. Ambedkar's mass conversion in 1956 identify as बौद्ध (bauddh), so the word carries social and political resonance beyond pure religious affiliation.
