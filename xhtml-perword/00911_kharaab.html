ख़राब (kharaab)

adjective A single core meaning of 'bad, spoiled, out of order' that stretches across food, machines, health, mood, and morals -- one adjective doing a lot of everyday work.
Sense 1 : spoiled, rotten (of food)
adjective
Synonyms: सड़ा हुआ (sadaa huaa, specifically rotten/decayed), बासी (baasii, stale rather than spoiled)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase every Indian household uses about milk in the summer heat: दूध ख़राब हो गया (doodh kharaab ho gayaa, 'the milk went bad') -- said with real regret, because nobody wants to waste दूध (doodh).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for सड़ा हुआ (sadaa huaa) by default, but ख़राब (kharaab) is the everyday word you'll actually hear when someone opens the fridge and says 'this has gone bad' -- सड़ा हुआ (sadaa huaa) is stronger and more visibly rotten.

Sense 2 : broken, out of order (of machines/devices)
adjective
Synonyms: बिगड़ा हुआ (bigdaa huaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly about appliances and vehicles: मेरी गाड़ी ख़राब हो गई (merii gaadii kharaab ho gaii, 'my car broke down') or टीवी ख़राब है (tiivii kharaab hai, 'the TV is not working'). It's the default word for 'malfunctioning' in daily complaints, far more common than a technical term.

Sense 3 : unwell, out of sorts (of health)
adjective
Synonyms: बीमार (biimaar, more specifically 'ill')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: तबीयत ख़राब होना (tabiiyat kharaab honaa) is the go-to phrase for 'feeling off' -- useful for skipping work or college without committing to a specific ailment, much like English 'I'm not feeling great' as a soft, deniable excuse.
Common Mistakes: 1) मेरी तबीयत ख़राब है (merii tabiiyat kharaab hai, 'I'm not feeling well') is milder than saying you are बीमार (biimaar, 'sick') -- it covers everything from a mild headache to genuine illness, so don't over-translate it as a diagnosis.

Sense 4 : bad, unpleasant, of poor quality (general/moral)
adjective
Synonyms: बुरा (buraa), घटिया (ghatiyaa, low-quality, harsher)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the catch-all sense: मौसम ख़राब है (mausam kharaab hai, 'the weather is bad'), मेरा मूड ख़राब है (meraa mood kharaab hai, 'my mood is bad'), उसकी आदत ख़राब है (uskii aadat kharaab hai, 'his habit is bad'). It's remarkably flexible -- almost any noun that can be 'good' in Hindi can also be ख़राब (kharaab).
Common Mistakes: 1) बुरा (buraa) and ख़राब (kharaab) overlap heavily, but ख़राब (kharaab) leans toward describing a state or condition (bad weather, bad mood, bad quality) while बुरा (buraa) leans toward moral or emotional judgment (a bad person, feeling bad) -- native speakers feel the difference even though dictionaries often just say 'bad' for both.
Sample Sentences:
