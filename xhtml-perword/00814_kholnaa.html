खोलना (kholnaa)

verb Basic transitive verb meaning 'to open' -- covers physical opening (doors, bottles, eyes), undoing/untying (knots, buttons, hair), and extended senses like opening a business or revealing a secret.
Sense 1 : to open (a physical thing)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first action verbs learners meet, and it is genuinely everyday: खिड़की खोलो (khidkii kholo, 'open the window') is something you hear in almost every Indian household, especially as the weather turns and families like fresh air circulating. Pair it mentally with its intransitive twin खुलना (khulnaa) -- the two form one of Hindi's classic transitive/intransitive pairs.
Common Mistakes: 1) खोलना (kholnaa) is transitive and pairs with the intransitive खुलना (khulnaa, 'to become open/to open by itself') -- learners often reach for खोलना (kholnaa) when describing a door that opens on its own, where खुलना (khulnaa) is correct. 2) In the past tense this verb takes ने (ne) since it is transitive: उसने दरवाज़ा खोला (usne darvaazaa kholaa, 'he opened the door').
Sample Sentences:
कृपया खिड़की खोल दो, यहाँ बहुत गर्मी है। Please open the window, it's very hot here.
(kripyaa khidkii khol do, yahaan bahut garmii hai।)

Sense 2 : to undo, untie, loosen
verb, transitive
Synonyms: उतारना (utaarnaa, for clothes/shoes)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this with गाँठ (gaanth, 'knot'), बटन (batan, 'button'), फीता (phiitaa, 'shoelace'), and बाल (baal, 'hair') -- the underlying image is always releasing something that was tied, fastened, or bound, a natural extension of the core 'open' meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) With hair, बाल खोलना (baal kholnaa) means 'to let one's hair down/undo a braid' -- learners sometimes assume 'open' doesn't apply to hair and reach for a different verb unnecessarily.
Sample Sentences:
पहले अपने जूते का फीता खोलो, फिर बैठो। First untie your shoelace, then sit down.

Sense 3 : to start up, set up (a business, shop, account)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa, 'to start/begin')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense is alive in everyday transactional Hindi -- बैंक खाता खोलना (baink khaataa kholnaa, 'to open a bank account') or दुकान खोलना (dukaan kholnaa, 'to open a shop') are phrases you will use constantly navigating daily life in India, from setting up a small business to your first bank visit.
Common Mistakes: 1) दुकान खोलना (dukaan kholnaa, 'to open a shop') and खाता खोलना (khaataa kholnaa, 'to open an account') use खोलना (kholnaa) just as English does, but learners sometimes overextend this to 'opening' an opportunity or a conversation, where Hindi prefers शुरू करना (shuroo karnaa).
Sample Sentences:
मैंने पिछले साल यह दुकान खोली थी। I had opened this shop last year.
(mainne pichhle saal yah dukaan kholii thii।)

Sense 4 : to reveal, disclose (a secret, feelings)
verb, transitive Often appears as दिल खोलना (dil kholnaa, 'to open one's heart') or राज़ खोलना (raaz kholnaa, 'to reveal a secret').
Synonyms: ज़ाहिर करना (zaahir karnaa, 'to make evident'), बताना (bataanaa, 'to tell')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: राज़ खोलना (raaz kholnaa, 'to reveal a secret') and दिल खोलना (dil kholnaa, 'to speak one's heart openly') both extend the physical image of opening to the emotional and confidential realm -- very natural once you notice Hindi consistently treats the heart and secrets as things that can be 'opened' just like a door.
Common Mistakes: 1) दिल खोलना (dil kholnaa) means opening up emotionally/speaking frankly, not a literal medical image -- learners occasionally under-use this idiom because they don't expect 'open' to extend to emotional disclosure so directly.
Sample Sentences:
