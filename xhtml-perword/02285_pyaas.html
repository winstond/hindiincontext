प्यास (pyaas)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'thirst', used both for the literal bodily sensation and, very commonly, as a metaphor for a deep craving or longing.
Sense 1 : physical thirst
noun, feminine Used with लगना (lagnaa) in the dative-subject frame -- मुझे प्यास लगी है (mujhe pyaas lagii hai, 'I am thirsty') -- rather than with any verb meaning 'to feel'.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Thirst and hunger both follow the same dative-subject pattern in Hindi (प्यास लगना (pyaas lagnaa) and भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa)), reflecting a broader pattern where bodily sensations 'arrive' or are 'felt to' you rather than being things you actively do. Water is offered freely and often in Delhi homes and shops, so प्यास बुझाना (pyaas bujhaanaa, 'to quench thirst') is a phrase you will hear constantly, especially in summer.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I am thirsty' with होना (honaa) as in English 'I am...', but the native frame uses लगना (lagnaa): मुझे प्यास लगी है (mujhe pyaas lagii hai, 'I am thirsty'), literally 'thirst is felt to me.' 2) प्यास (pyaas) itself doesn't take ने (ne) even in past constructions, since the experiencer is marked with को (ko) (often contracted to मुझे (mujhe), उसे (use), etc.), not with ने.
Sample Sentences:

(mujhe bahut pyaas lagii hai।)

Sense 2 : deep craving, longing, thirst (figurative)
noun, feminine Takes की (kii) to specify the object of longing: ज्ञान की प्यास (gyaan kii pyaas, 'thirst for knowledge').
Synonyms: चाह (chaah), लालसा (laalsaa, stronger, often literary), तड़प (tadap, more anguished, emotional longing)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense is everywhere in Hindi film songs, poetry, and motivational speech -- प्यास (pyaas) for love, success, knowledge, or freedom is a very natural extension, much like English 'thirst for adventure.' It pairs nicely with बुझाना (bujhaanaa, 'to quench') even in the figurative sense, e.g. प्यास बुझाना (pyaas bujhaanaa) for satisfying a longing.
