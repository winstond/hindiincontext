सूअर का मांस (sooar kaa maans)

noun A compound noun phrase meaning 'pork,' built from सूअर (sooar, 'pig') plus the genitive postposition का (kaa) and मांस (maans, 'meat/flesh'). One straightforward sense.
Sense 1 : pork (meat of the pig)
noun, masculine The का (kaa) here agrees with the following noun मांस (maans, masculine), so it stays का regardless of context -- it does not shift to की or के since it is fixed to मांस, not to सूअर.
Synonyms: पोर्क (pork, English loanword, common on menus)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Pork is a sensitive food word in India: it's avoided by many Hindus and is forbidden (haraam) for Muslims, so सूअर का मांस (sooar kaa maans) is much less commonly eaten or casually discussed than चिकन (chikan) or मटन (matan). You'll mostly encounter this phrase in restaurant menus, packaged food labels, or when explicitly clarifying dietary restrictions -- e.g. a host asking क्या आप सूअर का मांस खाते हैं? (kyaa aap sooar kaa maans khaate hain?, 'do you eat pork?') before serving a meal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say सूअर का गोश्त (sooar kaa gosht) thinking गोश्त (gosht) is interchangeable with मांस (maans) in all contexts -- गोश्त (gosht) is common for meat generally (especially mutton/chicken) but सूअर का मांस (sooar kaa maans) is the standard phrase for pork specifically, especially in writing and on labels. 2) Do not drop का (kaa) and just say सूअर मांस (sooar maans) -- the genitive link is required.
