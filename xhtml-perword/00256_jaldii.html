जल्दी (jaldii)

adverb/noun Covers both the adverb 'quickly/soon' and the noun 'haste, hurry' -- the two senses share a root feel but differ in grammatical role.
Sense 1 : quickly, soon, early
adverb
Synonyms: तेज़ी से (tezii se), फ़ौरन (fauran, immediately), शीघ्र (shiighr, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most-used words in daily Hindi -- parents, bosses, and rickshaw drivers alike say जल्दी करो (jaldii karo, 'hurry up') constantly. It captures both 'soon' and 'fast' in a way English splits into two separate words, so pay attention to context.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse जल्दी (jaldii) meaning 'soon/early' with तेज़ (tez) meaning 'fast' -- जल्दी आओ (jaldii aao, 'come quickly/soon') is about timing, not speed of motion. 2) जल्दी (jaldii) can mean both 'quickly' and 'early' depending on context: जल्दी सो जाओ (jaldii so jaao, 'go to sleep early') vs जल्दी करो (jaldii karo, 'hurry up') -- learners sometimes pick the wrong sense in translation.
Sample Sentences:
जल्दी करो, बस छूट जाएगी! Hurry up, the bus will be missed!
(jaldii karo, bas chhoot jaaegii!)

Sense 2 : haste, hurry
noun, feminine Commonly used with होना (honaa) in dative-subject constructions: मुझे जल्दी है (mujhe jaldii hai, 'I'm in a hurry').
Synonyms: जल्दबाज़ी (jaldbaazii, emphasizes rushed/hasty behavior), फुर्ती (phurtii, nimbleness/speed rather than hurry)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मुझे जल्दी है (mujhe jaldii hai, 'I'm in a hurry') is the classic line you hear from someone trying to skip the queue at a Delhi market or cut a chai-stall conversation short -- it's polite but firm, signaling 'let's wrap this up.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं जल्दी हूँ (main jaldii hoon) by analogy with English 'I am in a hurry,' but the correct construction is मुझे जल्दी है (mujhe jaldii hai) -- the hurry belongs to you dative-style, like liking or needing.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे जल्दी है, मैं बाद में बात करता हूँ। I'm in a hurry, I'll talk later.
(mujhe jaldii hai, main baad men baat kartaa hoon।)
