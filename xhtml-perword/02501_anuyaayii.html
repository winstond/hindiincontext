अनुयायी (anuyaayii)

noun/adjective A learned (Sanskrit-derived) word meaning a follower or adherent -- of a person, religion, ideology, or leader. Functions as both a noun and an adjective with the same underlying sense.
Sense 1 : follower, disciple, adherent
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: शिष्य (shishy, a disciple/student of a guru), भक्त (bhakt, devotee, more religious in tone), समर्थक (samarthak, supporter, broader political sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in news reports about godmen, political leaders, and religious movements in India -- 'गांधी के अनुयायी' (gaandhii ke anuyaayii, 'Gandhi's followers') is the classic textbook example. It carries a slightly formal, journalistic register compared to the more everyday भक्त (bhakt) or फैन (phain).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शिष्य (shishy) when they mean अनुयायी (anuyaayii) -- शिष्य implies a direct teacher-student bond, while अनुयायी (anuyaayii) works for followers of a religion, movement, or public figure one has never personally met. 2) This word stays the same for masculine and feminine referents in the singular (महिला अनुयायी (mahilaa anuyaayii, a female follower)) -- do not force a feminine ending onto it.
Sample Sentences:
महात्मा गांधी के लाखों अनुयायी थे। Mahatma Gandhi had millions of followers.
(mahaatmaa gaandhii ke laakhon anuyaayii the।)

Sense 2 : of or relating to following/adherence
adjective Used attributively, as in अनुयायी वर्ग (anuyaayii varg, 'the following/adherent class') -- less common than the noun use, mostly seen in formal or written descriptions of groups.
Synonyms: अनुगामी (anugaamii, literary, one who follows/comes after)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use shows up mainly in formal writing -- describing a अनुयायी वर्ग (anuyaayii varg) or अनुयायी समुदाय (anuyaayii samudaay, 'follower community') -- rather than in casual speech, where the noun sense dominates.
