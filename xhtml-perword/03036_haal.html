हाल (haal)

noun/adjective/adverb Mainly a noun meaning 'condition/state' (as in the everyday greeting हाल-चाल (haal-chaal)), with a secondary adjectival/adverbial use meaning 'recent' or 'recently'.
Sense 1 : condition, state (of a person, of affairs)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हालत (haalat), स्थिति (sthiti, somewhat formal), दशा (dashaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: क्या हाल है (kyaa haal hai, 'what's the state (of things)') and हाल-चाल पूछना (haal-chaal poochhnaa, 'to ask after someone's well-being') are the everyday greetings you will hear constantly in Delhi -- among friends it is casual and quick, almost like 'what's up', while asking an elder's हाल-चाल carries a warmer, more caring tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use हालत (haalat) and हाल (haal) interchangeably, which is fine in most everyday speech, but हाल is the word baked into the standard greeting फ्रेज़ हाल-चाल (haal-chaal) and क्या हाल है (kyaa haal hai, 'what's up/how are you'), not हालत. 2) हाल is masculine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take masculine forms even though it looks like it could pair with feminine हालत.
Sample Sentences:
क्या हाल है? What's up?
(kyaa haal hai?)

Sense 2 : recent
adjective Used almost always in the fixed phrase हाल का/की/के (haal kaa) before a noun, e.g. हाल की घटना (haal kii ghatnaa, 'a recent incident'); बare हाल alone rarely modifies a noun without का/की/के.
Synonyms: हाल का (haal kaa, full form), ताज़ा (taazaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this most often in news contexts: हाल की खबर (haal kii khabar, 'recent news') or हाल के दिनों में (haal ke dinon men, 'in recent days') -- a staple phrase of Hindi TV anchors and newspapers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop का/की/के and say हाल घटना instead of हाल की घटना (haal kii ghatnaa) -- unlike true invariant adjectives, this sense of हाल needs the possessive marker to link it to the noun.
Sample Sentences:
हाल की खबर सुनकर सब हैरान रह गए। Hearing the recent news, everyone was left stunned.
(haal kii khabar sunkar sab hairaan rah gae।)

Sense 3 : recently, lately
adverb Almost always appears as the fixed phrase हाल ही में (haal hii men) rather than हाल alone functioning adverbially.
Synonyms: हाल ही में (haal hii men, full/common form), अभी (abhii, broader meaning), हाल-फिलहाल (haal-philahaal, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हाल ही में (haal hii men) is the go-to phrase for 'recently' in both spoken and written Hindi -- you will hear it constantly in news broadcasts and casual storytelling alike, e.g. हाल ही में मैंने एक फ़िल्म देखी (haal hii men mainne ek film dekhii, 'I recently watched a movie').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use bare हाल as 'recently' the way they'd use अभी; in practice you need the full फ्रेज़ हाल ही में (haal hii men, 'recently') -- dropping ही में sounds incomplete or odd.
