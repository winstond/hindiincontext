भोगना (bhognaa)

verb A verb centered on 'undergoing' or 'experiencing' something, usually the consequences of one's actions or a stretch of time/suffering; it also has a more neutral sense of enjoying or partaking of something (especially food/pleasures), and appears in the fixed devotional/ritual phrase for offering food to a deity.
Sense 1 : to undergo, suffer the consequences of
verb, transitive Commonly takes an abstract object like कर्मों का फल (karmon kaa phal, 'the fruit of one's deeds') or सज़ा (sazaa, 'punishment'), reflecting the karmic framing of consequences as something one must 'consume' over time.
Synonyms: सहना (sahnaa), झेलना (jhelnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense is deeply tied to the Hindu idea of karma: elders will say कर्मों का फल भोगना पड़ता है (karmon kaa phal bhognaa padtaa hai, 'one must bear the fruit of one's deeds') as a kind of moral reassurance or warning. It's the verb you hear in serious, reflective conversation, not casual chit-chat about a bad day.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सहना (sahnaa) or झेलना (jhelnaa) in every context, but भोगना (bhognaa) carries a specific flavor of 'reaping what was sown' -- it is the natural verb when talking about karma, fate, or a prison sentence, and sounds odd for a one-off physical pain like a headache.

(karmon kaa phal har kisii ko bhognaa padtaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to enjoy, partake of (pleasures, comforts)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: उपभोग करना (upbhog karnaa, more formal), आनंद लेना (aanand lenaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in slightly formal or reflective speech about enjoying life's comforts, e.g. सुख भोगना (sukh bhognaa, 'to enjoy comforts/happiness') -- it frames pleasure the same way the other sense frames suffering, as something consumed or lived through rather than actively sought.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense sits close to the 'suffering' sense in meaning-space -- both are about 'undergoing' an experience -- so context (pleasant vs. painful) is what tells a learner which reading is meant, since the verb form itself doesn't change.
Sample Sentences:

(insaan ko apne kie hue karmon kaa phal bhognaa padtaa hai, chaahe achchhaa ho yaa buraa।)

Sense 3 : to offer food to a deity (ritual)
verb, transitive Almost always appears as the fixed phrase भोग लगाना (bhog lagaanaa) rather than भोगना (bhognaa) alone in this ritual sense; included here because the root meaning of 'offering/consumption by the deity' is directly tied to this verb.
Synonyms: भोग लगाना (bhog lagaanaa, fixed devotional phrase)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind भोग (bhog), the food offered to a deity before it's distributed as प्रसाद (prasaad); you'll hear it at temples and during daily पूजा (poojaa) when someone says भगवान को भोग लगाओ (bhagvaan ko bhog lagaao, 'offer the food to God').
Sample Sentences:
