संगत (sangat)

noun/adjective Two main threads: as a noun it means company/companionship (the people you keep, as in the proverb-flavored phrase about good and bad company), and in music it specifically means instrumental accompaniment. As an adjective it means fitting or appropriate to context.
Sense 1 : company, companionship, association with people
noun, feminine
Synonyms: साथ (saath), साथी (saathii, companion, not the abstract idea), दोस्ती (dostii, friendship, narrower)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word behind the classic parental warning बुरी संगत में मत पड़ो (burii sangat men mat pado, 'do not fall into bad company') -- you'll hear it from worried mothers and moral science textbooks alike. It reflects a very Indian social assumption: character is shaped by the company you keep, so the people around you are treated almost as a moral force acting on you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for साथ (saath) when they mean 'company' in the sense of who you associate with, but संगत (sangat) carries a moral/social weight -- it's used especially when talking about the influence good or bad company has on a person, as in बुरी संगत (burii sangat, 'bad company'). 2) संगत (sangat) is typically paired with an adjective (अच्छी, बुरी) when describing the quality of the company someone keeps -- bare संगत (sangat) alone sounds incomplete in this sense.

Sense 2 : musical accompaniment
noun, feminine Used with साथ देना (saath denaa) or करना (karnaa) -- e.g. तबले पर संगत करना (table par sangat karnaa, 'to accompany on the tabla').
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: At a classical music or qawwali performance, the तबला (tablaa) or हारमोनियम (haarmoniyam) player providing rhythmic or melodic support to the main singer is doing संगत (sangat) -- it's a term of respect, since good accompaniment is considered an art in itself, not a mere background role.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is specific to classical/devotional music contexts (a tabla or harmonium player doing संगत (sangat) for a singer) -- learners sometimes assume it means simply 'playing along' in a casual band sense, but it carries a classical-music register.

Sense 3 : fitting, appropriate, relevant
adjective
Synonyms: उचित (uchit), मुनासिब (munaasib), उपयुक्त (upyukt, formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this mostly in slightly formal or written registers -- discussing whether an argument, decision, or remark is संगत (sangat) ('apt', 'in keeping with the situation') versus असंगत (asangat, 'inconsistent' or 'inappropriate'), its common antonym.
Common Mistakes: 1) This adjectival sense is unrelated in feel to the 'company' noun sense for the learner's ear -- it means something closer to 'apt' or 'consistent with the context,' as in बात संगत लगी (baat sangat lagii, 'the point seemed reasonable/fitting'), so don't try to force a 'companionship' flavor into it.
