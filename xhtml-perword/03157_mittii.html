मिट्टी (mittii)

noun Basic word for earth/soil/clay/dust, with a rich set of figurative extensions -- from pottery material to mortal remains to a person's ruin. One noun, many culturally loaded metaphors built on the same core image of 'earth'.
Sense 1 : soil, earth (the ground substance)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: धूल (dhool, dust, narrower sense), भूमि (bhoomi, formal, land/ground)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you hear at every nursery and construction site in Delhi-NCR -- गमले की मिट्टी (gamle kii mittii, 'the soil in the flowerpot') is a daily phrase for anyone with a balcony garden. It's also the base for a whole family of extended meanings below, so getting comfortable with this literal sense first pays off everywhere else.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use ज़मीन (zamiin) and मिट्टी (mittii) interchangeably, but ज़मीन (zamiin) is 'land/ground' as a surface or property, while मिट्टी (mittii) is the actual earthy substance -- you dig up मिट्टी (mittii), you don't dig up ज़मीन (zamiin) in the same sense.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : clay (as a material for pottery, idols, etc.)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गीली मिट्टी (giilii mittii, 'wet clay', descriptive phrase rather than a true synonym)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the कुम्हार (kumhaar, 'potter') at a village mela shaping diyas and matkas -- that's मिट्टी (mittii) in its craftsman's sense. Around Diwali and Ganesh Chaturthi you'll hear मिट्टी के दिये (mittii ke diye, 'clay lamps') and मिट्टी की मूर्ति (mittii kii moorti, 'clay idol') constantly, often contrasted proudly with plastic or plaster alternatives.

Sense 3 : one's native land, homeland (figurative)
noun, feminine Used with possessives and often in phrases like अपनी मिट्टी से जुड़ना (apnii mittii se judnaa, 'to stay connected to one's roots').
Synonyms: मातृभूमि (maatribhoomi, formal/patriotic), वतन (vatan, carries an Urdu-poetic flavor)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in patriotic songs, farewell speeches, and NRIs talking about home -- अपनी मिट्टी की खुशबू (apnii mittii kii khushboo, 'the fragrance of one's own soil') is a very common sentimental image, tying identity directly to physical earth rather than to an abstract 'country' the way English does.

Sense 4 : mortal remains, dust (of the body after death)
noun, feminine Common in the set phrase मिट्टी में मिलना (mittii men milnaa, 'to be laid to rest / to turn to dust') and मिट्टी को अग्नि देना (mittii ko agni denaa, 'to cremate the mortal remains').
Synonyms: राख (raakh, ash, narrower/different image), अवशेष (avshesh, formal, remains)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the register you hear in eulogies and news reports on a leader's death -- मिट्टी को नमन (mittii ko naman, 'a salute to the mortal remains') -- echoing the mati-se-mati-milna cycle central to Hindu ideas of the body returning to the five elements.

Sense 5 : ruin, waste (figurative -- to spoil or destroy something's worth)
noun, feminine Almost always appears inside the idiom मिट्टी में मिलना/मिलाना (mittii men milaanaa, 'to ruin, reduce to nothing') or मिट्टी पलीद करना (mittii paliid karnaa, 'to ruin/disgrace someone').
Synonyms: बर्बादी (barbaadii, general ruin/waste, not tied to the earth image)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear उसने मेरी मिट्टी पलीद कर दी (usne merii mittii paliid kar dii, 'he completely disgraced me') in heated family arguments or workplace complaints -- it's a vivid, almost physical way of saying someone's reputation or effort was ground into the dirt.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often miss that मिट्टी पलीद करना (mittii paliid karnaa) is a fixed idiom meaning 'to thoroughly disgrace or ruin someone' -- it has nothing to do with actual soil and cannot be swapped with a literal translation.

Sense 6 : material/stuff someone is made of (figurative, character or worth)
noun, feminine Occurs in phrases like किस मिट्टी के बने हो (kis mittii ke bane ho, 'what stuff are you made of').
Synonyms: जौहर (jauhar, literary, valor/mettle)

Cultural Note: This mirrors the English 'what are you made of' almost exactly -- तुम किस मिट्टी के बने हो (tum kis mittii ke bane ho, 'what are you made of') is a common rhetorical jab at someone's courage or resilience, said half-mockingly among friends or seriously in a moment of confrontation.
