धूप (dhoop)

noun Mainly means sunshine/sunlight as experienced outdoors; also has a distinct sense of fragrant incense burned in worship, spelled and pronounced the same way.
Sense 1 : sunshine, sunlight
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सूरज की रोशनी (sooraj kii roshnii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi's winter, धूप (dhoop) is treasured -- people drag chairs onto rooftops or verandas specifically to sit in it, and you'll hear धूप में बैठो (dhoop men baitho, 'sit in the sun') as caring advice from elders. In summer, the same word turns harsh: तेज़ धूप (tez dhoop, 'strong sun') is something to avoid, paired with warnings like धूप में मत निकलो (dhoop men mat niklo, 'don't go out in the sun').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say सूरज (sooraj, 'sun') when they mean the light/warmth itself -- धूप (dhoop) is the sunlight you feel and sit in, not the sun as an object. 2) Sentences about weather with धूप (dhoop) typically use होना (honaa) or निकलना (nikalnaa) (धूप निकली है, dhoop niklii hai, 'the sun has come out') rather than a verb meaning 'to shine' directly on धूप (dhoop).

Sense 2 : incense, aromatic resin/smoke burned in worship
noun, feminine Refers to resinous incense burned on coals or embers, distinct from अगरबत्ती (agarbattii) which is stick incense; often used in the phrase धूप-दीप (dhoop-diip, 'incense and lamp') describing ritual worship items.
Synonyms: अगरबत्ती (agarbattii, stick incense, distinct item)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The smell of धूप (dhoop) burning during the evening आरती (aartii) is one of the most familiar sensory memories of an Indian household -- it's the same word as sunshine because both fill a space with warmth, one felt and one smelled.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with अगरबत्ती (agarbattii); धूप (dhoop) in this sense usually refers to loose resin or a dhoop-cone/stick burned during पूजा (poojaa), not the common incense stick sold everywhere.
