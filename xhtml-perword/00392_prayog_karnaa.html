प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the noun प्रयोग (prayog, 'use/experiment') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means 'to use' something, and in a more technical register, 'to experiment with' something. Related to the more everyday verb इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa).
Sense 1 : to use, to make use of
verb, transitive Takes the thing used as a direct object, often marked with का/की/के (kaa) when specifying 'use of X': जैसे 'इस चीज़ का प्रयोग करना' (is chiiz kaa prayog karnaa, 'to use this thing').
Synonyms: इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa), उपयोग करना (upyog karnaa, slightly more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see प्रयोग करें (prayog karen) on medicine labels, product instructions, and public notices -- it has that slightly official, printed-word feel, similar to how English instructions say 'utilize' rather than 'use.' In casual chatting between friends, इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa) is what you'll actually hear more.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for इस्तेमाल करना (istemaal karnaa) and miss that प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa) is just as common but sits at a slightly more careful, written-or-considered register -- you'll hear it a lot in instructions, notices, and formal speech. 2) The object of use is frequently marked with का (kaa) rather than simply being placed as a bare noun, e.g. 'इसका प्रयोग करें' (iskaa prayog karen, 'use this'), which surprises learners expecting a direct object with को (ko) or no marker at all.

Sense 2 : to experiment, to try out
verb, transitive
Synonyms: परीक्षण करना (pariikshan karnaa, more technical/scientific), आज़माना (aazmaanaa, everyday 'to try')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in school science classes -- प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa) is what you 'do' in a chemistry lab -- and also more loosely for trying something new, like a chef experimenting with a recipe.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss that in a science or lab context, प्रयोग (prayog) itself means 'experiment' as a noun, so प्रयोग करना (prayog karnaa) can mean 'to conduct/perform an experiment,' not just 'to use' -- context (a classroom, a lab, a recipe being tweaked) tells you which sense is meant.
