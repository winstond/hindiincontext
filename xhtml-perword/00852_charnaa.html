चरना (charnaa)

verb Basic verb meaning 'to graze' -- used mainly for animals eating grass in a field, and by extension as a mild figurative jab at humans.
Sense 1 : to graze (of animals)
verb, intransitive
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture cows and goats let loose in a field near a village -- गाय मैदान में चर रही है (gaay maidaan men char rahii hai, 'the cow is grazing in the field') is the classic image this verb calls up for any Hindi speaker.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use खाना (khaanaa) for grazing animals, but चरना (charnaa) is the natural verb for animals eating grass in a field -- खाना (khaanaa) sounds like you're describing a meal, not free grazing.
Sample Sentences:
गाय मैदान में चर रही है। The cow is grazing in the field.
(gaay maidaan men char rahii hai।)

Sense 2 : to be a fool, to act stupidly (figurative, mildly rude)
verb, intransitive Used almost always as a rhetorical jab, often in the imperative or as a dismissive comment on someone's behavior -- e.g. जाओ, घास चरो (jaao, ghaas charo, 'go graze on grass' -- i.e. 'get lost, you fool').
Synonyms: बेवकूफी करना (bevkoophii karnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: The insult works by comparing a person to a grazing animal -- calling someone's behavior fit only for a cow or goat munching grass, a very Indian way of mocking foolishness without swearing.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is informal and can come across as insulting -- do not use it with elders or in polite/formal settings; it is meant for banter among friends or as a sharp put-down.
Sample Sentences:
क्या चर रहे हो, सीधी बात समझ नहीं आती? What kind of idiot are you being -- can't you understand a simple thing?
(kyaa char rahe ho, siidhii baat samajh nahiin aatii?)
