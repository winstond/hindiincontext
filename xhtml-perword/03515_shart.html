शर्त (shart)

noun Primarily 'condition/stipulation' (as in an agreement) and 'bet/wager' -- two closely related senses that both come from the idea of an agreed-upon stake or term.
Sense 1 : condition, stipulation
noun, feminine Often used with पर (par) or की (kii) to attach the condition to a clause, e.g. इस शर्त पर (is shart par, 'on this condition').
Synonyms: शर्त (shart), नियम (niyam, covers 'rule' more broadly)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in negotiations, job offers, and even parenting: माँ ने शर्त रखी कि पहले होमवर्क करो (maan ne shart rakhii ki pahle homvark karo, 'Mom set the condition that you do homework first'). It's the everyday word for 'terms and conditions' in spoken life, well before you get to the legal शर्तें (sharten) printed on a contract.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for नियम (niyam) when they mean a specific negotiated condition rather than a general rule -- शर्त (shart) is the word for a condition attached to a deal, agreement, or permission. 2) The common phrase शर्त रखना (shart rakhnaa, 'to put forward a condition') uses रखना (rakhnaa) as a light verb, not लगाना (lagaanaa) which belongs to the 'bet' sense.
Sample Sentences:
उसने एक शर्त रखी कि मैं पहले माफी माँगूँ। He put forward a condition that I apologize first.
(usne ek shart rakhii ki main pahle maaphii maangoon।)

Sense 2 : bet, wager
noun, feminine Pairs with the verb लगाना (lagaanaa) to mean 'to place a bet' -- शर्त लगाना (shart lagaanaa).
Synonyms: बाज़ी (baazii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मैं शर्त लगाता हूँ (main shart lagaataa hoon, 'I bet') is used casually the way English speakers say 'I bet' or 'I'm sure' to express confidence, even with no money involved -- e.g. among friends arguing about cricket scores.
Common Mistakes: 1) The verb here is लगाना (lagaanaa) (शर्त लगाना, shart lagaanaa, 'to bet'), not रखना (rakhnaa) -- mixing up the two light verbs is a common slip since both senses share the noun शर्त (shart). 2) शर्त जीतना/हारना (shart jiitnaa/shart haarnaa) means 'to win/lose the bet,' distinct from winning or losing the underlying game.
