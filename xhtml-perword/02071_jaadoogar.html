जादूगर (jaadoogar)

noun A magician or sorcerer -- literally 'one who does जादू (jaadoo, magic)'. Has a straightforward literal sense and a common figurative sense for someone dazzlingly skillful.
Sense 1 : magician, sorcerer
noun, masculine
Synonyms: इंद्रजाली (indrjaalii, literary), मायावी (maayaavii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for the stage magician who pulls a rabbit out of a hat at a birthday party, but it also covers folk-tale sorcerers who cast real spells with जादू (jaadoo). Kids' TV and comics keep this word very alive: a जादूगर (jaadoogar) is a stock character in Hindi fairy tales, usually with a wand and a trick up his sleeve.

Sense 2 : someone dazzlingly skillful, a virtuoso (figurative)
noun, masculine Used predicatively with होना (honaa) or as a compliment tag after a name, much like calling someone a 'wizard' at something in English.
Synonyms: उस्ताद (ustaad, skilled expert), माहिर (maahir, expert)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Cricket commentary loves this word: a spin bowler who keeps fooling batsmen gets called a गेंद का जादूगर (gend kaa jaadoogar, 'wizard of the ball'). It carries the same warm, admiring tone as calling someone a 'magician' in English sports talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for जादूगर (jaadoogar) to mean 'genius' in a general intellectual sense, but it fits better for hands-on flair or performance skill -- a cook, a batsman, a salesman -- rather than for pure book-smarts, where होशियार (hoshiyaar) or प्रतिभाशाली (pratibhaashaalii) is more natural.
