बराबर (baraabar)

adjective/adverb Mainly an adjective/adverb meaning 'equal' or 'even', but it also does double duty as a common adverb meaning 'constantly, continuously' -- two related but distinct everyday uses.
Sense 1 : equal, same
adjective Often used with के (ke) in the frame X के बराबर (ke baraabar, 'equal to X') to compare two quantities.
Synonyms: समान (samaan), एक जैसा (ek jaisaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in shops and kitchens when splitting things evenly -- बराबर बराबर बाँट दो (baraabar baraabar baant do, 'divide it equally'), doubling the word for emphasis is very common in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the के (ke) before बराबर (baraabar) when comparing two things -- दो और दो चार के बराबर हैं (do aur do chaar ke baraabar hain, 'two and two are equal to four'), not just दो और दो चार बराबर हैं (do aur do chaar baraabar hain).
Sample Sentences:
दोनों भाइयों को हिस्सा बराबर बराबर बाँट दो। Divide the share equally between both brothers.
(donon bhaaiyon ko hissaa baraabar baraabar baant do।)

Sense 2 : constantly, continuously, all the time
adverb Placed before the verb to describe an ongoing, uninterrupted action, often with habitual or progressive tenses: वह बराबर फोन करता रहता है (vah baraabar phon kartaa rahtaa hai, 'he keeps calling constantly').
Synonyms: लगातार (lagaataar), हमेशा (hameshaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear from an exasperated parent or teacher -- तुम बराबर गलती करते हो (tum baraabar galtii karte ho, 'you keep making the same mistake') -- carrying a slightly weary, repetitive-complaint tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this sense with the 'equal' sense since the word looks identical -- context (presence of a verb of ongoing action vs. a comparison) is the only clue. 2) It pairs naturally with रहना (rahnaa) as a continuity marker, which learners may not expect from a word they first learned as 'equal.'
