आग (aag)

noun Basic word for 'fire' -- covers the literal element as well as the very common figurative uses for intense anger, passion, or fiery sensation that grow out of it.
Sense 1 : fire, flame
noun, feminine
Synonyms: अग्नि (agni, literary), आतिश (aatish, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आग लगना (aag lagnaa, 'for a fire to break out') is the standard way to talk about a fire starting -- fire is treated as something that attaches itself or catches, not something a person actively 'makes' happen. You'll hear this phrasing constantly in news reports about building or forest fires.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अग्नि (agni) thinking it's the everyday word because it shows up in ceremonies and compounds like अग्निशमन (agnishman, fire extinguishing) -- in daily speech आग (aag) is what people actually say for a fire in the kitchen, the jungle, or the news.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : intense anger, rage
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गुस्सा (gussaa), क्रोध (krodh, formal)

Sense 3 : intense passion, longing, or burning desire
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जोश (josh), जलन (jalan, envy-tinged burning)
Frequency: medium

Sense 4 : sharp, burning sensation (spice, heat, pain)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जलन (jalan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: At a dhaba table you'll hear friends laughingly say इस मिर्च में तो आग है (is mirch men to aag hai, 'this chili has fire in it') -- a vivid, everyday extension of the literal fire sense that a learner should recognize rather than translate word for word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'this chili is so hot' too literally with गरम (garam) alone; native speakers often reach for आग (aag) or जलन (jalan) to convey the burning bite of spice, e.g. मुँह में आग लग गई (munh men aag lag gaii, 'my mouth caught fire').
