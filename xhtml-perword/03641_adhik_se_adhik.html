अधिक से अधिक (adhik se adhik)

adverb A comparative-construction phrase built from अधिक (adhik, 'much/more') repeated with से (se) in between; it has two related senses: 'at most' (a ceiling on quantity) and 'to the maximum extent/as much as possible' (an adverbial push toward the upper limit).
Sense 1 : at most, at the maximum
adverb Typically precedes or follows a quantity/number to set its upper bound: अधिक से अधिक दस लोग (adhik se adhik das log, 'at most ten people').
Synonyms: ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा (zyaadaa se zyaadaa), बहुत हुआ तो (bahut huaa to, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in bargaining at a Delhi market -- a shopkeeper capping a discount will say अधिक से अधिक बीस रुपये कम कर सकता हूँ (adhik se adhik biis rupye kam kar saktaa hoon, 'I can reduce it by twenty rupees at most'), and the doubled-word structure (X से X) itself is a common Hindi pattern for expressing a superlative extreme, seen also in kam se kam (kam se kam, 'at least').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place it after the number when it should precede for the 'ceiling' reading, e.g. अधिक से अधिक पाँच दिन (adhik se adhik paanch din, 'five days at most') sounds more natural than पाँच दिन अधिक से अधिक (paanch din adhik se adhik). 2) Confusing this sense with the 'maximum effort' sense below -- context (a number/limit vs. a verb of effort) disambiguates.

Sense 2 : to the maximum extent, as much as possible
adverb Used with a verb of effort or action to push toward doing the most one can: अधिक से अधिक मेहनत करो (adhik se adhik mehnat karo, 'work as hard as possible').
Synonyms: जितना हो सके (jitnaa ho sake, 'as much as possible'), पूरी कोशिश से (poorii koshish se)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will often hear this in motivational or advisory speech -- a teacher or parent urging effort might say अधिक से अधिक कोशिश करो (adhik se adhik koshish karo, 'try as hard as you possibly can'), pairing naturally with verbs like कोशिश करना (koshish karnaa) or मेहनत करना (mehnat karnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'more from more,' missing that it functions idiomatically as 'to the fullest extent' -- there is no separate word-for-word logic to parse each time.
