के यहाँ (ke yahaan)

postposition A postpositional phrase meaning 'at/to the place of (someone)' -- used for homes, offices, or people's establishments, roughly like French 'chez' or English 'at so-and-so's place'.
Sense 1 : at the place/home of (someone)
postposition Attaches to a noun or pronoun in the oblique case (e.g. मेरे यहाँ (mere yahaan, 'at my place'), राम के यहाँ (raam ke yahaan, 'at Ram's place')); functions like a location marker for a person's home, family, or shop rather than a literal point in space.
Synonyms: के घर (ke ghar, specifically 'at the house of'), के पास (ke paas, near/at, less about a person's domain)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This phrase carries a warm, inviting sense of hospitality -- हमारे यहाँ आइए (hamaare yahaan aaie, 'do come to our place') is a very common, gracious Delhi-NCR invitation, often used by hosts to guests or by shopkeepers describing their own establishment (हमारी दुकान (hamaarii dukaan) implied through हमारे यहाँ (hamaare yahaan)). It also extends to family or professional context, as in डॉक्टर के यहाँ जाना (doktar ke yahaan jaanaa, 'to go to the doctor's [clinic/place]').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often substitute के पास (ke paas) here, but के पास (ke paas) suggests physical proximity ('near'), while के यहाँ (ke yahaan) means 'in the domain/household of' -- मेरे पास किताब है (mere paas kitaab hai, 'I have a book with me') versus मेरे यहाँ आओ (mere yahaan aao, 'come to my place'). 2) With pronouns, the oblique possessive form is used before यहाँ (yahaan) -- मेरे यहाँ (mere yahaan), not मैं के यहाँ (main ke yahaan).
Sample Sentences:
हमारे यहाँ आइए, चाय पीते हैं। Do come to our place, let's have some tea.
(hamaare yahaan aaie, chaay piite hain।)
