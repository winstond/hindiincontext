सबसे बढ़कर (sabse badhkar)

adverb A fixed adverbial expression meaning 'above all, most importantly' -- built from सबसे (sabse, 'than all') plus बढ़कर (badhkar, the perfective conjunctive participle of बढ़ना (badhnaa, 'to exceed, surpass'), so literally 'surpassing all.'
Sense 1 : above all, most importantly
adverb Typically opens or interjects into a sentence to single out the most important item in a list of qualities or reasons, e.g. before naming the biggest of several good points.
Synonyms: सबसे ज़्यादा (sabse zyaadaa, 'most of all'), खास तौर पर (khaas taur par, 'especially'), मुख्य रूप से (mukhy roop se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot when someone is praising a person's qualities -- a parent listing what makes their child special might say ..., और सबसे बढ़कर, वह ईमानदार है (aur sabse badhkar, vah iimaandaar hai, 'and above all, he is honest'), saving the weightiest point for last. It shares its root with बढ़ना (badhnaa, 'to increase/advance'), so the imagery is of one quality literally rising above the rest.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this word-for-word as 'from all increased,' missing that बढ़कर (badhkar) here functions idiomatically as 'surpassing,' not as a literal verb of growth. 2) It is not interchangeable with सबसे अच्छा (sabse achchhaa, 'the best') -- सबसे बढ़कर (sabse badhkar) introduces a point or quality, it does not itself mean 'best.'
Sample Sentences:
वह मेहनती है, ईमानदार है, और सबसे बढ़कर, वह सबकी मदद करता है। He is hardworking, he is honest, and above all, he helps everyone.
(vah mehantii hai, iimaandaar hai, aur sabse badhkar, vah sabkii madad kartaa hai।)
