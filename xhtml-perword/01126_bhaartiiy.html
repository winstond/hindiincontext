भारतीय (bhaartiiy)

adjective/noun Means 'Indian' -- used both as an adjective (Indian, of India) and as a noun (an Indian person). Derived from भारत (bhaarat, India).
Sense 1 : Indian (of or relating to India)
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see भारतीय (bhaartiiy) everywhere from news headlines to product labels -- भारतीय रेल (bhaartiiy rel, 'Indian Railways'), भारतीय सेना (bhaartiiy senaa, 'Indian Army') -- it is the standard formal way to say 'Indian,' more official-sounding than a casual reference to हिन्दुस्तान (hindustaan).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect भारतीय (bhaartiiy) like a variable adjective (adding -ई forms), but it stays invariant regardless of the gender or number of the noun it describes -- भारतीय संस्कृति (bhaartiiy sanskriti, 'Indian culture'), भारतीय लोग (bhaartiiy log, 'Indian people').

(bhaartiiy rel duniyaa ke sabse bade rel netvark men se ek hai।)

Sense 2 : an Indian person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: As a noun, भारतीय (bhaartiiy) is common in formal or written contexts -- एक भारतीय (ek bhaartiiy, 'an Indian') -- while in casual conversation people often just say हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) or describe themselves by region or state instead.
