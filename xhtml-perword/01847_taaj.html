ताज (taaj)

noun Primarily a noun meaning 'crown' -- literal (royal headwear) and figurative (topmost honor or distinction).
Sense 1 : crown (royal headwear)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मुकुट (mukut)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The word immediately calls to mind the Taj Mahal (taaj mahal), whose name literally means 'crown of palaces' -- a useful hook for remembering the word. In everyday speech, ताज (taaj) itself is mostly reserved for kings, queens, and pageant winners rather than anything you'd see day to day.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse ताज (taaj) with ताजा (taajaa, 'fresh') because of the similar spelling -- they are unrelated words.

Sense 2 : the top honor, pinnacle of achievement (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the phrase ताज पहनना (taaj pahannaa, 'to wear/win the crown') or ताज जीतना (taaj jiitnaa, 'to win the title'), used for sports and competitions.
Synonyms: खिताब (khitaab, used specifically for titles/championships)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this a lot in cricket and sports commentary -- ताज (taaj) or खिताब का ताज (khitaab kaa taaj, 'the crown of the title') gets used for whoever comes out on top of a tournament, much like English 'crowning' a champion.
