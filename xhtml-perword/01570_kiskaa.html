किसका (kiskaa)

pronoun The oblique interrogative pronoun किस (kis) plus the possessive marker का (kaa) -- literally 'of whom'. Used to ask who owns or is connected to something.
Sense 1 : whose (masculine singular object/possession)
pronoun Agrees with the possessed noun like other का (kaa)/की (kii)/के (ke) possessives: किसका (kiskaa) for masculine singular, किसकी (kiskii) for feminine, किसके (kiske) for masculine plural/oblique.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily mix-ups at home or at a shop -- यह किसका मोबाइल है? (yah kiskaa mobaail hai?, 'whose phone is this?') is a classic line in any Delhi household when something is left lying around. It pairs naturally with the plain possessive का (kaa), की (kii), के (ke), just built on the interrogative base किस (kis) instead of a naming word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often freeze the form as किसका (kiskaa) regardless of the possessed noun's gender/number -- but it must agree, e.g. यह किसकी किताब है? (yah kiskii kitaab hai?, 'whose book is this?') needs किसकी (kiskii) because किताब (kitaab) is feminine. 2) Do not confuse with किसे (kise) 'to whom' (dative) -- किसका (kiskaa) is strictly possessive.
Sample Sentences:
यह किसका मोबाइल है? Whose phone is this?
(yah kiskaa mobaail hai?)
