मँगाना (mangaanaa)

verb The causative of मँगना/मांगना (maangnaa, 'to ask for') -- it means 'to have (something) brought' or 'to order/send for' something, with someone else doing the actual fetching.
Sense 1 : to have something brought / to order something (e.g. food, goods)
verb, transitive Causative construction: the person who actually goes and gets the thing is marked with से (se), e.g. नौकर से मँगाना (naukar se mangaanaa, 'to have the servant fetch it').
Synonyms: ऑर्डर करना (ordar karnaa, for food/products from a shop or app), बुलवाना (bulvaanaa, for having a person or service called over)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday word for phone/app food orders in Delhi -- पिज़्ज़ा मँगाना (pizzaa mangaanaa, 'to order pizza') is something every household says on a lazy Sunday. It captures a very Hindi habit of thought: rather than 'ordering' as a transaction, you're framing it as getting someone (a delivery person, a servant, a shopkeeper) to bring it to you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use मँगाना (mangaanaa) and बुलाना (bulaanaa) interchangeably, but बुलाना (bulaanaa) means 'to call/summon a person to come,' while मँगाना (mangaanaa) means 'to have a thing sent/brought' -- you मँगाना (mangaanaa) a pizza, not a friend. 2) The one who does the fetching takes से (se), not को (ko): घर से खाना मँगाना (ghar se khaanaa mangaanaa, 'to have food sent from home') is correct, not घर को खाना मँगाना।

Sense 2 : to send for, summon indirectly through a request
verb, transitive Used when a message or request is relayed through an intermediary to bring a person or object over, distinct from directly summoning someone yourself.
Synonyms: बुलवाना (bulvaanaa, when the object is a person)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear elders say things like डॉक्टर को मँगवा लो (doktar ko mangvaa lo, 'get the doctor sent for') in a family emergency -- it carries a sense of arranging for someone's arrival through a chain of people, rather than going yourself.
