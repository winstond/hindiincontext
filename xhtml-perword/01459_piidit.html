पीड़ित (piidit)

adjective/noun पीड़ित (piidit) is primarily an adjective meaning 'afflicted, suffering, victimized' by some pain, disease, or disaster, and by extension functions as a noun meaning 'victim' or 'sufferer' (as in a news report).
Sense 1 : afflicted, suffering, distressed (by something)
adjective Takes से (se) marking the cause: बीमारी से पीड़ित (biimaarii se piidit, 'afflicted by illness').
Synonyms: दुखी (dukhii), त्रस्त (trast, formal), ग्रस्त (grast, often used with disease/affliction)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in newspapers and TV news -- flood victims, riot victims, disease sufferers are always described as 'X-पीड़ित' (X-piidit), e.g. बाढ़-पीड़ित (baadh-piidit, 'flood-afflicted'). It carries a formal, almost bureaucratic tone rather than the warmth of everyday conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try को (ko) for the cause of suffering, but पीड़ित (piidit) takes से (se): भूकंप से पीड़ित (bhookamp se piidit, 'earthquake-afflicted'), not भूकंप को पीड़ित. 2) This word is heavier and more formal than just 'sad' or 'in pain' -- for everyday physical pain, दर्द होना (dard honaa) or तकलीफ़ होना (takliif honaa) is more natural; पीड़ित (piidit) suits news, medical, or official contexts.

Sense 2 : victim, sufferer (a person affected by something)
noun, masculine/feminine Often appears compounded with the cause as a single hyphenated noun-adjective, e.g. दुर्घटना-पीड़ित (durghatnaa-piidit, 'accident victim'), functioning as a noun referring to the person.
Synonyms: शिकार (shikaar, more colloquial, used like 'prey/victim'), भुक्तभोगी (bhuktbhogii, formal, one who has personally suffered)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In courtroom dramas and news of crime, you will hear पीड़िता (piiditaa) as the feminine form specifically for a female victim -- this comes up often in reports of crimes against women, so recognizing it is useful even though the base form पीड़ित (piidit) can be used generically.
Common Mistakes: 1) पीड़ित (piidit) as a noun is used for people, not abstract losses -- you would not call a damaged building पीड़ित; that gets क्षतिग्रस्त (kshatigrast, 'damaged') instead. 2) In legal or police contexts, पीड़ित (piidit) specifically means 'the victim' (as opposed to आरोपी (aaropii, 'the accused')) -- worth knowing if you read Hindi crime reporting.
