उदास (udaas)

adjective An invariant adjective describing a sad, gloomy, or low-spirited state of mind; also extends to describe a dull, deserted, or melancholy atmosphere or place.
Sense 1 : sad, dejected, in low spirits
adjective
Synonyms: दुखी (dukhii), मलिन (malin, literary), उदासीन (udaasiin, different shade: indifferent rather than sad)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear a mother use when checking on a quiet child: क्या हुआ, उदास क्यों हो? (kyaa huaa, udaas kyon ho?, 'what happened, why are you looking sad?'). It carries a soft, almost tender concern, quite different from the sharper दुखी (dukhii) which implies actual hurt or sorrow.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दुखी (dukhii) and उदास (udaas) interchangeably, but उदास (udaas) leans toward a quiet, withdrawn low mood rather than active grief or pain, which दुखी (dukhii) can also carry. 2) The person feeling this state is usually the subject with होना (honaa) -- वह उदास है (vah udaas hai, 'he/she is sad') -- not a को (ko)-marked dative construction like with लगना (lagnaa).

Sense 2 : gloomy, dull, deserted (of a place, atmosphere, or scene)
adjective This sense extends the emotional meaning metonymically onto places, weather, or scenes that feel lifeless or joyless, e.g. an evening, a street, or a room.
Synonyms: सुनसान (sunsaan, deserted, different shade: emptiness rather than mood), वीरान (viiraan, literary, desolate)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this used for a rainy, empty evening or a house that feels too quiet after guests leave -- शाम बहुत उदास थी (shaam bahut udaas thii, 'the evening was very gloomy'). It's a lovely example of Hindi projecting inner feeling onto the outer world, the way English might say a 'melancholy sky.'
