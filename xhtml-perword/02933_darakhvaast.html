दरख़्वास्त (darakhvaast)

noun A formal word meaning 'application' or 'petition/request' -- one written document you submit to an office or authority, and the act of formally requesting something.
Sense 1 : written application / petition
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आवेदन (aavedan), प्रार्थना पत्र (praarthnaa patr, written request/letter)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a Persian-origin word (via Urdu) that shows up constantly in bureaucratic and school contexts -- students in North India grow up writing a दरख़्वास्त (darakhvaast) to the principal for leave, and office clerks still say ek darkhwaast de do (ek darakhvaast de do, 'submit an application') rather than reaching for the more Sanskritic आवेदन (aavedan). It carries a slightly old-fashioned, genteel flavor -- the kind of word your grandfather's generation used routinely.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for आवेदन (aavedan) in writing and दरख़्वास्त (darakhvaast) never comes to mind, but in spoken Hindi, especially among older speakers and in Urdu-influenced registers, दरख़्वास्त (darakhvaast) is very natural for 'application' -- don't assume it's rare just because textbooks favor आवेदन (aavedan). 2) The verb that pairs with it is usually देना (denaa) or करना (karnaa) -- दरख़्वास्त देना (darakhvaast denaa, 'to submit an application') is the common collocation, not लिखना (likhnaa) alone.

Sense 2 : a request, plea
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गुज़ारिश (guzaarish), विनती (vintii, more humble/pleading)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Beyond the paperwork sense, दरख़्वास्त (darakhvaast) can mean a personal plea or entreaty, close to गुज़ारिश (guzaarish) -- someone might say meri darkhwaast hai ki... (merii darakhvaast hai ki..., 'my request is that...') in a slightly formal but heartfelt tone, as if asking a favor with some ceremony.
