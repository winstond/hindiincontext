मुन्ना (munnaa)

noun An affectionate term for a young boy, used both as a common noun ('little boy') and as a pet name/term of endearment for a son of any age; the feminine counterpart मुन्नी (munnii) exists as a related but separate lemma.
Sense 1 : little boy, baby boy
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बच्चा (bachchaa), लड़का (ladkaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear शाबाश मुन्ने (shaabaash munne, 'well done, little one')! from grandparents and shopkeepers alike -- it is the default soft address for any small boy, related or not, much like 'sonny' in English but far more common in daily speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुन्ना (munnaa) is warmer and more affectionate than लड़का (ladkaa); do not reach for it as a neutral word for 'boy' in formal or descriptive contexts -- it signals fondness, the way an adult coos over a toddler. 2) It is almost always used for young children, not teenagers or grown men, even though it can survive as a pet name into adulthood (see next sense).

Sense 2 : pet name for one's son (used regardless of age)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बेटा (betaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: It is very common for मुन्ना (munnaa) to become a person's actual lifelong nickname within the family, distinct from the formal name on their school certificate -- you will meet grown men whose parents and siblings still call them मुन्ना (munnaa) at family gatherings.
Common Mistakes: 1) Many families keep calling a son मुन्ना (munnaa) well into his teens or even adulthood as a household nickname -- this is not baby talk gone wrong, it is a normal, lasting family pet name, sometimes the only name younger relatives ever use for him.
