नफ़रत करना (nafrat karnaa)

verb A verb phrase meaning 'to hate' -- built from the Persian-origin noun नफ़रत (nafrat, 'hatred') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'), following the common Hindi pattern of noun + करना forming an emotion verb.
Sense 1 : to hate, to feel hatred toward
verb, transitive The person or thing hated is marked with से (se): 'मैं झूठ से नफ़रत करता हूँ (main jhooth se nafrat kartaa hoon, 'I hate lying')'.
Synonyms: घिन करना (ghin karnaa, to feel disgust toward), नफ़रत होना (nafrat honaa, alternate construction, hatred to arise in someone), से चिढ़ना (se chidhnaa, to be irritated by/dislike)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: नफ़रत (nafrat) is a fairly strong word, closer to 'hatred' than mere dislike, so it shows up a lot in film dialogue, political rhetoric, and heartbreak songs -- pyaar aur nafrat (love and hatred) is a classic dramatic pairing. In everyday conversation about milder annoyance, Hindi speakers often reach for softer options like पसंद नहीं (pasand nahiin, 'don't like') rather than नफ़रत करना (nafrat karnaa), which is reserved for genuinely strong feeling.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the object with को (ko) by analogy with English 'hate someone,' but नफ़रत करना (nafrat karnaa) takes से (se), not को (ko). 2) Note the alternative construction नफ़रत होना (nafrat honaa) -- 'मुझे उससे नफ़रत होती है (mujhe usse nafrat hotii hai, 'I feel hatred toward him/her')' -- which uses a को-marked (dative) experiencer instead of an agentive subject; both are common but shift the grammar noticeably.
Sample Sentences:

(main jhooth se nafrat kartaa hoon।)
