फ़र्क़ पड़ना (farq padnaa)

verb A conjunct-verb phrase meaning 'to make a difference' or 'to matter', built from the noun फ़र्क़ (farq, 'difference') plus पड़ना (padnaa, 'to fall'). Almost always used with a को-marked person to say who is affected, or in the negative to say something doesn't matter.
Sense 1 : to matter, to make a difference (to someone)
verb, intransitive Takes a dative (को) subject for the person affected: मुझे फ़र्क़ पड़ता है (mujhe farq padtaa hai, 'it matters to me'). फ़र्क़ can be quantified with कोई (koii) or ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) etc., as in कोई फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (koii farq nahiin padtaa, 'it doesn't matter at all').
Synonyms: असर पड़ना (asar padnaa, 'to have an effect'), मतलब होना (matlab honaa, 'to be of concern')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi's classic dative-subject constructions, in the same family as अच्छा लगना (achchhaa lagnaa) or पसंद आना (pasand aanaa) -- the feeling or effect 'falls on' you rather than you actively 'mattering.' You'll hear कोई फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (koii farq nahiin padtaa) constantly in Delhi conversation as a shrugging, philosophical 'whatever, it's fine' -- a very Dilli-wala way of brushing something off.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the person affected the grammatical subject with ने, saying something like मैंने फ़र्क़ पड़ा (mainne farq padaa) -- wrong; the person takes को, not ने, since फ़र्क़ (farq) itself is the one 'falling.' 2) The negative कोई फ़र्क़ नहीं पड़ता (koii farq nahiin padtaa, 'it doesn't matter') is far more common in daily speech than the positive form, so learners should get comfortable with the negative first. 3) Don't confuse this with फ़र्क़ करना (farq karnaa), which means 'to differentiate/discriminate' rather than 'to matter.'
