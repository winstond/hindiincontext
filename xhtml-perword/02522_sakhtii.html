सख़्ती (sakhtii)

noun The abstract noun of सख़्त (sakht, 'strict/hard'); means 'strictness, harshness, severity' -- almost always about how a rule, person, or authority behaves, not about a physical object being hard.
Sense 1 : strictness, severity, harshness (of treatment, discipline, or enforcement)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कठोरता (kathortaa), कड़ाई (kadaaii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear सख़्ती से पालन करें (sakhtii se paalan karen, 'follow strictly') on government notices, and सख़्ती बरतना (sakhtii baratnaa, 'to exercise strictness') in news reports about police or officials cracking down on something -- the word has a slightly official, rule-enforcement flavor even in everyday speech, like a parent laying down the law about homework or curfew.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कठिनता (kathintaa, 'difficulty') when they mean सख़्ती (sakhtii) -- the two are unrelated; सख़्ती (sakhtii) is about severity of treatment or rules, not about a task being hard. 2) The common frame is सख़्ती से (sakhtii se, 'strictly/with strictness'), as in 'follow strictly' -- learners sometimes drop से (se) and just say सख़्ती करना (sakhtii karnaa, 'to be strict/crack down'), which is a different, also valid, but distinct construction meaning 'to get tough (on something)'.

पुलिस ने आज सड़क पर बहुत सख़्ती बरती। The police exercised a lot of strictness on the road today.
(pulis ne aaj sadak par bahut sakhtii bartii।)
