गर्व (garv)

noun A single core meaning: 'pride,' both the healthy self-respecting kind and the puffed-up arrogant kind, distinguished mainly by context and by which postposition frames the feeling.
Sense 1 : pride (positive: satisfaction, self-respect)
noun, masculine Typically appears with होना (honaa) in the frame X को Y पर गर्व है (X ko Y par garv hai, 'X is proud of Y') -- the feeler takes को, and the thing one is proud of takes पर.
Synonyms: अभिमान (abhimaan), फ़ख़्र (fakhr, common in Urdu-influenced speech), नाज़ (naaz, often paired with प्यार)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word a parent uses about a child's achievement or a person uses about their country or heritage -- मुझे भारतीय होने पर गर्व है (mujhe bhaartiiy hone par garv hai, 'I am proud to be Indian') is a very common sentence-shape you'll hear in patriotic contexts and school essays alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try *मैं गर्व करता हूँ (main garv kartaa hoon) on the model of English 'I feel proud,' but the natural construction is dative-subject: मुझे गर्व है (mujhe garv hai, 'I am proud'), not गर्व करना used this way with मैं as subject. 2) The thing you're proud of is marked with पर (par), not का or की -- मुझे अपने बेटे पर गर्व है (mujhe apne bete par garv hai, 'I am proud of my son'), never *अपने बेटे का।
Sample Sentences:
मुझे अपने बेटे पर गर्व है। I am proud of my son.
(mujhe apne bete par garv hai।)

Sense 2 : pride (negative: arrogance, conceit)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अभिमान (abhimaan), अहंकार (ahankaar), घमंड (ghamand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Where English needs two separate words, 'pride' and 'arrogance,' Hindi lets गर्व (garv) cover both, much like अभिमान (abhimaan) -- the moral coloring comes from context, not the word itself, so a teacher or elder warning against गर्व is warning against conceit, not against self-respect.
Common Mistakes: 1) Because गर्व (garv) can mean both healthy pride and conceit, listeners rely on tone and context (often घमंड (ghamand) is preferred when the negative sense is unambiguous) -- pairing गर्व with usne kiyaa ('did/showed') rather than the है (hai) construction tends to push it toward this negative, showing-off sense, as in उसने बहुत गर्व दिखाया (usne bahut garv dikhaayaa, 'he showed a lot of arrogance').
