मक्खी (makkhii)

noun A common noun meaning 'fly' (the insect); also used figuratively in a couple of everyday idioms about pettiness and imitation.
Sense 1 : the insect (housefly)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word constantly around food stalls and kitchens in India -- someone waving a hand and saying मक्खियाँ भगाओ (makkhiyaan bhagaao, 'shoo the flies away') is a classic scene at any Delhi dhaba in summer.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse मक्खी (makkhii) with मच्छर (machchhar, mosquito) -- मक्खी is specifically the fly that buzzes around food, not the biting mosquito.
Sample Sentences:
दुकान पर मक्खियाँ भिन्भिना रही थीं। Flies were buzzing around the shop.
(dukaan par makkhiyaan bhinbhinaa rahii thiin।)

Sense 2 : petty/trivial thing (in the idiom 'making a mountain of a fly')
noun, feminine Occurs almost exclusively inside the fixed idiom मक्खी को हाथी बनाना (makkhii ko haathii banaanaa, 'to make an elephant out of a fly') -- i.e. to blow something trivial out of proportion.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the Hindi cousin of English's 'making a mountain out of a molehill' -- except Hindi swaps in an elephant, which somehow makes the exaggeration feel even bigger.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense doesn't stand alone -- learners shouldn't try to use मक्खी (makkhii) by itself to mean 'a trivial thing'; it only carries that meaning inside the elephant idiom.
Sample Sentences:
तुम मक्खी का पहाड़ बना रहे हो, इतनी छोटी बात है ये। You're making a mountain out of a fly, it's such a small matter.
(tum makkhii kaa pahaad banaa rahe ho, itnii chhotii baat hai ye।)

Sense 3 : blind/unthinking imitator (in the idiom 'fly in the milk/ointment' sense of copying)
noun, feminine Found in the idiom मक्खी पर मक्खी मारना (makkhii par makkhii maarnaa, 'to swat a fly upon a fly'), meaning to imitate someone blindly without any originality of one's own.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This idiom often comes up when criticizing someone -- a student, a writer, a filmmaker -- for copying another's work without adding anything of their own; it carries a mildly disdainful tone.
Common Mistakes: 1) The idiom मक्खी पर मक्खी मारना (makkhii par makkhii maarnaa) is about mindless copying, not literal fly-swatting -- learners sometimes take it at face value.
Sample Sentences:
वह तो बस दूसरों की मक्खी पर मक्खी मारता है, अपना कुछ नहीं सोचता। He just copies others blindly, he never thinks of anything on his own.
(vah to bas doosron kii makkhii par makkhii maartaa hai, apnaa kuchh nahiin sochtaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
