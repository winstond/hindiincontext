दान (daan)

noun Mainly a noun meaning 'charitable donation, alms-giving' with strong religious/cultural roots; it also appears as a combining element in compound words like रक्तदान (raktdaan, blood donation).
Sense 1 : charitable donation, giving to the needy or to religious causes
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दक्षिणा (dakshinaa, specifically a donation given to a priest), चढ़ावा (chadhaavaa, offering made at a temple/shrine), खैरात (khairaat, almsgiving, informal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: दान (daan) carries deep roots in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain ethics as one of the cardinal virtues -- giving to temples, sadhus, or the poor is seen as spiritually meritorious, not just generous. You'll hear it constantly around festivals: दान-पुण्य (daan-puny, 'charity and merit') is a common paired phrase describing this whole ethic of giving.
Common Mistakes: 1) दान (daan) is used with देना (denaa) as in दान देना (daan denaa, 'to give in charity'), not with करना (karnaa) -- learners sometimes reach for करना (karnaa) by analogy with other noun+verb pairs. 2) दान (daan) implies a selfless, often religiously-motivated gift; it is not the word for a casual 'gift' (that is तोहफ़ा tohfaa or उपहार upahaar).

Sense 2 : donation of a body part, organ, or substance (as a compounding element)
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively as the second half of a compound noun: रक्तदान (raktdaan, blood donation), नेत्रदान (netrdaan, eye donation), अंगदान (angdaan, organ donation), देहदान (dehdaan, body donation).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This modern extension of दान (daan) shows how the old religious idea of meritorious giving was extended smoothly to civic causes like blood banks and organ donation camps -- you'll see रक्तदान शिविर (raktdaan shivir, 'blood donation camp') banners outside colleges and hospitals across Delhi-NCR.
