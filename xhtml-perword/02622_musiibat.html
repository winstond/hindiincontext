मुसीबत (musiibat)

noun A feminine noun meaning trouble, calamity, or difficulty -- used constantly in everyday speech both for real hardships and for exaggerated complaints about minor annoyances.
Sense 1 : trouble, difficulty, hardship
noun, feminine
Synonyms: परेशानी (pareshaanii), दिक्कत (dikkat), आफ़त (aafat, slightly more dramatic), संकट (sankat, formal, more like 'crisis')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word in half-joking complaints all the time -- कितनी मुसीबत है (kitnii musiibat hai, 'what a hassle') said about traffic, a broken फ्रिज (phrij), or a demanding relative. It has a slightly theatrical flavor, like English 'what a nightmare', even when the actual stakes are low.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुसीबत (musiibat) is feminine, so watch adjective and verb agreement: बड़ी मुसीबत (badii musiibat, 'a big problem'), not बड़ा. 2) Learners often reach for समस्या (samasyaa) for every 'problem', but मुसीबत (musiibat) carries more emotional weight -- it suggests real distress or a mess you're stuck in, not a neutral issue to be solved.

Sense 2 : a troublesome person or burden (metaphorical)
noun, feminine Used to describe a person or situation that is itself the source of ongoing hassle, e.g. यह लड़का तो मुसीबत है (yah ladkaa to musiibat hai, 'this boy is a real handful').
Synonyms: बला (balaa, colloquial, more vivid), झंझट (jhanjhat, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone मुसीबत (musiibat) -- as in तुम तो मुसीबत हो (tum to musiibat ho, 'you're such a pain') -- is common between close friends or family and is usually affectionate teasing rather than a genuine insult, much like calling someone a 'menace' in English.
