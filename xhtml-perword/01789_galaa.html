गला (galaa)

noun/verb Primarily the noun meaning 'throat/neck' (galaa), source of many idioms about voice, strangling, and emotional choking; also a colloquial intransitive verb sense meaning 'to melt/dissolve' (from गलना, galnaa).
Sense 1 : throat, neck
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कंठ (kanth, formal/literary), गर्दन (gardan, neck specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi builds a whole emotional vocabulary around गला (galaa): गला भर आना (galaa bhar aanaa, 'the throat fills up') for choking up with emotion, गला रुँधना (galaa rundhnaa, 'the throat gets clogged') for a voice thick with tears. Notice the body-as-agent pattern -- the throat does something to you, you don't just 'get a lump in your throat' as an object you possess.
Common Mistakes: 1) गला (galaa) refers to the throat/neck area generally, while गर्दन (gardan) is more specifically the neck as a body part you'd turn or crane -- for sore throat, hoarseness, or a hand at someone's throat, गला (galaa) is the natural word. 2) Learners often miss that गला (galaa) is grammatically masculine even though 'neck' body parts can feel feminine-coded in English framing.
Sample Sentences:
गला दुख रहा है, ठीक से बोला नहीं जा रहा। My throat hurts, I can't speak properly.
(galaa dukh rahaa hai, thiik se bolaa nahiin jaa rahaa।)

Sense 2 : voice quality (singing/speaking)
noun, masculine Used especially of singers: अच्छा गला होना (achchhaa galaa honaa, 'to have a good voice/throat') praises singing ability specifically.
Synonyms: आवाज़ (aavaaz), स्वर (svar, literary/musical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask about a classical singer in Delhi and you might hear उस्ताद का गला बहुत खुला हुआ है (ustaad kaa galaa bahut khulaa huaa hai, 'the maestro's voice is very open/free') -- गला (galaa) here stands in for the whole vocal instrument, much like English speaks of someone having 'pipes.'
Common Mistakes: 1) In music contexts, गला साफ़ करना (galaa saaf karnaa) means literally 'to clear one's throat' before singing/speaking, not a figurative idiom -- learners sometimes overtranslate it as something more dramatic.

Sense 3 : idiomatic 'throat' in fixed expressions (strangling, cutthroat competition, suppressing)
noun, masculine Common fixed phrases: गला घोंटना (galaa ghontnaa, 'to strangle'), गले की हड्डी (gale kii haddii, 'a bone in the throat' = a persistent problem), गला काटना (galaa kaatnaa, 'to cut throats' = ruthless/cutthroat, as in गला काट प्रतियोगिता, galaa kaat pratiyogitaa, 'cutthroat competition').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: These idioms show how much emotional and social weight गला (galaa) carries in Hindi -- it's the seat of both literal survival (breathing, swallowing) and figurative vulnerability (being at someone's mercy), which is why so many idioms of threat and helplessness cluster here.
Common Mistakes: 1) गला काटना (galaa kaatnaa) is almost always figurative in modern speech (ruthless dealing, cutthroat business) rather than literal violence -- don't read menace into every use. 2) गले की हड्डी (gale kii haddii) describes a problem too awkward to swallow or spit out -- learners sometimes mistranslate it as simply 'a big problem' and miss the stuck-in-between nuance.
Sample Sentences:
बाजार में इतनी होड़ है कि हर दुकानदार दूसरे का गला काटने को तैयार है। There's such cutthroat competition in the market that every shopkeeper is ready to slit the other's throat.
(baajaar men itnii hod hai ki har dukaandaar doosre kaa galaa kaatne ko taiyaar hai।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 4 : to melt, dissolve (colloquial variant of गलना)
verb, intransitive This verb use overlaps with the noun गला (galaa) only in spelling, not in meaning -- it is the ordinary verb गलना (galnaa) heard in casual speech with the final vowel dropped in some tenses/forms, not a separate lexical item.
Synonyms: गलना (galnaa, standard form), पिघलना (pighalnaa, more common for melting)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You may hear बर्फ़ गल गई (barf gal gaii, 'the ice melted') in casual speech where careful speakers would say गलना (galnaa) fully conjugated -- this is a minor pronunciation-driven variant, not a distinct word to actively study.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is essentially informal/dialectal shorthand for गलना (galnaa) and is not a common dictionary headword on its own -- learners are better served learning गलना (galnaa) directly; listed here only because गला (galaa) surfaces as a spoken form in some regional speech.
