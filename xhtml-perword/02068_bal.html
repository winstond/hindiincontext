बल (bal)

noun Mainly a noun meaning physical strength or force, common in everyday and formal registers alike; it also survives in a few fixed adverbial/idiomatic phrases built on this same root.
Sense 1 : physical strength, force, power
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शक्ति (shakti), ताकत (taakat), ज़ोर (zor)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet बल (bal) constantly in compounds rather than alone: मनोबल (manobal, 'morale'), आत्मबल (aatmbal, 'inner strength'), जनबल (janbal, 'manpower') -- Hindi loves stacking this root onto an abstract noun to name the 'force of X'. In plain conversation about physical muscle, ताकत (taakat) is what people actually reach for first.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ताकत (taakat) in every context, but बल (bal) is the word used in more formal, written, or abstract contexts (मनोबल manobal, 'morale/willpower'; सैन्य बल sainy bal, 'military force') -- it sounds slightly more literary or official than ताकत (taakat) in casual speech.
Sample Sentences:
हाथी में बहुत बल होता है। An elephant has a lot of strength.
(haathii men bahut bal hotaa hai।)

(bal aur buddhi donon kaa sahii santulan zaroorii hai।)

Sense 2 : military force, troops (as an institutional body)
noun, masculine Common in the compound सुरक्षा बल (surakshaa bal, 'security force') and similar official/news terms.
Synonyms: सेना (senaa), फ़ौज (fauj)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up daily in the news: पुलिस बल (pulis bal, 'police force'), सुरक्षा बल (surakshaa bal, 'security forces'). It is really the same root sense as physical strength, just applied to organized manpower -- a natural metonymy rather than an unrelated word.
Sample Sentences:
सीमा पर सुरक्षा बल तैनात किए गए हैं। Security forces have been deployed on the border.
(siimaa par surakshaa bal tainaat kie gae hain।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Passive
