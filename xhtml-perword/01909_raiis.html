रईस (raiis)

adjective/noun A borrowed Arabic-origin word meaning 'rich, wealthy' or 'a wealthy/aristocratic person' -- one core sense used as both adjective and noun.
Sense 1 : wealthy, rich, aristocratic
adjective
Synonyms: धनी (dhanii), दौलतमंद (daulatmand), अमीर (amiir)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of रईस (raiis) as describing someone with 'rईसी' (raiisii, 'aristocratic style/manner') -- the word often points to how someone lives (grand houses, fine clothes, unhurried manners), not just their bank balance. It is common in older Bollywood dialogue and in descriptions of Old Delhi or Lucknow nawabi families.
Common Mistakes: 1) रईस (raiis) carries a flavor of old-money style, class, and manner, not just having cash -- अमीर (amiir) is the more neutral, general word for 'rich' and is used far more often in everyday speech. 2) Learners sometimes use रईस (raiis) for someone who simply earns a lot; native speakers reserve it more for a person who lives and carries themselves like the wealthy, with a certain lifestyle or attitude attached.

Sense 2 : a wealthy person, aristocrat, nobleman
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धनवान (dhanvaan), अमीर आदमी (amiir aadmii), नवाब (navaab, connotation of nobility/nawab)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear रईस (raiis) used almost affectionately or admiringly about a person who spends generously and lives with flair -- 'बड़ा रईस आदमी है' (badaa raiis aadmii hai, 'he is quite the wealthy/grand man') -- carrying a whiff of old aristocracy rather than a modern tech millionaire.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a noun, रईस (raiis) usually refers to a man of wealth and standing; for a general 'rich person' regardless of connotation, अमीर (amiir) is the safer everyday default.
