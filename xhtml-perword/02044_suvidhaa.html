सुविधा (suvidhaa)

noun A feminine noun meaning a facility, amenity, or convenience -- something that makes life or a process easier. Common in both everyday speech (about home comforts) and formal/official contexts (about public services).
Sense 1 : facility, amenity (a provided convenience)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सहूलियत (sahooliyat), आराम (aaraam, partial overlap, focuses on comfort rather than provided service)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word everywhere in official India: सरकारी सुविधा (sarkaarii suvidhaa, 'government facility/benefit'), स्वास्थ्य सुविधा (svaasthy suvidhaa, 'healthcare facility'), or यात्री सुविधा (yaatrii suvidhaa, 'passenger amenity') on railway signage. It carries a slightly formal, official flavor even in casual use -- like when someone lists the सुविधाएँ (suvidhaaen) of a rented flat to a prospective tenant.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use आराम (aaraam) when they mean a provided service or amenity (like wifi, parking, elevator) -- सुविधा (suvidhaa) is the better word for facilities/amenities, while आराम (aaraam) is about personal comfort or rest. 2) In plural contexts like 'facilities available here,' the phrase is typically यहाँ ये सुविधाएँ हैं (yahaan ye suvidhaaen hain, 'these facilities are here'), with the plural सुविधाएँ (suvidhaaen) rather than a repeated singular.
Sample Sentences:
इस अस्पताल में सारी आधुनिक सुविधाएँ उपलब्ध हैं। This hospital has all modern facilities available.
(is asptaal men saarii aadhunik suvidhaaen uplabdh hain।)

Sense 2 : convenience (ease of doing something, opportunity)
noun, feminine Often appears in the postpositional phrase सुविधा के लिए (suvidhaa ke lie, 'for convenience') or आपकी सुविधा के अनुसार (aapkii suvidhaa ke anusaar, 'according to your convenience').
Synonyms: सहूलियत (sahooliyat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in polite scheduling talk -- अपनी सुविधा के अनुसार आ जाइए (apnii suvidhaa ke anusaar aa jaaie, 'come according to your own convenience') is the kind of gracious, non-pushy invitation you will hear from a Delhi host or a government office clerk alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to say 'whenever it's convenient for you' word-for-word from English; the natural Hindi frame uses जब आपकी सुविधा हो (jab aapkii suvidhaa ho, 'whenever it is convenient for you') with सुविधा होना (suvidhaa honaa) as the verb pairing, not a direct adjective translation of 'convenient.'
