भात (bhaat)

noun A regional/everyday word for cooked rice, distinct from the more common चावल (chaaval) which usually refers to uncooked rice grains; भात (bhaat) is the cooked staple served at a meal.
Sense 1 : cooked rice (as a food/meal staple)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चावल (chaaval, usually means uncooked rice but also used for the cooked dish), उबले चावल (uble chaaval)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In many North and East Indian homes, दाल-भात (daal-bhaat, 'lentils and rice') is the classic comfort-food combination, similar to how 'rice and dal' functions as an everyday staple in Nepali and Bihari/Bengali households alike. The word carries a homely, unpretentious warmth that चावल (chaaval) alone doesn't quite capture.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use चावल (chaaval) for everything rice-related; भात (bhaat) specifically evokes the plain boiled/steamed rice as served on a plate, especially in eastern and northern regional households, and sounds more homely than चावल (chaaval) in a restaurant-menu sense.
