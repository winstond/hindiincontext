फ़लाना (falaanaa)

adjective/pronoun A placeholder word meaning 'so-and-so' or 'such-and-such,' used when the speaker deliberately skips a name or detail; almost always paired with ढिमकाना (dhimkaanaa) in the fixed phrase फ़लाना-ढिमकाना (falaanaa-dhimkaanaa, 'so-and-so and whatnot').
Sense 1 : so-and-so, such-and-such (unnamed placeholder person/thing)
adjective Used attributively before a noun, e.g. फ़लाना आदमी (falaanaa aadmii, 'so-and-so man'), standing in for a name the speaker doesn't want to specify or can't recall.
Synonyms: अमुक (amuk, formal/literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you reach for when telling a story and you either don't remember someone's name or don't want to bother naming them: 'फ़लाना साहब ने कहा...' (falaanaa saahab ne kahaa, 'so-and-so sir said...'). It has a light, gossipy, slightly comic flavor, much like English 'what's-his-name' or 'such-and-such,' and is very common in everyday chatter among neighbors, relatives, and shopkeepers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use this as a real name-substitute in formal writing or documents; it belongs strictly to casual spoken narration, gossip, or storytelling. 2) It is almost always heard doubled with ढिमकाना (dhimkaanaa) as फ़लाना-ढिमकाना (falaanaa-dhimkaanaa) -- using फ़लाना (falaanaa) alone sounds a bit incomplete to native ears, though it is not wrong.

Sense 2 : so-and-so (as a stand-alone placeholder noun/pronoun)
pronoun Functions on its own as a noun-like placeholder without a following noun, e.g. 'फ़लाना आया था' (falaanaa aayaa thaa, 'so-and-so had come').
Synonyms: अमुक (amuk, formal/literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Same flavor as the adjectival use -- it fills the slot where a proper name would go, often when the speaker is being deliberately vague or is recounting hearsay: 'फ़लाना ने बताया कि...' (falaanaa ne bataayaa ki, 'so-and-so mentioned that...').
