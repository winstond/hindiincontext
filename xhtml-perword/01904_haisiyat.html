हैसियत (haisiyat)

noun A single core idea -- one's standing or capacity, whether financial, social, or in terms of role/status -- expressed in a small set of everyday fixed phrases.
Sense 1 : financial capacity, means (to afford something)
noun, feminine Common frame: X की हैसियत नहीं है (X kii haisiyat nahiin hai, 'X does not have the means') + infinitive verb, e.g. खरीदने की हैसियत (khariidne kii haisiyat, 'the means to buy').
Synonyms: क्षमता (kshamtaa, capacity), सामर्थ्य (saamarthy, somewhat formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in everyday Delhi talk about money: मेरी इतनी हैसियत नहीं है (merii itnii haisiyat nahiin hai, 'I can't afford that much') is a very common, slightly self-deprecating way to decline something expensive without sounding rude.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for क्षमता (kshamtaa) here, but हैसियत (haisiyat) is the natural choice specifically when talking about money or affording something, not general ability. 2) The construction takes की (kii) before हैसियत (haisiyat), not का (kaa) or के (ke), regardless of the gender of the thing being afforded -- हैसियत (haisiyat) itself is feminine and governs the postposition.
Sample Sentences:
मेरी इतनी हैसियत नहीं है कि मैं ये गाड़ी खरीद सकूँ। I don't have the means to buy this car.
(merii itnii haisiyat nahiin hai ki main ye gaadii khariid sakoon।)

Sense 2 : social standing, status, position
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रुतबा (rutbaa), मर्तबा (martbaa, somewhat formal), प्रतिष्ठा (pratishthaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense carries real social weight in Hindi -- अपनी हैसियत में रहो (apnii haisiyat men raho, 'stay within your station') is the kind of line an elder or boss might use to remind someone of a social or hierarchical boundary, reflecting how status-consciousness is baked into everyday speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) In put-downs like अपनी हैसियत देखो (apnii haisiyat dekho, 'know your place'), हैसियत (haisiyat) means social rank, not money -- learners sometimes mistranslate this as being about wealth when it is about knowing one's social place in a hierarchy or relationship.

Sense 3 : capacity or role in which one acts
noun, feminine Fixed frame: X की हैसियत से (X kii haisiyat se, 'in the capacity of X'), e.g. दोस्त की हैसियत से (dost kii haisiyat se, 'as a friend').
Synonyms: हैसियत से (haisiyat se, fixed phrase), रूप में (roop men, as/in the role of)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you will meet in slightly formal contexts -- speeches, official letters, or someone clarifying why they have a right to speak on a matter, as in एक पिता की हैसियत से (ek pitaa kii haisiyat se, 'as a father') mein kahtaa huun.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar with रूप में (roop men) for 'as/in the role of' may not realize हैसियत से (haisiyat se) is the more natural choice specifically when the role carries some authority or official capacity, e.g. एक अधिकारी की हैसियत से (ek adhikaarii kii haisiyat se, 'in the capacity of an official').
Sample Sentences:
मैं एक पिता की हैसियत से यह बात कह रहा हूँ। I am saying this in the capacity of a father.
(main ek pitaa kii haisiyat se yah baat kah rahaa hoon।)
