हद हो गई (had ho gaii)

interjection A fixed exclamatory phrase meaning 'that's the limit!' or 'this is too much!' -- used to express exasperation or disbelief at something. Built from हद (had, 'limit') plus the perfective of हो जाना (ho jaanaa, 'to become').
Sense 1 : expression of exasperation at something outrageous or excessive
interjection Functions as a standalone exclamatory sentence rather than a clause that takes further grammatical arguments; the subject of the exasperation is usually stated separately, often with की (kii): जैसे इसकी हद हो गई (iskii had ho gaii, 'this is too much regarding this').
Synonyms: हद है (had hai, present-tense variant), यह तो हद हो गई (yah to had ho gaii, fuller emphatic form), बहुत हो गया (bahut ho gayaa, 'that's enough now')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of those phrases you hear constantly in Delhi households and markets -- a mother scolding a child, a shopkeeper reacting to an unreasonable haggle, a friend venting about traffic. It pairs naturally with a following clause explaining the outrage, like हद हो गई, अब ये भी सुनना पड़ेगा (had ho gaii, ab ye bhii sunnaa padegaa, 'that's the limit, now I have to hear this too'). The underlying noun हद (had) also shows up in the common phrase हद से ज़्यादा (had se zyaadaa, 'beyond limit, excessively').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate this word-for-word as 'limit has become,' missing that it functions idiomatically as a fixed exclamation of frustration or shock, not a literal description. 2) It can express either anger or amused disbelief depending on tone, so learners should listen for the speaker's intonation rather than assuming it's always negative. 3) The present-tense version हद है (had hai) is equally common and interchangeable in most everyday contexts, so don't assume हो गई (ho gaii) is the only correct form.
