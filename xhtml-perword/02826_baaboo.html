बाबू (baaboo)

noun बाबू (baaboo) started as a respectful term for a gentleman or clerk and has spun off several everyday senses: a polite/affectionate address to a man or boy, a bureaucratic clerk (often with mild mockery), and a term of endearment for a little boy.
Sense 1 : affectionate term of address for a boy or son
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बेटा (betaa), सोना (sonaa, endearment)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear mothers, grandmothers, and aunties in Delhi call a little boy बाबू (baaboo) the way English might say 'sweetheart' or 'little man' -- it is warm, domestic, and very common at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use बाबू (baaboo) for a grown man expecting the same warm, childlike tone it has with boys -- with adult men the word shifts register toward the clerk sense or a more formal/joking address, so check context. 2) It is not gender-neutral -- it addresses boys/men, never girls or women.

Sense 2 : clerk or low-level office/government employee
noun, masculine
Synonyms: क्लर्क (klark), मुंशी (munshii, older term)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense goes back to British-era offices, where बाबू (baaboo) named Indian clerks who handled paperwork for the colonial administration -- the word still evokes government-office slowness and stamped forms, the classic sarkaari daftar (government office) image.
Common Mistakes: 1) The word can carry a gently mocking or bureaucratic-red-tape connotation (as in the old colonial 'babu' stereotype) -- it is not a neutral job title like 'employee,' so using it to describe someone's profession formally can sound sarcastic.

Sense 3 : polite/respectful address to a gentleman, often in Bihar/UP usage
noun, masculine
Synonyms: साहब (saahab)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In parts of Bihar and eastern UP, बाबू (baaboo) functions like साहब (saahab) -- a respectful way to address or refer to a landowning or educated gentleman; in Delhi-NCR speech this sense is less active and mostly recognized rather than used.
