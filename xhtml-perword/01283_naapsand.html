नापसंद (naapsand)

adjective A single-sense word meaning 'disliked' or 'displeasing' -- built from ना (naa, 'not/no') plus पसंद (pasand, 'liked'), the negative mirror of पसंद (pasand).
Sense 1 : disliked, distasteful
adjective Used with होना (honaa) or आना (aanaa) in a dative-subject frame: मुझे यह नापसंद है (mujhe yah naapsand hai, 'I dislike this') or मुझे यह नापसंद आया (mujhe yah naapsand aayaa, 'I disliked this / this struck me as displeasing').
Synonyms: अप्रिय (apriy, formal), नफ़रत भरा (nafrat bharaa, stronger)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of नापसंद (naapsand) as the more 'stated' cousin of the everyday negation पसंद नहीं (pasand nahiin) -- both mean 'don't like', but नापसंद (naapsand) shows up more in slightly formal contexts, like a boss explaining why a proposal was rejected, or a review calling a plan 'नापसंद' (naapsand). At the dinner table, most Delhi households will just say मुझे यह पसंद नहीं (mujhe yah pasand nahiin) rather than reach for this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make नापसंद (naapsand) agree in gender/number like a regular adjective, but it stays invariant regardless of what it describes, much like पसंद (pasand). 2) It's tempting to negate पसंद (pasand) with नहीं (nahiin) instead -- मुझे यह पसंद नहीं है (mujhe yah pasand nahiin hai) is far more common in casual speech than reaching for नापसंद (naapsand), which can sound a touch more deliberate or formal.
