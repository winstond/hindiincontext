क़ीमत (qiimat)

noun Primarily 'price/cost' in everyday market talk, extending naturally to a figurative 'worth/value' sense -- what something (or someone) is worth beyond money.
Sense 1 : price, cost
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दाम (daam), मूल्य (mooly, formal), भाव (bhaav, market rate)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear constantly at a Delhi bazaar or with a rickshaw-wallah -- 'इसकी क़ीमत बताओ' (iskii qiimat bataao, 'tell me its price') is practically a ritual opener to bargaining. Also spelled कीमत (kiimat) without the nuqta, which is extremely common and not considered wrong.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दाम (daam) or मूल्य (mooly) by default; क़ीमत (qiimat) is the everyday word you'll actually hear while shopping -- 'क़ीमत क्या है?' (qiimat kyaa hai?, 'What's the price?'). 2) The nuqta under the क़ is often dropped in casual writing (क़ीमत becomes कीमत), and both are read the same way by most speakers -- don't worry about it in speech.

Sense 2 : worth, value (figurative)
noun, feminine Common in the pattern X की क़ीमत (X kii qiimat, 'the worth/value of X') applied to time, relationships, effort, or life -- not just goods.
Synonyms: अहमियत (ahmiyat, importance), मूल्य (mooly, formal/abstract value)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in emotional or moralizing speech: 'वक़्त की क़ीमत समझो' (vaqt kii qiimat samjho, 'understand the value of time') is the kind of line elders say to kids, and 'हर चीज़ की एक क़ीमत होती है' (har chiiz kii ek qiimat hotii hai, 'everything has its price') carries a philosophical, almost proverbial weight.
Common Mistakes: 1) क़ीमत चुकाना (qiimat chukaanaa, 'to pay the price') is a set idiom for suffering consequences, not literally paying money -- learners sometimes miss the figurative reading. 2) Don't confuse with इज़्ज़त (izzat, 'honor/respect'), which is a related but distinct notion of social worth.
