चौरासी (chauraasii)

numeral/noun The cardinal number 'eighty-four' (chauraasii); also carries a well-known cultural resonance through the Hindu belief in चौरासी लाख योनियाँ (chauraasii laakh yoniyaan, 'eighty-four lakh forms of life'), the cycle of rebirth a soul is said to pass through before attaining moksha.
Sense 1 : the number 84
numeral
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Numerals in the 80s all begin with चौरा- (chauraa) or अस्सी (assii, eighty) as a base, but each one is really its own memorized word in spoken Hindi -- native speakers don't decompose them the way you might expect. If you're drilling numbers, treat चौरासी (chauraasii) as its own flashcard rather than trying to derive it logically.
Common Mistakes: 1) Like all Hindi numerals from 21-99, चौरासी (chauraasii) does not follow an obvious additive pattern from चौरा (chauraa) + असी (asii); it simply has to be learned as a whole word, the way English speakers just know 'eighty-four' rather than building it from parts. 2) Learners sometimes confuse it with चौरानवे (chauraanve, ninety-four) or चौबीस (chaubiis, twenty-four) because of the similar-sounding चौ- (chau) prefix; the rest of the word must be listened to carefully.

Sense 2 : the cycle of 8.4 million rebirths in Hindu cosmology
noun, feminine Used elliptically as a noun in phrases like चौरासी से मुक्ति (chauraasii se mukti, 'release from the eighty-four') or चौरासी काटना (chauraasii kaatnaa, 'to undergo/endure the cycle of eighty-four births'), where the full लाख योनियाँ (laakh yoniyaan) is dropped and just चौरासी (chauraasii) stands in for the whole concept.
Synonyms: चौरासी लाख योनियाँ (chauraasii laakh yoniyaan, 'eighty-four lakh forms of life', full traditional phrase)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: Older relatives, especially grandparents, might sigh and say something like चौरासी का चक्र काटना पड़ता है (chauraasii kaa chakr kaatnaa padtaa hai, 'one has to cut through the cycle of eighty-four') when talking about the soul's long journey through rebirths before reaching moksha (moksh). It's the kind of phrase you'll hear at a satsang (satsang) or from a spiritually-minded elder rather than in casual daily chat.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only surfaces in religious or philosophical speech (talk of moksha, punarjanm (punarjanm, rebirth), or karma); using चौरासी (chauraasii) this way in an ordinary counting context will just sound like the number and confuse the listener without more context. 2) Learners sometimes try to translate this literally as 'eighty-four' in English mid-sentence, but native speakers hear the whole cultural concept packed into the single word, similar to how 'nirvana' carries more than its literal sense in English.
