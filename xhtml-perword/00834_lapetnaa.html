लपेटना (lapetnaa)

verb A transitive verb meaning 'to wrap, fold, or roll something up' -- used for physical wrapping (a shawl, a roti) and also figuratively for wrapping someone up in trouble or vague talk.
Sense 1 : to wrap, fold, roll up (a physical object)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बाँधना (baandhnaa, to tie/bind, broader), मोड़ना (modnaa, to fold/bend)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a Delhi winter evening: someone pulls a shawl tight and says शॉल लपेट लो (shol lapet lo, 'wrap the shawl around yourself'). This is the verb's home turf -- wrapping cloth, rolling a roti around sabzi, coiling a wire. It is a very physical, hands-on verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse यह (yah) sense with बाँधना (baandhnaa): लपेटना (lapetnaa) is about coiling or folding something around/into itself (a shawl around the body, a roti rolled up), while बाँधना (baandhnaa) is about tying with a cord or knot -- they overlap but are not interchangeable in most contexts.

Sense 2 : to drag someone into trouble, implicate, entangle (figurative)
verb, transitive Often used in the passive/completed form, e.g. लपेट में आना (lapet men aanaa, 'to get caught up in something'), or with लेना (lenaa) as a compound: लपेट लेना (lapet lenaa).
Synonyms: फँसाना (phansaanaa, to trap/ensnare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in gossip or news-chat register: पुलिस ने उसे भी लपेट लिया (pulis ne use bhii lapet liyaa, 'the police roped him in too'). The image is of someone getting coiled into a net of trouble along with others -- a vivid metaphor extending the physical wrapping sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is often missed by learners who only know the 'wrap cloth' meaning; in casual speech किसी को लपेट लेना (kisii ko lapet lenaa) means to pull someone into a mess or scandal, not literally wrap them in anything.
