हफ़्ता (haftaa)

noun Means 'week' as a unit of time; also has a well-known colloquial extension meaning a regular bribe or protection-money payment ('haftaa vasuulii'). Also spelled हफ्ता (haphtaa) without the nuqta.
Sense 1 : week
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सप्ताह (saptaah, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is your everyday word for 'week' -- you will hear it constantly in things like 'हफ़्ते में दो बार' (hafte men do baar, 'twice a week') or 'पिछले हफ़्ते' (pichhle hafte, 'last week'). सप्ताह (saptaah) exists and is common in the news or on calendars, but in the bazaar or at home, हफ़्ता (haftaa) is king.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सप्ताह (saptaah) by default because it looks more 'textbook,' but हफ़्ता (haftaa) is what people actually say in daily conversation -- 'इस हफ़्ते' (is hafte, 'this week') is far more natural than 'इस सप्ताह' in casual speech. 2) The oblique form हफ़्ते (hafte) is used with postpositions like में (men) and से (se) -- learners sometimes leave it unchanged as हफ़्ता (haftaa) in these phrases.
Sample Sentences:
मैं हर हफ़्ते अपनी दादी से मिलने जाता हूँ। I go to meet my grandmother every week.
(main har hafte apnii daadii se milne jaataa hoon।)

Sense 2 : weekly bribe / protection money
noun, masculine Almost always appears in the fixed phrase हफ़्ता वसूलना (haftaa vasoolnaa, 'to collect protection money') or हफ़्ता देना (haftaa denaa, 'to pay off/pay protection money').
Synonyms: रंगदारी (rangdaarii, colloquial/underworld term)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense comes straight from the world of Bollywood gangster films and real news reports about local dadas (thugs) or police 'collecting हफ़्ता (haftaa)' from shopkeepers every week -- hence the name, since such payments were traditionally made on a weekly cycle. It carries a strong whiff of corruption and the underworld, so use it knowingly.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is easy to miss entirely -- a learner hearing 'दुकानदार हफ़्ता देता है' (dukaandaar haftaa detaa hai, 'the shopkeeper pays off [someone]') might wrongly assume it just means 'the shopkeeper pays [something] weekly' rather than understanding it as a bribe to local goons or corrupt police.

यहाँ हर दुकानदार को पुलिस को हफ़्ता देना पड़ता है। Here every shopkeeper has to pay protection money to the police.
