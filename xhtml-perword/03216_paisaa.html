पैसा (paisaa)

noun Two main senses: (1) money in general -- the everyday word for cash/wealth, almost always used in its plural/oblique form पैसे (paise); (2) the historical small coin unit (paisa), now mostly a fossil sense inside idioms and formal pricing.
Sense 1 : money, cash, wealth
noun, masculine In this everyday sense the word almost always appears in its oblique plural form पैसे (paise) -- 'मेरे पास पैसे हैं' (mere paas paise hain, 'I have money'), even when referring to an unspecified amount rather than literal coins.
Synonyms: धन (dhan, formal), दौलत (daulat), रुपया (rupyaa, often used loosely for 'money' too)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: पैसा (paisaa) is one of the first words every learner picks up, and in Delhi bargaining culture it's almost a verb of its own -- 'पैसे कम करो' (paise kam karo, 'lower the price') is heard in every bazaar. Note the family of related idioms: पैसा फेंकना (paisaa phenknaa, to throw money around) and पैसे वाला (paise vaalaa, a wealthy person) both lean on this sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say पैसा है (paisaa hai) when they mean 'I have money' generally; native speakers overwhelmingly use the plural पैसे हैं (paise hain) here -- the singular पैसा (paisaa) sounds like you're talking about one specific paisa-coin or a very small, countable sum. 2) पैसा कमाना (paisaa kamaanaa, 'to earn money') keeps the singular in this fixed collocation even though 'having money' takes the plural -- there's no clean rule, it's just how the idiom sits.

Sense 2 : a paisa (1/100 of a rupee); a small coin/unit of currency
noun, masculine This sense survives mainly in fixed prices (50 पैसे, 50 paise) and in idioms about small amounts; the physical coin itself is essentially obsolete in circulation today.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Older Delhi-wallahs will still say एक पैसा भी नहीं (ek paisaa bhii nahiin, 'not even a single paisa') to mean 'not a bit,' a fossil of the days when the paisa was real spending currency -- much like English 'I don't have a penny to my name.'
