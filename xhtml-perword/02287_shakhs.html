शख़्स (shakhs)

noun A single sense: 'person, individual.' A common everyday word borrowed from Persian/Urdu, frequently heard in spoken Hindi and Bollywood dialogue.
Sense 1 : person, individual
noun, masculine
Synonyms: व्यक्ति (vyakti), इंसान (insaan), आदमी (aadmii, often specifically 'man', but also used generically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word has a slightly literary, storytelling feel to it -- you will hear it constantly in Bollywood songs and dialogues ('woh shakhs jo...' vo shakhs jo) where English would just say 'that person' or 'that guy.' In plain daily chat about people around you, इंसान (insaan) or आदमी (aadmii) often feels more natural, while शख़्स (shakhs) adds a touch of narrative distance or intrigue, like referring to 'a certain individual' rather than just 'someone.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume शख़्स (shakhs) is neutral like 'person' in English, but it usually implies a specific, sometimes mysterious or notable individual -- 'ek shakhs' (ek shakhs, 'a certain person') often carries a slight narrative or dramatic flavor, as in storytelling or song lyrics. 2) The nuqta under 'ख' is often dropped in casual writing/typing (शक्स), but the pronunciation with the throaty 'ख़' sound is still expected in careful speech.
