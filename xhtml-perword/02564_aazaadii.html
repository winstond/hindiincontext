आज़ादी (aazaadii)

noun A single core sense: 'freedom/independence,' used both for political independence and personal freedom. Related to the adjective आज़ाद (aazaad, 'free').
Sense 1 : freedom, independence (political or personal)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa, formal), मुक्ति (mukti, liberation, often spiritual/political)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian schoolchild learns first for 1947: आज़ादी (aazaadii) is what people say, both in casual talk and on August 15th, far more than the more bookish स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa). It also stretches easily into everyday life, as in wanting आज़ादी (aazaadii) from parents' rules or a boss's control, so the word carries an emotional charge of liberation, not just a dry legal-political sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa) by default because it looks like a textbook 'freedom,' but आज़ादी (aazaadii) is what people actually say in daily speech, including for personal freedoms like आज़ादी से रहना (aazaadii se rahnaa, 'to live freely'), not just national independence. 2) When talking about being given permission or leeway (e.g. by parents), आज़ादी (aazaadii) with दी (dii) or मिली (milii) is natural; स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa) sounds stiff in that context.
Sample Sentences:
भारत को 1947 में आज़ादी मिली थी। India got its freedom in 1947.
(bhaarat ko 1947 men aazaadii milii thii।)
