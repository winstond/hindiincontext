शर्म (sharm)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'shame' or 'shyness/modesty' -- covers both the moral sting of embarrassment and the soft social virtue of bashfulness, depending on context.
Sense 1 : shame, embarrassment
noun, feminine Very commonly paired with the verb आना (aanaa) in the dative-subject frame: मुझे शर्म आती है (mujhe sharm aatii hai, 'I feel ashamed') -- shame is treated as something that 'comes to' you, not something you actively feel.
Synonyms: लज्जा (lajjaa, literary), शर्मिंदगी (sharmindgii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries real social weight in Indian family life -- elders invoke it constantly to enforce behavior, as in शर्म नहीं आती? (sharm nahiin aatii?, 'don't you feel ashamed?'), a classic parental rebuke. The idiom शर्म से डूब जाना (sharm se doob jaanaa, 'to drown in shame') captures how overwhelming this feeling is imagined to be.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं शर्म हूँ (main sharm hoon) by analogy with English 'I am ashamed' -- but Hindi uses the dative-subject construction शर्म आना (sharm aanaa) or शर्म लगना (sharm lagnaa) instead, with को (ko) marking the person who feels it. 2) शर्म करना (sharm karnaa) does not mean 'to feel shame' -- it means 'to have shame/decency' about doing something, often used as a scolding: कुछ तो शर्म करो (kuchh to sharm karo, 'have some shame').
Sample Sentences:

(tumhen yah karke sharm nahiin aatii?)

Sense 2 : shyness, modesty, bashfulness
noun, feminine In this sense शर्म (sharm) describes a positive social quality, especially expected of women or children in traditional settings, rather than a negative feeling.
Synonyms: हया (hayaa), संकोच (sankoch)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A new bride's शर्म (sharm) -- her downcast eyes and quiet manner -- is traditionally seen as a mark of good upbringing rather than weakness, though this expectation is increasingly questioned by younger generations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss that शर्मीला (sharmiilaa, 'shy [person]') is the adjective built from this sense, describing a personality trait rather than a momentary feeling.
