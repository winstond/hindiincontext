हरेक (harek)

adjective/pronoun A compound of हर (har, 'every') and एक (ek, 'one'), meaning 'each one' or 'every single one' -- used as both an adjective before a noun and a standalone pronoun.
Sense 1 : every, each
adjective
Synonyms: हर (har), प्रत्येक (pratyek, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of हरेक (harek) as हर (har) with a bit more emphatic punch -- it's what you'd use when you want to stress that not a single one is left out, like a teacher saying हरेक बच्चे को ध्यान से सुनना है (harek bachche ko dhyaan se sunnaa hai, 'each and every child must listen carefully').
Common Mistakes: 1) Like हर (har), हरेक (harek) is always followed by a singular noun even though the meaning is distributive/plural in sense: हरेक बच्चा (harek bachchaa, 'every child'), not a plural form. 2) Learners sometimes treat हरेक (harek) and हर (har) as interchangeable in all contexts, but हरेक (harek) carries slightly more emphasis on individuality ('each and every one') and is also comfortable standing alone as a pronoun, unlike हर (har) which almost always needs a following noun.

Sense 2 : each one, everyone (standalone)
pronoun Used without a following noun, standing in for 'each person/thing' already understood from context: हरेक अपनी जगह बैठ गया (harek apnii jagah baith gayaa, 'each one sat down in his place').
Synonyms: हर कोई (har koii), प्रत्येक (pratyek, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This pronoun use is handy when you don't want to repeat the noun -- like a shopkeeper saying हरेक का दाम अलग है (harek kaa daam alag hai, 'each one has a different price') without restating what 'each one' refers to.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a standalone pronoun, हरेक (harek) takes third-person singular verb agreement even when referring to a group -- learners sometimes want to pluralize the verb.
Sample Sentences:
हरेक का दाम अलग है। Each one has a different price.
(harek kaa daam alag hai।)
