सीधे हाथ (siidhe haath)

adverb A directional phrase meaning 'to/on the right hand (side)'; built from सीधा (siidhaa, 'straight/right') plus हाथ (haath, 'hand'), paralleling उल्टे हाथ (ulte haath, 'to the left').
Sense 1 : to/on the right-hand side (giving directions)
adverb Used as a directional adverb, typically with movement or location verbs: सीधे हाथ मुड़ना (siidhe haath mudnaa, 'to turn right').
Synonyms: दाहिने (daahine), दाईं तरफ़ (daaiin taraf), दाईं ओर (daaiin or)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday way auto-wallahs, rickshaw drivers, and locals give directions in Delhi -- you will hear सीधे हाथ (siidhe haath) and उल्टे हाथ (ulte haath) far more often than the more formal दाईं ओर (daaiin or) and बाईं ओर (baaiin or) in spoken directions.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse सीधे हाथ (siidhe haath, 'to the right') with सीधे (siidhe, 'straight ahead') used alone -- the हाथ (haath) part is essential to get the 'right side' meaning rather than 'straight'. 2) The opposite is उल्टे हाथ (ulte haath), not गलत हाथ (galat haath) -- उल्टा (ultaa) here means 'reverse/left', not 'wrong'.
Sample Sentences:

(siidhe haath mud jaanaa।)
