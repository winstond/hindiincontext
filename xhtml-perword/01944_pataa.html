पता (pataa)

noun/verb Two distinct senses share this spelling: the noun meaning 'address' or 'knowledge/awareness' (as in the very common phrase पता होना (pataa honaa)), and the same noun used with चलना (chalnaa) or लगना (lagnaa) to mean 'to find out.'
Sense 1 : knowledge, awareness (of a fact)
noun, masculine Used almost always with होना (honaa) in a dative-subject frame: X को पता है (X ko pataa hai) = 'X knows.' The knower takes को (ko), not ने (ne).
Synonyms: जानकारी (jaankaarii), इल्म (ilm, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first dative-subject constructions learners meet outside लगना (lagnaa): पता (pataa) behaves like a possessed state rather than an action, so Hindi frames 'knowing' as something that is present to you, not something you do. You will hear किसे पता? (kise pataa?, 'who knows?') used almost like a shrug in casual conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं पता हूँ (main pataa hoon) by analogy with 'I know' -- the correct frame is मुझे पता है (mujhe pataa hai), with the knower marked by को (ko) and पता (pataa) treated as a fixed noun-subject with है (hai). 2) In the past, this becomes मुझे पता था (mujhe pataa thaa), not मैंने पता किया (mainne pataa kiyaa) -- that phrasing means something else (see 'to find out' sense).
Sample Sentences:
मुझे इस बात का पता है। I know about this.
(mujhe is baat kaa pataa hai।)

Sense 2 : address (postal/location)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ठिकाना (thikaanaa, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Asking अपना पता बताइए (apnaa pataa bataaie, 'please tell me your address') is a routine, slightly formal request -- couriers, hotel clerks, and form-fillers all reach for पता (pataa) rather than any fancier word.
Common Mistakes: 1) घर का पता (ghar kaa pataa, 'home address') uses का (kaa) to link the place and the noun, which learners sometimes replace with में (men) or की (kii) by mistake, forgetting पता (pataa) is masculine.

Sense 3 : to find out, come to know (as part of the compound पता चलना/लगना)
verb, intransitive Functions only inside the fixed compounds पता चलना (pataa chalnaa) and पता लगना (pataa lagnaa), both intransitive with a को (ko)-marked experiencer: उसे पता चला (use pataa chalaa, 'he found out'). पता लगाना (pataa lagaanaa) is the transitive counterpart, 'to find out/investigate' with an agentive subject and ने (ne) in the past.
Synonyms: मालूम होना (maaloom honaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pair (पता चलना (pataa chalnaa) vs. पता लगाना (pataa lagaanaa)) is a nice small window into Hindi's habit of separating things that happen to you from things you deliberately do -- news 'arrives' at you (चलना (chalnaa)), but a detective 'applies effort' to dig it up (लगाना (lagaanaa)).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse पता चलना (pataa chalnaa) (something becomes known to you, intransitive) with पता लगाना (pataa lagaanaa) (you actively find something out, transitive, takes ने (ne) in the past). 2) मुझे पता चला (mujhe pataa chalaa) is often mistranslated word-for-word as 'to me knowledge moved' instead of the natural 'I found out' -- the compound should be learned as a unit, not built from parts.

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
