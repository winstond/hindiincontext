डराना (daraanaa)

verb The causative of डरना (darnaa, 'to be afraid'): डराना (daraanaa) means 'to scare, to frighten someone.' One straightforward transitive sense.
Sense 1 : to scare, to frighten
verb, transitive
Synonyms: भयभीत करना (bhaybhiit karnaa, formal), धमकाना (dhamkaanaa, to threaten/intimidate rather than simply scare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic causative pair: डरना (darnaa) 'to fear' becomes डराना (daraanaa) 'to make fear/scare' by the -आना (-aanaa) causative pattern, the same mechanism you see in सोना (sonaa) to सुलाना (sulaanaa) or बैठना (baithnaa) to बैठाना (baithaanaa). You'll hear it a lot from parents and grandparents scolding kids playfully with 'भूत आ जायेगा, मत डराओ अपने भाई को' (bhoot aa jaayegaa, mat daraao apne bhaaii ko, 'the ghost will come, don't scare your brother') -- ghost stories are a classic Indian household tool for this verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse डराना (daraanaa) with डरना (darnaa) itself -- remember डरना (darnaa) is what the frightened person does ('मैं डरता हूँ' main dartaa hoon, 'I am afraid'), while डराना (daraanaa) is what the frightener does to someone else ('वह मुझे डराता है' vah mujhe daraataa hai, 'he scares me'). 2) The person being scared takes को (ko), not से (se) -- 'बच्चे को डराना' (bachche ko daraanaa, 'to scare the child'), not 'बच्चे से'.

बच्चे भूतों की कहानियों से एक-दूसरे को डराते हैं। Children scare each other with ghost stories.
(bachche bhooton kii kahaaniyon se ek-doosre ko daraate hain।)
