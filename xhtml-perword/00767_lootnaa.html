लूटना (lootnaa)

verb One core sense: to loot, rob, or plunder -- used literally for dacoity/robbery and very commonly in extended/figurative senses for being overcharged or 'robbed' in a transaction.
Sense 1 : to loot, rob, plunder (people, a place, wealth)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: डाका डालना (daakaa daalnaa, to commit dacoity), चोरी करना (chorii karnaa, to steal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind डाकू (daakoo, 'dacoit/bandit'), a figure deeply embedded in Hindi cinema and folklore -- think of the countless films about dacoits of the Chambal region looting villages and travelers. It carries a sense of dramatic, often violent robbery, distinct from quieter theft.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use चोरी करना (chorii karnaa) and लूटना (lootnaa) interchangeably, but लूटना (lootnaa) implies open, often violent seizure (like dacoits robbing a bank or travelers), while चोरी (chorii) is stealthy theft where the victim doesn't see it happen. 2) The object of लूटना (lootnaa) can be the victim or the goods: बैंक लूटना (baink lootnaa, 'to rob the bank') and पैसे लूटना (paise lootnaa, 'to loot money') are both correct.

(daakuon ne raat men poore gaanv ko loot liyaa।)

Sense 2 : to rip off, overcharge, fleece (figurative)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: ठगना (thagnaa, to cheat/con), चूना लगाना (choonaa lagaanaa, colloquial idiom for cheating someone financially)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi markets and auto-rickshaw haggling -- अरे, ये तो लूट है! (are, ye to loot hai, 'hey, this is daylight robbery!') is a everyday exclamation over an inflated price, using the noun form लूट (loot) rather than the verb, but the two are closely linked in the same breath of complaint.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is extremely common in everyday complaints about shopping, taxis, or restaurants -- दुकानदार ने मुझे लूट लिया (dukaandaar ne mujhe loot liyaa, 'the shopkeeper ripped me off') -- and learners who only know the literal 'dacoity' meaning may miss this casual, non-violent use. 2) Often paired with the compound लूट लेना (loot lenaa) to emphasize the completed, decisive 'fleecing' rather than the plain verb alone.
