मांस (maans)

noun A noun meaning 'meat/flesh' -- one core sense, with a natural extension to 'flesh' in the bodily/anatomical sense. Also spelled मांस (maans) with the chandrabindu-less anusvara or माँस (maans) with a candra; both are heard, though माँस (maans) is somewhat more common in careful pronunciation.
Sense 1 : meat (as food)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गोश्त (gosht)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a country with a huge vegetarian population, the words for meat carry real social weight -- asking someone शाकाहारी या मांसाहारी? (shaakaahaarii yaa maansaahaarii?, 'vegetarian or non-vegetarian?') is a completely normal, non-awkward question at any Delhi dinner party or wedding buffet, unlike in many English-speaking contexts where it might feel intrusive.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मांस (maans) and गोश्त (gosht) as if fully interchangeable; in everyday speech गोश्त (gosht) is what you hear at the butcher's shop and on menus (especially for mutton/chicken preparations), while मांस (maans) sounds a touch more formal or clinical, closer to 'flesh/meat' in a general or written sense. 2) मांसाहारी (maansaahaarii, 'meat-eating/non-vegetarian') and its opposite शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii, 'vegetarian') are built directly from this word -- worth learning as a pair since they come up constantly on food labels and in conversation about diet.

Sense 2 : flesh (of the body)
noun, masculine Used for bodily flesh in anatomical, medical, or figurative descriptions, distinct from the food sense though the same word is used for both, much like English 'flesh' can shift between the two.
Synonyms: गोश्त (gosht)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense in phrases like हाड़-मांस का (haad-maans kaa, 'made of flesh and bone') used to emphasize someone's ordinary, mortal humanity -- a nice window into how Hindi builds vivid compound expressions from basic body-part words.
