मनाना (manaanaa)

verb The causative of मानना (maannaa, 'to believe/consider/obey'), covering (1) persuading/coaxing someone, (2) celebrating an occasion or festival, and (3) appeasing or placating someone who is upset.
Sense 1 : to persuade, coax, talk someone into something
verb, transitive Takes a person as object, often with a following infinitive clause expressing what they are being persuaded to do or accept.
Synonyms: राज़ी करना (raazii karnaa), मनवाना (manvaanaa, causative variant, subtly different force)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic word for the coaxing that happens when someone is sulking or refusing to budge -- a parent 'मनाता है' (manaataa hai) a child who won't eat, or friends 'मनाते हैं' (manaate hain) someone who is upset and giving silent treatment. It's a very relationship-oriented verb, tied closely to Hindi's rich vocabulary around moods and persuasion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse मनाना (manaanaa) with मनवाना (manvaanaa): मनाना (manaanaa) means to talk someone into agreeing or coming around, while मनवाना (manvaanaa) means to get your own point/demand accepted by someone else -- the direction of whose will 'wins' is different. 2) Don't mix this up with मानना (maannaa, 'to believe/accept') itself -- मनाना (manaanaa) is specifically the act of working on someone else to bring them to that state.

Sense 2 : to celebrate (a festival, occasion, birthday)
verb, transitive Takes the occasion or festival as its direct object: दिवाली मनाना (divaalii manaanaa, 'to celebrate Diwali'), जन्मदिन मनाना (janmdin manaanaa, 'to celebrate a birthday').
Synonyms: जश्न मनाना (jashn manaanaa, compound form, used for festive/joyous celebration specifically), उत्सव मनाना (utsav manaanaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly around festival season -- होली कैसे मनाई? (holii kaise manaaii?, 'how did you celebrate Holi?') is a completely normal way to catch up with someone in March. It's the verb every Hindi speaker reaches for when talking about festivals, weddings, and personal milestones alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for 'celebrate' verbs borrowed loosely from English rather than this native construction -- मनाना (manaanaa) is by far the natural, default way to say 'celebrate X' in Hindi for any festival or occasion.

Sense 3 : to appease, placate, pacify someone who is angry or upset
verb, transitive
Synonyms: शांत करना (shaant karnaa, broader, calming down anger generally), फुसलाना (phuslaanaa, leans more toward cajoling/coaxing with sweet talk)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word behind the common domestic scene of one family member sulking (रूठना, roothnaa) and another going to 'मनाने' (manaane) them -- a whole cultural ritual pair, रूठना-मनाना (roothnaa-manaanaa), that shows up in Hindi film songs and everyday family life alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps closely with the 'persuade' sense above -- context (someone visibly upset or angry vs. someone simply undecided) is what distinguishes 'appease' from 'persuade' rather than any difference in grammar.
