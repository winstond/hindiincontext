ज़ोरदार (zordaar)

adjective An adjective meaning 'forceful' or 'vigorous' -- built from ज़ोर (zor, 'force/strength') plus the Persian agentive suffix -दार (daar). It covers both literal physical force (a strong push, a loud sound) and figurative intensity (a rousing speech, a hearty laugh).
Sense 1 : forceful, powerful (physical intensity)
adjective
Synonyms: ताकतवर (taakatvar), शक्तिशाली (shaktishaalii, formal), तेज़ (tez, meaning scope: also covers 'fast/sharp')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in weather and traffic reports: ज़ोरदार बारिश (zordaar baarish, 'heavy/forceful rain') or ज़ोरदार धमाका (zordaar dhamaakaa, 'a loud explosion'). It paints the sound or impact as big and unmistakable, which is exactly the flavor a newsreader wants.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तेज़ (tez) for everything intense, but ज़ोरदार (zordaar) specifically implies force or power behind the thing (a punch, a jerk, an explosion), not just speed or sharpness. 2) It describes the intensity of an action or sound, not a person's general strength -- for 'he is strong,' ताकतवर (taakatvar) or मज़बूत (mazboot) fits better.

Sense 2 : rousing, enthusiastic, impressive (figurative intensity)
adjective
Synonyms: शानदार (shaandaar), जबरदस्त (jabardast), बढ़िया (badhiyaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for a speech that gets the crowd on its feet, or a cricket shot that draws gasps -- ज़ोरदार भाषण (zordaar bhaashan, 'a rousing speech') is a classic collocation you will hear on news channels covering political rallies.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes underuse this word for praise -- ज़ोरदार तालियाँ (zordaar taaliyaan, 'thunderous applause') and ज़ोरदार स्वागत (zordaar svaagat, 'a rousing welcome') are set phrases native speakers reach for automatically, so 'बहुत अच्छा (bahut achchhaa)' can sound flat by comparison in these contexts.
