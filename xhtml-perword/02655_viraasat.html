विरासत (viraasat)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'inheritance/heritage' -- covering both the concrete legal sense (property passed down) and the abstract cultural sense (heritage, legacy).
Sense 1 : inheritance, heritage, legacy
noun, feminine
Synonyms: धरोहर (dharohar), उत्तराधिकार (uttraadhikaar, formal, legal), मीरास (miiraas, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear विरासत में मिलना (viraasat men milnaa) constantly in everyday talk -- not just about property, but about a skill, a business, a way of cooking, even a temperament, that someone 'got as inheritance' from their family. It's also the word stamped on UNESCO heritage-site signage in India (विश्व विरासत स्थल, vishv viraasat sthal, 'World Heritage Site'), so the word sits comfortably between a family kitchen and a government plaque.
Common Mistakes: 1) विरासत (viraasat) is used for both physical property/wealth passed down ("पैतृक विरासत" paitrik viraasat, 'ancestral inheritance') and intangible cultural legacy ("सांस्कृतिक विरासत" saanskritik viraasat, 'cultural heritage') -- learners sometimes reach for a separate word for the abstract sense, but Hindi speakers use the same word for both. 2) It takes में (men) when talking about something passed down 'in' a family line ("यह हुनर उसे विरासत में मिला" yah hunar use viraasat men milaa, 'he got this skill as an inheritance') -- this विरासत में मिलना (viraasat men milnaa) construction is the most common everyday use of the word, more common than विरासत alone as a plain object.

यह हवेली उसे अपने परिवार से विरासत में मिली है। This mansion has come to her as an inheritance from her family.
(yah havelii use apne parivaar se viraasat men milii hai।)
