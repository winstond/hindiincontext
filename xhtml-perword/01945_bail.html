बैल (bail)

noun A single-sense everyday noun: the male bovine used for farm labor and cart-pulling, with a common figurative extension for a dull, hard-working, or foolish person.
Sense 1 : ox / bullock
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बैला (bailaa, rare)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In rural North India, the बैल (bail) pair yoked to a plough or cart was, until tractors became common, the backbone of farm work -- you'll still hear older villagers measure a farmer's wealth partly by how many बैल (bail) he owned. The word also does double duty as a mild insult, बैल की तरह (bail kii tarah, 'like an ox') for someone who works hard without using their brain, or just काम के बैल (kaam ke bail, 'workhorse ox') for a tireless drudge.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse बैल (bail) with गाय (gaay, 'cow') or भैंस (bhains, 'buffalo') -- बैल (bail) specifically refers to the castrated male ox used for plowing and pulling carts, not a generic 'bull' (which is साँड़ saand).

Sense 2 : dull-witted, brutish person (figurative)
noun, masculine Used as a colloquial insult or affectionate tease, often in the vocative or in phrases like बैल हो क्या (bail ho kyaa, 'are you an ox or what')
Synonyms: गधा (gadhaa, similar figurative insult), उल्लू (ulloo, similar figurative insult)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone बैल (bail) suggests they are strong but slow-witted or stubborn -- think of it as the Hindi cousin of calling someone an 'ox' or 'dumb brute' in English, usually said in exasperation among friends or family rather than as a serious insult.
