वाणी (vaanii)

noun A somewhat literary/formal word for 'voice' or 'speech' in the sense of one's manner of speaking or power of expression -- not the everyday word for the physical voice/sound (that is आवाज़ (aavaaz)).
Sense 1 : voice, speech, manner of speaking
noun, feminine
Synonyms: आवाज़ (aavaaz), वचन (vachan, literary), बोली (bolii, dialect/manner of speech)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet वाणी (vaanii) most often in set phrases praising how someone speaks -- मधुर वाणी (madhur vaanii, 'sweet-toned speech') or कर्ण-प्रिय वाणी (karn-priy vaanii, 'speech pleasant to the ear') -- and in formal or devotional contexts describing a person's gift of expression, such as a teacher's or leader's eloquence. It has a slightly elevated, almost reverent feel compared to the plain, physical आवाज़ (aavaaz).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for वाणी (vaanii) when they just mean the physical sound of someone's voice (e.g. 'speak in a low voice') -- in everyday speech आवाज़ (aavaaz) is the natural choice there, while वाणी (vaanii) is reserved for describing the quality or character of someone's speech, like मधुर वाणी (madhur vaanii, 'sweet speech').

Sense 2 : goddess of speech/learning (Saraswati), personified speech
noun, feminine Used as an epithet or metonym for the goddess Saraswati, who presides over speech, learning, and the arts.
Synonyms: सरस्वती (sarasvtii, name of the goddess), शारदा (shaardaa, literary/devotional)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: In devotional and literary contexts, वाणी (vaanii) can stand in for the goddess Saraswati herself, since she is considered the source and patron of speech and eloquence -- you will encounter this in hymns, school assembly prayers, or references to Vaani (vaanii) as a girl's name carrying this same blessing of eloquence.
