चाहना (chaahnaa)

verb Core verb of wanting/desiring; also the root behind the modal चाहिए (chaahie, 'should') and the frozen form चाहे (chaahe, 'whether/even if') seen in idioms. This entry covers only the base verb's own senses.
Sense 1 : to want, to wish for (something)
verb, transitive Commonly takes an infinitive + चाहना (chaahnaa) construction, e.g. जाना चाहता हूँ (jaanaa chaahtaa hoon, 'I want to go'), where चाहना (chaahnaa) agrees with the subject like a normal transitive verb.
Synonyms: इच्छा करना (ichchhaa karnaa, formal), चाह रखना (chaah rakhnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb Hindi speakers reach for when talking about wants and wishes in everyday life -- from a child saying मुझे खिलौना चाहिए नहीं, मैं खिलौना चाहता हूँ (mujhe khilaunaa chaahie nahiin, main khilaunaa chaahtaa hoon, 'I don't need a toy, I want a toy') to adults discussing life goals. Its subjunctive form चाहे (chaahe) survives independently in the common idiom चाहे कुछ भी हो (chaahe kuchh bhii ho, 'whatever happens'), which is worth recognizing as a relative.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चाहिए (chaahie) when they actually mean the plain verb चाहना (chaahnaa) -- चाहिए (chaahie) expresses obligation/need ('should'), while चाहना (chaahnaa) expresses personal desire ('want'); मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paanii chaahie, 'I need water') and मैं पानी चाहता हूँ (main paanii chaahtaa hoon, 'I want water') are not interchangeable in feel. 2) With a direct object, चाहना (chaahnaa) does not take ने (ne) in the past tense despite being transitive -- चाहा (chaahaa) agrees with the subject, not the object.

Sense 2 : to love, to care for (a person)
verb, transitive Used with a person as object to express affection, most often in romantic or deeply emotional contexts, e.g. मैं तुम्हें चाहता हूँ (main tumhen chaahtaa hoon, 'I love you' / 'I want you').
Synonyms: प्यार करना (pyaar karnaa), मोहब्बत करना (mohabbat karnaa, literary/romantic register), स्नेह करना (sneh karnaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in Bollywood song lyrics and romantic dialogue, where चाहना (chaahnaa) carries a softer, more poetic weight than the everyday प्यार करना (pyaar karnaa). It blurs desire and love in a way English keeps separate -- part of why this verb feels so central to Hindi film romance.
Common Mistakes: 1) मैं तुम्हें चाहता हूँ (main tumhen chaahtaa hoon) can mean either 'I want you' or 'I love you' depending on tone and context -- learners should listen for context rather than assuming one fixed meaning.
