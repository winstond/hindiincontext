मुख (mukh)

noun A somewhat formal/literary word for 'face' or 'mouth' (compare the everyday चेहरा (chehraa) and मुँह (munh)); it also lives on as a common adjective meaning 'main, chief' in compounds like मुख्य (mukhy) -- but as a standalone word today, its main job is the body-part sense plus a figurative 'front/opening' sense.
Sense 1 : face; mouth (as a body part)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चेहरा (chehraa), मुँह (munh), आनन (aanan, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet मुख (mukh) constantly in devotional contexts -- describing a deity's radiant face, as in the common phrase referring to the 'lotus face' (मुखकमल, mukhakmal) of a god or goddess -- which is why it carries a slightly reverent, elevated flavor even outside temple language.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this when चेहरा (chehraa) or मुँह (munh) would sound far more natural in everyday speech -- मुख (mukh) is the word you meet in formal writing, prayers, or poetic description of a face, not in casual conversation about someone's face.

Sense 2 : front part, opening, mouth of something (figurative/extended)
noun, masculine Used in compounds for the 'mouth' or 'front' of a non-human thing, e.g. नदी का मुख (nadii kaa mukh, 'the mouth of a river') or द्वारमुख (dvaarmukh, 'the face/front of a doorway').
Synonyms: द्वार (dvaar, entrance/opening sense), प्रवेश (pravesh, entrance sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same metaphor English speakers use for a 'river mouth' -- Hindi extends मुख (mukh) the same way, from a person's face to the 'face' or opening of a place, which makes this sense easy to grasp once you know the body-part meaning.
