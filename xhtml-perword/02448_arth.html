अर्थ (arth)

noun Primarily means 'meaning/sense' as in the meaning of a word; also extends to 'purpose/point' and, in compounds and formal registers, to 'wealth/money'.
Sense 1 : meaning, sense (of a word or statement)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मतलब (matlab), मायने (maayne, colloquial), आशय (aashay, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will see printed in dictionaries and textbooks -- 'शब्द का अर्थ' (shabd kaa arth, 'meaning of the word') is a classic classroom phrase in Indian schools. In daily chat, मतलब (matlab) is what people actually say more often; अर्थ (arth) shows up when things get a little more formal or written.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach only for मतलब (matlab) and forget अर्थ (arth) exists; in practice both are common, but अर्थ (arth) sounds a touch more bookish/precise, as in asking the meaning of a word in a dictionary or textbook context. 2) Do not confuse with अर्थात (arthaat, 'that is to say'), which is a related but separate connective word.

(is shabd kaa arth kyaa hai?)

Sense 2 : purpose, point, significance
noun, masculine Often appears in the negative construction 'कोई अर्थ नहीं' (koii arth nahiin, 'there's no point') to say something is pointless.
Synonyms: उद्देश्य (uddeshy, formal), मक़सद (maqsad)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense bridges 'meaning' and 'purpose' -- in Hindi, asking what something 'means' and asking what its 'point' is often use the very same word, which reflects a broader tendency to see significance and meaning as the same underlying idea.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'what's the point of this' too literally; the natural Hindi idiom is 'इसका क्या अर्थ है' (iskaa kyaa arth hai, 'what is the point/meaning of this') or 'इसमें क्या अर्थ है' (ismen kyaa arth hai), leaning on this extended sense of अर्थ (arth) rather than a literal 'point' word.

Sense 3 : wealth, money (formal/classical sense)
noun, masculine Survives mainly in compounds and set phrases rather than standalone conversational use.
Synonyms: धन (dhan), पैसा (paisaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same अर्थ (arth) found in classical Indian thought's four goals of life (पुरुषार्थ, purushaarth) -- धर्म, अर्थ, काम, मोक्ष (dharm, arth, kaam, moksh) -- where it stands for material prosperity. You'll meet it today mostly through words like अर्थशास्त्र (arthshaastr) rather than in everyday talk about cash.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is rarely used alone in casual speech; learners are far more likely to meet it inside the compound अर्थशास्त्र (arthshaastr, 'economics', literally 'the science of wealth') or the phrase अर्थव्यवस्था (arthavyvasthaa, 'economy') than as a standalone word for money.

(praachiin bhaartiiy chintan men dharm, arth, kaam aur moksh ko jiivan ke chaar purushaarth maanaa gayaa hai।)
