ब्राह्मण (braahman)

noun Refers to a person of the Brahmin caste/varna, traditionally associated with priesthood and scholarship; a single core meaning with a common extended use as a caste-surname/identity marker.
Sense 1 : member of the Brahmin caste; priest
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पंडित (pandit, often used for a priest specifically), भूसुर (bhoosur, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi-NCR speech you will hear this word in the context of caste surnames (Sharma, Mishra, Pandey, Tiwari, Dubey are common ब्राह्मण (braahman) surnames), matrimonial ads, and conversations about caste and reservation policy. It sits at the top of the traditional four-fold varna system (ब्राह्मण, क्षत्रिय, वैश्य, शूद्र), and discussing it often brushes up against politically sensitive territory around caste privilege and social justice, so tone and context matter a great deal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume ब्राह्मण (braahman) always means a practicing priest, but it primarily names the caste/varna; a ब्राह्मण (braahman) can be an engineer, teacher, or politician with no priestly role at all. 2) पंडित (pandit) is not a perfect synonym: it more specifically points to someone who performs rituals or is learned in scripture, while ब्राह्मण (braahman) names the caste identity itself.

Sense 2 : a woman of the Brahmin caste
noun, masculine The feminine form ब्राह्मणी (braahmnii) is the more usual way to specify a Brahmin woman; ब्राह्मण (braahman) itself defaults to the masculine or the generic/collective sense.
Synonyms: ब्राह्मणी (braahmnii)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense is included mainly so learners recognize the pattern: ब्राह्मण (braahman) is the base masculine/generic form, and ब्राह्मणी (braahmnii) is the dedicated feminine counterpart, similar to how many caste and community names work in Hindi.
