लड़की (ladkii)

noun लड़की (ladkii) is the everyday word for a young female person; it also gets used informally to mean 'daughter,' paralleling how लड़का (ladkaa) can mean 'son.'
Sense 1 : girl
noun, feminine
Synonyms: छोकरी (chhokrii, colloquial, sometimes disrespectful), बाला (baalaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: लड़की (ladkii) and लड़का (ladkaa) are the basic boy/girl pair you will hear constantly -- in schools, in matrimonial talk, in scolding a sibling. Family conversations often use age-graded terms too, like बच्ची (bachchii) for a little girl, before a child 'graduates' to लड़की (ladkii) as a teenager.
Common Mistakes: 1) लड़की (ladkii) is for girls and young women, not for a mature adult woman -- use औरत (aurat) or महिला (mahilaa) for that. 2) Learners sometimes reach for छोकरी (chhokrii) thinking it is a neutral synonym; in real speech it can sound dismissive or rough, so लड़की (ladkii) is the safe default.

(vah ladkii merii klaas men padhtii hai।)

Sense 2 : daughter (informal)
noun, feminine Used possessively -- मेरी लड़की (merii ladkii, 'my daughter/my girl') -- as a warmer, more casual stand-in for बेटी (betii).
Synonyms: बेटी (betii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hear a parent say मेरी लड़की (merii ladkii, 'my daughter') at home and it carries real affection, much like 'my girl' in English -- it is not a downgrade from बेटी (betii), just the everyday family register.
Common Mistakes: 1) In formal writing or introductions, लड़की (ladkii) for 'daughter' can sound underspecified or overly casual -- बेटी (betii) is the word to reach for in those contexts.
