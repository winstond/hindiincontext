इतवार (itvaar)

noun A colloquial, everyday word for 'Sunday,' used interchangeably with रविवार (ravivaar) in casual speech.
Sense 1 : Sunday (the day of the week)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रविवार (ravivaar)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: इतवार (itvaar) is the word you will hear far more often in real conversation -- at the sabzi mandi, on the phone with family, or when someone is making weekend plans -- while रविवार (ravivaar) leans slightly more formal or bookish, though both are entirely standard and used by everyone. The word likely traces back through Persian/Urdu influence on everyday Hindi, much like the other common day names such as सोमवार (somvaar) versus its more colloquial counterpart पीर (piir) in some dialects, though पीर (piir) is regional and not used in Delhi-NCR.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume इतवार (itvaar) is informal-only and avoid it, but it is extremely common in daily speech, markets, and casual planning -- रविवार (ravivaar) is more likely to show up in written contexts like calendars, schedules, and news. 2) Like other day names, it takes को (ko) when marking 'on Sunday' -- इतवार को (itvaar ko, 'on Sunday') -- rather than a locative postposition a learner might guess at.

(aaj itvaar hai, islie skool band hai।)
