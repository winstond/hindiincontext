निदेशक (nideshak)

noun A formal, Sanskrit-rooted noun meaning 'director' -- used for institutional heads (schools, companies, government bodies) and, in one common extension, for film directors.
Sense 1 : director, head of an organization/institution
noun, masculine
Synonyms: प्रधान (pradhaan, formal), प्रमुख (pramukh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you see on office nameplates and government letterheads -- निदेशक, राष्ट्रीय संग्रहालय (nideshak, raashtriiy sangrahaalay, 'Director, National Museum') is the kind of formal title it attaches to. It has a slightly bureaucratic, official flavor rather than a casual workplace one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use निदेशक (nideshak) loosely for any 'manager' or 'boss'; in everyday office speech, बॉस (bos) or मैनेजर (mainejar) is far more common for a direct supervisor, while निदेशक (nideshak) is reserved for a formal, often title-bearing headship (school principal-equivalent roles, institute heads, board directors).

Sense 2 : film/theater director
noun, masculine
Synonyms: डायरेक्टर (daayrektar, common in casual film talk)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will see निदेशक (nideshak) printed in a film's opening credits or on a National Award citation, but in everyday film gossip with friends, people almost always switch to the English loanword डायरेक्टर (daayrektar).
Common Mistakes: 1) In casual conversation about movies, native speakers overwhelmingly say डायरेक्टर (daayrektar) rather than निदेशक (nideshak); the latter appears mainly in written credits, award citations, or formal reviews, so using it in spoken film chat can sound stilted.
