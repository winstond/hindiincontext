मसरूफ़ (masroof)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'busy, occupied (with work/tasks)', borrowed from Persian/Urdu and common in everyday Hindi-Urdu speech across North India.
Sense 1 : busy, occupied
adjective
Synonyms: व्यस्त (vyast), मशगूल (mashgool, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: मसरूफ़ (masroof) sits comfortably next to व्यस्त (vyast) in everyday speech, but it carries a slightly more Urdu-inflected, conversational flavor -- you will hear it often in Delhi's mixed Hindi-Urdu register, especially in phone excuses like मैं मसरूफ़ हूँ (main masroof hoon, 'I am busy'). It pairs naturally with the noun मसरूफ़ियत (masroofiyat, 'busy-ness, engagement'), which a learner will meet in more formal or literary contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write/say मसरूफ (masrooph) -- acceptable in casual writing, but the nuqta under फ़ (f) reflects the original Persian sound and is preferred in careful speech. 2) Used with में (men) for the activity one is busy 'in', e.g. काम में मसरूफ़ (kaam men masroof, 'busy in work') -- learners often reach for से (se) by analogy with English 'busy with', which sounds off here.
