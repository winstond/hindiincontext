धोना (dhonaa)

verb A basic everyday verb meaning 'to wash'; also has a figurative sense of 'washing away' guilt, sin, or a debt.
Sense 1 : to wash (clothes, dishes, hands, etc.)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: साफ़ करना (saaf karnaa, general cleaning, not just with water)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly around the house: कपड़े धोना (kapde dhonaa, 'to wash clothes'), बर्तन धोना (bartan dhonaa, 'to wash dishes'), हाथ धोना (haath dhonaa, 'to wash hands'). In many Indian homes hand-washing before and after meals is routine, so हाथ धो लो (haath dho lo, 'go wash your hands') is a very common thing to hear from parents.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for साफ़ करना (saaf karnaa) when they specifically mean washing with water -- धोना (dhonaa) is the word for that, while साफ़ करना (saaf karnaa) covers cleaning more broadly (wiping, dusting, etc.).

Sense 2 : to wash away, cleanse (figuratively: guilt, sin, shame, debt)
verb, transitive Often used with abstract nouns like पाप (paap, 'sin') or बदनामी (badnaamii, 'disgrace') as the object, treating the abstract thing as something physically washable.
Synonyms: मिटाना (mitaanaa, to erase/wipe out)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in phrases like गंगा में पाप धोना (gangaa men paap dhonaa, 'to wash away sins in the Ganges') -- the idea of water physically cleansing something as intangible as sin or guilt is deeply embedded in Hindi, much more so than in everyday English usage.

Sense 3 : to squander, ruin (money, name, reputation) -- used idiomatically
verb, transitive Appears in the fixed idiom हाथ धोना (haath dhonaa, 'to lose/be deprived of something', literally 'to wash one's hands of'), used with से (se) marking what was lost, e.g. नौकरी से हाथ धोना (naukrii se haath dhonaa, 'to lose one's job').
Synonyms: बर्बाद करना (barbaad karnaa, to ruin/waste)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: हाथ धो बैठना (haath dho baithnaa) or नौकरी से हाथ धोना (naukrii se haath dhonaa) are common in news and everyday complaints about lost jobs, money, or opportunities -- a nice parallel to the English 'to wash one's hands of' but with an opposite emotional flavor: in English it is about disowning responsibility, in Hindi it is about being the one who suffers a loss.
Common Mistakes: 1) हाथ धोना (haath dhonaa) here does not mean the literal 'wash hands' sense -- with से (se) marking the lost thing, it flips to meaning 'to lose/be deprived of,' which surprises learners who only know the literal sense.
