स्वयं (svayan)

pronoun/adverb A formal, literary-tinged word for 'self, oneself,' used both as a reflexive pronoun and as an emphatic adverb meaning 'personally, on one's own.' It overlaps heavily in meaning with the everyday word अपने आप (apne aap) and खुद (khud), but sits a register higher.
Sense 1 : oneself (reflexive)
pronoun Functions as an invariant reflexive pronoun referring back to the subject, regardless of the subject's person, number, or gender.
Synonyms: खुद (khud), अपने आप (apne aap), आप (aap, formal/literary reflexive use)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet स्वयं (svayan) in speeches, official announcements, essays, and school textbooks far more than in casual chai-time conversation, where खुद (khud) or अपने आप (apne aap) rule. A minister saying स्वयं (svayan) उपस्थित थे (svayan upasthit the, 'was personally present') sounds dignified in a way खुद (khud) उपस्थित था (khud upasthit thaa) would not quite match.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to खुद (khud) in all contexts; स्वयं (svayan) is not wrong but sounds noticeably more formal or written, so using it constantly in casual speech can feel stiff. 2) Some learners try to inflect स्वयं (svayan) like a noun (adding case endings); it never changes form no matter what role it plays in the sentence.

Sense 2 : personally, by oneself, on one's own
adverb Used to emphasize that the subject did something without help or through direct personal involvement, often placed right before the verb or the doer.
Synonyms: खुद (khud), आप ही (aap hii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the सर स्वयं आए (sar svayan aae, 'the sir/teacher came personally') register -- you hear it when someone's involvement is being highlighted as noteworthy, like a boss or elder doing something themselves rather than delegating it, which in Indian workplace and family culture often carries a note of respect or surprise.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this emphatic-adverb use with the pronoun sense; here स्वयं (svayan) is not standing in for a noun phrase but intensifying 'by one's own effort/agency,' similar to English 'personally' or 'himself/herself' used for emphasis (e.g., 'the principal himself came').
