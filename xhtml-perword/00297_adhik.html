अधिक (adhik)

adjective/adverb A common formal/written way to say 'more' or 'excess' -- covers 'more' as a quantity adjective and 'too much / excessive' as a related shade, plus an adverbial use meaning 'more' or 'excessively.' Closely paired with the everyday word ज़्यादा (zyaadaa), which learners will hear far more often in casual speech.
Sense 1 : more, greater in quantity
adjective
Synonyms: ज़्यादा (zyaadaa), बहुत (bahut)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in newspapers, official announcements, and school textbooks -- it is the word of reports and notices, while ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) is the word of the kitchen and the street. A Delhi shopkeeper is far more likely to say ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) mat लो (mat lo, 'don't take too much') than to reach for अधिक (adhik) in that moment.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अधिक (adhik) in casual speech where a native speaker would default to ज़्यादा (zyaadaa); अधिक (adhik) is not wrong but sounds a notch more formal or written, like reaching for 'greater' instead of 'more' in English.

Sense 2 : excessive, too much
adjective
Synonyms: ज़्यादा (zyaadaa), अत्यधिक (atydhik, more emphatic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in cautionary or medical register -- a doctor might warn अधिक तेल (adhik tel, 'excess oil') is bad for health, where the word carries a slight tone of 'too much, beyond the healthy limit' rather than neutral 'more.'

Sense 3 : more, to a greater extent
adverb Used before adjectives or verbs to intensify, similar to how ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) works in casual speech: अधिक अच्छा (adhik achchhaa, 'better/more good').
Synonyms: ज़्यादा (zyaadaa), अधिकतर (adhiktar, mostly/for the most part)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In formal writing or speeches, you will hear अधिक (adhik) used the way English uses 'more' before a comparative idea, giving sentences a slightly polished, official ring.
