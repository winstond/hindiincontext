मालूम नहीं (maaloom nahiin)

phrase A common phrase meaning 'I don't know' or 'not known' -- built from मालूम (maaloom, 'known') plus the negator नहीं (nahiin); usually paired with a को-marked experiencer (e.g. मुझे मालूम नहीं (mujhe maaloom nahiin, 'I don't know')).
Sense 1 : I don't know / not known
phrase Functions as a dative-subject construction: the knower takes को (or its pronoun form), and मालूम (maaloom) behaves like an adjective describing what is or isn't 'known' to that person, with नहीं (nahiin) negating it. The subject is very often dropped entirely in casual speech, leaving just मालूम नहीं (maaloom nahiin) as a standalone reply.
Synonyms: पता नहीं (pataa nahiin), मुझे नहीं पता (mujhe nahiin pataa), ज्ञात नहीं (gyaat nahiin, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most-used hedging phrases in daily Hindi -- rickshaw drivers, shopkeepers, and friends alike toss out मालूम नहीं (maaloom nahiin) or पता नहीं (pataa nahiin) with a shrug when they genuinely don't know or don't want to commit to an answer. It pairs with the same dative-subject logic you've already seen in पसंद है (pasand hai) and चाहिए (chaahie) -- in Hindi, knowledge 'is' or 'isn't' to you, rather than something you 'do'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make मालूम (maaloom) 'agree' or conjugate like a verb -- it stays invariant here since the real predicate structure is the को-experiencer plus the adjective-like मालूम (maaloom), not a verb taking an object. 2) Overusing मालूम नहीं (maaloom nahiin) in every 'I don't know' context: पता नहीं (pataa nahiin) is the more everyday, casual default in Delhi speech, while मालूम नहीं (maaloom nahiin) can sound slightly more formal or deliberate depending on tone. 3) Forgetting that the experiencer takes को/मुझे-type marking, not ने -- learners who have just learned ने-marking sometimes wrongly reach for it here since the sentence describes a mental state.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे मालूम नहीं वो कहाँ गया है। I don't know where he has gone.
(mujhe maaloom nahiin vo kahaan gayaa hai।)
