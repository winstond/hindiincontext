दूरी (doorii)

noun Means 'distance' -- both physical/spatial distance and figurative/emotional distance between people. Related to the adjective दूर (door, 'far').
Sense 1 : physical distance
noun, feminine
Synonyms: फ़ासला (faaslaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: दूरी (doorii) is what you'll see on road signs and hear in directions -- 'दिल्ली की दूरी' (dillii kii doorii, 'the distance to Delhi') is a completely everyday phrase. It's built from the adjective दूर (door) plus the abstract-noun suffix ी, a very productive pattern in Hindi (compare गरम (garam) -> गरमी (garmii)).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दूर (door) where a noun is needed; दूर (door) is the adjective/adverb ('far'), while दूरी (doorii) is the noun ('distance') -- e.g. 'यह दूरी बहुत ज़्यादा है' (yah doorii bahut zyaadaa hai, 'this distance is very great'), not 'यह दूर बहुत ज़्यादा है' (yah door bahut zyaadaa hai).
Sample Sentences:
दिल्ली की दूरी यहाँ से लगभग दो सौ किलोमीटर है। The distance to Delhi from here is about two hundred kilometers.
(dillii kii doorii yahaan se lagbhag do sau kilomiitar hai।)

Sense 2 : emotional or relational distance
noun, feminine
Synonyms: अलगाव (algaav, emphasizes separation more than distance)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative sense is very common in everyday talk about relationships -- 'हमारे बीच दूरी आ गई है' (hamaare biich doorii aa gaii hai, 'a distance has come between us') is how people describe a friendship or marriage cooling off, without necessarily saying anything harsher like fighting or breakup. It reflects the same body/space metaphor English uses ('growing apart'), so it should feel intuitive.
Sample Sentences:
हमारे बीच दूरी आ गई है। A distance has come between us.
(hamaare biich doorii aa gaii hai।)
