के लिए (ke lie)

postposition A compound postposition meaning 'for'/'for the sake of' -- used after an oblique noun or pronoun to mark purpose, beneficiary, or duration. Closely related to के वास्ते (ke vaaste), a more colloquial synonym.
Sense 1 : for (benefit/purpose)
postposition Follows a noun or pronoun in the oblique case: मेरे लिए (mere lie, 'for me'), बच्चों के लिए (bachchon ke lie, 'for the children'). Can also precede an infinitive to express purpose: पढ़ने के लिए (padhne ke lie, 'in order to study').
Synonyms: के वास्ते (ke vaaste, colloquial), हेतु (hetu, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily life -- from a shopkeeper asking क्या चाहिए, किसके लिए? (kyaa chaahie, kiske lie?, 'What do you need, for whom?') to a mother saying यह तुम्हारे लिए है (yah tumhaare lie hai, 'this is for you'). It's one of the first postpositions learners internalize because it's so central to expressing care, purpose, and giving.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop के (ke) and use only लिए (lie), which sounds incomplete outside of very casual speech. 2) When the object is a pronoun, remember to use the oblique/possessive form first (मेरे, तेरे, उसके) rather than the direct form (मैं, तू, वह) before लिए (lie).
Sample Sentences:

(yah tumhaare lie hai।)

Sense 2 : in order to (purpose with infinitive)
postposition Attaches to the oblique infinitive (verb stem + ने (ne) + के लिए (ke lie)) to express the goal of an action: वह पैसे कमाने के लिए काम करता है (vah paise kamaane ke lie kaam kartaa hai, 'he works in order to earn money').
Synonyms: के वास्ते (ke vaaste, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday way to answer 'why did you do that?' -- teachers and parents alike use this pattern constantly, e.g. अच्छे नंबर लाने के लिए मेहनत करो (achchhe nambar laane ke lie mehnat karo, 'work hard in order to get good marks').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use के लिए (ke lie) where ताकि (taaki) with a subjunctive clause is more natural, especially when the subject of the purpose clause differs from the main clause's subject.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : for (duration)
postposition Marks a stretch of time an action is intended to span: वह एक हफ़्ते के लिए दिल्ली जा रहा है (vah ek hafte ke lie dillii jaa rahaa hai, 'he is going to Delhi for a week').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Handy when talking about travel plans or leave from work/school -- छुट्टी के लिए (chhuttii ke lie, 'for vacation') is a very common phrase you'll hear before any long weekend.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with से...तक (se...tak) which marks a defined start-to-end range rather than an intended duration.

मैं दो दिन के लिए दिल्ली जा रहा हूँ। I am going to Delhi for two days.
