चला आना (chalaa aanaa)

verb A compound verb built from चलना (chalnaa, 'to move/walk') plus आना (aanaa, 'to come'), describing something that has been going on continuously since the past up to now, or someone who has come along/arrived after a journey. One core sense, with a closely related idiomatic extension about long-standing customs or traditions.
Sense 1 : to have been going on continuously (a practice, custom, or state persisting from the past to now)
verb, intransitive Typically used with a time expression (से (se) 'since') to frame a habit, custom, or situation as unbroken continuity reaching into the present, often in perfective forms like चला आ रहा है (chalaa aa rahaa hai) or चला आया है (chalaa aayaa hai).
Synonyms: जारी रहना (jaarii rahnaa), चलते रहना (chalte rahnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly about family or social traditions: यह रिवाज़ पुराने समय से चला आ रहा है (yah rivaaz puraane samay se chalaa aa rahaa hai, 'this custom has been continuing since olden times'). It carries a gentle reverence for continuity -- the sense that something has survived the passage of time on its own momentum, almost like the custom itself is walking forward through history.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop आना (aanaa) and just say चल रहा है (chal rahaa hai), which only means 'is happening/running' right now -- it loses the sense of an unbroken thread from the past. 2) This sense is almost always intransitive and impersonal in feel (a custom, tradition, or situation is the subject), not used with a personal ने (ne)-marked agent doing the continuing.
Sample Sentences:
यह रिवाज़ पुराने समय से चला आ रहा है। This custom has been continuing since olden times.
(yah rivaaz puraane samay se chalaa aa rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to come along, arrive after moving/walking (literal motion completed by arrival)
verb, intransitive Here चलना (chalnaa) contributes the sense of having set out or moved, and आना (aanaa) completes it with arrival -- used for a person or vehicle that travels and then arrives.
Synonyms: चलकर आना (chalkar aanaa), आ पहुँचना (aa pahunchnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Said of a guest or relative who has traveled some distance to reach you: वह दिल्ली से चला आया (vah dillii se chalaa aayaa, 'he came along from Delhi') -- it has a slightly folksy, storytelling warmth, as if narrating someone's journey rather than just stating arrival.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with the 'ongoing custom' sense; context (a person as subject, a concrete destination) signals this literal-motion reading instead.
Sample Sentences:
