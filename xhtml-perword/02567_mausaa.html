मौसा (mausaa)

noun मौसा (mausaa) means 'maternal aunt's husband' -- the uncle-by-marriage on the mother's side. It pairs with मौसी (mausii, mother's sister), the two words sharing the same root.
Sense 1 : husband of one's mother's sister
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian families track relatives by exact relationship rather than lumping everyone under 'uncle,' and मौसा (mausaa) is a good example -- it tells you immediately that this man married into the family through the mother's sister. Kids often address him respectfully alongside मौसी (mausii), and the pair मौसा-मौसी (mausaa-mausii) is a common way to refer to them together at family gatherings.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चाचा (chaachaa) or मामा (maamaa) for any 'uncle,' but Hindi kinship terms are precise about which side of the family and which relation is involved -- मौसा (mausaa) specifically marks the husband of the mother's sister, distinct from मामा (maamaa, mother's brother) and चाचा (chaachaa, father's younger brother).

(mere mausaa dillii men rahte hain।)
