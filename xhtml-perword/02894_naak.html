नाक (naak)

noun The everyday word for 'nose'; also carries a strong figurative sense of honor/pride that shows up constantly in idioms.
Sense 1 : nose (body part)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: नासिका (naasikaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Nose-related body idioms are everywhere in daily Hindi -- नाक बहना (naak bahnaa, 'nose to run') for a runny nose, or नाक पर मक्खी न बैठने देना (naak par makkhii n baithne denaa, 'to not let a fly sit on one's nose') for being overly fastidious. This literal sense is the anchor for the honor-related figurative sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget नाक (naak) is feminine, leading to wrong adjective/verb agreement -- say मेरी नाक (merii naak, 'my nose'), not मेरा नाक (meraa naak).

Sense 2 : honor, pride, reputation (figurative)
noun, feminine Almost always appears inside fixed idiomatic expressions rather than as a free-standing noun for 'honor'.
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त (izzat), मान-सम्मान (maan-sammaan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind classic expressions like नाक कटना (naak katnaa, 'to be disgraced', literally 'for the nose to be cut') and नाक रखना (naak rakhnaa, 'to save someone's honor'). The image of the nose standing for family or personal dignity runs deep in North Indian culture -- think of how 'losing face' works in English, but rooted specifically in the nose rather than the whole face.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is idiom-bound -- you can't freely substitute इज़्ज़त (izzat) for नाक (naak) inside these fixed phrases and expect the same idiom to work; the idioms are learned as whole chunks, not built word by word.

(uskii vajah se poore khaandaan kii naak kat gaii।)
