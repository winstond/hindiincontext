झुग्गी बस्ती (jhuggii bastii)

noun A compound noun meaning 'slum settlement' or 'shantytown' -- literally a cluster (बस्ती, bastii) of makeshift huts (झुग्गी, jhuggii). It has one core meaning; you will also hear the parts used separately.
Sense 1 : slum, shantytown, informal settlement of makeshift huts
noun, feminine
Synonyms: झुग्गी (jhuggii, shorter, very common alone), बस्ती (bastii, broader, can mean any settlement/neighborhood), झोपड़पट्टी (jhopadpattii, common especially in news/Mumbai-inflected usage), मलिन बस्ती (malin bastii, formal/bureaucratic term)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Every Indian city has its झुग्गी बस्ती (jhuggii bastii) -- clusters of huts built from tin sheets, tarpaulin, and brick, often right beside gleaming apartment towers, a visual contrast that is very much part of urban life here. In Delhi you will hear this term constantly in news reports about demolition drives (अतिक्रमण हटाना, atikrman hataanaa) or resettlement colonies, so it carries real socio-political weight, not just a neutral geography label.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use बस्ती (bastii) alone thinking it always means 'slum' -- but बस्ती (bastii) on its own just means 'settlement' or 'locality' and can refer to any neighborhood, poor or not; the slum sense really comes from pairing it with झुग्गी (jhuggii). 2) झुग्गी (jhuggii) alone is often used colloquially to mean the whole slum area, not just one hut, so don't assume it always refers to a single dwelling.
