पतंग (patang)

noun Mainly means 'kite' (the flying toy) -- the word every Delhi kid shouts about around Independence Day and Makar Sankranti. It has a rarer old poetic sense of 'moth', which is deprioritized here.
Sense 1 : kite (flying toy)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: गुड्डी (guddii, regional/colloquial term for a small kite)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kite-flying is a huge deal in Delhi and North India around 15 August and Makar Sankranti (14 January) -- rooftops fill up with people shouting पेच लड़ाना (pech ladaanaa, 'to duel kites') as they try to cut each other's strings. If you visit Delhi around Independence Day, the sky over any old-city neighborhood will be dotted with पतंगें (patangen).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पतंग (patang) with डोर (dor) or मांझा (maanjhaa) -- पतंग (patang) is the kite itself, while the string/thread it flies on has its own separate words. 2) The verb usually paired with it is उड़ाना (udaanaa, 'to fly [something]') for flying a kite, not उड़ना (udnaa), which describes the kite itself flying.

(bachche chhat par patang udaa rahe hain।)

Sense 2 : moth (literary)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: परवाना (parvaanaa, literary term for moth, often paired with candle imagery)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense shows up mainly in older poetry, where the moth circling a flame is a classic image of doomed love -- but in everyday Delhi speech, if you say पतंग (patang), everyone will think 'kite,' not 'moth.'
