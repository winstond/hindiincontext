जँभाई (jambhaaii)

noun This word means 'yawn,' the noun form. It is almost always seen paired with the verb आना (aanaa) or लेना (lenaa) in the fixed expression for yawning.
Sense 1 : yawn
noun, feminine Occurs mainly in the pattern X को जँभाई आना (X ko jambhaaii aanaa), 'a yawn comes to X,' framing yawning as involuntary, like feeling sleepy or hungry.
Synonyms: उबासी (ubaasii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Like नींद आना (niind aanaa, 'sleep comes') and भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa, 'hunger strikes'), जँभाई आना (jambhaaii aanaa) belongs to that very Hindi family of involuntary-body verbs where the person is a को-marked dative experiencer, not a doer. If you catch someone yawning in a Delhi household, don't be surprised if an elder says भगवान का नाम लो (bhagvaan kaa naam lo, 'take God's name'), a common superstition-tinged reflex to a yawn or sneeze.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to say 'I yawned' with a subject-agent construction like मैंने जँभाई की (mainne jambhaaii kii); native speakers overwhelmingly prefer the दुसरा को-marked pattern मुझे जँभाई आई (mujhe jambhaaii aaii, 'I yawned,' literally 'a yawn came to me'), treating the yawn as something that happens to you rather than something you actively do. 2) Some learners confuse जँभाई (jambhaaii) with उबासी (ubaasii); both mean 'yawn' and are largely interchangeable, though जँभाई (jambhaaii) is the one more often heard in casual home speech in Delhi-NCR.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे जँभाई आ रही है। I'm yawning.
(mujhe jambhaaii aa rahii hai।)
