अकेले (akele)

adverb/adjective Mainly used as an adverb meaning 'alone, by oneself'; also functions as the oblique/plural form of the adjective अकेला (akelaa) when describing a masculine plural or oblique noun.
Sense 1 : alone, by oneself
adverb Used invariantly as an adverb regardless of the subject's gender or number: मैं अकेले जाता हूँ (main akele jaataa hoon, 'I go alone' - masculine speaker), मैं अकेले जाती हूँ (main akele jaatii hoon, 'I go alone' - feminine speaker).
Synonyms: एकांत में (ekaant men, more literary/formal), खुद (khud, by oneself, emphasizes self-reliance rather than solitude)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, अकेले (akele) carries a mild emotional undertone beyond simple solitude -- अकेले रहना (akele rahnaa, 'to live alone') often evokes ideas about family and independence, since living apart from family is still notable in much of Indian society. Parents might worry aloud, 'अकेले मत जाओ (akele mat jaao, 'do not go alone')' as much out of safety concern as convention.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to match अकेले (akele) to the subject's gender as if it were a regular adjective form, saying अकेला (akelaa) or अकेली (akelii) for 'I go alone' -- but the adverbial sense is frozen in this one oblique-looking form for everyone. 2) अकेले में (akele men) means 'in private/alone together' (e.g. talking privately), which learners can confuse with simply 'alone' -- it specifically implies away from others' eyes or ears.

Sense 2 : alone, solitary (inflected form)
adjective This is the masculine plural/oblique inflection of अकेला (akelaa), used when modifying a masculine plural or oblique-case noun: अकेले लड़के (akele ladke, 'the lone boys / to the lone boy').
Synonyms: तनहा (tanahaa, literary/poetic, evokes loneliness more than mere solitude)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This form is simply the grammatical shadow of अकेला (akelaa) rather than a separate word to learn afresh -- once you know अकेला's (akelaa) pattern, this slots right in.
