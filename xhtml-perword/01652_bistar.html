बिस्तर (bistar)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'bed' or 'bedding' -- the mattress-and-covers setup, not the furniture frame itself. One core sense with a couple of everyday extended uses tied to it.
Sense 1 : bed / bedding (the mattress, sheets, and covers laid out to sleep on)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शय्या (shayyaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In many Indian homes, especially outside big cities, बिस्तर (bistar) refers to bedding that gets rolled out on the floor at night and put away in the morning -- there may be no fixed 'bed' furniture at all, which is a very different mental picture from the Western built-in bed frame.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for खाट (khaat) or पलंग (palang) thinking they mean the same thing -- पलंग (palang) is the bed frame/furniture itself, while बिस्तर (bistar) is the soft bedding on top of it (or spread on the floor); you can have a बिस्तर (bistar) without any पलंग (palang) at all, e.g. bedding rolled out on the floor. 2) बिस्तर लगाना (bistar lagaanaa) means 'to make/lay out the bed' -- learners sometimes try to translate 'make the bed' more literally with बनाना (banaanaa), which sounds off here.

Sense 2 : being bedridden / confined to bed (illness)
noun, masculine Occurs mainly in the fixed phrase बिस्तर पर होना (bistar par honaa, 'to be confined to bed') or बिस्तर से लगना (bistar se lagnaa, 'to become bedridden'), describing prolonged illness rather than ordinary sleep.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Family members will often say कई दिनों से बिस्तर पर हैं (kaii dinon se bistar par hain, 'have been bedridden for several days') about an elderly relative -- it carries a note of concern and seriousness, more than just 'resting in bed.'
Common Mistakes: 1) बिस्तर से लग जाना (bistar se lag jaanaa) doesn't mean 'to touch the bed' -- it idiomatically means someone has fallen seriously ill and taken to bed for an extended period, which surprises learners who parse it word by word.

Sense 3 : packing up and leaving (idiomatic, 'to fold up one's bedding')
noun, masculine Lives in the idiom बिस्तर बाँधना (bistar baandhnaa, literally 'to tie up the bedding') or बिस्तर-गोल करना (bistar-gol karnaa, colloquial), meaning to pack up and leave, often for good, sometimes under pressure or defeat.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in a joking or pointed tone -- अब यहाँ से बिस्तर बाँधो (ab yahaan se bistar baandho, 'now pack up and get out of here') -- it has a slightly dramatic, finality-tinged flavor, similar to English 'pack your bags.'
Common Mistakes: 1) बिस्तर बाँधना (bistar baandhnaa) is used figuratively even when no actual bedding is involved -- e.g. about someone being forced to quit a job or leave a place, so a learner shouldn't take it literally in most contexts.
