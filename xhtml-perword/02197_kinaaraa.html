किनारा (kinaaraa)

noun/verb Primarily a physical-world noun meaning 'edge, bank, shore' (kinaaraa), with a common figurative extension meaning 'distance oneself, withdraw' via the compound किनारा करना (kinaaraa karnaa).
Sense 1 : edge, bank, shore
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तट (tat, literary), साहिल (saahil), छोर (chhor, end/tip sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture the Yamuna's किनारा (kinaaraa) where people go for a walk in the evening, or the किनारा (kinaaraa) of a सारी (saarii) with its decorative border -- the word stretches easily from riverbanks to fabric edges to the rim of a plate. It's a very tactile, visual word that Hindi speakers reach for constantly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तट (tat) by default because it is the 'dictionary' word, but किनारा (kinaaraa) is what people actually say for a riverbank, roadside, or the edge of a table in everyday speech. 2) साहिल (saahil) leans toward sea/ocean shores in poetic or romantic contexts, while किनारा (kinaaraa) is the general, all-purpose word for any edge.

Sense 2 : to distance oneself, avoid, withdraw (from someone/something)
verb, intransitive Occurs as the compound किनारा करना (kinaaraa karnaa), with the person or thing being avoided marked by से (se): किसी से किनारा करना (kisii se kinaaraa karnaa, 'to distance oneself from someone').
Synonyms: बचना (bachnaa), दूर रहना (door rahnaa), अलग होना (alag honaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the verb for the quiet social move of pulling back from someone -- not a dramatic fight, just a gradual किनारा करना (kinaaraa karnaa) after a falling-out, the kind of thing relatives describe when explaining why two cousins don't talk much anymore. It's the same किनारा (kinaaraa) as 'riverbank' -- the image is of standing at the edge, keeping your distance from the water (or the person).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to mark the avoided person/thing with को (ko) by analogy with other verbs, but किनारा करना (kinaaraa karnaa) specifically takes से (se). 2) This phrase implies a deliberate, often social or emotional withdrawal -- avoiding someone at a gathering, distancing from a friend after a dispute -- rather than physically dodging an object, which would use बचना (bachnaa) instead.
