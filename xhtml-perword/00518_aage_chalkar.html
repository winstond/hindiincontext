आगे चलकर (aage chalkar)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'later on, in the course of time, going forward' -- literally 'having walked ahead.' Used to introduce a later stage in a story, career, or life's events.
Sense 1 : later on, in due course, eventually
adverb Functions as a sentence-initial time adverb, typically opening a new clause about a subsequent stage in a narrative (career, life, history). Built from चलकर (chalkar), the conjunctive participle of चलना (chalnaa, 'to walk/move'), so it literally frames time as a path one walks forward along.
Synonyms: बाद में (baad men), आगे (aage, shorter variant, less specific), कालांतर में (kaalaantar men, literary/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in biographical writing and spoken storytelling: 'वह एक छोटे गाँव से थे, आगे चलकर वे प्रधानमंत्री बने' (vah ek chhote gaanv se the, aage chalkar ve pradhaanmantrii bane, 'He was from a small village; later on, he became prime minister'). The चलकर (chalkar) piece is the same conjunctive-participle pattern as in करके (karke) or देखकर (dekhkar), so once a learner has that pattern down, this phrase stops feeling like a special idiom and more like 'walking forward' extended metaphorically into time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as literal movement ('walking ahead') rather than its idiomatic sense of time passing -- it almost never describes actual walking in modern usage. 2) It is used for narrating a real sequence of events (biography, history, plot), not for hypothetical future plans -- for that, learners should reach for आगे (aage) or भविष्य में (bhavishy men) instead.

(vah ek chhote gaanv se the, aage chalkar ve pradhaanmantrii bane।)
