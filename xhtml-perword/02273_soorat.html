सूरत (soorat)

noun Mainly means face/appearance (सूरत (soorat)), extending into figurative 'condition/form' and the fixed expression सूरत-ए-हाल (soorat-e-haal) meaning 'state of affairs'; also the proper noun for the city in Gujarat.
Sense 1 : face, facial appearance
noun, feminine
Synonyms: चेहरा (chehraa), शक्ल (shakl)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear सूरत (soorat) in the common saying सूरत से सीधा लगता है (soorat se siidhaa lagtaa hai, 'he looks innocent/decent from his face') -- Hindi speakers often judge character from सूरत (soorat) in everyday chatter, much like English 'he has an honest face.' It also pairs with सीरत (siirat, 'inner character') in the popular contrastive phrase सूरत नहीं सीरत देखो (soorat nahiin siirat dekho, 'look at the character, not the face'), a common piece of moral advice from elders.
Common Mistakes: 1) सूरत (soorat) leans toward describing the overall look or beauty of a face, while शक्ल (shakl) is more neutral/physical and often used in mildly negative contexts like शक्ल बिगड़ गई (shakl bigad gaii, 'the face got disfigured'). 2) Learners sometimes use सूरत (soorat) where चेहरा (chehraa) would sound more natural in plain physical descriptions -- सूरत (soorat) often carries a slight aesthetic or emotional coloring.

Sense 2 : form, condition, state (figurative)
noun, feminine Commonly appears in the fixed phrase सूरत-ए-हाल (soorat-e-haal, 'the state of affairs') and in expressions like इस सूरत में (is soorat men, 'in this case/situation').
Synonyms: हालत (haalat), हाल (haal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will often meet this sense in slightly formal or written contexts, such as news commentary discussing सूरत-ए-हाल (soorat-e-haal) of a city or economy. In spoken Delhi Hindi, हालत (haalat) is more common for everyday 'condition,' so this sense of सूरत (soorat) has a faintly literary or formal flavor.
Common Mistakes: 1) इस सूरत में (is soorat men) means 'in this case' or 'under this circumstance' -- learners sometimes confuse this with a literal reference to someone's face, when it is actually about a hypothetical situation.
