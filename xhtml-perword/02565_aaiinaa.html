आईना (aaiinaa)

noun A common noun meaning 'mirror', used both literally and in figurative expressions about showing someone the truth about themselves. Also spelled आइना (aainaa) -- both spellings are current.
Sense 1 : mirror (physical object)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शीशा (shiishaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian household has that one आईना (aaiinaa) by the front door for a last check before stepping out -- checking your बाल (baal, hair) or टीका (tiikaa, forehead mark) on the way to work or a wedding is a small daily ritual.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse आईना (aaiinaa) with शीशा (shiishaa); both are common and largely interchangeable for 'mirror', though शीशा (shiishaa) can also mean 'glass' as a material, so context decides.
Sample Sentences:
दरवाज़े के पास एक बड़ा आईना लगा है। There's a big mirror near the door.
(darvaaze ke paas ek badaa aaiinaa lagaa hai।)

Sense 2 : figurative reflection of the truth or one's true self
noun, masculine Common in fixed phrases like आईना दिखाना (aaiinaa dikhaanaa, 'to show someone a mirror' -- i.e., to confront them with an unpleasant truth about themselves).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the idiom you will hear in a heated family argument or a fiery political speech -- "मैं तुम्हें आईना दिखा दूँगा (main tumhen aaiinaa dikhaa doongaa, 'I will show you your true self')" -- it carries a confrontational, slightly dramatic edge, much like English 'hold up a mirror to' but used more forcefully in everyday Hindi speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) आईना दिखाना (aaiinaa dikhaanaa) does not mean literally holding up a mirror -- it means bluntly confronting someone with a hard truth about their own behavior, often in an argument or criticism.
Sample Sentences:
मैं तुम्हें आईना दिखा दूँगा। I will show you your true self.
(main tumhen aaiinaa dikhaa doongaa।)
