शराबी (sharaabii)

noun/adjective This word has two closely related senses: an adjective meaning 'drunk/alcoholic (habitual)' and a noun meaning 'a drunkard/alcoholic (person)'. Both derive from शराब (sharaab, 'liquor').
Sense 1 : a drunkard, an alcoholic (person)
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: पियक्कड़ (piyakkad, colloquial), नशेड़ी (nashedii, covers addiction to any intoxicant, not just alcohol)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Calling someone शराबी (sharaabii) is a real social judgment in India, not a neutral descriptor -- it marks a person as someone whose drinking has become a defining, often shameful, trait, frequently invoked in family arguments or in describing a neighborhood's problem drinker.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this as a one-off insult for someone who is merely drunk tonight; शराबी (sharaabii) implies a habitual drinker, not someone who just had too much at a party -- for the latter, नशे में (nashe men, 'intoxicated') or पिया हुआ (piyaa huaa, 'having drunk') is more accurate.

Sense 2 : drunk, alcoholic (as a description of a person's character or state)
adjective
Synonyms: नशे में (nashe men, describes a temporary state rather than a habit)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: As an adjective, शराबी (sharaabii) baap (baap) or शराबी (sharaabii) पति (pati) are common phrases in everyday complaints and in Hindi film dialogue, painting a whole household picture -- the word does a lot of narrative work in just two syllables.
