आश्रम (aashram)

noun आश्रम (aashram) primarily means a hermitage or spiritual retreat -- a place set apart for religious, meditative, or communal life -- and by extension is used for certain care institutions like old-age homes.
Sense 1 : hermitage, spiritual retreat/ashram
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मठ (math, more monastic), आध्यात्मिक केंद्र (aadhyaatmik kendr)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the many आश्रम (aashram) along the Ganga in Rishikesh and Haridwar, or Gandhi's Sabarmati Ashram -- these are living communities, not museums. The word also carries a classical layer: in the traditional Hindu life-scheme, a person's life is divided into four आश्रम (aashram, 'life-stages') -- student, householder, retiree, and renunciate -- so the word has always meant 'a stage or place set apart for disciplined living,' not just a physical building.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes picture a casual 'yoga retreat' resort; in everyday Hindi आश्रम (aashram) more often evokes a guru-led community with a resident spiritual teacher, disciples, and a daily rhythm of prayer, study, and simple living, not a vacation spot.

Sense 2 : care institution, especially old-age home
noun, masculine Usually appears as part of a compound like वृद्धाश्रम (vriddhaashram, 'old-age home') or अनाथाश्रम (anaathaashram, 'orphanage') rather than standing alone with this meaning.
Synonyms: वृद्धाश्रम (vriddhaashram, specific compound for old-age home)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense reflects a real social shift in urban India, where nuclear families increasingly send elderly parents to a वृद्धाश्रम (vriddhaashram) -- a topic often discussed with some cultural anxiety, since traditional expectation holds that children care for aging parents at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) On its own, आश्रम (aashram) defaults to the spiritual-retreat sense; the 'old-age home' meaning is usually signaled by the compound वृद्धाश्रम (vriddhaashram), so hearing bare आश्रम (aashram) in conversation about elderly parents can be ambiguous without context.
