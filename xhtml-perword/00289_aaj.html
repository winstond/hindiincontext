आज (aaj)

adverb A simple time adverb meaning 'today'; it also has an extended sense referring to the present era or modern times, as in 'nowadays.'
Sense 1 : today (this calendar day)
adverb
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आज (aaj) is one of the very first words every learner picks up, right alongside कल (kal) -- and famously कल (kal) does double duty for both 'yesterday' and 'tomorrow,' which makes आज (aaj) the stable anchor point in any conversation about time. You will hear it constantly in weather reports, plans, and greetings like आज (aaj) कैसा (kaisaa) दिन (din) है (hai, 'how is today')? .
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes add a postposition after आज (aaj) when talking about actions happening on that day, e.g. saying आज को (aaj ko) -- native speakers just say आज (aaj) जाऊँगा (jaaoongaa, 'will go'), no को (ko) needed, unlike with named days like सोमवार को (somvaar ko, 'on Monday'). 2) आज (aaj) is used with both past and future tense freely -- it does not lock you into one tense the way 'today' sometimes nudges English speakers.
Sample Sentences:
आज मौसम बहुत अच्छा है। The weather is very nice today.
(aaj mausam bahut achchhaa hai।)

Sense 2 : nowadays, these days, in the present era
adverb
Synonyms: आजकल (aajkal, more common for this exact sense), वर्तमान (vartmaan, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in reflective or generational comments, like something an elder might say about how times have changed: आज (aaj) के (ke) बच्चे (bachche) मोबाइल (mobaail) पर (par) लगे (lage) रहते (rahte) हैं (hain, 'today's children stay glued to their mobiles'). It is the same word stretched from a single day to an entire era, much like English 'today' can mean 'in this day and age.'
Common Mistakes: 1) For this extended sense many speakers actually reach for आजकल (aajkal) rather than आज (aaj) alone; using आज (aaj) by itself for 'nowadays' works but can sound slightly more literary or emphatic, as in आज (aaj) के (ke) ज़माने (zamaane) में (men, 'in today's era').
Sample Sentences:
आज के बच्चे मोबाइल पर लगे रहते हैं। Nowadays' children stay glued to their mobiles.
(aaj ke bachche mobaail par lage rahte hain।)
