गुरु (guru)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning teacher/guru/spiritual master, with a secondary adjective sense meaning heavy/important/serious. The adjective sense is far less common in everyday speech than the noun.
Sense 1 : teacher, spiritual guide, mentor
noun, masculine
Synonyms: शिक्षक (shikshak), उस्ताद (ustaad), आचार्य (aachaary, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In North Indian culture the guru-shishya (teacher-disciple) (guru-shishy) relationship carries deep respect, historically rooted in classical music, dance, and wrestling akhadas (akhaadaa) where the guru's word was law. You will also hear it used lightly on the street, as in अरे गुरु, सुन (are guru, sun, 'hey buddy, listen'), showing how a weighty word softens into everyday camaraderie.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often think गुरु (guru) only means a New-Age 'spiritual guru' as in English; in everyday Hindi it is used far more broadly, for a music/dance teacher, a wrestling coach, or even informally for someone skilled and respected in any field. 2) It is also used as a casual, friendly form of address between men (like 'boss' or 'buddy'), which surprises learners expecting only a reverent religious sense.

Sense 2 : heavy, weighty, serious (formal/literary register)
adjective
Synonyms: भारी (bhaarii), गंभीर (gambhiir)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older, literal sense of गुरु (guru) as 'heavy' is the root from which the 'teacher' sense grew -- a guru is literally one whose words and presence carry weight. In modern speech this adjective use is rare and mostly confined to formal or literary Hindi, so learners will meet it far less than the noun sense.

Sense 3 : Jupiter (the planet, in astrology)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बृहस्पति (brihaspti)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In Hindi astrology (jyotish) (jyotish), गुरु (guru) is the standard name for the planet Jupiter, considered the guru of the gods -- a source of wisdom and good fortune. You will hear this in phrases like गुरु की दशा (guru kii dashaa, 'the period ruled by Jupiter') when people discuss horoscopes.
