भटकना (bhataknaa)

verb One core meaning: to wander or roam aimlessly, which extends naturally into a figurative sense of being confused or led astray (mentally, morally, or in an argument).
Sense 1 : to wander, roam aimlessly (physically)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: घूमना (ghoomnaa, broader: also just 'to roam/go around' without the lost feel), आवारा फिरना (aavaaraa phirnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture someone lost in the narrow lanes of old Delhi asking रास्ता भटक गया (raastaa bhatak gayaa, 'I lost my way') -- this verb is the go-to for that specific feeling of having no clear path, not just casual roaming.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for घूमना (ghoomnaa) for any 'wandering,' but भटकना (bhataknaa) specifically carries a sense of being lost or directionless -- you would not use it for a pleasant evening stroll.
Sample Sentences:

(vah raastaa bhatak gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to be confused, go astray (mentally, morally, or in reasoning)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: भ्रमित होना (bhramit honaa, more formal/bookish), बहकना (bahaknaa, leans toward moral straying)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear parents or teachers say बच्चा भटक गया है (bachchaa bhatak gayaa hai, 'the kid has gone astray') about a young person who has drifted from studies or good company -- it is a gentle but worried way of describing someone losing direction in life, not a harsh accusation.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is very common in speech about someone's thinking or life choices, e.g. दिमाग भटक गया (dimaag bhatak gayaa, 'my mind wandered/got confused') -- learners sometimes think भटकना (bhataknaa) is only physical and miss this everyday abstract use.
