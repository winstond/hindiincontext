गाना (gaanaa)

verb/noun Two closely linked senses: as a verb, गाना (gaanaa) means 'to sing'; as a noun (masculine), गाना (gaanaa) means 'a song.' The overlap is not coincidental -- Hindi infinitives regularly double as verbal nouns.
Sense 1 : to sing
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Singing together is a favorite pastime in Delhi-NCR households -- long train journeys and festival evenings often turn into a round of अंताक्षरी (antaakshrii), where everyone takes turns singing film गाने (gaane). Note that गाना (gaanaa) as a verb and गाना (gaanaa) as a noun share one form, a pattern you will meet again with other Hindi infinitives that moonlight as masculine nouns.
Common Mistakes: 1) The verb गाना (gaanaa) and the noun गाना (gaanaa, 'song') are spelled identically, so a sentence like उसने गाना गाया (usne gaanaa gaayaa, 'she sang a song') can look like a stutter or a repeated word -- it is actually object ('song') followed by the verb ('sang'), not the same word twice. 2) Learners sometimes try to use गाना (gaanaa) for humming or reciting -- for a tune hummed under one's breath, गुनगुनाना (gungunaanaa) is the word natives reach for instead.

Sense 2 : song
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गीत (giit, slightly more literary/poetic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गाना बजाना (gaanaa bajaanaa, 'singing and playing music') is the standard phrase for the general hum of music-making at a wedding or party -- you will hear aunties say यहाँ तो गाना बजाना चल रहा है (yahaan to gaanaa bajaanaa chal rahaa hai, 'there's music going on here') as they walk into a सगाई (sagaaii) function.
Common Mistakes: 1) गीत (giit) tends to be reserved for devotional, classical, or poetic songs, while गाना (gaanaa) is the everyday word covering everything from Bollywood hits to nursery rhymes -- reaching for गीत (giit) in casual conversation about the radio can sound a touch formal.
