धुलाई (dhulaaii)

noun Means 'washing' -- both the act/process of washing (clothes, vehicles, etc.) and, by extension, the fee charged for it. It also has a common figurative sense meaning a beating or thrashing.
Sense 1 : washing (the act or process)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सफाई (saphaaii, cleaning, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word a lot around the local dhobi (dhobii, washerman) or laundromat -- people say कपड़े धुलाई के लिए दिए (kapde dhulaaii ke lie die, 'gave clothes for washing') rather than using the verb धोना (dhonaa) directly, since it is someone else doing the washing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse धुलाई (dhulaaii) with धोना (dhonaa) -- धोना (dhonaa) is the verb 'to wash,' while धुलाई (dhulaaii) is the noun naming the activity or job of washing, often used with कराना (karaanaa, to get something done), as in कपड़ों की धुलाई कराना (kapdon kii dhulaaii karaanaa, 'to get clothes washed').
Sample Sentences:
मैंने कपड़े धुलाई के लिए दिए हैं। I have given the clothes for washing.

Sense 2 : washing charge/fee
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: At the laundry or dry cleaner, you will often be quoted a per-piece धुलाई (dhulaaii) rate -- this extension from 'the act of washing' to 'the price for that act' is a very natural, everyday shift in Hindi, similar to how 'delivery' can mean both the act and its fee in English.

Sense 3 : a beating, thrashing (figurative)
noun, feminine Common in the phrase धुलाई करना (dhulaaii karnaa, 'to beat someone up') or धुलाई होना (dhulaaii honaa, 'to get beaten up'), paralleling how पिटाई (pitaaii) works.
Synonyms: पिटाई (pitaaii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a classic bit of Hindi wordplay: getting yelled at or beaten up is described the same way as getting your laundry cleaned -- as if the person's mistakes are being 'scrubbed out' of them. You will hear parents jokingly threaten अच्छी धुलाई होगी (achchhii dhulaaii hogii, 'you'll get a good thrashing') without any real violence implied.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is informal and used mostly in speech about scuffles, scoldings, or fights -- do not use it in a formal or written complaint; प्रताड़ना (prataadnaa) or मारपीट (maarpiit) would be more appropriate there.
Sample Sentences:
अगर तुमने फिर शरारत की, तो अच्छी धुलाई होगी। If you misbehave again, you'll get a good thrashing.
