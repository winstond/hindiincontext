पड़ना (padnaa)

verb पड़ना (padnaa) is a versatile intransitive verb with three faces to learn: (1) its core meaning 'to fall / land / occur / be situated', (2) a necessitive modal 'to have to' (dative subject + infinitive + पड़ना), and (3) an inceptive vector verb 'to suddenly begin/happen' (verb stem + पड़ना). The infinitive-vs-stem distinction is what tells the two grammaticalized senses apart.
Sense 1 : to fall / land / occur / be situated
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: गिरना (girnaa, physical falling only), होना (honaa, to happen)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of पड़ना (padnaa) as picturing something 'landing' on the world or on you -- rain falls (बारिश पड़ती है, baarish padtii hai, 'it rains'), an effect falls on you (असर पड़ना, asar padnaa), a need falls upon you (ज़रूरत पड़ना, zaroorat padnaa). This is also why a village or landmark 'falls' on your route in Hindi (रास्ते में पड़ना, raaste men padnaa, 'to lie on the way') -- many things English treats as static states, Hindi treats as things that fall into place.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse पड़ना (padnaa) with गिरना (girnaa) -- गिरना is for a person or object tumbling down, while पड़ना is used for things landing, settling, or abstract things arising (बारिश पड़ना (baarish padnaa, 'to rain'), असर पड़ना (asar padnaa, 'to have an effect')); they are not interchangeable. 2) Phrases like ज़रूरत पड़ना (zaroorat padnaa) or काम पड़ना (kaam padnaa) use पड़ना idiomatically with abstract nouns to mean 'arise/come up' -- don't translate word-for-word as 'fall'.

Sense 2 : to have to (necessitive modal, external compulsion)
verb, intransitive Frame: dative subject (को) + verb infinitive + पड़ना. The tense of पड़ना itself modulates how strong or immediate the obligation feels -- subjunctive softens it, plain future states it firmly, future + ही makes it most urgent, and the habitual present marks a recurring duty.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi's core 'have to' modals, alongside चाहिए (chaahie) -- but पड़ना carries a flavor of external compulsion or reluctant necessity forced by circumstance, not a calm 'should'. A Delhi parent might sigh, "क्या करूँ, जाना पड़ेगा" (kyaa karoon, jaanaa padegaa, 'what to do, I'll have to go') -- a note of resignation is baked into the word itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) The subject takes को (ko), never ने (ne) -- even in past-tense sentences describing a completed obligation, because पड़ना is intransitive and the logical subject is a dative experiencer, not an agent. 2) The verb before पड़ना must be the infinitive (e.g. जाना (jaanaa), देना (denaa)), not the bare stem -- using the stem instead shifts the meaning to the unrelated 'suddenly happen' sense of पड़ना. 3) Tense changes the force: शायद...रहना पड़े (shaayad...rahnaa pade, 'might have to stay') is soft, ...देना पड़ेगा (...denaa padegaa, 'will have to pay') is firm, and ...जाना ही पड़ेगा (...jaanaa hii padegaa, 'simply must go right now') is most urgent -- do not treat these as interchangeable.

Sense 3 : to suddenly begin/happen (inceptive vector verb)
verb, intransitive Frame: verb stem (not infinitive) + पड़ना, marking a sudden, unexpected occurrence the subject had no prior knowledge of, often with a downward or abrupt momentum: उसे बुरा लग पड़ा (use buraa lag padaa, 'he suddenly felt bad'), पर्दा खुल पड़ा (pardaa khul padaa, 'the curtain suddenly flew open').
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This 'sudden happening' use of पड़ना belongs to a small family of Hindi vector verbs (उठना, uthnaa; जाना, jaanaa; पड़ना, padnaa) that attach to a bare verb stem to color an action with a particular flavor -- here, unexpectedness and a slight abrupt or downward momentum. It shows up more in narrative and literary Hindi than in casual chat, so treat it as a 'recognize it' sense before an 'actively use it' one.
Common Mistakes: 1) This construction takes the bare verb stem right before पड़ना (padnaa) -- e.g. लग पड़ना (lag padnaa) 'to suddenly start feeling', not the infinitive लगना (lagnaa) + पड़ना, which would wrongly suggest the 'have to' modal sense. 2) This sense is only for uncontrolled, unexpected events -- it does not apply to deliberate or planned actions.
