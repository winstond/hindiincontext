पड़ना (padnaa)

verb A hugely productive verb built on the core image of 'falling' -- physically falling/landing (rain, light, objects), lying in a resting state (पड़ा/padaa), being situated on a route, abstract things 'falling' onto you (effect, need, trouble), falling under a category, turning out costly, getting caught up in a state (with में (men)), and two grammaticalized uses: the necessitive 'have to' modal (infinitive + पड़ना (padnaa)) and the inceptive 'suddenly happen' vector verb (verb stem + पड़ना (padnaa)).
Sense 1 : to fall/land on (rain, light, objects)
verb, intransitive Subject is the thing falling (rain, snow, hail, sunlight, shadow, a leaf); the surface or person it lands on takes पर (par).
Synonyms: गिरना (girnaa, for objects falling, not weather/light)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the most literal 'falling' sense and the conceptual root the whole word's other meanings grow from -- think of धूप पड़ना (dhoop padnaa, 'sunlight falls/lands') as the same mental picture as बारिश पड़ना (baarish padnaa), just with light instead of water.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गिरना (girnaa) for rain or sunlight, but Hindi uses पड़ना (padnaa) for precipitation and light/shadow -- बारिश गिर रही है (baarish gir rahii hai) sounds odd; बारिश पड़ रही है (baarish pad rahii hai, 'it's raining') is natural.
Sample Sentences:
आज सुबह से बारिश पड़ रही है। It has been raining since this morning.
(aaj subah se baarish pad rahii hai।)

Sense 2 : to lie (in a resting state, as a result of having been put or fallen)
verb, intransitive Almost always appears as the perfective participle पड़ा/पड़ी/पड़े (padaa) used predicatively with होना (honaa) -- किताब मेज़ पर पड़ी है (kitaab mez par padii hai, 'the book is lying on the table'). Combined with रहना (rahnaa) as पड़ा रहना (padaa rahnaa), it adds the sense of continuing to lie there over time (see रहना (rahnaa) for that construction).
Synonyms: लेटना (letnaa, the act of lying down, not the resulting state)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: There's a mild negative shading to this sense when used of a person -- वह दिन भर बिस्तर पर पड़ा रहा (vah din bhar bistar par padaa rahaa, 'he lay in bed all day') can carry a tone of laziness or being unwell, unlike the neutral लेटना (letnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) This describes a resulting state, not the action -- 'he lay down on the bed' is वह बिस्तर पर लेटा (vah bistar par letaa), but 'he is lying on the bed' (already in that position) is वह बिस्तर पर पड़ा है (vah bistar par padaa hai).
Sample Sentences:

(kitaab mez par padii hai।)

Sense 4 : to befall, to be exerted on (effect, need, trouble, pressure, etc.)
verb, intransitive Productive frame: abstract noun + पर (par) + पड़ना (padnaa), where the abstract noun is the grammatical subject -- असर पड़ना (asar padnaa, 'an effect falls'), फ़र्क़ पड़ना (farq padnaa, 'a difference results'), ज़रूरत पड़ना (zaroorat padnaa, 'a need arises'), मुसीबत पड़ना (musiibat padnaa, 'trouble befalls'). This is one sense covering many nouns, not a separate sense per collocation.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi treats effects, needs, and troubles as things that 'fall' onto you from outside rather than things you simply 'have' -- सेहत पर बुरा असर पड़ेगा (sehat par buraa asar padegaa, 'it will have a bad effect on [his] health') frames health as a surface being struck, which is a very different conceptual picture from the English 'affect'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse this with getting caught up in a state (sense below): मुसीबत पड़ना (musiibat padnaa, 'trouble befalls someone', person marked with पर (par)) is the mirror image of मुसीबत में पड़ना (musiibat men padnaa, 'to get into trouble', person is the one who enters), and they are easy to mix up.
Sample Sentences:
सेहत पर बुरा असर पड़ेगा। It will have a bad effect on [his] health.
(sehat par buraa asar padegaa।)

Sense 5 : to turn out to be (costly, heavy, hard) for someone
verb, intransitive Frame: adjective + (dative person, often with को (ko)) + पड़ना (padnaa) -- यह सौदा मुझे महँगा पड़ा (yah saudaa mujhe mahangaa padaa, 'this deal turned out costly for me'), उसकी लापरवाही उसे भारी पड़ी (uskii laaparvaahii use bhaarii padii, 'his carelessness cost him dearly'). One sense covering many adjectives, not one per adjective.
Synonyms: साबित होना (saabit honaa, more neutral, not limited to costly/burdensome outcomes)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a favorite construction for warnings and cautionary remarks -- लापरवाही भारी पड़ सकती है (laaparvaahii bhaarii pad saktii hai, 'carelessness can cost you dearly') is the kind of line an older relative might say to you.
Common Mistakes: 1) The person who suffers the consequence is marked with को (ko), not ने (ne) -- learners used to agent marking sometimes want to mark the affected person as the doer, but they are the dative experiencer here, not an agent.

Sense 8 : to have to (necessitive modal: strong obligation, often reluctant/externally compelled)
verb, intransitive Frame: dative-subject (को (ko)) + verb infinitive + पड़ना (padnaa). Tense modulates the obligation's strength: subjunctive is softest/tentative (मुझे जाना पड़े (mujhe jaanaa pade)), simple future is a strong prediction of compulsion, future + ही (hii) is most immediate/forceful, and habitual present marks a recurring obligation. Distinct from होगा-type presumptive/inferential mood, which पड़ना does not express.
Synonyms: चाहिए (chaahie, softer 'should', not compulsion), मजबूर होना (majboor honaa, emphasizes being forced)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the construction behind countless everyday complaints -- मुझे जल्दी उठना पड़ता है (mujhe jaldii uthnaa padtaa hai, 'I have to get up early') -- and it always carries a faint sigh of 'I don't really want to, but...' that चाहिए (chaahie) lacks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चाहिए (chaahie) here, but चाहिए (chaahie) is a recommendation ('should'), while पड़ना (padnaa) conveys real external compulsion, often with reluctance -- 'I had no choice but to.' 2) The obligation-bearer is always marked with को (ko) (dative subject), never ने (ne), even though the main verb may otherwise be transitive.

Sense 9 : to suddenly happen/begin (inceptive vector verb)
verb, intransitive Frame: verb stem (not infinitive) + पड़ना (padnaa), marking an abrupt, unforeseen occurrence, often with a downward or sudden-onset flavor -- उसे बुरा लग पड़ा (use buraa lag padaa, 'he suddenly felt bad'), वह कमरे में आ पड़ी (vah kamre men aa padii, 'she suddenly burst into the room'). This is a distinct grammaticalized compound-verb pattern, not the same frame as the necessitive sense above; the stem-vs-infinitive distinction is what tells them apart.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This vector-verb use is mostly found in narrative, literary, or storytelling Hindi -- describing a sudden twist in a tale -- rather than in everyday conversation, so you will meet it more in reading than in speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) This looks superficially like the necessitive 'have to' sense but is grammatically different -- necessitive पड़ना (padnaa) follows a full infinitive (जाना पड़ना (jaanaa padnaa)), while this inceptive sense follows a bare stem (जा पड़ना (jaa padnaa)) and has nothing to do with obligation.
