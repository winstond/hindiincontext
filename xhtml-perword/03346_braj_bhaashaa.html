ब्रज भाषा (braj bhaashaa)

noun The name of a historic dialect/literary language of the Braj region (around Mathura-Vrindavan), famous as the language of Krishna devotional poetry. It is a proper noun referring to this specific language variety.
Sense 1 : Braj Bhasha, a Hindi dialect associated with the Mathura-Vrindavan region and its devotional poetry
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ब्रजभाषा (brajbhaashaa, one-word variant)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: If you have ever heard a Krishna bhajan or read Surdas (soordaas) or Tulsidas's (tulsiidaas) Krishna-poems, you have heard Braj Bhasha -- it has a soft, musical quality that modern Khari Boli (khadii bolii) Hindi lost when it became the standard. Many Delhi-NCR families still sing Braj bhajans at festivals like Janmashtami even though they speak standard Hindi at home, so the language survives as a devotional register more than a spoken one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume ब्रज भाषा (braj bhaashaa) is just an old name for modern Hindi; in fact it is a distinct dialect with its own grammar and verb forms, still used today mainly in poetry, bhajans, and some rural speech around Mathura-Vrindavan, not in everyday conversational Hindi. 2) The word भाषा (bhaashaa, 'language') here is feminine, so any adjective describing ब्रज भाषा (braj bhaashaa) takes feminine agreement, e.g. मीठी ब्रज भाषा (miithii braj bhaashaa, 'sweet Braj Bhasha').
