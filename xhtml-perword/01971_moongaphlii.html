मूँगफली (moongaphlii)

noun A single-sense concrete noun: the peanut/groundnut. Literally a compound of मूँग (moong, 'mung bean') and फली (phalii, 'pod'), though the plant is unrelated to the mung bean -- an old naming-by-resemblance case, not a live idiom source.
Sense 1 : peanut, groundnut
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Roasted मूँगफली (moongaphlii) sold in a folded newspaper cone by a thele-wala (cart vendor) is one of the defining sounds and smells of a Delhi winter evening -- the seller's call and the crunch of shells being cracked open on a park bench are as much a part of it as the taste. It's also a common movie-hall snack, sold alongside chana and popcorn.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to pluralize it like a count noun in English ('two peanuts' = दो मूँगफली, do moongaphlii) -- fine for whole peanuts-in-shell as units, but when talking about the roasted snack in bulk (the usual street-food context), Hindi treats it more like a mass noun, so मूँगफली खा रहा हूँ (moongaphlii khaa rahaa hoon, 'I'm eating peanuts') stays singular in form even though the meaning is plural.
