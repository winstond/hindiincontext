चबाना (chabaanaa)

verb A concrete, everyday verb meaning 'to chew.' It has a literal sense (chewing food) and a figurative sense (drawing out or mumbling words), both alive in daily speech.
Sense 1 : to chew (food)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian mothers and grandmothers say अच्छी तरह चबाकर खाओ (achchhii tarah chabaakar khaao, 'eat chewing it well') constantly at meals -- there's a strong cultural emphasis on chewing slowly for digestion, often tied to older Ayurvedic-style advice passed down at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for खाना (khaanaa, 'to eat') when they mean the specific act of chewing -- अच्छी तरह चबाओ (achchhii tarah chabaao, 'chew well') is what a mother says at the table, not खाओ (khaao).
Sample Sentences:
खाना अच्छी तरह चबाकर खाओ। Eat your food chewing it well.
(khaanaa achchhii tarah chabaakar khaao।)

Sense 2 : to mumble or drawl words (figurative)
verb, transitive Used when someone speaks unclearly, as if the words are being 'chewed' rather than spoken crisply -- शब्द चबाना (shabd chabaanaa, 'to chew one's words').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this used affectionately or critically about someone's speech -- टीचर कहती है, साफ़ बोलो, शब्द मत चबाओ (tiichar kahtii hai, saaf bolo, shabd mat chabaao, 'the teacher says, speak clearly, don't chew your words') -- it paints speech as something physically mangled in the mouth, much like the English 'mumble.'
Sample Sentences:
टीचर कहती है, साफ़ बोलो, शब्द मत चबाओ। The teacher says, speak clearly, don't mumble your words.
(tiichar kahtii hai, saaf bolo, shabd mat chabaao।)

(vah hameshaa shabd chabaataa hai, islie uskii baat samajh men nahiin aatii।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
