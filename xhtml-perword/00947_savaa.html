सवा (savaa)

numeral सवा (savaa) is an invariant numeral meaning 'one and a quarter' -- used before a unit (time, weight, currency, number) to mean 'that unit plus a quarter more.' It pairs naturally with साढ़े (saadhe, 'and a half') and पौने (paune, 'a quarter less than') in the same time/quantity system.
Sense 1 : one and a quarter (of a unit)
numeral Placed directly before the unit noun or number it modifies: सवा घंटा (savaa ghantaa, 'an hour and a quarter'), सवा दो किलो (savaa do kilo, 'two and a quarter kilos'). Unlike ordinary numerals, सवा (savaa) itself never inflects.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word lives mostly in everyday talk about time and shopping: सवा बजे है (savaa baje hai, 'it's a quarter past the hour') is something you hear constantly in Delhi markets and offices when people are fixing a meeting time. It belongs to a neat little family with साढ़े (saadhe, 'half past/plus') and पौने (paune, 'quarter to') -- once you learn all three, telling time and quantities in Hindi becomes much more natural than doing the math yourself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'one and a quarter' as एक और चौथाई (ek aur chauthaaii) instead of the single word सवा (savaa) -- Hindi has a dedicated word here, so the literal English calque sounds foreign. 2) When used with numbers above one, सवा (savaa) attaches to the *next* whole number, not the current one -- सवा दो (savaa do) means 2.25, not 1.25, which trips learners doing quick math in their head.
