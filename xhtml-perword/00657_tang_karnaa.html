तंग करना (tang karnaa)

verb A compound verb built on तंग (tang, 'tight/distressed') + करना (karnaa, 'to do'), meaning 'to bother, harass, or tease someone.' One core sense, used constantly in family and school contexts.
Sense 1 : to bother, harass, pester, or tease someone
verb, transitive Takes the person bothered as a direct object, usually marked with को: बच्चे को तंग करना (bachche ko tang karnaa, 'to bother the child').
Synonyms: परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa), सताना (sataanaa), छेड़ना (chhednaa, teasing sense specifically)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the go-to phrase Indian parents and teachers use for kids pestering each other -- मत तंग करो! (mat tang karo, 'stop bothering [him/her]!') is heard constantly in Indian households and classrooms. It has a lighter, more domestic flavor than परेशान करना (pareshaan karnaa), which can sound a touch more serious or adult.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use तंग होना (tang honaa) when they mean the active 'to bother someone' -- तंग होना (tang honaa) means 'to become distressed/fed up' (intransitive, describes your own state), while तंग करना (tang karnaa) is what you do *to* someone else. 2) In the past tense, since करना (karnaa) is transitive, the subject takes ने (ne): उसने मुझे तंग किया (usne mujhe tang kiyaa, 'he bothered me') -- not उसने तंग हुआ, which mixes the two verbs.
Sample Sentences:
मत तंग करो! Stop bothering him!
(mat tang karo!)
