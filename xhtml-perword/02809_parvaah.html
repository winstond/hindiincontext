परवाह (parvaah)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'care' or 'concern', almost always used in the negative/questioning frame -- 'not caring' -- rather than to express that someone does care.
Sense 1 : care, concern, worry (about something/someone)
noun, feminine Takes की (kii) to mark the thing being cared about: किसी की परवाह (kisii kii parvaah, 'concern for someone'). Commonly paired with करना (karnaa) as a light verb: परवाह करना (parvaah karnaa, 'to care about').
Synonyms: चिंता (chintaa), फ़िक्र (fikr), सरोकार (sarokaar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word lives mostly in its negative form -- कोई परवाह नहीं (koii parvaah nahiin, 'no concern at all') is the classic dismissive shrug you'll hear constantly in Delhi markets and homes alike, roughly 'whatever, I don't care.' Note the dative-subject frame here: it's मुझे परवाह है (mujhe parvaah hai, literally 'to me there is concern'), the same pattern as पसंद (pasand) or ज़रूरत (zaroorat) -- the feeling arrives to you rather than you actively doing it.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I care about you' with a direct verb; Hindi routes this through the noun + करना (karnaa) construction, not a single verb. 2) The object of concern is marked with की (kii), not को (ko) -- मुझे तुम्हारी परवाह है (mujhe tumhaarii parvaah hai, 'I care about you'), not तुम्हें। 3) In the very common negative pattern, है is often dropped in casual speech: मुझे कोई परवाह नहीं (mujhe koii parvaah nahiin, 'I don't care at all'), which can look grammatically incomplete to a learner expecting a verb.

(mujhe tumhaarii bahut parvaah hai।)
