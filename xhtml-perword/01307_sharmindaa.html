शरमिंदा (sharmindaa)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'ashamed, embarrassed' -- describes the emotional state of a person, always used with होना (honaa) or a causative like करना (karnaa).
Sense 1 : ashamed, embarrassed
adjective
Synonyms: लज्जित (lajjit, literary), शर्मसार (sharmsaar, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries a slightly softer, more relational shade than pure guilt -- it is the feeling of having let someone down in front of others, often family. You'll hear it a lot in the context of izzat (izzat, honor/reputation) culture, as in बच्चों की हरकतों से माँ-बाप शरमिंदा हो जाते हैं (bachchon kii harakton se maan-baap sharmindaa ho jaate hain, 'parents get embarrassed by their children's behavior').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use this with an ने-marked subject as if it were a normal transitive verb of action -- but शरमिंदा (sharmindaa) is an adjective describing a state, so the person feeling it just takes होना (honaa): राम शरमिंदा हुआ (raam sharmindaa huaa, 'Ram became ashamed'), never राम ने शरमिंदा किया (raam ne sharmindaa kiyaa) to mean 'Ram felt ashamed.' 2) To say someone *made* another person ashamed, you need a separate person as the subject with शरमिंदा करना (sharmindaa karnaa): उसने मुझे शरमिंदा किया (usne mujhe sharmindaa kiyaa, 'He made me ashamed/embarrassed').
Sample Sentences:
मुझे अपनी गलती पर बहुत शर्मिंदा हूँ। I am very ashamed of my mistake.
(mujhe apnii galtii par bahut sharmindaa hoon।)
