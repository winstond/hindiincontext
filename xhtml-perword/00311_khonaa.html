खोना (khonaa)

verb Mainly 'to lose' something you had (an object, a chance, one's way) -- also extends to losing something intangible like composure or a person emotionally. A related sense means 'to get lost/absorbed' in thought or activity, usually as खो जाना (kho jaanaa).
Sense 1 : to lose (an object, opportunity, or ability)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: गुम करना (gum karnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A mother scolding a child often says अपनी चीज़ें मत खोया करो (apnii chiizen mat khoyaa karo, 'don't keep losing your things') -- खोना (khonaa) carries a note of carelessness, unlike simply 'not having' something.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use खोना (khonaa) where गुम होना (gum honaa, to become lost) is more natural for objects that went missing on their own -- खोना (khonaa) puts the blame/agency on the subject ('I lost it'), while गुम होना (gum honaa) describes the object's state.

(mainne apnaa fon kho diyaa।)

Sense 2 : to lose (a person, emotionally or through death)

Sense 3 : to become lost, absorbed, or disappear into (as खो जाना)
verb, intransitive This sense is almost always realized as the compound खो जाना (kho jaanaa) -- the intransitive 'go' completes the verb into a state-change meaning, distinct from the plain transitive 'lose' senses above.
Synonyms: गुम हो जाना (gum ho jaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: खो जाना (kho jaanaa) is the phrase for a child lost in a mela (melaa, fair) as well as for someone lost in music or memory -- Hindi uses the same verb for physical and mental 'losing yourself,' which is a nice window into how the language frames absorption.
Common Mistakes: 1) वह अपने विचारों में खो गया (vah apne vichaaron men kho gayaa, 'he got lost in his thoughts') uses खो जाना (kho jaanaa) for mental absorption -- learners often reach for सोचना (sochnaa, 'to think') instead, missing this very idiomatic frame.
Sample Sentences:

(mele men bachchaa kho gayaa।)
