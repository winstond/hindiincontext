आभारी (aabhaarii)

adjective A formal adjective meaning 'grateful, obliged' -- used to thank someone in a somewhat elevated or written register, related in meaning to the more everyday शुक्रगुज़ार (shukrguzaar).
Sense 1 : grateful, obliged, thankful
adjective Used with का (kaa) marking the person or thing one is grateful for/to, as in उसका आभारी हूँ (uskaa aabhaarii hoon, 'I am grateful to him'), and often paired with होना (honaa) rather than any other verb.
Synonyms: शुक्रगुज़ार (shukrguzaar), कृतज्ञ (kritagy, literary), एहसानमंद (ehsaanmand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: आभारी (aabhaarii) shows up on formal invitation cards, in speeches, and in written thank-you notes -- it has a slightly ceremonious flavor compared to the everyday शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) or धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad) that you would actually say out loud to a friend. Politicians and hosts closing a formal event will often say कार्यक्रम में आने के लिए आपका आभारी हूँ (kaaryakram men aane ke lie aapkaa aabhaarii hoon, 'I am grateful to you for coming to the event'), which is a good model sentence to imitate.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for धन्यवाद (dhanyvaad) or शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) in every 'thank you' context, but आभारी (aabhaarii) is an adjective describing a state of being grateful (मैं आभारी हूँ, main aabhaarii hoon, 'I am grateful') rather than an interjection you toss out when someone passes the salt. 2) The person/thing you are grateful to or for is marked with का/की/के (kaa), not को (ko) -- साथ देने के लिए आपका आभारी हूँ (saath dene ke lie aapkaa aabhaarii hoon, 'I am grateful to you for your support'), which trips up learners used to को-marked recipients elsewhere.
Sample Sentences:
मैं कार्यक्रम में आने के लिए आपका आभारी हूँ। I am grateful to you for coming to the event.
(main kaaryakram men aane ke lie aapkaa aabhaarii hoon।)
