खुले आम (khule aam)

adverb An adverbial phrase meaning 'openly, publicly, in full view' -- literally 'in the open, public (space)'. Related to खुला (khulaa, 'open') and आम (aam, 'common/public' in this sense, not the fruit).
Sense 1 : openly, publicly, without hiding
adverb Functions as a fixed adverbial phrase; it does not inflect and typically sits right before the verb it modifies, e.g. खुले आम धमकी दी (khule aam dhamkii dii, 'openly threatened').
Synonyms: सरेआम (sareaam), खुल्लम खुल्ला (khullam khullaa, colloquial), प्रकट रूप से (prakat roop se, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in news reports about crimes committed खुले आम (khule aam, 'in broad daylight/publicly') -- like a theft or an assault happening in full public view, with a crowd watching but nobody stepping in. It carries a mild undertone of shock or disapproval: something that should have been hidden or shameful was instead done brazenly, for everyone to see.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect आम (aam) here or treat it like the fruit आम (aam, 'mango') -- in this phrase it means 'public/common' and the whole expression is frozen, so only खुले (khule) shows any agreement-like form and even that doesn't change. 2) It's easy to confuse with सरेआम (sareaam), which means the same thing and is arguably more common in news/crime reporting -- both are safe to use interchangeably in most contexts.

चोर ने खुले आम उसका मोबाइल छीन लिया। The thief snatched his mobile phone right out in the open.
(chor ne khule aam uskaa mobaail chhiin liyaa।)
