पैर (pair)

noun Basic word for the leg/foot (pair), and the anchor for many common idioms about status, respect, and being grounded.
Sense 1 : leg / foot (body part)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: टांग (taang, leg specifically, informal), चरण (charan, literary/respectful)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Touching someone's पैर (pair) -- पैर छूना (pair chhoonaa, to touch someone's feet) -- is a real everyday gesture of respect to elders, teachers, and at weddings, not just a book phrase. Feet are also considered the 'lowest' and least pure part of the body, which is why pointing your feet at someone or at a deity is a genuine faux pas worth knowing.
Common Mistakes: 1) पैर (pair) covers both 'leg' and 'foot' in everyday speech; Hindi does not force the distinction English does, so learners often hunt for a separate 'foot' word when none is needed. 2) टांग (taang) is used specifically for the leg (and can sound a bit crude/informal in some contexts), while पैर (pair) is the neutral, polite default for the whole limb.

Sense 2 : foot/base of an object (extended/figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तल (tal, base, more formal/technical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like English says 'foot of the hill' or 'leg of the table,' Hindi extends पैर (pair) the same way -- मेज़ का पैर (mez kaa pair, the table's leg), पहाड़ के पैर में (pahaad ke pair men, at the foot of the mountain). It is a natural metaphor that maps almost one-to-one onto English, so it is an easy extension for learners to pick up.

Sense 3 : footing, ground, standing (idiomatic, as in stability/position)
noun, masculine This sense lives almost entirely inside fixed idioms rather than as a freestanding noun meaning -- see CulturalNote.
Synonyms: जगह (jagah, place/foothold, meaning scope: only covers the 'place' sense, not the idiom)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of business or career talk in Delhi: लोग कहते हैं 'बाज़ार में पैर जमा लिए' (baazaar men pair jamaa lie, 'they've established a foothold in the market') -- the same body-grounded logic as 'get a foothold' in English, but पैर (pair) does double duty as both the literal foot and this figurative sense of stability.
Common Mistakes: 1) पैर जमाना (pair jamaanaa, to establish a foothold/get settled) and पैर उखड़ना (pair ukhadnaa, to lose one's footing/be destabilized) are compound-verb idioms; learners sometimes try to translate them word-for-word instead of learning them as set phrases.

उसने बाज़ार में अपने पैर जमा लिए हैं। He has established a foothold in the market.

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
