ज़हर (zahar)

noun/adjective Means 'poison' both literally and figuratively (as in bitter feelings or venomous words); also used as an adjective/adverb in fixed idiomatic expressions meaning 'extremely bitter or unpleasant.'
Sense 1 : poison, venom (literal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: विष (vish, literary), गरल (garal, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word in everyday warnings, like ज़हर मत खाना (zahar mat khaanaa, 'don't eat poison') said half-jokingly to someone eating something spoiled, or in news about ज़हरीली शराब (zahriilii sharaab, 'poisoned liquor') tragedies that occasionally make headlines in India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes write ज़हर (zahar) without the nuqta as जहर (jahar); both spellings are heard, but ज़हर (zahar) with the nuqta is the more standard written form. 2) Do not confuse with ज़हरीला (zahriilaa, 'poisonous'), which is the adjective form, not this noun.

Sense 2 : bitterness, venom, hostility (figurative, toward a person or in words/relationships)
noun, masculine Common in the pattern दिल में ज़हर भरना (dil men zahar bharnaa, 'to fill the heart with venom') and ज़हर उगलना (zahar ugalnaa, 'to spew venom,' i.e. to speak spitefully.
Synonyms: कड़वाहट (kadvaahat), नफ़रत (nafrat, stronger, 'hatred')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative sense shows up constantly in political and social commentary in Hindi media -- politicians are accused of ज़हर फैलाना (zahar phailaanaa, 'spreading poison,' i.e. spreading hatred or communal tension), a very live and current usage.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often miss that ज़हर उगलना (zahar ugalnaa) is used specifically for someone speaking hatefully or spitefully, not for literal vomiting. 2) The phrase ज़हर घोलना (zahar gholnaa, 'to dissolve poison') is used figuratively for 'poisoning' a relationship or atmosphere, e.g. समाज में ज़हर घोलना (samaaj men zahar gholnaa, 'to poison society'), and learners sometimes take it too literally.
Sample Sentences:

(kuchh netaa samaaj men nafrat kaa zahar phailaate hain।)

Sense 3 : extremely bitter, harsh (of taste or words, in fixed comparative expressions)
adjective Used almost exclusively in the fixed simile ज़हर जैसा (zahar jaisaa, 'bitter as poison') or ज़हर सा (zahar saa) describing taste or words; functions as an invariant intensifier rather than a fully inflecting adjective.
Synonyms: कड़वा (kadvaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in everyday complaints about food or medicine, like यह दवाई ज़हर जैसी लगती है (yah davaaii zahar jaisii lagtii hai, 'this medicine tastes like poison'), a very relatable, common exaggeration.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to inflect ज़हर (zahar) like a regular आ-ending adjective (e.g. producing ज़हरी or ज़हरे), but it stays invariant here since it is really the noun used attributively -- the inflecting form is the separate adjective ज़हरीला (zahriilaa).
