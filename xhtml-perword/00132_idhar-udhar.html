इधर-उधर (idhar-udhar)

adverb A reduplicated adverb built from इधर (idhar, 'here') and उधर (udhar, 'there'), meaning 'here and there' or 'around, aimlessly'; it also has an extended sense of evasive, roundabout talk or behavior.
Sense 1 : here and there, around (physical movement or scattering)
adverb
Synonyms: यहाँ-वहाँ (yahaan-vahaan)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in daily scoldings: चीज़ें इधर-उधर मत फेंको (chiizen idhar-udhar mat phenko, 'don't throw things all over the place') is a classic line every Indian household has heard. It captures the Hindi love of reduplication to express diffuseness -- compare थोड़ा-थोड़ा (thodaa-thodaa) or धीरे-धीरे (dhiire-dhiire).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as two separate words rather than a fixed pair, and try to insert other words between them (e.g. 'इधर बहुत उधर'); it functions as a single adverbial unit. 2) It describes scattered, undirected movement or placement, not a specific destination -- do not use it where you would name an actual place.
Sample Sentences:
चीज़ें इधर-उधर मत फेंको। Don't throw things all over the place.
(chiizen idhar-udhar mat phenko।)

Sense 2 : aimlessly wandering, without direction
adverb Common with verbs of motion like घूमना (ghoomnaa) and भटकना (bhataknaa) to describe wandering with no fixed goal.
Synonyms: बेमतलब (bematlab, of purposeless action)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A parent asking a teenager इधर-उधर घूमने की बजाय पढ़ाई करो (idhar-udhar ghoomne kii bajaay padhaaii karo, 'instead of wandering around, study') is a very familiar tone in Indian homes -- it carries a mild note of disapproval about wasted, unfocused time.

इधर-उधर घूमने की बजाय पढ़ाई करो। Instead of wandering around aimlessly, study.

Sense 3 : evasive, beating around the bush (in speech or answers)
adverb Used especially with बात करना (baat karnaa) or जवाब देना (javaab denaa) to mean giving a non-answer or dodging the real topic.
Synonyms: टाल-मटोल (taal-matol, of evasive behavior specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: इधर-उधर की बातें मत करो, सीधा जवाब दो (idhar-udhar kii baaten mat karo, siidhaa javaab do, 'stop beating around the bush, give a straight answer') is something you might hear from an exasperated teacher or parent -- it extends the spatial scattering into a scattering of the conversation itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense can be missed by learners who only know the literal 'here and there' meaning; इधर-उधर की बातें (idhar-udhar kii baaten, 'this-and-that talk/small talk') is a set phrase meaning idle or evasive chatter, not literal locations.
Sample Sentences:
इधर-उधर की बातें मत करो, सीधा जवाब दो। Stop beating around the bush, give a straight answer.
(idhar-udhar kii baaten mat karo, siidhaa javaab do।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active
जब भी मैं पैसों के बारे में पूछता हूँ, वह इधर-उधर की बातें करने लगता है। Whenever I ask about the money, he starts beating around the bush.
(jab bhii main paison ke baare men poochhtaa hoon, vah idhar-udhar kii baaten karne lagtaa hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
