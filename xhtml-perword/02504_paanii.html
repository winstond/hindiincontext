पानी (paanii)

noun Mainly means 'water' in everyday speech, with extended figurative uses covering shine/quality of a gemstone and, in idioms, courage or self-respect.
Sense 1 : water
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जल (jal, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Offering a guest पानी (paanii) the moment they sit down is such a basic hospitality reflex in most Indian homes that its absence would be noticed. You will also hear पानी (paanii) used loosely for any drinkable liquid in context, like नारियल का पानी (naariyal kaa paanii, 'coconut water').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जल (jal) by default because it looks like a 'dictionary' word, but in daily speech पानी (paanii) is what people actually ask for, order, or offer -- जल (jal) shows up mainly in religious, formal, or written contexts (like गंगाजल (gangaajal)). 2) पानी पीना (paanii piinaa, 'to drink water') is the everyday phrase; learners sometimes overthink the verb choice here when पीना (piinaa) is simply the right one.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे प्यास लगी है, क्या आप पानी दे सकते हैं? I'm thirsty, can you give me some water?
(mujhe pyaas lagii hai, kyaa aap paanii de sakte hain?)

Sense 2 : self-respect, honor, courage
noun, masculine Lives inside idioms such as पानी उतर जाना (paanii utar jaanaa, 'to lose one's honor/be disgraced') and पानी भरना (paanii bharnaa, 'to be far inferior to someone,' literally 'to fetch water for someone').
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त (izzat), ग़ैरत (ghairat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi ties self-respect to पानी (paanii) the way English ties it to 'face' -- so किसी का पानी उतार देना (kisii kaa paanii utaar denaa, 'to strip someone of their dignity') is a vivid, commonly heard insult in arguments and dramatic dialogue alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) पानी भरना (paanii bharnaa) does not mean literally fetching water in its idiomatic use -- it means someone doesn't even measure up to another person, as in किसी के आगे पानी भरना (kisii ke aage paanii bharnaa, 'to be no match for someone'). 2) पानी उतरना (paanii utarnaa) for 'losing face' can confuse learners who only know पानी (paanii) as liquid water; this sense is entirely figurative.
Sample Sentences:
उसने सबके सामने मेरा पानी उतार दिया। He stripped me of my dignity in front of everyone.
(usne sabke saamne meraa paanii utaar diyaa।)

Sense 3 : luster, quality (of a gem)
noun, masculine Used almost exclusively in the fixed phrase किसी पानी का हीरा होना (kisii paanii kaa hiiraa honaa) or similar constructions describing quality/caliber of a person or gem.
Synonyms: चमक (chamak)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This comes from the old jeweler's practice of grading diamonds by their 'water' or clarity -- पहले पानी का हीरा (pahle paanii kaa hiiraa, 'a first-water diamond') still gets used to praise a person as top-notch, much like the English idiom.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only survives in set expressions like पहले पानी का (pahle paanii kaa, 'first-rate, top quality') -- learners shouldn't try to use पानी (paanii) alone to mean 'shine' outside such fixed phrases.
