बाद (baad)

postposition/conjunction Mainly used in the postposition-like frame X के बाद (ke baad, 'after X'), and in a couple of common fixed conjunctive phrases built on the same root.
Sense 1 : after (in time)
postposition Always follows the oblique of a noun/pronoun/verbal noun plus के: X के बाद (ke baad, 'after X').
Synonyms: उपरांत (upraant, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first 'after' words learners meet, and it pairs naturally with पहले (pahle, 'before') in comparisons like स्कूल के बाद (skool ke baad, 'after school') vs स्कूल के पहले (skool ke pahle, 'before school'). Once you have यह (yah) + के बाद down, you'll hear it constantly in daily planning talk -- खाने के बाद (khaane ke baad, 'after eating') is a classic.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop के (ke) and say just X बाद (X baad), which sounds wrong -- the के is required: दो घंटे के बाद (do ghante ke baad, 'after two hours'), not दो घंटे बाद alone in careful speech (though this shorter form is also heard colloquially, it's not the safe default to produce). 2) Confusing बाद (baad) with पीछे (piichhe) -- पीछे is about physical/spatial 'behind,' while बाद is strictly about time.

(khaane ke baad ham baazaar jaaenge।)

Sense 2 : afterward, then, later (as a sentence-linking adverbial)
conjunction Used adverbially as बाद में (baad men) to mean 'later/afterward' without a following noun -- functions as a free-standing time adverb linking two clauses or events.
Synonyms: फिर (phir), बाद में (baad men)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बाद में मिलते हैं (baad men milte hain, 'let's meet later') is something you'll hear constantly when plans get pushed -- a very Delhi way of politely postponing without committing to a time.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use bare बाद (baad) where बाद में (baad men) is needed -- without में (men), बाद by itself cannot stand alone as 'afterward'; it needs either के बाद with a noun, or में to function adverbially on its own.
