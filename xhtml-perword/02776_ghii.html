घी (ghii)

noun A single, concrete sense: clarified butter, a staple of the Indian kitchen. Its cultural weight (purity, richness, festivity) is folded into the one sense rather than split into separate meanings.
Sense 1 : clarified butter
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ghee is not just a cooking fat here -- it shows up in temple lamps, on festival food, drizzled over dal or hot parathas as a mark of care, and even in the idiom उल्टे बाँस बरेली को ...ok scratch that, more relevantly: घी के दीये जलाना (ghii ke diiye jalaanaa, 'to light ghee lamps') is what people say when celebrating great news, since it's traditionally the fuel for auspicious lamps. Homemade घी (ghii) made by simmering butter slowly on the stove is a point of quiet pride in many households, distinct from the store-bought tin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate घी (ghii) as 'butter' and use it interchangeably with मक्खन (makkhan) -- but मक्खन (makkhan) is the unclarified butter you'd churn from cream, while घी (ghii) is the cooked-down, ghee form; a recipe or a grandmother will not accept one for the other. 2) घी (ghii) is grammatically masculine, so watch agreement: यह घी अच्छा है (yah ghii achchhaa hai, 'this ghee is good'), not अच्छी.
