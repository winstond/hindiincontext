नम (nam)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'damp/moist'; also carries a common figurative extension for eyes welling up with emotion.
Sense 1 : damp, moist
adjective
Synonyms: गीला (giilaa), भीगा (bhiigaa, slightly wet/soaked), तर (tar, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear नम (nam) used for the air or soil, as in नम हवा (nam havaa, 'moist air'), especially describing the monsoon season or early mornings in Delhi when there's dew on the grass.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not confuse with गीला (giilaa) -- नम (nam) suggests a slight, often pleasant or natural dampness (morning air, soil after dew), while गीला (giilaa) means properly wet, as after being soaked or rained on.

Sense 2 : moist with tears, welling up (of eyes)
adjective Almost always used with आँखें (aankhen, 'eyes') as in आँखें नम होना (aankhen nam honaa, 'eyes to well up') -- the eyes are the grammatical subject, not the person.
Synonyms: भीगा (bhiigaa, of eyes, poetic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a favorite phrase in Hindi film dialogue and news reporting for describing suppressed emotion -- आँखें नम हो गईं (aankhen nam ho gaiin, 'eyes welled up') is a gentler, more literary way to say someone got emotional than saying they cried outright.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to say 'I became नम (nam)' -- but the word attaches to आँखें (aankhen), so you say आँखें नम हो गईं (aankhen nam ho gaiin, 'the eyes became moist'), not a construction centered on the person directly.
