कला (kalaa)

noun कला (kalaa) most commonly means 'art' or 'skill/craft' -- a single core sense that covers both fine art and the notion of an artful ability or trick.
Sense 1 : art (as a field or discipline)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कलाकारी (kalaakaarii, craftsmanship/artistry), हुनर (hunar, skill, more informal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this word everywhere from ललित कला (lalit kalaa, 'fine arts') college names to Republic Day parade descriptions of 'लोक कला' (lok kalaa, 'folk art'). It carries a certain respect in Hindi -- calling something कला elevates it above mere काम (kaam, 'work').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse कला (kalaa) with कलम (kalam, 'pen') because of the similar start -- they are unrelated. 2) कला (kalaa) is used for both 'art' as a discipline (जैसे संगीत कला, sangiit kalaa, 'the art of music') and for a specific 'skill/knack,' so context decides which sense is meant.

Sense 2 : skill, knack, craft (a specific learned ability)
noun, feminine Often used in compound phrases like बातचीत की कला (baatchiit kii kalaa, 'the art of conversation') where English would also say 'art' but the sense is really practical skill, not fine art.
Synonyms: हुनर (hunar), गुण (gun, quality/virtue, broader meaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a grandmother's cooking being praised as एक कला (ek kalaa, 'an art') -- Hindi speakers extend कला generously to any well-honed skill, not just paintings and dance.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners overuse the 'fine art' sense and miss that कला (kalaa) is very natural for everyday skills too, like खाना बनाने की कला (khaanaa banaane kii kalaa, 'the art of cooking').
