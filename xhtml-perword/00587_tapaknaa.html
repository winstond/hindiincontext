टपकना (tapaknaa)

verb A concrete, everyday verb meaning 'to drip/leak' (of liquids) that extends naturally to 'to show up unannounced' when used of people. Both senses are intransitive.
Sense 1 : to drip, to leak, to trickle down
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: चूना (choonaa), रिसना (risnaa, to seep/ooze, slower and less droplet-like)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for a leaky tap dripping through the night (नल टपक रहा है, nal tapak rahaa hai, 'the tap is dripping') or for sweat running down someone's face in the Delhi summer (पसीना टपकना, pasiinaa tapaknaa). Very much a kitchen-and-monsoon-season word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चूना (choonaa) and टपकना (tapaknaa) as fully interchangeable -- टपकना (tapaknaa) specifically evokes drop-by-drop falling (a tap, a wet umbrella, sweat), while चूना (choonaa) is the more general word for a roof or vessel leaking.
Sample Sentences:

(raatbhar nal tapaktaa rahaa।)

Sense 2 : to turn up unannounced or suddenly (of a person)
verb, intransitive Almost always used with a locative or temporal phrase implying surprise, e.g. अचानक टपक पड़ना (achaanak tapak padnaa, 'to suddenly turn up') -- often paired with पड़ना as a compound verb to sharpen the suddenness.
Synonyms: आ धमकना (aa dhamaknaa, compound verb, slightly more forceful/intrusive), प्रकट होना (prakat honaa, more formal/dramatic)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Very much a chatty, informal usage -- friends tease each other with lines like तुम कहाँ से टपक पड़े? (tum kahaan se tapak pade?, 'where did you spring from?'). The image is the same as sense one: a person 'dripping in' as if from nowhere, which is exactly the wit behind the extension.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense carries a mildly humorous or faintly disapproving tone -- it is not neutral like 'to arrive.' Using it about an expected, welcome guest sounds off; it fits best for someone who appears uninvited or at an odd moment.
