मुश्किल से (mushkil se)

adverb An adverbial phrase built from मुश्किल (mushkil, 'difficult') plus से (se); means 'with difficulty' or 'hardly, barely,' covering both a literal sense (something done only through great effort) and an extended sense (something that just barely happened or almost didn't happen).
Sense 1 : with great difficulty, only through struggle
adverb
Synonyms: बड़ी मुश्किल से (badii mushkil se, emphatic variant), कठिनाई से (kathinaaii se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture someone hauling a heavy suitcase up a train platform staircase and gasping वह मुश्किल से ऊपर पहुँचा (vah mushkil se oopar pahunchaa, 'he barely managed to get up') -- that physical strain is the everyday flavor this phrase carries, even when used about non-physical struggles like exams or finances.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use मुश्किल (mushkil) alone as an adverb, dropping से (se) -- the postposition is required to turn the adjective/noun into this adverbial sense. 2) Word order matters less than in English: मुश्किल से (mushkil se) usually sits right before the verb it modifies, not necessarily at the start of the sentence.
Sample Sentences:
वह मुश्किल से ऊपर पहुँचा। He managed to get up there only with great difficulty.
(vah mushkil se oopar pahunchaa।)

Sense 2 : barely, just narrowly (an event that almost failed to happen)
adverb This sense modifies whether an event happened at all (a near miss), rather than describing effort expended -- compare 'she barely caught the train' versus 'she caught the train with difficulty.'
Synonyms: बाल-बाल (baal-baal, idiomatic, narrow escape sense only)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used for close calls: मुश्किल से ट्रेन पकड़ी (mushkil se tren pakdii, 'barely caught the train') is the classic example -- every Delhi commuter racing across a platform as the doors buzz shut knows this feeling.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners conflate this with the 'with effort' sense; context (a time limit, a deadline, a near-failure) signals this narrower 'just barely' reading rather than physical/mental struggle.
