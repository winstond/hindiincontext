जवान (javaan)

adjective/noun Two related senses: as an adjective it means 'young' (in the prime of youth), and as a noun it refers specifically to a soldier, especially a rank-and-file one in the Indian armed forces.
Sense 1 : young, youthful, in one's prime
adjective
Synonyms: युवा (yuvaa), तरुण (tarun, literary), नौजवान (naujvaan)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear जवान (javaan) often in the phrase जवान होना (javaan honaa, 'to come of age/grow up'), used affectionately by parents watching a child mature -- 'बेटा जवान हो गया है' (betaa javaan ho gayaa hai, 'the son has grown into a young man') carries real warmth and a touch of pride.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use जवान (javaan) interchangeably with छोटा (chhotaa, 'small/younger'), but जवान (javaan) specifically means 'in the prime of youth,' not just 'younger than someone else' -- for that comparative sense, छोटा (chhotaa) is correct.

Sense 2 : soldier (especially a common/rank-and-file soldier)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सिपाही (sipaahii), फ़ौजी (faujii, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense of जवान (javaan) is deeply woven into Indian civic and patriotic life -- it is the standard, respectful term for soldiers on the border, as in slogans and news headlines like सीमा पर जवान (siimaa par javaan, 'soldiers at the border'). It carries a warmer, more human tone than the more official सैनिक (sainik), evoking the ordinary young men who serve.
