फंदा (phandaa)

noun Primarily a physical 'noose/snare/loop,' with a common figurative extension to a trap or entanglement (often romantic or situational) that a person gets caught in. Also note the variant spelling फ़ंदा (fandaa) with the nuqta.
Sense 1 : noose, snare, loop (physical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जाल (jaal, net/trap, broader sense), पाश (paash, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word in news reports about suicides (phandaa lagaanaa, 'to hang oneself') as well as in hunting/trapping contexts -- शिकारी ने फंदा लगाया (shikaarii ne phandaa lagaayaa, 'the hunter set a snare'). It is a fairly blunt, concrete word, not euphemistic.
Common Mistakes: 1) फंदा (phandaa) refers to a loop of rope/string, whether used to catch an animal, hang oneself, or even a knitting loop -- learners sometimes default only to 'noose' and miss the broader 'loop/snare' sense.

(pulis ko kamre men ek phandaa latkaa huaa milaa।)

Sense 2 : trap, entanglement (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the fixed expression फंदे में फंसना (phande men phansnaa, 'to get caught in a trap') -- often used lightly about romantic entanglements or tricky situations, not just literal danger.
Synonyms: जाल (jaal, trap/web), चक्कर (chakkar, colloquial, situational mess/entanglement)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Bollywood and everyday banter love this metaphor -- शादी का फंदा (shaadii kaa phandaa, 'the trap of marriage') is a common half-joking phrase older relatives or friends use to tease someone about getting married, framing commitment as a loop you get caught in.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate this too literally as 'noose' in figurative contexts -- इश्क़ का फंदा (ishq kaa phandaa, 'the trap of love') is playful/idiomatic, not sinister. 2) The verb pairing is फंसना (phansnaa, to get caught) or फंसाना (phansaanaa, to entrap someone) -- फंदा (phandaa) itself is the noun, not the action.
