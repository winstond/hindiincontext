ठहराना (thahraanaa)

verb The causative of ठहरना (thaharnaa, 'to stop/stay'); core senses are 'to make something stop or stay' and, by extension, 'to declare/hold someone as' (e.g. guilty, responsible).
Sense 1 : to declare, hold, judge (someone/something to be a certain way)
verb, transitive Takes the frame X को Y ठहराना ('to declare/hold X as Y'), where Y is typically an adjective or noun describing a verdict or judgment, e.g. दोषी ठहराना (doshii thahraanaa, 'to hold guilty').
Synonyms: करार देना (karaar denaa), घोषित करना (ghoshit karnaa, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the courtroom and news-headline verb: अदालत ने उसे दोषी ठहराया (adaalat ne use doshii thahraayaa, 'the court held him guilty') is a stock phrase you will hear constantly on Hindi news channels; it also extends to everyday blame, as in किसी को ज़िम्मेदार ठहराना (kisii ko zimmedaar thahraanaa, 'to hold someone responsible').
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is almost always used with को (ko) marking the person or thing being judged -- दोषी को नहीं, बल्कि उसे दोषी ठहराया गया (use doshii thahraayaa gayaa, 'he was declared guilty') -- learners sometimes try to use से (se) here by analogy with other verbs, which is wrong.
Sample Sentences:

(adaalat ne use doshii thahraayaa।)

Sense 2 : to make stop, halt (something in motion)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: रोकना (roknaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of a rickshaw-wallah or a boatman being told गाड़ी यहाँ ठहरा दो (gaadii yahaan thahraa do, 'stop the vehicle here') -- the verb carries a sense of bringing something to rest at a spot, related to ठहरना (thaharnaa, 'to stay/halt') of which this is the causative.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this with रोकना (roknaa); रोकना is the more general everyday word for 'stop,' while ठहराना (thahraanaa) leans toward bringing something to rest at a place (a vehicle, a boat) rather than blocking or preventing motion in general.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : to let someone stay, put someone up (lodging)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: टिकाना (tikaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the natural verb for hosting -- a relative visiting Delhi might say हमने उन्हें अपने घर ठहराया (hamne unhen apne ghar thahraayaa, 'we put them up at our house'), reflecting the deep hospitality norm of giving guests a place to stay rather than sending them to a hotel.
Sample Sentences:
