चोरी होना (chorii honaa)

verb A compound formed from चोरी (chorii, 'theft') plus होना (honaa, 'to happen'), meaning 'to get stolen' -- the standard way Hindi describes a theft happening to an item, framing the object as undergoing the event rather than naming a thief.
Sense 1 : to be stolen, to get stolen
verb, intransitive The thing stolen is the grammatical subject and takes intransitive past-tense agreement (no ने): मेरी साइकिल चोरी हो गई (merii saaikil chorii ho gaii, 'my bicycle got stolen'). The person affected, if mentioned, is marked with की: मेरी साइकिल चोरी हो गई vs. मेरा चोरी हो गया।
Synonyms: चुराया जाना (churaayaa jaanaa, passive construction, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you will actually hear in a Delhi market or a housing society WhatsApp group -- मेरी स्कूटी चोरी हो गई (merii skootii chorii ho gaii, 'my scooter got stolen') -- because Hindi prefers describing the event happening to the object over naming a culprit, much like English 'my phone got stolen' rather than 'someone stole my phone.' It pairs naturally with a police-report context: चोरी की रिपोर्ट (chorii kii riport, 'theft report') is the next phrase you will need.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to build a passive with चुराना (churaanaa) plus जाना (jaanaa) where चोरी होना (chorii honaa) would sound far more natural in everyday speech -- चुराया जाना (churaayaa jaanaa) exists but reads as stiffer and more formal. 2) The perfective is almost always paired with हो गई/हो गया/हो गए (ho gaii, ho gayaa, ho gae) rather than plain हुई/हुआ (huii, huaa) -- dropping गया/गई sounds incomplete, like reporting the theft as an ongoing state rather than a completed event. 3) The thief, if named, takes से (se) not ने: उससे मेरा फ़ोन चोरी हुआ (usse meraa fon chorii huaa) is wrong -- native speakers simply say किसी ने मेरा फ़ोन चुराया (kisii ne meraa fon churaayaa) if they want to name an agent, switching constructions entirely rather than forcing an agent into चोरी होना (chorii honaa).
Sample Sentences:
कल रात मेरी स्कूटी चोरी हो गई। My scooter got stolen last night.
(kal raat merii skootii chorii ho gaii।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Passive
इस कॉलोनी में आजकल बहुत चोरियाँ हो रही हैं। A lot of thefts are happening in this colony these days.
(is kolonii men aajkal bahut choriyaan ho rahii hain।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Continuous -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Passive

(agar tum gaadii yahaan khadii karoge to chorii ho jaaegii।)

(puraanii dillii ke baazaar men aksar mobaail chorii ho jaate hain।)

पहले भी उसकी साइकिल चोरी हो चुकी है। His bicycle has already been stolen before too.
(pahle bhii uskii saaikil chorii ho chukii hai।)

सावधान रहो, वरना तुम्हारा बटुआ चोरी हो जाएगा। Be careful, or else your wallet will get stolen.
(saavdhaan raho, varnaa tumhaaraa batuaa chorii ho jaaegaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Passive
अगर मैंने गेट बंद न किया होता, तो साइकिल ज़रूर चोरी हो जाती। If I hadn't closed the gate, the bicycle would definitely have gotten stolen.

हमें डर है कि कहीं हमारा सामान चोरी न हो जाए। We are afraid that our luggage might get stolen.
(hamen dar hai ki kahiin hamaaraa saamaan chorii n ho jaae।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Subjunctive -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Passive
थाने जाकर उसने चोरी की रिपोर्ट लिखवाई क्योंकि उसका लैपटॉप चोरी हो गया था। He went to the police station and filed a theft report because his laptop had been stolen.
(thaane jaakar usne chorii kii riport likhvaaii kyonki uskaa laiptop chorii ho gayaa thaa।)

(maidam, meraa pars chorii ho gayaa hai, kripyaa madad kiijie।)

(sosaaitii ke vhaatsaep grup par usne likhaa ki uskii baaik chorii ho gaii hai।)
