अनाथ (anaath)

noun/adjective A single core meaning: 'orphan' -- used both as a noun (a person, especially a child, without parents) and as an adjective (parentless, helpless/forsaken).
Sense 1 : orphan (a person without parents)
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: अनाथ (anaath) literally breaks down as अ- (a, 'without') + नाथ (naath, 'protector/lord'), so the word's root sense is 'one without a protector' rather than just 'parentless' -- this is why it can extend to a broader sense of being helpless or unsupported (see the adjective sense below). अनाथालय (anaathaalay) 'orphanage' is a very common derived word built from this root.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat अनाथ (anaath) as inflecting like a regular आ-ending adjective/noun, but it stays invariant in all forms -- अनाथ बच्चे (anaath bachche, 'orphan children'), not a changed ending. 2) The common compound अनाथालय (anaathaalay, 'orphanage') is often the more natural word to reach for when talking about the institution, rather than describing a 'home for अनाथ (anaath)' with a longer phrase.

Sense 2 : orphaned, parentless; by extension, helpless or forsaken
adjective
Synonyms: बेसहारा (besahaaraa, helpless/without support), निराश्रित (niraashrit, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Beyond the literal 'parentless,' अनाथ (anaath) can be used figuratively for anyone or anything left without support or protection -- a cause, a nation, or a person in a difficult situation can be described as feeling अनाथ (anaath), echoing its root meaning 'without a protector.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Because अनाथ (anaath) is grammatically invariant, learners familiar with the standard -आ/-ई adjective pattern may wrongly try to change its ending for gender or number agreement -- it stays the same in every context: अनाथ लड़का (anaath ladkaa, 'orphan boy'), अनाथ लड़की (anaath ladkii, 'orphan girl').
