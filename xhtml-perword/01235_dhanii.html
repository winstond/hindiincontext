धनी (dhanii)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'wealthy, rich'; also used as a noun for 'a wealthy person' and, in an older/literary register, 'master, lord, husband.'
Sense 1 : wealthy, rich
adjective
Synonyms: अमीर (amiir), मालदार (maaldaar, colloquial), संपन्न (sampann, means well-off, broader than just money), दौलतमंद (daulatmand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear अमीर (amiir) far more in daily conversation -- गाड़ी वाला अमीर आदमी (gaadii vaalaa amiir aadmii, 'the rich man with the car')-type remarks -- while धनी (dhanii) shows up more in writing, news, or slightly elevated speech, like describing a 'धनी परिवार' (dhanii parivaar, 'a wealthy family') in a newspaper article.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often overuse धनी (dhanii) where अमीर (amiir) is more natural in casual speech; धनी (dhanii) leans slightly more formal/written, closer to 'affluent' than everyday 'rich.' 2) Don't confuse with धनि (dhani), an interjection meaning 'blessed/well done' seen in older poetry -- unrelated in feel despite the near-identical spelling.

Sense 2 : a wealthy person
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun by simply placing it in a noun slot, e.g. एक धनी (ek dhanii, 'a wealthy person'), without any suffix change.
Synonyms: अमीर (amiir), धनवान (dhanvaan), पैसे वाला (paise vaalaa, colloquial)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This noun use is less common than just saying अमीर आदमी (amiir aadmii, 'rich man') or अमीर औरत (amiir aurat, 'rich woman'); धनी (dhanii) as a standalone noun feels a bit more bookish, the kind of word you'd meet in a moral fable contrasting धनी और निर्धन (dhanii aur nirdhan, 'the rich and the poor').

Sense 3 : master, lord, husband (older/literary usage)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वामी (svaamii, literary), पति (pati, meaning specifically husband)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This older sense treats धनी (dhanii) as 'the one who owns/possesses' -- extending naturally from 'wealthy' to 'one who has authority,' hence 'master' or, in devotional and folk contexts, 'lord' as in addressing God. It survives mainly in bhajans, folk songs, and older literature rather than everyday talk.
