ख़ून (khoon)

noun Primarily the physical noun 'blood' (khoon); it also extends figuratively into bloodline/lineage and into common idioms about killing, ruthlessness, and passion. Also spelled without the nuqta as खून (khoon).
Sense 1 : blood (the bodily fluid)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रक्त (rakt, formal/medical), लहू (lahoo)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly in everyday complaints about injuries -- ख़ून निकल आया (khoon nikal aayaa, 'blood came out') is the natural way to describe even a small cut, where English might just say 'I'm bleeding.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for रक्त (rakt) in everyday speech; native speakers overwhelmingly say ख़ून (khoon) in conversation and save रक्त (rakt) for medical or formal contexts like blood donation camps or textbooks.

Sense 2 : bloodline, lineage, hereditary trait
noun, masculine Common in the frame X का ख़ून है (X kaa khoon hai, 'it's in X's blood') to mean an inherited trait or family disposition.
Synonyms: ख़ानदान (khaandaan, lineage/family, broader scope), नस्ल (nasl, breed/lineage)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like in English, Hindi uses blood to talk about inherited character: बाप का ख़ून है (baap kaa khoon hai, 'it's his father's blood [in him]') is a classic line explaining why a son turned out brave, hot-tempered, or talented, usually said with a mix of pride and resignation.

Sense 3 : killing, bloodshed (in fixed idioms)
noun, masculine Lives mainly in set phrases like ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa, 'to commit murder') and ख़ून-ख़राबा (khoon-kharaabaa, 'bloodshed/violence').
Synonyms: हत्या (hatyaa, formal, refers to the act of murder itself), क़त्ल (qatl)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: News reports and dramatic Bollywood dialogue alike favor ख़ून (khoon) over the more clinical हत्या (hatyaa) when the tone is emotional or accusatory -- 'तुमने उसका ख़ून किया' (tumne uskaa khoon kiyaa, 'you murdered him') hits harder than the courtroom-style हत्या (hatyaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa) means 'to murder someone,' not just 'to cause blood' -- learners sometimes misparse it literally instead of recognizing it as the standard verb for killing.
Sample Sentences:
तुमने उसका ख़ून किया है! You have murdered him!
(tumne uskaa khoon kiyaa hai!)

Sense 4 : hot-bloodedness, passion, vigor (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in जवान ख़ून (javaan khoon, 'young blood') referring to youthful energy or impulsiveness, and ख़ून खौलना (khoon khaulnaa, 'blood to boil,' meaning to become furious).
Synonyms: जोश (josh, enthusiasm/zeal, broader scope), जज़्बा (jazbaa, fervor/spirit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ख़ून खौल उठा (khoon khaul uthaa, 'his blood boiled up') is the go-to phrase for righteous anger, especially in speeches or dramatic storytelling about injustice -- it carries a more visceral punch than a plain 'he got angry.'
