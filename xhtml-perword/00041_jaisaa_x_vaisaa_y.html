जैसा x वैसा y (jaisaa x vaisaa y)

pattern A correlative frame pairing जैसा (jaisaa, 'as/like') with वैसा (vaisaa, 'so/thus') to link two matching or corresponding things -- the backbone of many Hindi proverbs.
Sense 1 : correlative construction expressing correspondence or similarity between two things
pattern जैसा (jaisaa) is the relative half and वैसा (vaisaa) the correlative half; both inflect like ordinary -आ adjectives (जैसा/जैसे/जैसी, वैसा/वैसे/वैसी) to agree with the noun each modifies -- e.g. जैसी सोच वैसा काम (jaisii soch vaisaa kaam, 'as the thinking, so the work').
Synonyms: जैसे...वैसे (jaise...vaise, adverbial variant used for actions/manner rather than nouns)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This frame powers some of Hindi's best-loved proverbs: जैसा करोगे वैसा भरोगे (jaisaa karoge vaisaa bharoge, 'as you sow, so shall you reap') and जैसा देश वैसा भेष (jaisaa desh vaisaa bhesh, 'when in Rome, do as the Romans do' -- literally 'as the land, so the attire'). Parents and elders lean on this pattern constantly to teach cause-and-consequence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Forgetting that जैसा (jaisaa) and वैसा (vaisaa) must each agree in gender/number with the noun they describe, not with each other by default. 2) Mixing up जैसा (jaisaa) (adjective, agrees with a noun) with जैसे (jaise) (adverb, invariant, used for manner of an action) -- e.g. जैसे चाहो वैसे करो (jaise chaaho vaise karo, 'do it however you like') needs जैसे/वैसे, not जैसा/वैसा. 3) Reversing the order: the जैसा-clause must come first and वैसा-clause second, unlike English where 'so' can sometimes lead.
