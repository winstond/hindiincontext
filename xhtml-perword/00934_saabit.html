साबित (saabit)

adjective An adjective used almost exclusively with करना (karnaa) or होना (honaa) to talk about proving or being proven -- 'साबित करना' (saabit karnaa) 'to prove' and 'साबित होना' (saabit honaa) 'to turn out to be, be proven'.
Sense 1 : proven, established (as fact)
adjective Used predicatively with होना (honaa) to mean 'to be/turn out proven' -- e.g. वह सही साबित हुआ (vah sahii saabit huaa, 'he was proven right/he turned out to be right').
Synonyms: प्रमाणित (pramaanit, formal), सिद्ध (siddh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word shows up constantly in news and courtroom drama on Indian TV -- आरोप साबित नहीं हुआ (aarop saabit nahiin huaa, 'the charge was not proven') is a stock phrase. Note the pairing: साबित करना (saabit karnaa) is the transitive 'to prove (something)', while साबित होना (saabit honaa) is the intransitive 'to be/turn out proven' -- the same adjective riding on two different light verbs, a very common Hindi pattern worth internalizing early.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use साबित (saabit) as a standalone verb; it is an adjective and always needs करना (karnaa) or होना (honaa) beside it to function in a sentence. 2) साबित होना (saabit honaa) is frequently used where English says 'turned out to be' rather than literally 'was proven' -- e.g. यह फैसला गलत साबित हुआ (yah phaislaa galat saabit huaa, 'this decision turned out to be wrong'), which can feel like an odd extension to learners expecting 'prove' only in legal/logical contexts.

(uskaa aarop adaalat men saabit nahiin huaa।)
