खंडहर (khandahar)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'ruins' or 'a ruined structure' -- the crumbled remains of a building, fort, or city.
Sense 1 : ruins, a derelict/ruined building
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भग्नावशेष (bhagnaavshesh, literary), वीरान इमारत (viiraan imaarat, describes an abandoned/desolate building rather than the ruins themselves)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Old forts and havelis across Delhi-NCR (Tughlaqabad, Feroz Shah Kotla) are commonly called खंडहर (khandahar), and the word carries a slightly poetic, wistful tone -- it evokes lost grandeur rather than just physical damage. You'll often hear it in phrases like किसी शहर के खंडहर (kisii shahar ke khandahar, 'the ruins of some city'), used almost as a metaphor for anything that once thrived and is now abandoned, including relationships or empires.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use खंडहर (khandahar) for any 'old building,' but it specifically implies decay and abandonment, not just age -- a well-kept old haveli is पुरानी हवेली (puraanii havelii), not खंडहर (khandahar). 2) It is often used in the plural sense even in singular form (like 'ruins' in English), referring to the whole site rather than one broken piece.
