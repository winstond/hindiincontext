ग़म (gham)

noun A single core sense meaning grief or sorrow, but a very culturally loaded word: it carries the weight of shayari, ghazals, and everyday emotional talk about life's sadness.
Sense 1 : grief, sorrow, sadness
noun, masculine Very commonly used with करना (karnaa) in the fixed phrase ग़म करना (gham karnaa, 'to grieve/worry') and with खाना (khaanaa) in ग़म खाना (gham khaanaa, 'to swallow one's sorrow').
Synonyms: दुख (dukh), उदासी (udaasii), रंज (ranj, somewhat literary), अफ़सोस (afsos, closer to 'regret')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: ग़म (gham) is one of the most-used words in Urdu-Hindi poetry and film lyrics -- ग़म-ए-दिल (gham-e-dil, 'sorrow of the heart') and भुलाना ग़म (bhulaanaa gham, 'to forget one's sorrows') are stock phrases you will hear constantly in ghazals and old songs. In everyday speech, people also say कोई ग़म नहीं (koii gham nahiin, 'no worries at all') as a casual, reassuring phrase, which shows how the word has settled comfortably into daily conversation too.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दुख (dukh) in every context, but ग़म (gham) has a more poetic, romantic-melancholy flavor picked up from Urdu shayari and Bollywood songs -- it is not just plain 'sadness,' it is sadness with a certain depth or style. 2) The nuqta on the ग़ is often dropped in casual writing (गम), but the pronunciation with the Urdu 'gh' sound is standard in careful speech.
