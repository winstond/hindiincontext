वाला (vaalaa)

adjective An extremely versatile suffix-word: attached to nouns, infinitives, or oblique phrases it means 'the one who/that (does/has/is related to) X'; it also has an independent use marking imminent action ('about to'). Inflects like an adjective (वाला/वाले/वाली).
Sense 1 : the one who does/has/relates to (agentive/possessive suffix)
adjective, masculine/feminine Attaches to the oblique form of a noun to mean 'the one associated with that noun': दूध वाला (doodh vaalaa, 'the milkman'), चाय वाली (chaay vaalii, 'the tea-seller woman'). Agrees in gender/number with the person or thing described.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most useful words in spoken Hindi -- every neighborhood has its दूध वाला (doodh vaalaa, 'milkman'), सब्ज़ी वाला (sabzii vaalaa, 'vegetable seller'), and अखबार वाला (akhbaar vaalaa, 'newspaperman'), and you'll hear these compounds constantly in daily Delhi life. It's less a formal grammatical suffix in the speaker's mind and more a handy way to instantly turn any noun into 'the person/thing associated with it.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget that the preceding noun/adjective must be in the oblique form when applicable, e.g. घर वाला (ghar vaalaa) not घरा वाला. 2) वाला (vaalaa) changes form to वाली (vaalii) or वाले (vaale) to agree with the noun it describes, not with the noun it's attached to -- चाय वाला (chaay vaalaa) is masculine even though चाय (chaay) is feminine, because it refers to a male vendor.

Sense 2 : the one who does (habitual doer, attached to verb stem + ने)
adjective, masculine/feminine Attaches to the oblique infinitive (verb stem + ने वाला, e.g. पढ़ने वाला padhne vaalaa, 'the one who reads/studies') to describe someone characterized by an action, often habitually.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This pattern turns any verb into a describing word for 'the type of person who does that' -- गाने वाला (gaane vaalaa, 'singer, one who sings'), बोलने वाला (bolne vaalaa, 'the one who's talking'). It's a go-to way to avoid needing a separate noun for every profession or habitual role.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this with the 'about to' sense (below) -- पढ़ने वाला लड़का (padhne vaalaa ladkaa, 'the boy who studies/reads') versus वह पढ़ने वाला है (vah padhne vaalaa hai, 'he is about to study'); context and sentence position disambiguate them. 2) The infinitive must stay in its ने (ne) form; do not use the bare verb stem alone.

Sense 3 : about to (imminent future)
adjective Frame: [subject] + [verb stem]ने वाला/वाली/वाले + है/था etc. -- मैं जाने वाला हूँ (main jaane vaalaa hoon, 'I am about to go'). Expresses an action on the verge of happening, agreeing with the subject.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the natural Hindi way to say 'just about to' -- ट्रेन छूटने वाली है (tren chhootne vaalii hai, 'the train is about to leave') -- and you'll hear it constantly at railway stations and in everyday urgency. It builds directly on the agentive sense above (an action so imminent it's as if the subject has 'become the one who does it').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners drop the auxiliary है (hai)/था (thaa) that this construction requires -- वाला (vaalaa) alone doesn't complete the sentence. 2) Tense is carried entirely by the auxiliary (है/था/होगा), not by वाला (vaalaa) itself, so बारिश होने वाली थी (baarish hone vaalii thii, 'it was about to rain') shifts smoothly to the past this way.
Sample Sentences:
ट्रेन छूटने वाली है, जल्दी करो। The train is about to leave, hurry up.
(tren chhootne vaalii hai, jaldii karo।)
