न कि (n ki)

conjunction A correlative connective meaning 'and not' or 'rather than' -- used to sharply exclude an alternative after stating what is actually the case.
Sense 1 : and not / rather than (correction or contrast)
conjunction Follows the element being affirmed and precedes the rejected alternative: 'X, न कि Y' = 'X, not Y.' It sharpens a contrast already set up earlier in the sentence, rather than introducing a new clause on its own.
Synonyms: बल्कि (balki), न (n, shorter variant in some frames)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot in careful, explanatory speech -- a teacher correcting a misconception might say something like 'यह सही जवाब है, न कि वह' (yah sahii javaab hai, n ki vah, 'this is the right answer, not that one'). It has a slightly formal, deliberate flavor, common in writing, speeches, and classroom correction, more than in casual chat between friends.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often place न कि (n ki) at the start of the sentence, but it almost always comes after the correct/affirmed item, introducing the rejected one second. 2) Confusing न कि (n ki) with बल्कि (balki): बल्कि (balki) usually adds or intensifies ('in fact, even'), while न कि (n ki) specifically excludes an alternative ('and not this other thing'). 3) Learners sometimes insert है (hai) or other verbs between the two contrasted elements, but न कि (n ki) typically joins two noun phrases or parallel elements directly without a verb in between.
Sample Sentences:
यह सही जवाब है, न कि वह। This is the right answer, and not that one.
(yah sahii javaab hai, n ki vah।)
