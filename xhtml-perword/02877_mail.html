मैल (mail)

noun Mainly a concrete noun for physical grime or dirt (especially the kind that rubs off skin or collects in clothes/water), with a common figurative extension to inner impurity like malice or ill feeling.
Sense 1 : physical dirt/grime
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गंदगी (gandgii), मलिनता (malintaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the satisfying scrub in an Indian bathroom -- rubbing your arm with a loofah or soap and seeing the मैल (mail) roll off in little grey strands; that visual is exactly what a Hindi speaker pictures with this word. It is also the word used for the dark ring of grime that collects on a shirt collar or the inside of a used pot.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गंदगी (gandgii) for all kinds of dirt, but मैल (mail) is specifically the grime that builds up on skin, clothes, or utensils and rubs/washes off -- not general mess or trash on the ground. 2) मैल (mail) is grammatically masculine despite ending in ऐ, which can trip up learners expecting feminine agreement.

Sense 2 : figurative impurity of mind/heart, malice
noun, masculine Common in the phrase दिल का मैल (dil kaa mail, 'the grime of the heart' -- i.e., lingering resentment or ill will) and in expressions about मैल निकालना/धोना (mail nikaalnaa/mail dhonaa, to purge one's ill feeling).
Synonyms: खोट (khot), कपट (kapat, more literary/formal), द्वेष (dvesh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi treats grudges and bad feeling as a kind of residue that clings to the heart, the same way grime clings to skin -- so making peace with someone is often described as washing away this मैल (mail), echoing the very physical, everyday image behind the word's literal sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only works with heart/mind words like दिल (dil) or मन (man) attached -- मैल (mail) alone in a sentence about a person's character can sound incomplete or ambiguous to a listener expecting the literal-dirt sense.
