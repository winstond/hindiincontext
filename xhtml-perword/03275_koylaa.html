कोयला (koylaa)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'coal' or 'charcoal' -- the black fuel used in stoves, tandoors, and industry -- with a common figurative sense describing something or someone turned pitch-black or morally 'blackened'.
Sense 1 : coal, charcoal (the fuel)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अंगार (angaar, embers/glowing coal, not identical), कार्बन (kaarban, technical/chemical term)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR you will still hear कोयले की अंगीठी (koyle kii angiithii, 'coal brazier') mentioned by older folks who remember winters before gas heaters became common, and roadside vendors roasting corn or peanuts over कोयला (koylaa) is a familiar sight even today. It is also the word behind India's coal-mining belt (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh), so you will meet it often in news about energy and mining.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse कोयला (koylaa) with अंगारा (angaaraa) -- कोयला is the raw fuel (coal/charcoal), while अंगारा refers specifically to a live, glowing ember. 2) In cooking contexts (tandoor, barbecue), कोयला (koylaa) is the standard word for the charcoal used, not 'चारकोल' which is rarely heard in everyday speech despite being borrowed in writing.

Sense 2 : something blackened or soot-covered (figurative)
noun, masculine Used almost always in the fixed comparison कोयले जैसा/सा (koyle jaisaa, 'black as coal') or in the idiom मुँह कोयला होना (munh koylaa honaa, 'to be disgraced/have one's face blackened').
Synonyms: काला (kaalaa, as an adjective substitute, not a true synonym)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The idiom मुँह काला करना/होना (munh kaalaa karnaa, 'to blacken the face') is far more common than the कोयला version, but you will still hear कोयला होना (koylaa honaa) used the same way in scoldings, like a parent saying 'तुमने मेरा मुँह कोयला कर दिया' ('you have blackened my face/disgraced me'). It taps into a wider Hindi idiom family where the face (मुँह) is the seat of honor and reputation.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुँह कोयला होना (munh koylaa honaa) does not literally mean a dirty face -- it is an idiom for public disgrace or shame, similar to 'to blacken one's name' in English, and learners sometimes take it too literally.
