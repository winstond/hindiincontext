सोना (sonaa)

verb/noun Two unrelated senses live in this one spelling: the verb 'to sleep' and the noun 'gold' -- context always makes clear which one is meant.
Sense 1 : to sleep, to go to bed
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: निद्रा लेना (nidraa lenaa, literary), आराम करना (aaraam karnaa, rest, partial overlap)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Just like नींद आना (niind aanaa), Hindi treats sleep partly as something that happens to you, but सोना (sonaa) itself is a normal, fully agentive verb -- you can command someone with सो जाओ (so jaao, 'go to sleep!'), the classic line a parent uses on a child at bedtime.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for नींद आना (niind aanaa, 'to feel sleepy/to fall asleep') when they mean the ongoing act of sleeping -- सोना (sonaa) is the deliberate act of going to sleep or being asleep, while नींद आना (niind aanaa) describes sleepiness arriving on its own. 2) सो जाना (so jaanaa) (with जाना (jaanaa) added) is extremely common in speech to mean 'fall asleep, go off to sleep' -- plain सोना (sonaa) alone can sound slightly flatter or more general (e.g. habitual sleeping).

मैं रोज़ रात को ग्यारह बजे सोता हूँ। I sleep at eleven o'clock every night.
(main roz raat ko gyaarah baje sotaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : gold
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्वर्ण (svarn, literary/formal), कंचन (kanchan, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: सोना (sonaa) is everywhere in Indian life, not just jewelry shops -- gold is bought for weddings, festivals like धनतेरस (dhanteras) and दिवाली (divaalii), and treated as a form of savings. The common saying सोने पे सुहागा (sone pe suhaagaa, 'gilding on gold,' i.e. the icing on the cake) shows how the word carries a sense of something already good becoming even better.
Sample Sentences:
दिवाली पर लोग सोना खरीदना शुभ मानते हैं। People consider it auspicious to buy gold on Diwali.
(divaalii par log sonaa khariidnaa shubh maante hain।)
