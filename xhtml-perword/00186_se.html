से (se)

postposition A core postposition with a wide spread of meanings all built around the idea of a source or point of reference: 'from' (place/time/source), 'with/by means of' (instrument), 'than' (comparison), and 'with' (manner/accompaniment/emotion).
Sense 1 : from (place, time, or source)
postposition Marks the point of origin: दिल्ली से (dillii se, 'from Delhi'), सुबह से (subah se, 'since morning').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Note the neat overlap with English 'from' for both place and time, which makes this sense the easiest of से (se)'s jobs for a learner to grasp first.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use से (se) for 'since [a point in time]' but then wrongly keep using perfective tense throughout, when Hindi actually needs the continuous/habitual structure to show an ongoing state ('मैं सुबह से यहाँ हूँ' (main subah se yahaan hoon, 'I have been here since morning'), not a simple past sentence. 2) Confusing से (se) 'from' with का (kaa) when expressing origin of a noun phrase, e.g. 'दिल्ली से आदमी' vs 'दिल्ली का आदमी' -- both are correct but mean subtly different things ('a man from Delhi, just arrived' vs 'a man belonging to/native of Delhi').
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : by means of, with (instrument)
postposition Marks the tool or means: चाकू से काटना (chaakoo se kaatnaa, 'to cut with a knife'), बस से जाना (bas se jaanaa, 'to go by bus').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you use dozens of times a day without noticing -- हाथ से खाना (haath se khaanaa, 'to eat with your hand') is a perfect everyday example of Hindi treating body parts as 'instruments' too.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for के द्वारा (ke dvaaraa) for 'by means of' out of formal-writing habit, when spoken Hindi almost always just uses से (se) for instruments and modes of transport.

Sense 3 : than (comparison)
postposition Attaches to the standard of comparison: वह मुझसे बड़ा है (vah mujhse badaa hai, 'he is bigger than me').
Synonyms: की तुलना में (kii tulnaa men, more formal/explicit)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This comparative use of से (se) is baked into daily speech -- 'ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा' (zyaadaa se zyaadaa, 'at most/maximum') and 'कम से कम' (kam se kam, 'at least') are two extremely common fixed phrases built on this very sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to build comparisons the English way with a separate word for 'than', forgetting से (se) itself carries that meaning -- there is no extra word needed. 2) मुझसे (mujhse) is the contracted oblique form of मैं (main) + से (se); learners sometimes mistakenly write मैं से (main se) as two separate words.
Sample Sentences:

(yah kitaab us kitaab se achchhii hai।)

Sense 4 : with (manner, emotion, or accompanying state)
postposition Marks the manner or emotional state accompanying an action: प्यार से बोलना (pyaar se bolnaa, 'to speak with love/affectionately'), डर से काँपना (dar se kaampnaa, 'to tremble with/from fear').
Synonyms: के साथ (ke saath, used more for accompaniment by people; से (se) covers manner/emotion)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense quietly does double duty for both 'manner' and 'cause' -- ध्यान से सुनो (dhyaan se suno, 'listen carefully/with attention') and गुस्से से चिल्लाया (gusse se chillaayaa, 'shouted out of/with anger') both use the same से (se), which is a very Hindi way of treating an emotion as the 'means' behind an action.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this से (se) with के साथ (ke saath); से (se) is used for the manner or the cause of an emotional/physical state, while के साथ (ke saath) is for accompanying another person or thing -- 'दोस्त के साथ जाना' (dost ke saath jaanaa, 'to go with a friend') versus 'खुशी से रोना' (khushii se ronaa, 'to cry with/from happiness').
