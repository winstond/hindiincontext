क़िस्म (qism)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'type' or 'kind'; also extends into an idiom about someone's personality or nature.
Sense 1 : type, kind, sort
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रकार (prakaar), तरह (tarah), किस्म (kism, variant spelling without nuqta)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Walk into any Delhi market and you'll hear this word constantly: a fruit seller might ask कौन सी क़िस्म चाहिए (kaun sii qism chaahie, 'which kind do you want')? when you're picking mangoes, since there are dozens of named varieties. It's a wonderfully practical, market-floor word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for तरह (tarah) in every context, but क़िस्म (qism) is the natural choice when talking about varieties or categories of a product, like fruit, cloth, or tea, while तरह (tarah) leans more toward manner or way of doing something. 2) The nuqta under क़ is often dropped in casual writing and typing (किस्म), which is completely normal and not a mistake, just a variant spelling.

Sense 2 : a person's nature or type (of character)
noun, feminine Commonly used in the fixed phrase इस क़िस्म का (is qism kaa, 'this sort of') or उस क़िस्म का (us qism kaa) to describe a type of person or behavior, often with a mildly judgmental or dismissive tone.
Synonyms: स्वभाव (svabhaav, nature/temperament, not interchangeable in all contexts), टाइप (taaip, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear elders size someone up with a phrase like यह किस क़िस्म का लड़का है (yah kis qism kaa ladkaa hai, 'what sort of boy is this'), a classic bit of Delhi aunty/uncle skepticism about a new person in the family's orbit.
Common Mistakes: 1) When used about people, क़िस्म (qism) often carries a faint edge of criticism or distancing, as in वह अजीब क़िस्म का आदमी है (vah ajiib qism kaa aadmii hai, 'he's a strange sort of man'); learners sometimes use it in neutral contexts where a native speaker would just say तरह का (tarah kaa) instead.
