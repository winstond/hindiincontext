सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to be audible / to be heard' -- it frames hearing as something that happens to the perceiver rather than something they actively do, paired with दिखाई देना (dikhaaii denaa, 'to be visible') as the auditory counterpart.
Sense 1 : to be audible, to be heard (involuntary perception)
verb, intransitive Used with a dative (को-marked) experiencer: X को Y सुनाई देता है (X ko Y sunaaii detaa hai, 'X can hear Y'), on the same pattern as दिखाई देना (dikhaaii denaa) and पसंद आना (pasand aanaa). The subject of agreement is the thing heard (Y), not the experiencer.
Synonyms: सुनना (sunnaa, not a true synonym -- सुनना is the active 'to listen/hear', while सुनाई देना is involuntary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This dative-subject construction reflects how Hindi treats sensory perception as something that arrives at you rather than something you do -- much like नींद आना (niind aanaa, 'sleep comes') or समझ में आना (samajh men aanaa, 'to come to be understood'). It is extremely common in everyday speech for describing faint or distant sounds: 'ठीक से सुनाई नहीं देता' (thiik se sunaaii nahiin detaa, 'it's not audible properly') is a classic complaint on a bad phone call.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use यह/यह व्यक्ति (yah) as a normal subject with ने (ne), forgetting the experiencer takes को (ko) instead, since सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa) is intransitive and never takes ने. 2) Confusing सुनाई देना (sunaaii denaa) with सुनना (sunnaa): 'मुझे आवाज़ सुनाई दी' (mujhe aavaaz sunaaii dii, 'I heard/could hear the sound') describes perception happening to you, whereas 'मैंने आवाज़ सुनी' (mainne aavaaz sunii, 'I heard the sound') presents you as the deliberate listener. 3) The verb agrees with the sound/thing heard, not with the person -- so 'मुझे बातें सुनाई दीं' (mujhe baaten sunaaii diin) takes feminine plural agreement with बातें, not with मुझे.

(mujhe tumhaarii aavaaz sunaaii de rahii hai।)

Sense 2 : to have the ability to hear (of a person's hearing, as a general capacity)
verb, intransitive Same को-marked frame as the main sense, but used generically/habitually to describe someone's hearing capacity rather than a single instance of perception: उसे कम सुनाई देता है (use kam sunaaii detaa hai, 'he can't hear well / his hearing is poor').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly about grandparents -- 'दादी को अब कम सुनाई देता है' (daadii ko ab kam sunaaii detaa hai, 'Grandma can't hear well now') is a gentle, everyday way to note hearing decline without the more clinical-sounding बहरापन (bahraapan, 'deafness').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बहरा होना (bahraa honaa, 'to be deaf') when a softer, more common way to describe hearing loss is simply कम सुनाई देना (kam sunaaii denaa, 'to hear little'), which is how this is usually phrased in daily conversation, including to doctors or about elderly relatives.
