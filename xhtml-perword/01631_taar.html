तार (taar)

noun Covers a physical wire/string/thread, and by extension an electric telegram sent along wires; also lives in a couple of common idioms about staying connected or getting into sync with someone.
Sense 1 : wire, string, thread (thin strand of metal or material)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: डोरी (dorii, thread/cord, narrower), सिम (sim, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the तार (taar) you see strung between electric poles, holding up a clothesline in the balcony, or forming the strings of a musical instrument like a sitar. It is a very concrete, everyday word -- a Delhi household uses it for anything from a phone charger's wire to the wire fencing on a rooftop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse तार (taar) with तारा (taaraa, 'star') because they look and sound similar -- keep the final vowel distinct.

Sense 2 : connection, rapport (figurative link between people)
noun, masculine This figurative sense lives mainly inside the fixed expression तार जुड़ना/मिलना (taar judnaa/taar milnaa, 'to click, to hit it off') and तार जोड़ना (taar jodnaa, 'to re-establish contact').
Synonyms: रिश्ता (rishtaa, broader, relationship), तालमेल (taalmel, sync/coordination)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: When two people just 'get' each other -- old friends picking up right where they left off, or two strangers who unexpectedly hit it off at a wedding -- Hindi speakers say उनका तार मिल गया (unkaa taar mil gayaa, 'their wires connected'), treating rapport as a literal electrical connection clicking into place.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense almost never appears alone -- it needs the verb जुड़ना (judnaa) or मिलना (milnaa) attached to make sense as 'clicking' with someone; बare तार (taar) won't carry this meaning by itself.

पहली ही मुलाकात में हमारा तार मिल गया। We hit it off right from our first meeting.
(pahlii hii mulaakaat men hamaaraa taar mil gayaa।)

Sense 3 : telegram (message sent by telegraph)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तार-संदेश (taar-sandesh, formal/rare)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Before phones were common, sending a तार (taar) was how families shared urgent news -- a birth, a death, an exam result -- and the phrase तार भेजना (taar bhejnaa, 'to send a telegram') still shows up in older films and novels even though telegraph offices have mostly disappeared from daily life now.
Sample Sentences:
