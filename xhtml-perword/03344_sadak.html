सड़क (sadak)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'road' or 'street' -- the physical thoroughfare, with one extended sense referring to the street as a social/political space.
Sense 1 : road, street (physical thoroughfare)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रास्ता (raastaa, also means 'way/path' more broadly), मार्ग (maarg, formal), गली (galii, narrower -- a lane, not a main road)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, सड़क (sadak) is the word you hear constantly in traffic contexts -- सड़क जाम है (sadak jaam hai, 'the road is jammed') is practically a daily complaint. Street signs and government schemes (like सड़क सुरक्षा, sadak surakshaa, 'road safety') use this word rather than रास्ता (raastaa), which feels more like 'the way to get somewhere' than the physical road itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सड़क (sadak) and गली (galii) interchangeably, but गली (galii) is a narrow lane/alley inside a neighborhood while सड़क (sadak) is a proper road that vehicles use. 2) The postposition पर (par) is used for location 'on the road' -- सड़क पर (sadak par, 'on the road') -- not में (men), which would sound odd here.

Sense 2 : the street as public/political space (protest, agitation)
noun, feminine Used mainly in fixed phrases like सड़क पर उतरना (sadak par utarnaa, 'to take to the streets') describing public protest.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear सड़क पर उतरना (sadak par utarnaa, 'to come down onto the street') in news reports whenever citizens protest -- लोग सड़क पर उतर आए (log sadak par utar aae, 'people took to the streets'). It's the standard journalistic way to talk about public agitation in Hindi media.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense only appears in set expressions, not as a free-standing noun -- a learner should not say things like 'the street decided' using सड़क (sadak) alone without a verb like उतरना (utarnaa) or a similar frame.
