बस (bas)

noun/particle/interjection/verb Covers two unrelated words that happen to share a spelling: बस (bas) the loanword for 'bus,' and बस (bas) the native particle/interjection meaning 'enough' or 'just, only,' plus its closely linked verb sense meaning 'to be within one's control.'
Sense 1 : bus (vehicle)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a straightforward English loanword, borrowed along with the vehicle itself; it behaves like any ordinary Hindi feminine noun. You will hear it everywhere: बस स्टॉप (bas stop, 'bus stop'), बस अड्डा (bas addaa, 'bus station').

Sense 2 : just, only, merely
particle Placed directly before the word or phrase it limits: बस इतना ही (bas itnaa hii, 'just this much').
Synonyms: सिर्फ़ (sirf), केवल (keval, slightly more formal), मात्र (maatr, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This बस (bas) is unrelated to the 'bus' word above -- pure coincidence of sound. It is one of the most versatile small words in spoken Hindi, doing double duty as 'just/only' and, as its own interjection, as 'enough, stop.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सिर्फ़ (sirf) in every case, but बस (bas) has a softer, more conversational feel and is extremely common in casual speech where सिर्फ़ (sirf) would sound a touch more deliberate. 2) बस (bas) can open a sentence on its own as a dismissive 'that's it' -- बस, हो गया (bas, ho gayaa, 'that's it, it's done') -- a standalone use that सिर्फ़ (sirf) cannot replicate.
Sample Sentences:

(bas thodaa saa doodh bachaa hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : enough! stop! that's it!
interjection
Synonyms: बहुत हुआ (bahut huaa, phrase variant), काफ़ी है (kaafii hai, phrase variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Say बस! (bas!) with a raised hand at a wedding buffet and everyone instantly understands 'no more, I'm full' -- it is the go-to word for politely (or firmly) shutting something down, whether it's food, a story, or a scolding.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used alone as a full utterance to cut off a conversation, an offer of more food, or an argument -- बस बस, मत बोलो (bas bas, mat bolo, 'enough, enough, stop talking') -- learners sometimes forget it can stand completely by itself with no verb attached.
Sample Sentences:
बस! अब और नहीं। Enough! No more now.
(bas! ab aur nahiin।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active
बस करो, बहुत हो गया! Stop it, that's more than enough!
(bas karo, bahut ho gayaa!)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active

Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Aspect: Continuous -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active

Sense 4 : to be within one's control, to be manageable (usually negated)
verb, intransitive Almost always appears with a दिल पर, हाथ में (dil par, haath men) type phrase and a negation: मेरा दिल नहीं बसता (meraa dil nahiin bastaa) is not idiomatic -- the real frame is मेरे बस में नहीं है (mere bas men nahiin hai, 'it's not in my control'), where बस (bas) functions almost like a noun inside the fixed postpositional phrase के बस में (ke bas men).
Synonyms: क़ाबू में होना (qaaboo men honaa, phrase variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Related to बसना (basnaa, 'to settle, reside') and its causative बसाना (basaanaa) -- the idea underneath है 'in whose territory does this matter settle/rest,' which is a nice bridge to the vehicle-and-settling image behind the whole बस-family of words.
Common Mistakes: 1) This बस (bas) shows up almost exclusively inside the fixed phrase के बस में (होना/न होना) (ke bas men, 'to be (not) in someone's control') -- learners rarely need to conjugate it as a free-standing verb, so encountering it outside that frame can be confusing.
Sample Sentences:

(yah mere bas kii baat nahiin hai।)
