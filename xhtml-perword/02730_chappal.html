चप्पल (chappal)

noun A single, simple sense: the everyday flip-flop or slipper, one of the first household-object nouns learners pick up.
Sense 1 : slipper, flip-flop, sandal
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सैंडल (saindal, often specifically a strapped sandal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Chappals are the default footwear at home in most North Indian households -- you kick off your जूते (joote, shoes) at the door but often shuffle around the house in चप्पल (chappal). There's also a well-known mother's gesture: threatening naughty kids with चप्पल मारूँगी (chappal maaroongii, 'I'll hit you with my slipper') -- a stock, half-joking line every Delhi kid has heard at least once.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume चप्पल (chappal) is singular-only in everyday speech, but Hindi speakers almost always talk about the pair -- check the morphology table for how the plural is used when referring to 'a pair of slippers.' 2) Don't confuse चप्पल (chappal) with जूता (jootaa), which means a proper shoe (with a back strap or laces); the two are not interchangeable even though both go on your feet.
