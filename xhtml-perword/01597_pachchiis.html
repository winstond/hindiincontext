पच्चीस (pachchiis)

numeral/noun The number twenty-five (pachchiis); used as a numeral adjective and, in a couple of common idioms, to mean 'a lot of small problems' or 'countless.'
Sense 1 : the number 25
numeral
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear पच्चीस (pachchiis) constantly in markets for prices -- पच्चीस रुपये (pachchiis rupye, 'twenty-five rupees') is a classic haggling number for small items like a cup of chai or a bag of chips.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect पच्चीस (pachchiis) like an adjective for gender or number -- it doesn't change at all, whether counting मिनट (minat, minutes) or रुपये (rupye, rupees).

Sense 2 : a great number of (small troubles, questions, complaints)
noun, masculine Common in the idiom पच्चीस झमेले (pachchiis jhamele, 'twenty-five hassles') or पच्चीस सवाल (pachchiis savaal, 'twenty-five questions'), where पच्चीस functions loosely as 'so many/countless' rather than the literal number.
Synonyms: बीस (biis, used similarly in some idioms), अनगिनत (anginat, countless)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a lovely bit of Hindi color: just as English exaggerates with 'a million things to do,' Hindi often reaches for पच्चीस (pachchiis) to mean 'way too many small annoying things,' as in उसके पच्चीस नखरे हैं (uske pachchiis nakhre hain, 'he/she has a million fussy demands').
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't take पच्चीस सवाल (pachchiis savaal) literally as exactly 25 questions -- native speakers use it to mean 'a whole bunch of annoying questions,' much like English 'a hundred questions.'
