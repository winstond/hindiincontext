स्वागत करना (svaagat karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to welcome' -- स्वागत (svaagat) is the noun 'welcome' and करना (karnaa) 'to do' combines with it in the standard noun+करना (karnaa) verb-phrase pattern.
Sense 1 : to welcome, to greet (someone arriving)
verb, transitive The person welcomed is marked with का (kaa) agreeing with स्वागत (svaagat), not with को (ko): उसका स्वागत किया (uskaa svaagat kiyaa, 'welcomed him/her'), not उसे स्वागत किया (use svaagat kiyaa).
Synonyms: अभिनंदन करना (abhinandan karnaa, formal), आदर सत्कार करना (aadar satkaar karnaa, formal, elaborate hospitality)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this phrase constantly at Indian weddings, offices, and even at the start of TV shows and speeches -- आपका स्वागत है (aapkaa svaagat hai, 'you are welcome, welcome to you') is the standard formula a host or anchor uses to open an event, functionally like English 'welcome, everyone.' Garlanding guests with flowers (माला (maalaa)) or applying tilak (tilak) is a common physical gesture that accompanies this verb in formal contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the welcomed person with को (ko) by analogy with other verbs, but the correct pattern uses का/की/के (kaa/kii/ke) before स्वागत (svaagat), e.g. मेहमानों का स्वागत (mehmaanon kaa svaagat, 'welcome of the guests'). 2) As a ने (ne)-taking transitive compound, the perfective agrees with स्वागत (svaagat) itself (masculine), not with the subject or the welcomed person: उन्होंने स्वागत किया (unhonne svaagat kiyaa), उन्होंने स्वागत की नहीं (unhonne svaagat kii nahiin) is wrong.
