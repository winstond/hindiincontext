रोक (rok)

noun The verbal noun / stem-noun of रोकना (roknaa, 'to stop'), used mainly to mean a stoppage, restriction, or ban; it also appears in the fixed pair रोक-टोक (rok-tok) meaning interference or restrictions in general.
Sense 1 : ban, restriction, prohibition
noun, feminine Commonly used with लगाना (lagaanaa) as रोक लगाना (rok lagaanaa, 'to impose a restriction') and with हटाना (hataanaa) or उठाना (uthaanaa) meaning to lift a restriction.
Synonyms: प्रतिबंध (pratibandh, formal), पाबंदी (paabandii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this word constantly in Indian news tickers and government notices -- सरकार ने शराब की बिक्री पर रोक लगा दी (sarkaar ne sharaab kii bikrii par rok lagaa dii, 'the government banned liquor sales') is a very typical headline construction. It pairs naturally with टोक (tok, 'interruption/objection') in the common expression रोक-टोक (rok-tok), used for the general fuss of restrictions and interference, as in बच्चों पर बहुत रोक-टोक है (bachchon par bahut rok-tok hai, 'there are a lot of restrictions on the kids').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for प्रतिबंध (pratibandh) by default from textbooks, but रोक (rok) is the word actually used in everyday speech and news headlines for bans, e.g. plastic bags or traffic. 2) रोक (rok) needs लगाना (lagaanaa) as its verb partner (रोक लगाना, rok lagaanaa) -- learners sometimes try to use रोकना (roknaa) directly where the noun+verb combination is expected instead.
Sample Sentences:
सरकार ने शराब की बिक्री पर रोक लगा दी। The government banned the sale of liquor.
(sarkaar ne sharaab kii bikrii par rok lagaa dii।)

Sense 2 : act of stopping, halting
noun, feminine Used for a physical or procedural stopping/blocking, distinct from an official ban -- e.g. traffic being halted, a payment being stopped.
Synonyms: रुकावट (rukaavat)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Bank forms and cheques use this sense directly: भुगतान पर रोक (bhugtaan par rok, 'stop on payment') is standard banking language, so this word shows up often in formal and financial contexts even though it's a very plain, everyday noun.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes conflate this with रुकावट (rukaavat); रोक (rok) leans toward an active act of stopping something (often by an agent), while रुकावट (rukaavat) leans toward an obstacle or hindrance that exists.
