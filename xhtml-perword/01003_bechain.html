बेचैन (bechain)

adjective A single-sense adjective describing restlessness or unease, in the body or the mind. Formed from the Persian prefix बे- (be, 'without') plus चैन (chain, 'peace/rest') -- literally 'without peace.'
Sense 1 : restless, uneasy, agitated
adjective Commonly paired with होना (honaa) to describe a state ('to be restless') or with करना (karnaa) in the causative-feeling sense 'X makes me restless,' e.g. with को (ko)-marked experiencer: दिल बेचैन होना (dil bechain honaa, 'the heart to become restless').
Synonyms: अशांत (ashaant, literary), व्याकुल (vyaakul, literary), परेशान (pareshaan, overlaps but leans more toward 'worried/troubled'), अधीर (adhiir, leans toward 'impatient')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बेचैन (bechain) captures that specific fidgety, can't-sit-still feeling -- waiting for exam results, or a mother waiting for her child to come home late at night. It's a favorite word in Bollywood lyrics and shayari for describing lovesick agitation, e.g. दिल बेचैन है (dil bechain hai, 'the heart is restless'). Related noun is बेचैनी (bechainii, 'restlessness'), which you'll hear just as often as the adjective itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for परेशान (pareshaan) when they mean बेचैन (bechain) -- परेशान (pareshaan) suggests being troubled or hassled by a problem, while बेचैन (bechain) is more physical/visceral, a squirming inner restlessness even without a clear cause. 2) The experiencer typically takes को (ko), not ने (ne) -- मुझे बेचैनी हो रही है (mujhe bechainii ho rahii hai, 'I'm feeling restless') rather than a subject-agent construction, since this is a dative-subject feeling verb pattern like लगना (lagnaa).
