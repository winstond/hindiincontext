बात बनना (baat bannaa)

verb A compound expression built on बात (baat, 'thing/matter/talk') plus बनना (bannaa, 'to become/form'); mainly used idiomatically to talk about a matter working out favorably, or, negatively, about something not working out or an excuse/matter going nowhere.
Sense 1 : for things to work out, for a matter to succeed
verb, intransitive Almost always used with negation (बात नहीं बनी baat nahiin banii) or with a conditional/hopeful frame (अगर बात बन गई तो agar baat ban gaii to); the subject is the abstract बात (baat), not a person, so the person involved is usually marked with की तरफ़ से (kii taraf se) or simply left out of the sentence.
Synonyms: काम बनना (kaam bannaa), बात बन जाना (baat ban jaanaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi deal-making and matchmaking language -- you'll hear it after a business negotiation, a job interview, or a rishta (rishtaa, 'marriage match') meeting: बात बन गई (baat ban gaii) means the deal or match clicked, बात नहीं बनी (baat nahiin banii) means it fell through. It pairs naturally with सकना (saknaa) and पड़ना (padnaa) constructions when hedging about outcomes.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make a person the grammatical subject (e.g. 'main ne baat banaayi' meaning 'I made it work'), but that sentence actually means 'I made up an excuse/story' -- a different sense (see the transitive sense below). To say things worked out for you, बात is the subject: मेरी बात बन गई (merii baat ban gaii) 'my thing worked out,' not 'main ne banaayi.' 2) It is almost always negative or hedged in real speech -- bare positive claims like 'baat ban gayi' happen, but learners overuse the positive form when native speakers more often report failure: बात नहीं बनी (baat nahiin banii).

Sense 2 : for a plan/excuse/story to be concocted or take shape (agentive, transitive use)
verb, intransitive This sense actually belongs to the transitive verb बात बनाना (baat banaanaa) -- included here because learners frequently confuse it with the intransitive सेंस above; बनना (bannaa) itself does not take an agentive subject making excuses, only बनाना (banaanaa) does.
Synonyms: बात बनाना (baat banaanaa, the transitive counterpart), कहानी बनाना (kahaanii banaanaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Listed here mainly as a warning sign-post: if you are tempted to translate 'he's making excuses' word for word, you want बात बनाना (baat banaanaa), the causative partner of this entry, not बात बनना (baat bannaa) itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not use बात बनना (baat bannaa) when you mean someone deliberately invented an excuse -- that requires the causative बनाना (banaanaa): उसने बात बनाई (usne baat banaaii, 'he made up a story'), never उसकी बात बनी (uskii baat banii) for this meaning (that would revert to sense 1, 'his thing worked out').
