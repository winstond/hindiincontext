कुछ (kuchh)

pronoun/adjective/adverb A very common indefinite word covering 'something', 'some (amount/quantity)', and 'a bit/somewhat' -- the exact English translation depends heavily on context.
Sense 1 : something (indefinite pronoun)
pronoun
Synonyms: कोई चीज़ (koii chiiz, something/some thing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: कुछ (kuchh) is one of the first 'filler' words learners lean on, much like English 'something' -- you will hear it constantly in hedging phrases like कुछ पता नहीं (kuchh pataa nahiin, 'no idea at all') or कुछ भी (kuchh bhii, 'anything at all'), which add emphasis rather than changing the core meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to add a plural marker or an article; कुछ (kuchh) as 'something' is invariant and needs neither. 2) In questions like क्या कुछ हुआ? (kyaa kuchh huaa?, 'did something happen?'), कुछ (kuchh) pairs naturally with negation later (कुछ नहीं हुआ (kuchh nahiin huaa, 'nothing happened')) -- learners sometimes reach for कोई नहीं (koii nahiin) instead, which is for people, not things.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे कुछ कहना है। I have to say something.
(mujhe kuchh kahnaa hai।)

Sense 2 : some (quantity/amount, uncountable or plural)
adjective Placed before a noun to mean 'some amount of' (कुछ पैसे (kuchh paise, 'some money')) or 'some (unspecified) people/things' with plural nouns.
Synonyms: थोड़ा (thodaa, a little), कई (kaii, several, for countables)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Because कुछ (kuchh) never changes form, it is often the safer choice for beginners over थोड़ा (thodaa) or कई (kaii), which require matching gender and number -- you will hear shopkeepers and family members use it constantly when offering or asking for a bit of something, like कुछ चाय लो (kuchh chaay lo, 'have some tea').
Common Mistakes: 1) With countable plural nouns, कुछ (kuchh) means 'a few/some' (कुछ लोग (kuchh log, 'some people')), while with mass nouns it means 'a bit of' (कुछ पानी (kuchh paanii, 'some water')) -- learners sometimes confuse this with थोड़ा (thodaa), which leans more toward 'a small amount' and agrees in gender/number, unlike invariant कुछ (kuchh).
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : somewhat, a little, rather
adverb Modifies an adjective or verb to soften or partially qualify it, similar to 'a bit' or 'rather' in English: कुछ अजीब (kuchh ajiib, 'a bit strange').
Synonyms: थोड़ा (thodaa, a little), ज़रा (zaraa, a bit)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same word wearing a third hat -- once you know कुछ (kuchh) as 'something' and 'some', hearing it soften an adjective (कुछ थका हुआ हूँ (kuchh thakaa huaa hoon, 'I'm a bit tired')) feels like a natural extension rather than a new word to memorize.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this adverbial use, कुछ (kuchh) sits right before the adjective it softens, not the noun -- learners sometimes misplace it as if it were quantifying the noun instead, e.g. confusing कुछ अजीब बात (kuchh ajiib baat, 'a somewhat strange thing') with कुछ बात (kuchh baat, 'some matter').
Sample Sentences:
