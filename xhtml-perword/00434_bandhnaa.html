बँधना (bandhnaa)

verb The intransitive partner of बाँधना (baandhnaa, 'to tie'): बँधना (bandhnaa) means 'to get tied / to become bound', physically or figuratively (a relationship, a bond, a schedule).
Sense 1 : to be tied, to get tied up (physical)
verb, intransitive
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a goat or a cow being tethered outside a house in the evening -- बकरी बँध गई (bakrii bandh gaii, 'the goat got tied up') is exactly the kind of everyday rural/urban-fringe image Hindi speakers reach for with this verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बाँधना (baandhnaa) with a passive-like construction when they mean the rope/object itself got tied on its own or by unspecified agency -- बँधना (bandhnaa) is the natural intransitive choice here, not a passive of बाँधना (baandhnaa). 2) The subject of बँधना (bandhnaa) is the thing being tied (rope, animal, hair), not the person doing the tying -- से (se) marks the instrument or agent if mentioned, e.g. रस्सी से (rassii se, 'with a rope').
Sample Sentences:
शाम को बकरी खम्भे से बँध गई। In the evening, the goat got tied to the pole.
(shaam ko bakrii khambhe se bandh gaii।)

Sense 2 : to be bound, committed, or tied down (figurative: relationship, obligation, schedule)
verb, intransitive Often used with में (men) to mark what one is bound into, e.g. रिश्ते में बँधना (rishte men bandhnaa, 'to be bound in a relationship').
Synonyms: जुड़ना (judnaa, to be connected), बंधन में आना (bandhan men aanaa, to come into a bond)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word behind the common complaint समय का पाबंद हूँ, अब हर काम में बँध गया हूँ (ab har kaam men bandh gayaa hoon, 'now I'm tied down in every task') -- Hindi speakers use बँधना (bandhnaa) to talk about marriage, routines, and obligations as things that wrap around you, echoing the literal image of a rope.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a full passive of 'to bind someone,' but बँधना (bandhnaa) here describes a state or condition of being tied down -- emotionally, socially, or by routine -- rather than a direct passive action performed on the subject by an agent.
