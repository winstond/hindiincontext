झकझक (jhakjhak)

adjective/noun Two distinct senses: (1) an adjective meaning 'bright, shiny, spotlessly clean' -- often intensified as झकाझक (jhakaajhak); and (2) a noun meaning 'bickering, pointless arguing, hassle,' close in meaning to बकबक (bakbak).
Sense 1 : bright, shiny, spotlessly clean
adjective
Synonyms: चमकदार (chamakdaar), साफ़-सुथरा (saaf-suthraa), झकाझक (jhakaajhak, reduplicated-intensive variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word used for sunlight bouncing off a river or for a kitchen counter scrubbed till it gleams -- it evokes a visual dazzle, not just cleanliness in the abstract. Housewives and shopkeepers alike reach for झकाझक (jhakaajhak) as a proud boast: 'sab kuch jhakaajhak hai' ('everything is spotless') is a small everyday flex about a well-kept home or shop.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a plain color/shine word like चमकदार (chamakdaar), but झकझक (jhakjhak) carries a stronger sense of dazzling or spotless cleanliness -- it is often used for freshly washed clothes, polished floors, or sunlit water rather than just any shiny object. 2) The intensified form झकाझक (jhakaajhak) is what you will hear most often in casual speech for 'spotlessly clean' (e.g. a spotless kitchen); using plain झकझक (jhakjhak) is more common in descriptions of light or water glistening.

Sense 2 : bickering, pointless arguing or chatter, hassle
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बकबक (bakbak), किचकिच (kichkich), झिकझिक (jhikjhik, variant form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word for the low-grade, tiresome back-and-forth you want no part of -- a neighbor's daily complaints, a vendor haggling past the point of reason, or family members bickering over nothing. Saying इस झकझक में मत पड़ो (is jhakjhak men mat pado, 'don't get into this bickering') is a very ordinary piece of advice you would give a friend.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense has nothing to do with the 'shiny/clean' adjective meaning -- learners familiar with one sense sometimes assume a shared root meaning, but in everyday use these are heard as two unrelated words. 2) It is used dismissively, often with फ़ालतू (faaltoo, 'useless') or बेकार (bekaar, 'pointless') attached, to complain about being dragged into unnecessary arguments or nagging, not to describe a serious fight.
