औरत (aurat)

noun A common everyday word for 'woman'; it has one core sense but carries register and tone nuances the learner should pick up on.
Sense 1 : woman
noun, feminine
Synonyms: महिला (mahilaa, formal), स्त्री (strii, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear constantly in daily life -- in markets, on the street, in family conversation -- while महिला (mahilaa) shows up more in news reports, government schemes, and polite formal address (like 'महिला सुरक्षा' (mahilaa surakshaa, 'women's safety') in official contexts). Getting a feel for when to reach for औरत (aurat) versus महिला (mahilaa) is a small but real marker of register awareness in Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) औरत (aurat) is the plain, everyday word for 'woman' -- it is not rude or slang, but in polite or formal contexts (announcements, official forms, addressing someone respectfully) महिला (mahilaa) is preferred. Using औरत (aurat) about a specific woman in front of her can sound blunt depending on tone, so many speakers default to महिला (mahilaa) in mixed or formal company. 2) Learners sometimes assume स्त्री (strii) and औरत (aurat) are freely interchangeable in speech; स्त्री (strii) leans literary/formal and is rarer in casual conversation.

Sense 2 : wife (colloquial)
noun, feminine Used possessively (e.g. 'मेरी औरत' (merii aurat, 'my wife')) in informal, often rural or working-class speech; not the neutral default term for 'wife' in polite usage.
Synonyms: पत्नी (patnii, formal), बीवी (biivii, colloquial), घरवाली (gharvaalii, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll mostly hear this usage in villages, older generations, or in Bollywood dialogue meant to sound rustic or blunt -- a city-dwelling professional would rarely call his wife 'मेरी औरत' (merii aurat) in polite company.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense can sound crude or old-fashioned in urban middle-class speech -- पत्नी (patnii) or बीवी (biivii) are the safer everyday choices when referring to someone's wife respectfully.
