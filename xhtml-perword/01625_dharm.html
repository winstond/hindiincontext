धर्म (dharm)

noun A rich word covering religion/faith, but also duty, righteousness, and the moral/cosmic order underlying Hindu and broader Indian thought -- the senses below map its everyday-religious use versus its deeper duty/ethics use.
Sense 1 : religion, faith
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मज़हब (mazahab, often used in Muslim contexts), पंथ (panth, sect/faith path)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In daily conversation, धर्म (dharm) is the word people reach for when asking about someone's faith -- आपका धर्म क्या है (aapkaa dharm kyaa hai, 'what is your religion') is a genuinely common form question on official documents. It carries more weight than the English 'religion' often does, since it's tied to identity, family tradition, and social expectation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat धर्म (dharm) as a perfect one-to-one match for 'religion,' but it also covers duty and moral order (see the other sense) -- context decides which shade is meant. 2) मज़हब (mazahab) is common in everyday speech too, especially when discussing communal identity (हिंदू (hindoo) vs मुस्लिम (muslim)), so don't assume धर्म (dharm) is the only word you'll hear.
Sample Sentences:

(aapkaa dharm kyaa hai?)

Sense 2 : duty, moral/righteous conduct
noun, masculine Frequently appears in fixed phrases like अपना धर्म निभाना (apnaa dharm nibhaanaa, 'to fulfill one's duty') and इंसानियत का धर्म (insaaniyat kaa dharm, 'the duty of humanity').
Synonyms: कर्तव्य (kartavy), फ़र्ज़ (farz)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the धर्म (dharm) of the Bhagavad Gita and everyday moral speech -- the idea that every person, role, and relationship (a mother, a soldier, a teacher) carries its own righteous duty, its own 'dharma.' You'll hear elders invoke it when telling someone to simply do the right thing: अपना धर्म निभाओ (apnaa dharm nibhaao, 'do your duty'), with no temple or scripture in sight.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is not about religion at all -- a sentence like यह मेरा धर्म है (yah meraa dharm hai, 'this is my duty') can be said by an atheist and makes complete sense. Learners coming from English sometimes force a religious reading where none is intended.
