हृदय (hriday)

noun A more formal/literary word for 'heart' (both the physical organ and the seat of emotion), sitting one register above everyday दिल (dil).
Sense 1 : heart (seat of emotion, feeling, or inner self)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दिल (dil), मन (man, mind/heart as seat of thought)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: हृदय (hriday) shows up in solemn or elevated contexts -- national anthems, speeches, greeting-card Hindi, or phrases like हृदय से धन्यवाद (hriday se dhanyvaad, 'thanks from the heart') -- where it lends a formal, heartfelt weight that casual दिल (dil) doesn't carry.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday romantic or emotional speech ('my heart says', 'you broke my heart'), native speakers reach for दिल (dil), not हृदय (hriday) -- using हृदय (hriday) here can sound stiff or like a speech/essay rather than natural conversation.

मैं आपका हृदय से धन्यवाद करता हूँ। I thank you from the heart.
(main aapkaa hriday se dhanyvaad kartaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : heart (physical organ)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दिल (dil)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see हृदय (hriday) on hospital signboards and in newspaper health columns -- हृदय रोग विशेषज्ञ (hriday rog visheshagy, 'cardiologist') is the standard formal title -- while friends and family almost always talk about दिल (dil) at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for this word in casual medical talk, but a doctor or a normal conversation about health will almost always say दिल (dil) instead -- हृदय (hriday) sounds bookish or formal in that context. 2) In compounds like हृदय रोग (hriday rog, 'heart disease') or हृदयाघात (hridyaaghaat, 'heart attack'), हृदय (hriday) is actually the standard, expected term -- so the register shifts depending on whether you're speaking casually or naming a medical condition.
