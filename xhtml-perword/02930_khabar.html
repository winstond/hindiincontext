ख़बर (khabar)

noun A common everyday feminine noun meaning 'news' or 'information'; it also anchors several idioms about being aware or unaware of something. Sometimes spelled without the nuqta as खबर (khabar).
Sense 1 : news, a report or piece of information
noun, feminine
Synonyms: समाचार (samaachaar, formal), सूचना (soochnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: ख़बर (khabar) is the word you'll hear anchoring TV news channels' taglines and headlines all over Delhi -- ताज़ा ख़बर (taazaa khabar, 'breaking/fresh news') flashes constantly on screens. It's also the word used casually among friends and family for any piece of information, not just formal news.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for समाचार (samaachaar) by default because it's the word taught for 'news broadcast,' but in everyday speech ख़बर (khabar) is far more natural, whether you mean a TV bulletin or just casual news about someone. 2) The verb pairing मिलना (milnaa) is common: ख़बर मिलना (khabar milnaa, 'to receive news') uses a को-marked recipient, e.g. मुझे ख़बर मिली (mujhe khabar milii, 'I got the news') -- learners sometimes wrongly try to make the recipient the subject with ने (ne).

Sense 2 : awareness, knowledge of a situation (esp. in idioms of being unaware)
noun, feminine Almost always used with होना (honaa) in constructions like किसी को ख़बर होना (kisii ko khabar honaa, 'for someone to be aware'), structurally parallel to पता होना (pataa honaa).
Synonyms: पता (pataa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense grows naturally out of the 'news' meaning: if news of something hasn't reached you, you have no ख़बर (khabar) of it. You'll often hear elders say ज़रा भी ख़बर नहीं (zaraa bhii khabar nahiin, 'not even the slightest clue') when scolding someone for being oblivious.
Common Mistakes: 1) The negative idiom ख़बर न होना (khabar n honaa, 'to have no clue') is very common in scolding or surprised tones -- e.g. तुम्हें कुछ ख़बर नहीं है (tumhen kuchh khabar nahiin hai, 'you have no idea/clue') -- learners often default to पता (pataa) here and don't realize ख़बर (khabar) works the same way and sounds just as natural, sometimes even more emphatic.

Sense 3 : a scolding, comeuppance (in the idiom 'ख़बर लेना')
noun, feminine Lives almost entirely in the fixed idiom ख़बर लेना (khabar lenaa, 'to take someone to task') -- the object of the scolding is marked with की (kii), as in उसकी ख़बर लेना (uskii khabar lenaa, 'to give him a piece of one's mind').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a favorite line in Hindi films and family arguments alike -- a parent or elder brother threatening ख़बर लूँगा (khabar loongaa, 'I'll sort him out') carries a half-joking, half-serious menace that's instantly recognizable to any Hindi speaker.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not confuse this ख़बर लेना (khabar lenaa) with 'taking news' -- it has nothing to do with information at all; it's a threat/warning idiom, often heard as अभी उसकी ख़बर लेता हूँ (abhii uskii khabar letaa hoon, 'I'll deal with him right now').
