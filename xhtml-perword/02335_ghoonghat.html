घूँघट (ghoonghat)

noun A veil or the act of veiling the face -- specifically the fold of a woman's saree or dupatta pulled forward over the face. One core sense, with a common extended use for the practice/custom of veiling itself.
Sense 1 : the veil/face-covering itself
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पर्दा (pardaa, broader term, also covers seclusion generally), नकाब (nakaab, associated with Muslim face-covering rather than saree veiling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: घूँघट (ghoonghat) is deeply tied to North Indian rural and traditional family life: a new bride (बहू, bahoo) is traditionally expected to keep her घूँघट (ghoonghat) down in front of elders or in-laws as a sign of modesty and respect, especially in villages of Haryana, Rajasthan, and western UP. In Delhi-NCR itself the practice is largely faded in daily life but remains a strong symbol in songs, soap operas, and conversations about tradition versus modernity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पर्दा (pardaa) as a direct substitute, but पर्दा (pardaa) is broader (curtain, seclusion, or veiling in general), while घूँघट (ghoonghat) specifically names the cloth drawn over the face, usually the end of a saree or dupatta. 2) The common verb pairing is घूँघट करना (ghoonghat karnaa) or घूँघट लेना (ghoonghat lenaa) 'to veil oneself' and घूँघट उठाना (ghoonghat uthaanaa) or घूँघट हटाना (ghoonghat hataanaa) 'to lift/remove the veil' -- learners sometimes try to pair it with the wrong verb.

Sense 2 : the custom/practice of veiling
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पर्दा प्रथा (pardaa prathaa, more formal, names the custom explicitly)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Used abstractly, as in घूँघट की प्रथा (ghoonghat kii prathaa, 'the custom of veiling'), this sense comes up whenever people discuss changing gender roles or rural versus urban attitudes -- a topic Delhi families with village roots often debate at weddings and family gatherings.
