रूखा (rookhaa)

adjective Primarily describes a physical texture that is dry or rough (skin, bread, hair), with a common extended sense describing a person's manner as dry, curt, or unfriendly.
Sense 1 : dry, rough (of texture)
adjective
Synonyms: सूखा (sookhaa), खुरदरा (khuradraa, rough texture)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot around winter in Delhi when everyone's skin gets रूखा (rookhaa) from the dry cold -- लोशन लगाओ, त्वचा रूखी हो गई है (loshan lagaao, tvachaa rookhii ho gaii hai, 'apply lotion, your skin has gotten dry') is a classic mother's line.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse रूखा (rookhaa) with सूखा (sookhaa, 'dried up/dried out') -- रूखा (rookhaa) specifically stresses a rough, harsh-to-touch quality (like chapped skin or stale bread), while सूखा (sookhaa) just means dry/not wet, without that harsh texture implication.

(sardiyon men merii tvachaa bahut rookhii ho jaatii hai।)

Sense 2 : curt, cold, unfriendly (of manner or speech)
adjective
Synonyms: कठोर (kathor, harsher, more about severity), रूखा-सूखा (rookhaa-sookhaa, emphatic doubled form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for that flat, unwelcoming tone -- जब कोई रूखे स्वर में बात करता है (jab koii rookhe svar men baat kartaa hai, 'when someone speaks in a cold/curt tone'), it signals emotional distance or annoyance, the opposite of the warmth Hindi speakers usually prize in conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense describes tone or behavior, not physical dryness -- उसने रूखा जवाब दिया (usne rookhaa javaab diyaa, 'he gave a curt/cold reply') has nothing to do with texture, so do not default to translating it as 'dry' in English, which sounds odd; 'curt' or 'cold' usually fits better.
Sample Sentences:
उसने बहुत रूखे स्वर में जवाब दिया। He replied in a very curt tone.
