नज़र लगना (nazar lagnaa)

verb A cultural-belief idiom meaning 'to be afflicted by the evil eye' -- literally 'for a gaze to attach/strike'. Built from नज़र (nazar, gaze/glance) plus लगना (lagnaa, to strike/attach).
Sense 1 : to be affected by the evil eye
verb, intransitive The affected person or thing takes को: बच्चे को नज़र लग गई (bachche ko nazar lag gaii, 'the child was struck by the evil eye'). The one whose gaze caused it (if named) takes की नज़र: उसकी नज़र लग गई (uskii nazar lag gaii, 'his/her gaze struck [someone]').
Synonyms: नज़र लग जाना (nazar lag jaanaa, with completive), बुरी नज़र लगना (burii nazar lagnaa, with explicit 'evil')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This reflects a widespread belief across Indian households that excessive praise or an envious/admiring glance -- even unintentional -- can bring misfortune, especially to babies, brides, or anything newly acquired (a car, a house). Families ward it off with rituals like waving chillies and salt (नज़र उतारना, nazar utaarnaa, 'to remove the evil eye') or dabbing a black kaajal dot on a baby's forehead. You will often hear an elder say टोकना नहीं (toknaa nahiin, 'don't jinx it') right after someone compliments a child, precisely to avoid triggering नज़र लगना.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make the admired person or thing the grammatical subject with a normal transitive frame; instead the affected party is marked with को and नज़र is the subject that 'lands' on them, mirroring other involuntary-experience verbs like दर्द होना (dard honaa). 2) This almost always appears with लग जाना (lag jaanaa) rather than bare लगना, since the affliction is felt as a sudden, completed event.
