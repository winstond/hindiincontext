फेंकना (phenknaa)

verb Basic transitive verb meaning 'to throw'; also has a colloquial figurative sense of boasting/exaggerating -- 'talking big'.
Sense 1 : to throw, hurl, toss
verb, transitive
Synonyms: फेंक देना (phenk denaa, with completive), उछालना (uchhaalnaa, to toss/fling upward), डालना (daalnaa, to throw/put in casually)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in daily life -- कूड़ा फेंको (koodaa phenko, throw out the trash) is a staple household command, and बॉल फेंको (bol phenko, throw the ball) is standard playground Hindi. The verb pairs very naturally with देना (denaa) as a completive: फेंक दो (phenk do, go ahead and throw it/throw it away).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the compound-verb form फेंक देना (phenk denaa) which native speakers use by default for a completed throw -- bare फेंका (phenkaa) alone can sound incomplete or overly literary in casual speech. 2) With कूड़ा फेंकना (koodaa phenknaa, to throw away trash), को (ko) marks the destination/recipient if there is one -- e.g. मुझ पर फेंकना (mujh par phenknaa, to throw at me) uses पर (par), not को (ko).
Sample Sentences:
कूड़ा फेंको, कमरा गंदा हो रहा है। Throw out the trash, the room is getting dirty.
(koodaa phenko, kamraa gandaa ho rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to boast, talk big, exaggerate (colloquial)
verb, intransitive Used intransitively/absolutely in this sense, often without a direct object -- वह बहुत फेंकता है (vah bahut phenktaa hai, 'he talks a lot of nonsense/exaggerates a lot').
Synonyms: गप्प मारना (gapp maarnaa, to chat/bluff casually), डींग मारना (diing maarnaa, to boast/brag), बड़ी-बड़ी बातें करना (badii-badii baaten karnaa, to talk big)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is classic college-friends-group Hindi -- कितना फेंकता है यार! (kitnaa phenktaa hai yaar!, 'dude, he exaggerates so much!') is the kind of line you'd hear when someone claims an unbelievable achievement. It is playful teasing more than a serious accusation of lying.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is strictly colloquial and can sound odd or even rude in formal writing or polite address to elders/strangers -- stick to it with friends and peers. 2) Learners sometimes miss that no object is needed here, unlike the literal throwing sense -- फेंकना (phenknaa) alone can mean 'to boast' in context.
Sample Sentences:
