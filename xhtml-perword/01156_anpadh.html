अनपढ़ (anpadh)

adjective/noun A common adjective/noun meaning 'illiterate' or 'uneducated', built from the negative prefix अ- (a) plus पढ़ (padh, 'read'). Same word serves as both a descriptive adjective and a noun referring to an illiterate person.
Sense 1 : illiterate, unlettered
adjective
Synonyms: निरक्षर (nirakshar, formal), गैर-पढ़ा-लिखा (gair-padhaa-likhaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word a lot in the context of literacy campaigns and rural development -- सरकार अनपढ़ लोगों को पढ़ाना चाहती है (sarkaar anpadh logon ko padhaanaa chaahtii hai, 'the government wants to teach illiterate people') is the kind of sentence that shows up in news and NGO talk. But used about a person directly, it stings a bit, similar to calling someone 'uneducated' in English -- so context and tone matter a lot.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse अनपढ़ (anpadh) with अज्ञानी (agyaanii, 'ignorant') -- अनपढ़ (anpadh) is specifically about lacking formal education/literacy, not general foolishness or lack of worldly sense. 2) Calling someone अनपढ़ (anpadh) to their face is quite blunt and can come across as an insult, even though it is descriptively neutral in a sentence about literacy statistics.

Sense 2 : an illiterate person
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun simply by using the same adjective form with an implied 'person' -- अनपढ़ (anpadh) alone can mean 'an illiterate (person)', taking gender agreement from context rather than changing form.
Synonyms: निरक्षर (nirakshar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This noun use is common in proverbs and folk sayings about the value of education, where अनपढ़ (anpadh) stands in contrast to पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa, 'educated') -- a pairing you will hear constantly in everyday conversation about schooling and opportunity.
