ज़्यादातर (zyaadaatar)

adverb/adjective One core meaning: 'mostly / most of' -- used both as an adjective (quantifying a noun) and as an adverb (modifying the whole statement). The two uses share the same word and are split below by grammatical role.
Sense 1 : mostly, usually, most of the time
adverb
Synonyms: आम तौर पर (aam taur par), अक्सर (aksar, mostly about frequency rather than proportion), सामान्यतः (saamaanytah, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in casual descriptions of habits: लोग ज़्यादातर (zyaadaatar) घर पर खाना खाते हैं is the kind of line a Delhi aunty might say about the neighborhood. It is a very natural, everyday word, nowhere near as formal as सामान्यतः (saamaanytah).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with अक्सर (aksar): ज़्यादातर (zyaadaatar) points more to 'the majority of the time/cases,' while अक्सर (aksar) is closer to 'often, frequently' -- there is overlap but they are not perfect swaps. 2) As an adverb it sits before the verb phrase it modifies (often near the front of the sentence), not right before the noun it might seem to describe.

Sense 2 : most (of), the majority of
adjective Functions like an invariant quantifier placed before the noun it quantifies, e.g. ज़्यादातर लोग (zyaadaatar log, 'most people') -- it does not change form for gender or number.
Synonyms: अधिकांश (adhikaansh, formal), बहुत सारे (bahut saare, covers 'a lot of' more than strictly 'majority')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you reach for constantly in daily chatter -- ज़्यादातर दुकानें बंद हैं (zyaadaatar dukaanen band hain, 'most shops are closed') is exactly the sort of line you would hear about a market on a holiday. Its formal cousin अधिकांश (adhikaansh) shows up more in news reports and writing.
