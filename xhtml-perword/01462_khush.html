ख़ुश (khush)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'happy' or 'pleased' -- one of the most common feeling-words in everyday Hindi, used constantly in greetings, blessings, and small talk.
Sense 1 : happy, pleased, glad
adjective Almost always paired with होना (honaa) 'to be happy' or करना (karnaa) 'to make/please someone'; the person one is happy with/about is marked with से (se), e.g. 'happy with you' is तुमसे ख़ुश (tumse khush).
Synonyms: प्रसन्न (prasann, formal), आनंदित (aanandit, literary), सुखी (sukhii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear ख़ुश (khush) constantly in blessings from elders, like ख़ुश रहो (khush raho, 'stay happy/be well'), which functions almost like a farewell blessing rather than a literal command. Note the nuqta under ख़ (from Persian/Urdu) -- in casual speech and texting it's often dropped and written as खुश (khush), which is an equally common and accepted spelling.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make this adjective agree in gender/number the way कड़ा (kadaa) does -- but ख़ुश (khush) never changes form, regardless of the subject's gender or number. 2) To say 'happy with/about,' use से (se), not के साथ (ke saath) or पर (par): मैं तुमसे ख़ुश हूँ (main tumse khush hoon, 'I am happy with you'), not के साथ. 3) 'To make someone happy' is ख़ुश करना (khush karnaa), while 'to become happy' is ख़ुश होना (khush honaa) -- mixing these up is a very common error for beginners.
