पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa)

adjective A compound adjective built from the past participles of पढ़ना (padhnaa, 'to read/study') and लिखना (likhnaa, 'to write'), meaning 'educated' or 'literate'. One core sense, used of people.
Sense 1 : educated, literate
adjective
Synonyms: शिक्षित (shikshit, formal), साक्षर (saakshar, literacy-specific -- can mean simply 'able to read and write' rather than broadly 'educated')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday word Indians use to size someone up socially -- a पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa) इंसान (insaan, 'person') is someone from a 'good,' schooled background, and you'll hear parents use it as a badge of pride about their kids or as a criterion in matchmaking conversations. It carries a slightly broader, warmer sense than the more clinical शिक्षित (shikshit), which shows up more in official or written contexts.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as two separate adjectives rather than a fixed compound -- both halves always appear together and inflect together, agreeing as a single unit with the noun they describe (e.g. पढ़ी-लिखी लड़की (padhii-likhii ladkii, 'educated girl')). 2) पढ़ा-लिखा (padhaa-likhaa) describes a person's general education/literacy level, not the act of having read a specific book -- for 'I have read this book' you'd use मैंने यह किताब पढ़ी है (mainne yah kitaab padhii hai, 'I have read this book'), not this word.
