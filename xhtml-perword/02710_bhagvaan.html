भगवान (bhagvaan)

noun/interjection The everyday Hindi word for God, mainly in the general/monotheistic sense used across faiths in casual speech; also lives inside common exclamations and set phrases.
Sense 1 : God, the Divine
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ईश्वर (iishvar), प्रभु (prabhu, slightly more devotional/formal), खुदा (khudaa, used especially by Urdu/Muslim speakers), परमात्मा (parmaatmaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear भगवान (bhagvaan) constantly in daily Delhi life, not just in temples: भगवान का शुक्र है (bhagvaan kaa shukr hai, 'thank God') when relief arrives, भगवान जाने (bhagvaan jaane, 'God knows') when someone shrugs off an unanswerable question, and भगवान करे (bhagvaan kare, 'may God grant it') as a blessing before an exam or journey. It sits alongside ईश्वर (iishvar) and खुदा (khudaa) as everyday synonyms people reach for depending on habit rather than strict religious identity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume भगवान (bhagvaan) refers only to a specific Hindu deity, but in everyday speech it functions as a generic word for God, much like the English word, and is used regardless of the speaker's specific religious practice. 2) It is grammatically masculine and singular even when used to address the divine in the abstract, so verb agreement stays masculine singular.

Sense 2 : exclamation of shock, appeal, or exasperation
interjection Used as a standalone exclamation, often as हे भगवान (he bhagvaan) or भगवान! (bhagvaan), reacting to surprise, distress, or frustration -- functionally parallel to English 'Oh my God.'
Synonyms: हे भगवान (he bhagvaan, full exclamatory form), अरे बाबा (are baabaa, colloquial alternative)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Drop a sudden भगवान! (bhagvaan) or हे भगवान! (he bhagvaan) when the milk boils over or an auto driver cuts you off, and you will sound completely natural -- it is the reflexive exclamation of Delhi households and streets alike, cutting across religious lines the same way its noun sense does.
