सनकी (sankii)

adjective/noun An adjective (also used as a noun for a person) describing someone eccentric or given to whims and sudden obsessions; related to the noun सनक (sanak, 'whim, fad, mania').
Sense 1 : eccentric, whimsical, given to odd fixations
adjective
Synonyms: पागल (paagal, covers 'crazy' more broadly), झक्की (jhakkii, colloquial), अजीब (ajiib, meaning scope: just 'strange', weaker than सनकी)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic word for the lovable-but-odd relative -- the chacha (chaachaa, 'uncle') who has a strange rule for everything, or a boss with bizarre pet peeves. It carries a teasing, semi-affectionate tone rather than outright insult, much like calling someone a 'crank' or 'oddball' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पागल (paagal) to mean 'weird person', but सनकी (sankii) specifically implies someone fixated on odd habits or sudden whims, not clinical madness -- it's closer to 'that uncle who suddenly decides to only wear white on Tuesdays' than to actual insanity. 2) It is mildly mocking/affectionate rather than clinical or cruel, so using it about a stranger's serious mental illness would sound off and even insensitive.

Sense 2 : an eccentric person, a crank
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun by dropping any following noun -- वह सनकी है (vah sankii hai, 'he is an eccentric') -- and inflects like a regular masculine/feminine -ई adjective-turned-noun depending on the person described.
Synonyms: पागल (paagal, broader: 'madman'), झक्की (jhakkii, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this used almost fondly in family gossip -- मोहल्ले का सनकी (mohalle kaa sankii, 'the neighborhood eccentric') is a recognizable stock character in Indian small talk, much like the 'crazy cat lady' or 'weird neighbor' trope in English.
