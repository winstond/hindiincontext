घोंटना (ghontnaa)

verb A physical verb built around squeezing/choking pressure on the throat; its main literal sense is 'to strangle/choke,' with a common extended sense of grinding or crushing something (like betel leaf paste) and a vivid idiomatic sense about stifling one's voice or feelings.
Sense 1 : to strangle, throttle, choke (someone)
verb, transitive Often used with गला (galaa, throat) as the object, as in गला घोंटना (galaa ghontnaa, to strangle by the throat), even though घोंटना (ghontnaa) alone can also take a person as direct object.
Synonyms: गला दबाना (galaa dabaanaa, lit. 'to press the throat'), दम घोंटना (dam ghontnaa, to suffocate)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll most often meet this word in the fixed phrase गला घोंटना (galaa ghontnaa, 'to strangle the throat'), which appears constantly in crime news, thrillers, and everyday dramatic speech about murder or suffocation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a mild word for 'to hurt,' but it specifically evokes choking/strangling pressure on the throat and is fairly intense/violent in tone -- not used casually for minor discomfort. 2) Confusion with घूँटना (ghoontnaa) or घूंट (ghoont, a sip) due to similar sound; घोंटना (ghontnaa) has no connection to drinking or sipping.

Sense 2 : to stifle, suppress, choke back (one's voice, feelings, or a sound)
verb, transitive Used figuratively with abstract objects like आवाज़ (aavaaz, voice), जज़्बात (jazbaat, feelings), or दर्द (dard, pain) -- as in आवाज़ घोंटना (aavaaz ghontnaa, 'to choke/stifle one's voice').
Synonyms: दबाना (dabaanaa, to suppress), दबा देना (dabaa denaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense captures a very Hindi way of describing emotional suppression as physical strangulation -- so आवाज़ घोंट देना (aavaaz ghont denaa) evokes someone forcibly swallowing their words or holding back a cry, a vivid, almost bodily metaphor common in poetry, songs, and emotional dialogue.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative extension is easy to miss for learners who only know the literal 'strangle' sense; here nothing physical is being choked, the object is an emotion, sound, or expression being forcibly suppressed.

Sense 3 : to grind or crush into a fine paste (e.g., betel leaf, herbs)
verb, transitive Classic collocation is पान घोंटना (paan ghontnaa), referring to grinding betel leaf with lime and areca nut into a smooth paste before chewing.
Synonyms: पीसना (piisnaa), महीन करना (mahiin karnaa, to make fine)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense comes alive at any paan (paan) shop, where the paanwala rhythmically grinds the leaf mixture with a small pestle -- a very visual, tactile use of घोंटना (ghontnaa) tied to everyday Delhi street life.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar only with the 'strangle' sense are often surprised this same verb is used for a harmless kitchen/paan-shop action of grinding leaves into paste -- context (the object being ground) is what signals this sense, not a different verb.
