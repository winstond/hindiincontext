पहले (pahle)

adverb/postposition Covers 'before/earlier' as a time adverb, 'first' as an ordering adverb, and its use as a postposition meaning 'before (something)' when paired with से (se).
Sense 1 : earlier, before (in time), previously
adverb
Synonyms: पूर्व (poorv, formal), इससे पहले (isse pahle, variant form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday word Delhi households use to sequence a story: पहले (pahle) यह हुआ, फिर वह हुआ (phir vah huaa, 'then that happened') -- it is the natural partner of फिर (phir, 'then') and बाद में (baad men, 'afterward') in narrating events in order.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget that when comparing to a specific point in time, पहले (pahle) needs से (se) attached to that point: मेरे आने से पहले (mere aane se pahle, 'before my coming') -- dropping से (se) here sounds broken. 2) पहले (pahle) alone at the start of a sentence just means 'earlier/at first,' not 'first' in a numbered sequence -- for that, learners often reach for सबसे पहले (sabse pahle, 'first of all') instead.
Sample Sentences:

(pahle main dillii men rahtaa thaa।)

Sense 2 : first, ahead of others (in order or priority)
adverb
Synonyms: सबसे पहले (sabse pahle, 'first of all', variant form), प्रथम (pratham, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in queues and classrooms: पहले आप (pahle aap, 'after you' / literally 'first you') is a small ritual courtesy phrase, almost a catchphrase of Indian politeness, offering someone else the priority of going first.

Sense 3 : before (a point in time or a person/thing), ahead of
postposition Takes an oblique-case noun phrase marked with से (se) before it: खाने से पहले (khaane se pahle, 'before eating').
Synonyms: पूर्व (poorv, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This से...पहले (se...pahle) frame is the mirror image of के बाद (ke baad, 'after'), and the two are almost always taught and used as a pair by Hindi speakers when sequencing events.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place पहले (pahle) before the noun phrase as English word order would suggest, but Hindi requires the से-marked phrase first: मीटिंग से पहले (miiting se pahle, 'before the meeting'), not पहले मीटिंग से.
Sample Sentences:
मीटिंग से पहले मुझे यह रिपोर्ट पढ़नी है। I need to read this report before the meeting.
(miiting se pahle mujhe yah riport padhnii hai।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
