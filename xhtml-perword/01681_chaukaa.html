चौका (chaukaa)

noun Primarily a kitchen word for the traditional cooking area/hearth space; also has cricket and card-game senses referring to a set of four.
Sense 1 : the kitchen / cooking hearth area
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रसोई (rasoii), किचन (kichan, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In many North Indian homes, especially older or more traditional ones, चौका (chaukaa) refers to the small, often floor-level cooking spot that is kept ritually pure -- you'll hear grandmothers say things like चौके में मत आओ (chauke men mat aao, 'don't come into the kitchen area') to someone who hasn't bathed yet. It's a word that carries a whiff of an older domestic order, even as most young Delhi households now just say किचन (kichan).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume चौका (chaukaa) is just a casual synonym of रसोई (rasoii); in many traditional and older households it specifically evokes the clean, ritually maintained cooking space (sometimes with rules about who may enter it), not just 'kitchen' in the modern flat sense.

Sense 2 : a boundary shot worth four runs (cricket)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चार रन (chaar ran, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every cricket commentator's favorite word: जोरदार चौका! (jordaar chaukaa!, 'a powerful four!') is a stock line during a match broadcast. Its cousin छक्का (chhakkaa) covers the six -- learning both together will get you a long way at any Delhi rooftop cricket-watching gathering.

Sense 3 : a set of four (cards, dice, etc.)
noun, masculine
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this at the card table during a game of रम्मी (rammii, 'rummy') or teen patti when someone lays down four of a kind -- चौका लगा! (chaukaa lagaa!, 'got a four!'). It's the same underlying idea as the cricket sense: a grouping of four.
