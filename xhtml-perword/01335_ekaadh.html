एकाध (ekaadh)

adjective A single-sense quantifier meaning 'one or two, a couple of' -- used to hint at a small, imprecise number.
Sense 1 : one or two, a couple of (imprecise small quantity)
adjective Placed directly before the noun it quantifies, like other quantity words: एकाध किताब (ekaadh kitaab, 'one or two books'). The noun that follows generally stays in the singular even when the meaning is plural-ish.
Synonyms: दो-चार (do-chaar, 'a few'), कुछ (kuchh, less specific), इकलौता (iklautaa, meaning scope: only 'sole/single', not interchangeable)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is a nice example of Hindi's comfort with fuzzy quantities -- एक (ek, 'one') plus आध (aadh, related to 'half') fuse into a word that shrugs off precision, much like English 'a couple of' but even looser. You will hear it constantly in casual talk: एकाध दिन में आ जाऊँगा (ekaadh din men aa jaaoongaa, 'I will come in a day or two'), where nobody actually cares if it is one day or two.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes pluralize the following noun out of habit (e.g. treating it like 'a couple of books' in English) -- Hindi speakers keep the noun singular: एकाध किताब (ekaadh kitaab, 'a couple of books'), not एकाध किताबें. 2) एकाध (ekaadh) is vaguer than दो (do, 'two') -- it signals 'maybe one, maybe two, no need to count exactly,' so swapping in दो changes the tone from casual estimate to precise count.
