जादू (jaadoo)

noun/adjective Primarily a noun meaning 'magic' or 'a magic spell', with a common figurative extension to 'charm/enchanting effect'; it also appears in a fixed idiom about someone's influence taking over. A related adjective sense describes something as 'magical'.
Sense 1 : magic; a magic trick or spell
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जादूगरी (jaadoogrii, the craft/art of magic), टोना (tonaa, sorcery, a spell)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Street performers called जादूगर (jaadoogar, magician) doing card tricks or 'disappearing coin' acts at fairs and weddings are a classic sight in Delhi-NCR, and this is the everyday image the word जादू (jaadoo) evokes before any spooky connotation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use जादू (jaadoo) for a 'magic trick' at a stage show and for 'sorcery/black magic' interchangeably; in careful speech, टोना-टोटका (tonaa-totkaa) leans toward the darker, superstitious sense while जादू (jaadoo) alone is neutral and covers both stage magic and folk magic.

Sense 2 : captivating charm, spell-like effect (figurative)
noun, masculine Commonly used with चलना (chalnaa) or सिर चढ़ना (sir chadhnaa) to describe someone's charm or influence 'working' on people, e.g. उसकी आवाज़ का जादू (uskii aavaaz kaa jaadoo, 'the magic of her voice').
Synonyms: असर (asar, effect, impact), कशिश (kashish, allure, pull)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly about singers, actors, and cricketers -- फ़िल्मी अंदाज़ में उसकी अदाओं का जादू (uskii adaaon kaa jaadoo, 'the magic of her mannerisms') is the kind of line that shows up in film reviews and casual gossip alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is almost always used with a possessive or genitive phrase (किसी का जादू, kisii kaa jaadoo) -- saying just जादू (jaadoo) alone without specifying whose charm it is sounds incomplete to a native speaker in this sense.

Sense 3 : used in the idiom 'सिर पर जादू चढ़ना/होना' -- to be completely under someone's spell/influence
noun, masculine Occurs in the fixed idiomatic frame सिर पर जादू होना/चढ़ना (sir par jaadoo honaa / sir par jaadoo chadhnaa), describing someone so infatuated or obsessed that they seem enchanted.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This idiom is often used half-teasingly about someone newly in love or oddly loyal to a person or brand -- 'उस पर तो जादू चढ़ गया है' (us par to jaadoo chadh gayaa hai, 'he's completely under her spell') -- a lighthearted way of saying someone has lost their normal judgment.

Sense 4 : magical, enchanting (used attributively, often in compounds)
adjective जादू (jaadoo) itself is rarely used as a standalone adjective; जादुई (jaaduii) is the standard derived adjective learners will encounter far more often in this sense.
Synonyms: जादुई (jaaduii, the more common adjective form), मनमोहक (manmohak, enchanting, captivating)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: जादू की छड़ी (jaadoo kii chhadii, 'magic wand') is the classic phrase from fairy tales and is a good example of how जादू (jaadoo) modifies nouns through की (kii) rather than inflecting like a true adjective.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जादू (jaadoo) as an adjective (e.g. trying to say 'a magic wand') when जादुई (jaaduii) छड़ी (chhadii) is the natural phrase; जादू (jaadoo) mainly stays a noun even in compound nouns like जादू की छड़ी (jaadoo kii chhadii, 'magic wand'), where it is functioning as a noun modifier via की (kii), not as an adjective proper.
Sample Sentences:
