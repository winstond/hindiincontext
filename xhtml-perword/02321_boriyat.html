बोरियत (boriyat)

noun A single, straightforward sense: the feeling of boredom. Borrowed from English 'bore' plus the Persian/Urdu abstract-noun suffix (iyat), a hybrid coinage common in everyday Hindi-Urdu.
Sense 1 : boredom
noun, feminine Commonly paired with होना (honaa) as बोरियत होना (boriyat honaa, 'to feel bored') using a dative-subject frame: मुझे बोरियत हो रही है (mujhe boriyat ho rahii hai, 'I am feeling bored').
Synonyms: उकताहट (uktaahat), ऊब (oob)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is a great example of how casually English gets absorbed into Hindi: बोर (bor) comes straight from English 'bore', and ियत (iyat) is a native Urdu abstract-noun suffix also seen in words like शराफ़त (sharaafat, 'decency') -- Hindi speakers happily bolt the two together. In everyday Delhi speech, you will hear the adjective form बोर होना (bor honaa, 'to become bored') at least as often as this noun.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं बोरियत हूँ (main boriyat hoon) by analogy with English 'I am bored', but Hindi treats boredom as something that happens to you, not a state you are in -- use मुझे बोरियत हो रही है (mujhe boriyat ho rahii hai) or मैं बोर हो रहा हूँ (main bor ho rahaa hoon) instead. 2) बोरियत (boriyat) is the noun for the feeling itself; do not use it where the adjective बोर (bor, 'bored/boring') is needed, as in वह फ़िल्म बोर थी (vah film bor thii, 'that film was boring').

मुझे इस काम में बहुत बोरियत होती है। I feel a lot of boredom doing this work.
(mujhe is kaam men bahut boriyat hotii hai।)
