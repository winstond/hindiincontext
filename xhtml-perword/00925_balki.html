बल्कि (balki)

conjunction A single-sense connective meaning 'rather' or 'but rather' -- used to correct or upgrade a preceding statement, almost always paired with a negation earlier in the sentence.
Sense 1 : rather, but rather (correcting/upgrading a prior statement)
conjunction Typically completes a न सिर्फ़...बल्कि... (n sirf...balki..., 'not only... but also...') or नहीं...बल्कि... (nahiin...balki...) frame, where the first clause is negated or limited and बल्कि (balki) introduces the stronger or truer alternative.
Synonyms: वरन (varan, literary), बल्कि तो (balki to, emphatic variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of बल्कि (balki) as Hindi's way of saying 'no wait, it's actually even more than that' -- it upgrades a claim rather than just opposing it. For example, 'वह ग़रीब नहीं है, बल्कि बहुत अमीर है' (vah ghariib nahiin hai, balki bahut amiir hai, 'He is not poor, rather he's very rich') shows how it swaps in a stronger truth after knocking down a weaker or wrong one. You will hear it constantly in News Hindi and everyday argument alike, e.g. when someone corrects a common misconception.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use बल्कि (balki) as a plain 'but,' where लेकिन (lekin) or पर (par) is correct -- बल्कि (balki) specifically replaces or upgrades the first idea rather than just contrasting with it. 2) It almost always needs a negative or limiting setup before it (नहीं (nahiin), न सिर्फ़ (n sirf)); using it standalone like 'but rather' in English without that setup sounds off in Hindi. 3) Word order: बल्कि (balki) sits at the start of its own clause, not mid-clause like 'rather' can in English.

वह ग़रीब नहीं है, बल्कि बहुत अमीर है। He is not poor, rather he's very rich.
(vah ghariib nahiin hai, balki bahut amiir hai।)
