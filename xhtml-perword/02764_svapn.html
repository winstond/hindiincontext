स्वप्न (svapn)

noun A more literary/formal word for 'dream' -- both the sleep-vision and the aspiration sense, paralleling English 'dream'. The everyday word for this is सपना (sapnaa).
Sense 1 : dream, aspiration (figurative)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सपना (sapnaa), आकांक्षा (aakaankshaa, formal), ख्वाब (khvaab)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind famous phrases like भारत का स्वप्न (bhaarat kaa svapn, 'India's dream') in political and motivational speech, echoing the same register as English 'the dream of a nation.' It is the word you will meet in essays about great leaders' visions, not in casual chat about last night's sleep.
Common Mistakes: 1) Both स्वप्न (svapn) and सपना (sapnaa) work here, but स्वप्न (svapn) carries a slightly more elevated, inspirational tone -- useful in speeches, essays, or when talking about someone's life ambition, whereas सपना (sapnaa) is what you would use casually among friends.

Sense 2 : dream (during sleep)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सपना (sapnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This word shares its root with English 'somnolent' and Latin 'somnus' -- both go back to the same ancient Indo-European word for sleep, a nice bridge for English speakers. In speech, Delhi-NCR folks almost always say सपना (sapnaa); स्वप्न (svapn) shows up in poetry, song lyrics, and slightly formal or philosophical writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्वप्न (svapn) in everyday speech about last night's dream, where सपना (sapnaa) is what people actually say; स्वप्न (svapn) sounds bookish or poetic in that context. 2) The verb used with this noun is देखना (dekhnaa, 'to see') -- you 'see' a dream in Hindi, you do not 'have' one.
