कहाँ (kahaan)

adverb An interrogative adverb asking about location ('where'), also used in fixed idiomatic patterns expressing rhetorical contrast or disbelief.
Sense 1 : at/in/to what place
adverb
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the first question words every learner picks up, right alongside क्या (kyaa) and कौन (kaun) -- you will hear it constantly in daily life, from rickshaw drivers asking कहाँ जाना है? (kahaan jaanaa hai?, 'where do you need to go?') to mothers calling out कहाँ हो? (kahaan ho?, 'where are you?').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget कहाँ (kahaan) can mean both 'where (location)' and 'where to (destination)' without needing a separate postposition -- तुम कहाँ जा रहे हो? (tum kahaan jaa rahe ho?, 'where are you going?') needs no को (ko). 2) Confusing कहाँ (kahaan) with कहीं (kahiin, 'somewhere/anywhere') -- the first is a direct question word, the second is an indefinite.
Sample Sentences:
तुम कहाँ जा रहे हो? Where are you going?
(tum kahaan jaa rahe ho?)

Sense 2 : rhetorical contrast: 'as if', 'hardly', expressing a mismatch or unlikelihood
adverb Used in a doubled or contrastive frame -- often कहाँ ... कहाँ (kahaan ... kahaan) -- to highlight a gap between two things, or alone at the start of a clause to dismiss a suggestion as unlikely.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This rhetorical use is a favorite of Delhi aunties and uncles delivering a dry putdown -- कहाँ तुम, कहाँ यह काम! (kahaan tum, kahaan yah kaam!, 'you and this job? no way!') conveys disbelief with real comic timing. The proverb कहाँ राजा भोज, कहाँ गंगू तेली (kahaan raajaa bhoj, kahaan gangoo telii) is widely known and worth learning as a set phrase for 'comparing apples to oranges.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate this too literally as a location question when it is actually rhetorical -- वह कहाँ आने वाला है! (vah kahaan aane vaalaa hai!, 'as if he's coming!') has nothing to do with place. 2) Missing the doubled structure कहाँ राजा भोज, कहाँ गंगू तेली (kahaan raajaa bhoj, kahaan gangoo telii, 'chalk and cheese' -- literally 'where King Bhoj, where Gangu the oil-presser') which signals an unbridgeable contrast, not a real question about location.
Sample Sentences:
कहाँ तुम, कहाँ यह काम! You and this job? No way!
(kahaan tum, kahaan yah kaam!)
