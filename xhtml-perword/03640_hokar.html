होकर (hokar)

adverb/postposition Built from हो (ho, stem of होना, 'to be/become') plus the conjunctive-participle suffix कर (kar). One sense works as an adverb meaning 'having become/having been [X]' before a following action; the other works as a postposition meaning 'via, by way of.'
Sense 1 : having become / having been (completed state before the main action)
adverb Attaches to an adjective or noun complement (खुश होकर khush hokar, 'having become happy') to show the state was completed just before the main verb's action; grammatically it patterns exactly like other conjunctive participles such as खुलकर (khulkar) or मिलकर (milkar).
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same participle logic you see in बढ़कर (badhkar, 'having exceeded') or मिलकर (milkar, 'having met/together') -- Hindi loves chaining a completed state onto the next action this way instead of using 'because' or 'and then.' Think of it as Hindi's compact way of saying 'X-ed, and then...' in a single breath.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for a conjunction like क्योंकि (kyonki) to link the state and the action, but होकर (hokar) already does this job on its own and needs no separate connector: खुश होकर मुस्कुराया (khush hokar muskuraayaa, 'having become happy, he smiled'), not खुश होकर, इसलिए मुस्कुराया. 2) Do not confuse this होकर (hokar) with the postposition sense meaning 'via' -- the two look identical but the adverbial one always follows an adjective or noun describing a state, not a place name.

Sense 2 : via, by way of, through (a place, on a route)
postposition Follows a place name directly, with no additional case marking needed on the place: दिल्ली होकर (dillii hokar, 'via Delhi').
Synonyms: के रास्ते (ke raaste), के मार्ग से (ke maarg se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly from train and bus conductors or in travel planning -- दिल्ली होकर जयपुर जाना है (dillii hokar jaypur jaanaa hai, 'have to go to Jaipur via Delhi') is a classic way Delhi-NCR folks describe multi-leg journeys.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes insert से (se) before होकर (hokar) by analogy with other route expressions, but the place name attaches directly: मुंबई होकर जाना है (mumbaii hokar jaanaa hai, 'have to go via Mumbai'), not मुंबई से होकर।
Sample Sentences:
