कतार (kataar)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'a line/queue,' used both for people waiting in a queue and for rows of objects lined up.
Sense 1 : line, queue, row
noun, feminine
Synonyms: पंक्ति (pankti, formal), लाइन (laain, colloquial, from English)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see कतार (kataar) on signboards at banks, ration shops, and railway counters -- 'कृपया कतार में खड़े हों' (kripyaa kataar men khade hon, 'please stand in line') is a familiar civic instruction. In everyday chat, though, most Delhi-NCR speakers will just say लाइन (laain) lagaana instead -- so कतार carries a slightly more formal, written flavor even though it's not obscure at all.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for लाइन (laain) by default since it is borrowed from English and very common in speech, but कतार (kataar) is the word you will see in written/official contexts (signs, announcements) and in more careful speech -- both are correct, but knowing कतार (kataar) rounds out register range. 2) The common verb pairing is कतार में लगना (kataar men lagnaa, 'to get in line') or कतार लगाना (kataar lagaanaa, 'to form a line') -- learners sometimes try to use कतार as a verb directly, but it always needs a supporting verb like लगना or लगाना.
