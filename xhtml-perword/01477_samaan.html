समान (samaan)

adjective/noun Two distinct uses: an adjective meaning 'similar/equal' and a noun meaning 'goods/belongings' (a common variant of सामान (saamaan)).
Sense 1 : similar, equal, alike
adjective Typically used with से (se) to mark the point of comparison, e.g. 'X, Y के समान है' (X, Y ke samaan hai, 'X is like Y').
Synonyms: बराबर (baraabar), सदृश (sadrish, literary), तुल्य (tuly, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will see in formal writing, legal or constitutional language (like 'समान अधिकार' (samaan adhikaar, 'equal rights')), and school essays, whereas casual speech leans on जैसा (jaisaa) or बराबर (baraabar) instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जैसा (jaisaa) in speech and समान (samaan) in writing without realizing the two are largely interchangeable in this sense, but समान (samaan) sounds noticeably more formal/bookish. 2) The comparison particle is के समान (ke samaan), not से समान (se samaan) -- के is required before समान when comparing two nouns.

Sense 2 : goods, luggage, belongings
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सामान (saamaan, dominant spelling)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will hear this at train stations and airports constantly -- 'अपना सामान (apnaa saamaan, 'your belongings') संभाल कर रखें' is standard signage and announcement language, though most learners will encounter it spelled सामान (saamaan).
Common Mistakes: 1) This is simply a spelling variant of सामान (saamaan) -- both are heard and read, but सामान (saamaan) is far more common in everyday writing; don't treat समान (samaan) in this sense as a separate or unusual word.
