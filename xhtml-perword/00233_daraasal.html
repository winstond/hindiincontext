दरअसल (daraasal)

adverb A discourse adverb meaning 'actually, in fact, to tell the truth' -- used to introduce a correction, clarification, or a slightly more honest version of what was just said.
Sense 1 : actually / in fact / to tell the truth
adverb Typically opens a clause or sentence, often followed by a comma pause in writing; frequently used to soften a correction or admission.
Synonyms: असल में (asal men), वास्तव में (vaastav men, slightly more formal), सच तो यह है कि (sach to yah hai ki, longer phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic 'let me be honest with you' word -- you'll hear it constantly when someone is about to admit they forgot something, correct an earlier statement, or explain the real reason behind something. For example, someone might say 'मैं आया, लेकिन दरअसल मुझे रास्ता नहीं पता था' (main aayaa, lekin daraasal mujhe raastaa nahiin pataa thaa, 'I came, but actually I didn't know the way') -- note how it signals a shift from the polite version of events to the real one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often overuse वास्तव में (vaastav men) where a native speaker would reach for दरअसल (daraasal) in casual speech -- the latter sounds much more natural in conversation. 2) It is not used mid-sentence to mean 'really' as an intensifier (like 'really good'); for that, सच में (sach men) or वाकई (vaakaii) is the right choice.

(daraasal, mujhe kal kii miiting ke baare men pataa hii nahiin thaa।)
