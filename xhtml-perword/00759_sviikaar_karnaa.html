स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa)

verb A compound built from the noun स्वीकार (sviikaar, 'acceptance') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it covers accepting a proposal, admitting/confessing something, and formally agreeing to terms.
Sense 1 : to accept (an offer, proposal, gift, request)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: मान लेना (maan lenaa), क़बूल करना (qabool karnaa, often used for accepting proposals or requests, slightly more colloquial/Urdu-inflected)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear at weddings, job offers, and formal ceremonies -- when a family 'accepts' a marriage proposal, or a company 'accepts' your application, स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa) is the polite, official verb of choice, versus the more everyday मान लेना (maan lenaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मानना (maannaa) when स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa) is meant -- मानना (maannaa) leans toward 'to believe/obey,' while स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa) is the more formal, deliberate act of accepting something offered. 2) Because करना (karnaa) is transitive, the subject takes ने (ne) in the past tense, e.g. उसने प्रस्ताव स्वीकार किया (usne prastaav sviikaar kiyaa, 'he/she accepted the proposal') -- easy to forget since स्वीकार (sviikaar) itself looks like a static noun.

Sense 2 : to admit, confess (a fact, mistake, or truth)
verb, transitive Often followed by a clause with कि (ki), e.g. उसने स्वीकार किया कि वह गलत था (usne sviikaar kiyaa ki vah galat thaa, 'he admitted that he was wrong').
Synonyms: मान लेना (maan lenaa), क़बूल करना (qabool karnaa, especially in a confession or admission-of-guilt context)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in courtrooms, interviews, and heartfelt conversations alike -- अपनी गलती स्वीकार करना (apnii galtii sviikaar karnaa, 'to admit one's own mistake') is a common phrase in moral or parental advice, carrying a sense of maturity and honesty.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may reach for मानना (maannaa) alone to mean 'admit,' but स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa) carries a more formal, almost legal or introspective weight, closer to English 'admit/confess' than casual 'agree.'
