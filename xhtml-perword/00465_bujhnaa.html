बुझना (bujhnaa)

verb One core sense: to be extinguished (a flame, light, or fire going out) -- and a figurative extension of that same image to feelings, hope, or thirst fading/being satisfied. Its transitive partner is बुझाना (bujhaanaa, 'to extinguish/quench'), a separate causative lemma.
Sense 1 : to go out, be extinguished (flame, light)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: ठंडा होना (thandaa honaa, of embers/fire)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a diya (diyaa) flickering and going dark at the end of Diwali night, or a chulha's (choolhaa) embers dying down -- बुझना (bujhnaa) is the verb Hindi reaches for in both cases. Its causative partner बुझाना (bujhaanaa) is what you'd use to say someone blew out or put out the flame.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बंद होना (band honaa) for 'the light went out,' but बंद होना (band honaa) is for switches/devices being turned off, while बुझना (bujhnaa) is specifically about a flame or glow dying out -- a candle, diya, or fire uses बुझना (bujhnaa), not बंद होना (band honaa).

Sense 2 : figurative: hope, feeling, or life fading/being extinguished
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: मिटना (mitnaa, of hope/existence), खत्म होना (khatm honaa, of feeling)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi extends the image of a flame dying out to inner life: उम्मीद बुझ गई (ummiid bujh gaii, 'hope was extinguished') or आँखों की चमक बुझ गई (aankhon kii chamak bujh gaii, 'the sparkle in the eyes faded') both treat feeling as a small flame that can go dark, the same poetic logic English uses when it says a spirit was 'snuffed out.'

Sense 3 : figurative: thirst being quenched/satisfied
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: मिटना (mitnaa, of thirst/hunger)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as a flame is बुझती है (bujhtii hai), thirst is imagined the same way -- as a fire inside that water puts out, so प्यास बुझ गई (pyaas bujh gaii) literally means 'the thirst went out,' a lovely example of Hindi reusing one physical image across very different domains.
Common Mistakes: 1) प्यास बुझना (pyaas bujhnaa) sounds odd to English ears since English 'quench' is transitive ('to quench one's thirst'), but Hindi frames it the other way: the thirst itself बुझती है (bujhtii hai, 'goes out/is quenched'), with the person taking a की (kii) possessive, not a direct object slot.
Sample Sentences:

(itnaa paanii piine ke baad bhii merii pyaas nahiin bujhii।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
