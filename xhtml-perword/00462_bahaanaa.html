बहाना (bahaanaa)

noun/verb Two distinct lives in one word: as a noun it means 'excuse, pretext' (borrowed sense you will hear constantly), and as a verb it means 'to make (something) flow' -- the causative of बहना (bahnaa, 'to flow').
Sense 1 : excuse, pretext
noun, masculine
Synonyms: उपाय (upaay, less common in this sense), मिस (mis, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बहाना बनाना (bahaanaa banaanaa, 'to make an excuse') is the phrase you will hear in every household when someone dodges a chore or an invitation -- a mother saying अब क्या बहाना है? (ab kyaa bahaanaa hai?, 'what excuse is it now?') is practically a genre of its own. It also shows up in the idiom बहाने से (bahaane se, 'under the pretext of, on the pretext that'), useful for describing sneaky or roundabout behavior.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with माफी (maaphii, 'apology') -- बहाना (bahaanaa) is the excuse or pretext you give, not an apology for wrongdoing. 2) The common verb pairing is बहाना बनाना (bahaanaa banaanaa, 'to make an excuse') -- learners sometimes try to use करना (karnaa) instead, which sounds off here.
Sample Sentences:
वह हमेशा कोई न कोई बहाना बना लेता है। He always comes up with some excuse or the other.
(vah hameshaa koii n koii bahaanaa banaa letaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to make flow, to shed
verb, transitive Causative of बहना (bahnaa, 'to flow'); takes a direct object that is the thing set flowing, e.g. आँसू बहाना (aansoo bahaanaa, 'to shed tears') or पानी बहाना (paanii bahaanaa, 'to let water flow/waste').
Synonyms: बहा देना (bahaa denaa, compound-verb variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb frames flowing as something caused by an agent, which is a nice window into how Hindi builds causatives from simple verbs (बहना (bahnaa) becomes बहाना (bahaanaa)) -- the same pattern you will meet again and again elsewhere, like सोना (sonaa)/सुलाना (sulaanaa). खून बहाना (khoon bahaanaa, 'to shed blood') is a common set phrase in news and speeches about violence or sacrifice.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes mix this up with बहना (bahnaa) itself -- बहना (bahnaa) is what the river or the tears do on their own (intransitive), while बहाना (bahaanaa) is what a person does to cause that flowing (transitive).
