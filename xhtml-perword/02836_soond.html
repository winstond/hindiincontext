सूँड़ (soond)

noun A concrete noun meaning an elephant's trunk, or by extension a similar tube-like snout/proboscis on another creature.
Sense 1 : elephant's trunk
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is one of the first animal-body-part words Hindi-speaking kids learn, right alongside हाथी (haathii, 'elephant') itself -- picture books and nursery rhymes about हाथी almost always mention his सूँड़. Elephants are common sights at temples and in wedding processions across North India, so the word comes up naturally in everyday cultural conversation, not just in zoo vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse सूँड़ (soond) with सूंड (soond) -- both spellings (with chandrabindu nasalization vowel vs. anusvara consonant) are seen for this word, so do not treat one as wrong if you encounter the other. 2) This word is unrelated to सुंदर (sundar, 'beautiful') despite the superficial similarity in sound -- a beginner's-ear mix-up, not a real cognate.

Sense 2 : trunk/snout of other animals (e.g. a pig, or a proboscis-like organ)
noun, feminine Extended by shape-resemblance from the primary elephant-trunk sense to describe similar tube-like snouts on other creatures, such as a pig's snout.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Native speakers reach for सूँड़ (soond) any time something looks like a trunk -- a pig's snout, sometimes even a mosquito's proboscis in a science-class context -- rather than switching to a separate word, much as English speakers stretch 'trunk' occasionally beyond elephants.
