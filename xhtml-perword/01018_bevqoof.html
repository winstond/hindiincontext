बेवक़ूफ़ (bevqoof)

adjective/noun A common everyday word meaning 'foolish, stupid' (adjective) or 'a fool, idiot' (noun), used the same way in casual scolding or teasing.
Sense 1 : foolish, stupid, silly
adjective
Synonyms: मूर्ख (moorkh, formal), बुद्धू (buddhoo), उल्लू (ulloo, colloquial, 'owl' used figuratively), नासमझ (naasmajh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic Hindi-Urdu hybrid word (बे- + वक़ूफ़ (vaqoof, 'sense, understanding')), literally 'without sense.' You'll hear it constantly in Delhi households, said half-exasperated, half-affectionate, like a mother telling her son अरे बेवक़ूफ़ (are bevqoof, 'oh you fool') when he forgets his tiffin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget this adjective is invariant -- it does not change form for masculine/feminine or singular/plural, unlike native आ-ending adjectives such as अच्छा (achchhaa). 2) It can sting more than English 'silly' in some contexts, especially said sharply to a stranger or elder -- among friends and family it is usually light and teasing.

(are bevqoof, toone apnaa tifin phir bhool diyaa।)

Sense 2 : a fool, an idiot
noun, masculine/feminine Same invariant form as the adjective; functions as a noun when used with articles/quantifiers like एक बेवक़ूफ़ (ek bevqoof, 'a fool') or as a direct address.
Synonyms: मूर्ख (moorkh, formal), बुद्धू (buddhoo), उल्लू का पट्ठा (ulloo kaa patthaa, colloquial idiom, 'son of an owl')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Calling someone बेवक़ूफ़ (bevqoof) as a noun (rather than describing them with it) feels a bit more direct and personal, like English 'you fool' versus 'that's foolish.' It's a staple of Bollywood banter and everyday sibling arguments alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a noun of variable gender it takes m/f agreement from context (वह बेवक़ूफ़ है (vah bevqoof hai, 'he/she is a fool')) rather than changing its own form -- learners sometimes try to feminize the word itself, which is incorrect.
