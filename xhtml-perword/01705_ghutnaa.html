घुटना (ghutnaa)

noun/verb Two senses share this form: the noun घुटना (ghutnaa) meaning 'knee', and the verb घुटना (ghutnaa) meaning 'to be suffocated/stifled' or 'to be brewed/steeped' (of tea, etc.), used mostly figuratively for feeling stifled.
Sense 1 : knee
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जानु (jaanu, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday phrases like घुटनों के दर्द (ghutnon ke dard, 'knee pain') -- knee pain is almost a cultural rite of passage for aging relatives in India, endlessly discussed over chai. The idiom घुटने टेकना (ghutne teknaa, 'to kneel, to give up/surrender') literally means 'to place the knees down' and is used just like English 'to kneel before' someone, both literally and in the sense of admitting defeat.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget this is masculine and try to agree adjectives/verbs with it as feminine -- घुटना (ghutnaa) follows the regular masculine -आ noun pattern (घुटने ghutne in oblique/plural).

Sense 2 : to be suffocated, to feel stifled
verb, intransitive Almost always appears as दम घुटना (dam ghutnaa, 'breath gets suffocated') rather than the bare verb alone -- घुटना (ghutnaa by itself, without दम dam) sounds incomplete to native ears in this sense.
Synonyms: घबराना (ghabraanaa, to feel anxious/panicked -- close but not identical), दम घुटना (dam ghutnaa, set phrase, same idea)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi loves to locate suffering in the body rather than saying 'I feel suffocated' directly -- दम घुटना (dam ghutnaa) literally has your 'breath' as the grammatical subject doing the choking, the same body-as-agent logic you see in भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa, hunger) or गुस्सा आना (gussaa aanaa, anger arriving). People use it heavily for stifling emotional situations, e.g. a suffocating job or marriage, not just literal lack of air.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use this transitively ('to suffocate someone') -- this sense of घुटना (ghutnaa) is intransitive; to suffocate *someone else* you need the causative घोंटना (ghontnaa) or गला घोंटना (galaa ghontnaa, 'to strangle'), a different verb, not a form of this one. 2) The figurative use (feeling emotionally stifled, as in a suffocating environment or relationship) is far more common in daily speech than the literal 'can't breathe' sense.

Sense 3 : to be brewed/steeped, to simmer slowly
verb, intransitive Used for something (tea, a stew, a marinade) sitting and steeping over slow heat until it thickens or intensifies in flavor; also extended figuratively to a feeling or grudge that 'stews' inside someone over time.
Synonyms: पकना (paknaa, to cook/ripen -- broader term), गलना (galnaa, to melt/dissolve -- related but distinct)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the verb your grandmother uses when she says the chai needs to घुटना (ghutnaa) a little longer on low flame before it's ready -- it captures that slow, patient reduction that's central to good Indian tea and slow-cooked curries alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may not realize this culinary sense extends figuratively -- मन में गुस्सा घुटना (man men gussaa ghutnaa, 'anger simmering in the heart') uses the same verb as chai brewing on the stove.
