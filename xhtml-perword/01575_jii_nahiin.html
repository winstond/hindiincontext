जी नहीं (jii nahiin)

interjection A polite, softened way of saying 'no' -- जी (jii) is a respect particle added before नहीं (nahiin, 'no') to make the refusal or negation gentler and more courteous.
Sense 1 : polite no
interjection
Synonyms: नहीं जी (nahiin jii, word order variant, less common), नहीं साहब (nahiin saahab, more formal/deferential)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly from shopkeepers, auto drivers, students addressing teachers, and juniors addressing seniors -- it is the polite refusal of daily Delhi-NCR life, like saying 'no, sir' or 'no, ma'am' without needing to specify gender. It pairs naturally with its opposite जी हाँ (jii haan, 'yes, sir/ma'am'), and learning both together is the efficient way to absorb this politeness register.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often think जी (jii) here means 'yes' because जी हाँ (jii haan) exists as its counterpart -- but जी (jii) itself is just a respect marker, not a yes/no word; the actual answer comes from हाँ (haan) or नहीं (nahiin). 2) Using जी नहीं (jii nahiin) with close friends or peers can sound oddly formal or even sarcastic -- it belongs with elders, strangers, teachers, or service contexts.
Sample Sentences:
जी नहीं, मुझे चाय नहीं चाहिए। No, sir/ma'am, I do not want tea.
(jii nahiin, mujhe chaay nahiin chaahie।)
