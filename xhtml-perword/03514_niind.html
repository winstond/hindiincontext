नींद (niind)

noun A feminine noun meaning 'sleep,' almost always paired with आना (aanaa, 'to come') to describe sleep arriving to a person; also extends to a couple of fixed idiomatic uses.
Sense 1 : sleep (the state or need for it)
noun, feminine Almost always appears with a dative-marked experiencer plus आना (aanaa): मुझे नींद आ रही है (mujhe niind aa rahii hai, 'I am feeling sleepy'). नींद (niind) itself is just the noun 'sleep'; it is not a verb.
Synonyms: निद्रा (nidraa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic example of Hindi's 'body/mind as receiver' pattern: sleep, hunger, anger, and even love are things that arrive to you (आना, aanaa) rather than things you actively produce. मुझे नींद आ रही है (mujhe niind aa rahii hai, 'I'm getting sleepy') is far more natural than any construction where 'I' is the grammatical agent of sleep.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to say 'I sleep' with नींद (niind) directly as a subject doing an action, e.g. मैं नींद करता हूँ (main niind kartaa hoon) -- wrong; Hindi treats sleep as something that comes to you, so the natural frame is मुझे नींद आती है (mujhe niind aatii hai) or simply सोना (sonaa, 'to sleep') as the verb. 2) Don't confuse नींद (niind, the noun 'sleep') with सोना (sonaa, the verb 'to sleep') -- they are not interchangeable in a sentence frame.

Sense 2 : a period of sleep, nap, or dream-state (idiomatic/extended uses)
noun, feminine Seen in fixed expressions like नींद टूटना (niind tootnaa, 'sleep to break/be interrupted') and नींद उड़ना (niind udnaa, 'to lose one's sleep [from worry]', literally 'sleep to fly away').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Worry-driven sleeplessness is almost always phrased as नींद उड़ जाना (niind ud jaanaa, 'for sleep to fly away'), a vivid image Delhi families use constantly when talking about exam stress or a sick relative -- e.g. उसकी नींद उड़ गई है (uskii niind ud gaii hai, 'he/she has lost all sleep [from worry]').
Common Mistakes: 1) नींद उड़ना (niind udnaa) does not mean physically flying anywhere -- it means someone is so worried or anxious that they cannot sleep; learners sometimes take it too literally. 2) नींद टूटना (niind tootnaa, 'to wake up abruptly') uses टूटना (tootnaa, 'to break') rather than खुलना (khulnaa) -- both exist, but टूटना (tootnaa) specifically implies an unwanted, sudden interruption.
