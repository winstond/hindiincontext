जीतना (jiitnaa)

verb One core meaning: to win or to conquer. Both senses (winning a game/contest and conquering a place or heart) grow from the same root idea of overcoming something.
Sense 1 : to win (a game, match, election, prize)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: जीत हासिल करना (jiit haasil karnaa, compound phrase)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly during election season and cricket matches -- भारत मैच जीत गया (bhaarat maich jiit gayaa, 'India won the match') is a classic newsroom line, with जीत जाना (jiit jaanaa) adding a sense of finality or achievement to the plain verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैंने मैच जीता (mainne maich jiitaa, 'I won the match') correctly with ने (ne) marking since जीतना (jiitnaa) is transitive, but then forget object agreement and leave the verb in masculine singular even when the object is feminine, e.g. it should be मैंने बाज़ी जीती (mainne baazii jiitii, 'I won the bet'), not जीता (jiitaa).
Sample Sentences:
भारत मैच जीत गया। India won the match.
(bhaarat maich jiit gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to conquer or win over (a place, person, or heart)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: फ़तह करना (fatah karnaa, formal/historical), दिल जीतना (dil jiitnaa, fixed idiom for winning someone's heart)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in history lessons (राजा ने किला जीत लिया, raajaa ne kilaa jiit liyaa, 'the king conquered the fort') and equally in romance -- दिल जीतना (dil jiitnaa) is a phrase you will hear in countless film dialogues and songs about winning someone's affection, not just on a battlefield.
Common Mistakes: 1) दिल जीतना (dil jiitnaa, 'to win someone's heart') is a fixed idiom -- learners sometimes try to translate 'to win someone over' word for word with को (ko) instead of using this set phrase, e.g. उसने मेरा दिल जीत लिया (usne meraa dil jiit liyaa, 'he/she won my heart') rather than a literal calque.
Sample Sentences:
राजा ने किला जीत लिया। The king conquered the fort.
(raajaa ne kilaa jiit liyaa।)
