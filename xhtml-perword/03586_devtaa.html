देवता (devtaa)

noun Primarily means 'god/deity' in the Hindu pantheon sense; also used figuratively for a person of exceptional kindness or goodness.
Sense 1 : a god, deity
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भगवान (bhagvaan), ईश्वर (iishvar), सुर (sur, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi households often speak of देवता (devtaa) in the plural, casually, since Hinduism recognizes many gods (तैंतीस करोड़ देवता, taintiis karod devtaa, 'thirty-three crore gods' is a common saying about their sheer number). You will hear it in everyday phrases like घर के देवता (ghar ke devtaa, 'the household deities') referring to the gods a family worships at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use देवता (devtaa) interchangeably with भगवान (bhagvaan), but देवता (devtaa) usually refers to one of many gods in the pantheon (Indra, Agni, etc.), while भगवान (bhagvaan) is used for God in a more singular, supreme sense, especially in monotheistic-style speech. 2) The feminine counterpart is देवी (devii, goddess) -- देवता (devtaa) itself is grammatically masculine even when discussing deities in a general or plural sense.

(hindoo dharm men taintiis karod devtaa maane jaate hain।)

Sense 2 : a saintly, exceptionally kind person (figurative)
noun, masculine Used as a warm compliment about a real person's character, not about an actual deity.
Synonyms: फ़रिश्ता (farishtaa, used similarly for a kind person), भला मानस (bhalaa maanas)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear elders say of someone unusually patient or generous, वो तो देवता है (vo to devtaa hai, 'he's a saint'), the same warm exaggeration English speakers make with 'he's an angel' or 'a saint.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative use is almost always about someone's kindness or patience, e.g. calling a helpful stranger or a patient teacher देवता आदमी हैं (devtaa aadmii hain, 'he's an angel of a man') -- it does not imply any religious reverence toward that person.
