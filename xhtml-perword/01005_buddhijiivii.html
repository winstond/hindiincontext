बुद्धिजीवी (buddhijiivii)

noun/adjective A single-sense noun/adjective meaning 'intellectual' -- someone who lives by the mind (बुद्धि (buddhi, 'intellect') + जीवी (jiivii, 'one who lives by')). Often used in modern social/political commentary, sometimes with a wry or critical edge.
Sense 1 : intellectual, member of the educated/thinking class
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: विचारक (vichaarak, thinker), विद्वान (vidvaan, scholar)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word a lot on Indian news debate shows and in political arguments, often thrown around as a label for writers, professors, and commentators. Depending on who's speaking, it can be a genuine compliment or a pointed jab -- calling someone a 'बुद्धिजीवी' (buddhijiivii) in a heated argument can imply they're all talk and disconnected from real life, similar to how 'intellectual' can be used sarcastically in English too.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat this as a neutral synonym for 'educated person,' but in casual conversation it can carry a mildly sarcastic edge, implying someone who talks a lot about ideas but is out of touch with ground realities -- especially in phrases like तथाकथित बुद्धिजीवी (tathaakthit buddhijiivii, 'so-called intellectual'). 2) It's grammatically m/f invariant as a noun referring to a person of either gender, unlike many -ई ending nouns that shift with gender.

Sense 2 : intellectual (as a descriptor)
adjective Used before a noun to describe something as belonging to or characteristic of the intellectual class, e.g. बुद्धिजीवी वर्ग (buddhijiivii varg, 'the intellectual class').
Synonyms: वैचारिक (vaichaarik, ideological/intellectual in nature)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This adjectival use shows up often in phrases like बुद्धिजीवी वर्ग (buddhijiivii varg, 'intellectual class') in newspaper editorials and political speeches, framing intellectuals as a distinct social group rather than describing an individual trait.
