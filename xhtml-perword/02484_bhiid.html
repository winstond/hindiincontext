भीड़ (bhiid)

noun Mainly means a crowd or throng of people; also used figuratively for a chaotic press of people or things.
Sense 1 : crowd of people
noun, feminine
Synonyms: जमघट (jamghat, less common), हुजूम (hujoom)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Anyone who has tried to board a Delhi Metro at rush hour knows भीड़ (bhiid) in their bones -- it's the go-to word for the crush at a mela, a market, a bus stop, or outside a temple during a festival. You'll often hear भीड़ में मत जाओ (bhiid men mat jaao, 'don't go into the crowd') as a parental warning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to pluralize it as भीड़ें (bhiiden) to mean 'many people,' but native speakers usually say भीड़ (bhiid) itself or भारी भीड़ (bhaarii bhiid, 'a heavy/huge crowd') rather than pluralizing casually. 2) भीड़ (bhiid) takes है (hai) as a mass noun (singular agreement) even though it refers to many people -- भीड़ है (bhiid hai, 'there is a crowd'), not भीड़ हैं (bhiid hain).

(dillii metro men subah ke vaqt bahut bhiid hotii hai।)

Sense 2 : undifferentiated mass, herd-like anonymity (figurative)
noun, feminine Used in phrases like भीड़ का हिस्सा (bhiid kaa hissaa, 'part of the crowd') to talk about losing individuality within a group.
Synonyms: जनसमूह (janasmooh, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense shows up in reflective or philosophical talk -- someone might say भीड़ में खो जाना (bhiid men kho jaanaa, 'to get lost in the crowd') meaning to lose one's individual identity, a common theme in Hindi film dialogues and self-help style speech.
