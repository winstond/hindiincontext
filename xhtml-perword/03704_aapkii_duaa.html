आपकी दुआ (aapkii duaa)

phrase A polite formulaic phrase meaning 'your blessing/kind wishes', used as a modest response when someone credits you with success or well-being. Related to दुआ (duaa, 'prayer, blessing') and आप (aap, the respectful 'you').
Sense 1 : modest response crediting the listener's good wishes for one's own success/well-being
phrase Typically used as a stand-alone reply to a question like कैसे हो? (kaise ho?, 'how are you?') or as a tag after describing one's own good fortune, functioning as a polite deflection of credit rather than a literal statement about prayer.
Synonyms: आपकी मेहरबानी (aapkii meharbaanii, 'your kindness'), आपका आशीर्वाद (aapkaa aashiirvaad, 'your blessing', often used toward elders)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic humble comeback when someone says आप बहुत अच्छे लग रहे हैं (aap bahut achchhe lag rahe hain, 'you're looking very well') or asks after your health or success -- instead of taking credit, you deflect it graciously back to the asker's good wishes. It works beautifully with both elders and peers, and is one of those small phrases that makes an English speaker's Hindi suddenly sound native and courteous.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate this too literally as 'your prayer' and miss that it functions as a set modest reply, similar to saying 'I'm well, thanks to you' in English -- it is not really about religious prayer. 2) It is used regardless of the listener's actual religious practice; it is a social courtesy, not a devotional statement.
Sample Sentences:
सब आपकी दुआ है। It's all thanks to your blessing.
(sab aapkii duaa hai।)
