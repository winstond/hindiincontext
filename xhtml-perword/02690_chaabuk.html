चाबुक (chaabuk)

noun/adjective Primarily a physical noun meaning 'whip', with a common figurative adjective sense meaning 'sharp, quick-witted, alert'.
Sense 1 : whip
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कोड़ा (kodaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word in the context of tonga- and horse-drawn cart drivers flicking a चाबुक (chaabuk) to urge the animal on -- a scene still visible in older parts of Delhi and in many small towns. It also shows up often in historical dramas and political speeches as a symbol of oppression, as in 'चाबुक चलाना' (chaabuk chalaanaa, 'to wield the whip').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse चाबुक (chaabuk) with कोड़ा (kodaa); both mean 'whip' and are largely interchangeable, though चाबुक (chaabuk) is the more common word in everyday speech and in phrases about horses and tongas.

Sense 2 : sharp, quick-witted
adjective Used colloquially to describe a person's mind or reflexes, as in चाबुक दिमाग़ (chaabuk dimaagh, 'sharp mind').
Synonyms: तेज़ (tez), फुर्तीला (phurtiilaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense grows naturally out of the whip's snap -- something that acts fast and stings -- so a चाबुक (chaabuk) mind is one that reacts instantly, like the crack of a whip. You will hear friends praise someone as 'बड़ा चाबुक है' (badaa chaabuk hai, 'he's really sharp') in casual banter.
Common Mistakes: 1) This adjective use is casual and mostly heard in spoken Hindi about people's cleverness or speed of thinking -- it is not used for objects or situations the way तेज़ (tez) can be.
