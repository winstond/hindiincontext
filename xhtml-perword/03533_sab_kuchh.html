सब कुछ (sab kuchh)

pronoun A fixed pronoun phrase meaning 'everything, all of it' -- built from सब (sab, 'all') plus कुछ (kuchh, 'something'). It has a single core sense used constantly in everyday speech, and an idiomatic extension where it means 'the most important thing' to someone.
Sense 1 : everything, all of it
pronoun Grammatically invariant; behaves as a singular neuter-like pronoun and takes third-person singular verb agreement regardless of what it stands for.
Synonyms: सारा कुछ (saaraa kuchh, less common variant), हर चीज़ (har chiiz, 'every thing', slightly more itemized feel)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in daily life -- a shopkeeper asking अब सब कुछ ठीक है? (ab sab kuchh thiik hai, 'is everything fine now?'), or a mother reassuring a child सब कुछ ठीक हो जाएगा (sab kuchh thiik ho jaaegaa, 'everything will be fine'). It has a comforting, all-encompassing weight that English 'everything' carries too, but Hindi speakers reach for it more readily as a way to wrap up a whole situation in one word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to pluralize the verb with सब कुछ (sab kuchh), for example writing सब कुछ हैं (sab kuchh hain) instead of सब कुछ है (sab kuchh hai) -- it always takes singular agreement, since it is treated as one whole mass, not a collection of countable things. 2) Do not confuse with सभी (sabhii) or सब (sab) alone used for people/countable things ('everyone', 'all of them') -- सब कुछ (sab kuchh) is reserved for things/situations, not people.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे सब कुछ याद है। I remember everything.
(mujhe sab kuchh yaad hai।)

Sense 2 : the most important thing, one's all (in one's life)
pronoun Occurs in the frame X के लिए सब कुछ होना (X ke lie sab kuchh honaa, 'to be everything to X'), where सब कुछ (sab kuchh) functions as a predicate noun describing total emotional importance.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the line at the emotional heart of countless Hindi film dialogues and songs -- तुम मेरा सब कुछ हो (tum meraa sab kuchh ho, 'you are my everything') is the kind of declaration a lover, or a mother about her child, might make. It captures how सब कुछ (sab kuchh) can shift from a neutral quantifier to an intensely personal claim of total devotion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'you are my everything' too literally with तुम मेरा सब कुछ हो (tum meraa sab kuchh ho) -- this is actually correct and very natural, but learners often forget the possessive मेरा (meraa) is required before सब कुछ (sab kuchh) in this sense, unlike the plain 'everything' sense which needs no possessive.
Sample Sentences:
तुम मेरा सब कुछ हो। You are my everything.
