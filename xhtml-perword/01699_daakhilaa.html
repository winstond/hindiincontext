दाख़िला (daakhilaa)

noun This word means 'admission' or 'enrollment' -- most often used for schools, colleges, and courses. It's a Persian/Urdu-origin noun very common in everyday spoken Hindi, especially around exam and school-admission season.
Sense 1 : admission, enrollment (to a school/college/course)
noun, masculine Commonly used with लेना (lenaa, 'to take admission') and मिलना (milnaa, 'to get admission'), e.g. दाख़िला लेना (daakhilaa lenaa, 'to take admission') and दाख़िला मिलना (daakhilaa milnaa, 'to get admission').
Synonyms: प्रवेश (pravesh, slightly more formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every March-April in Delhi, you'll hear parents anxiously discussing दाख़िला (daakhilaa) season -- nursery admissions in particular are famously stressful, with long queues and point-based lottery systems at good schools. The word's Persian root (from Urdu دخل) is a good reminder of how deeply Persian vocabulary is woven into everyday bureaucratic and educational Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say दाख़िला करना (daakhilaa karnaa) by analogy with 'to do admission,' but the natural verbs are दाख़िला लेना (daakhilaa lenaa, 'to take admission') for the student's own action and दाख़िला देना (daakhilaa denaa, 'to grant admission') for the institution's action. 2) The person seeking admission is often marked with को (ko), as in उसे दाख़िला मिल गया (use daakhilaa mil gayaa, 'he got admission') -- learners sometimes try ने (ne) here by mistake since मिलना (milnaa) takes a dative subject, not an agentive one.
