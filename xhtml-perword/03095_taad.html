ताड़ (taad)

noun/verb Two distinct uses: the palm/palmyra tree (a concrete noun), and the colloquial verb-forming idiom ताड़ लेना (taad lenaa) meaning to sense or figure someone out.
Sense 1 : palm tree
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ताड़ का पेड़ (taad kaa ped, fuller form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ताड़ (taad) refers to the tall palmyra palm often seen lining village roads and temple grounds in parts of India; ताड़ी (taadii), the fermented sap tapped from it, is a well-known country liquor, so the tree carries a cultural association beyond just being a plant.

(gaanv kii sadak ke kinaare oonche taad ke ped khade the।)

Sense 2 : to sense, guess, figure out (someone's intention)
verb, transitive Almost always used in the compound form ताड़ लेना (taad lenaa) or ताड़ जाना (taad jaanaa), rarely as a bare verb.
Synonyms: समझ जाना (samajh jaanaa), भाँप लेना (bhaamp lenaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb sense is unrelated in meaning to the palm tree noun despite sharing the same root word; it is a lively, informal favorite in everyday Delhi speech, often used when someone catches on to a secret, a flirtation, or a trick, like ताड़ गया क्या? (taad gayaa kyaa?, 'did you catch on?').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for समझना (samajhnaa) in every context, missing that ताड़ लेना (taad lenaa) specifically implies catching someone's hidden intention or mood, often with a hint of cleverness or being caught red-handed, e.g. मैंने उसकी चाल ताड़ ली (mainne uskii chaal taad lii, 'I saw through his move'). 2) This sense is distinctly colloquial and should not be used in formal writing.
Sample Sentences:
मैं तुरंत ताड़ गया कि वो झूठ बोल रहा है। I immediately caught on that he was lying.
(main turant taad gayaa ki vo jhooth bol rahaa hai।)
