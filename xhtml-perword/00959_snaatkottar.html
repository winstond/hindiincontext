स्नातकोत्तर (snaatkottar)

adjective/noun A formal, education-register word meaning 'postgraduate' -- built from स्नातक (snaatak, 'graduate') plus उत्तर (uttar, 'later, after'), literally 'after-graduate'. Used mainly as an adjective before degree/course names, and occasionally as a noun for the degree or the person holding it.
Sense 1 : postgraduate (describing a degree, course, or level of study)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this word constantly on Indian university signage, admission forms, and job listings -- स्नातकोत्तर पाठ्यक्रम (snaatkottar paathyakram, 'postgraduate course') is bureaucratic-official vocabulary, the mirror image of स्नातक (snaatak, 'undergraduate/graduate'). In casual speech people often just say 'पोस्ट-ग्रेजुएशन' (post-grejueshan) or 'PG' instead, reserving स्नातकोत्तर for forms and formal writing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect it like a regular -आ adjective (e.g. treating it as स्नातकोत्तरा/स्नातकोत्तरे); it stays fixed regardless of the gender or number of the noun it modifies, e.g. स्नातकोत्तर डिग्री (snaatkottar digrii, 'postgraduate degree') and स्नातकोत्तर छात्र (snaatkottar chhaatr, 'postgraduate student'). 2) It is almost always used attributively right before the noun (course, degree, program) rather than as a standalone predicate describing a person's status -- for that, phrases with की डिग्री (kii digrii) or किया हुआ (kiyaa huaa) are more natural.

(yah snaatkottar paathyakram do saal kaa hai।)

Sense 2 : a postgraduate (person who holds a postgraduate degree)
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun to refer to the person, most often in official or descriptive contexts, e.g. listing someone's qualification.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This nominal use is less common than the adjectival one; you will more often hear it as part of a fuller phrase like स्नातकोत्तर उपाधिधारी (snaatkottar upaadhidhaarii, 'holder of a postgraduate qualification') in formal bios and CVs.
