काला (kaalaa)

adjective Primarily the color adjective 'black,' with several vivid figurative extensions -- illegality/corruption, darkness of mood, and skin tone -- that Hindi speakers reach for constantly in everyday talk.
Sense 1 : black (color)
adjective
Synonyms: श्याम (shyaam, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: काला (kaalaa) is one of the first color adjectives learners meet, right alongside सफ़ेद (safed, 'white') and लाल (laal, 'red'). It inflects like a normal -आ adjective: काला (kaalaa) चश्मा (chashmaa), काली (kaalii) बिल्ली (billii), काले (kaale) कपड़े (kapde).

Sense 2 : illegal, illicit, corrupt (of money, markets, activity)
adjective Almost always appears fused with a noun in set phrases: काला बाज़ार (kaalaa baazaar, 'black market'), काला धन (kaalaa dhan, 'black/illicit money').
Synonyms: गैरकानूनी (gairkaanoonii), अवैध (avaidh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense is inseparable from Indian public life -- news anchors, politicians, and ordinary people all talk about काला धन (kaalaa dhan) stashed abroad or a काला बाज़ारी (kaalaa baazaarii, 'black-marketeering') racket for scarce goods like cooking gas or movie tickets. The English word 'black' used this way is actually a direct borrowing of this Hindi/Indian-English usage.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'black money' or 'black market' word-for-word without realizing Hindi already has this exact calque built in -- काला धन (kaalaa dhan) and काला बाज़ार (kaalaa baazaar) are the natural, everyday terms, not awkward borrowings.

Sense 3 : dark, gloomy, ominous (of mood, day, fate)
adjective
Synonyms: अंधकारमय (andhkaarmay, literary), स्याह (syaah, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English speaks of a 'black day' or 'black mood,' Hindi uses काला (kaalaa) figuratively for misfortune -- काले दिन (kaale din, 'dark/bad days') is a common way to describe a rough patch in life, echoing how black signals mourning or dread across many cultures.

Sense 4 : dark-skinned, dark-complexioned
adjective
Synonyms: सांवला (saanvlaa, softer/more polite term)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Colorism runs deep in everyday Hindi speech -- families and matchmakers alike comment on being गोरा (goraa, 'fair') versus काला (kaalaa, 'dark') far more bluntly than most English speakers would find comfortable. As a learner, it's worth knowing this word exists and is common, while also sensing that सांवला (saanvlaa) is the kinder choice in polite company.
Common Mistakes: 1) Calling a person directly काला (kaalaa) or काली (kaalii) can sound blunt or even rude in front of them; सांवला (saanvlaa) or गोरा-सांवला (goraa-saanvlaa) are the gentler, more socially acceptable words for describing someone's complexion.
Sample Sentences:
वो थोड़ी काली है, पर बहुत सुंदर है। She's a bit dark-skinned, but she's very beautiful.
