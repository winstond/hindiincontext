जातीय (jaatiiy)

adjective An adjective meaning 'ethnic' or 'caste-related,' formed from जाति (jaati, 'caste/community/ethnicity') plus the adjectival suffix -ईय; the two senses split along which meaning of जाति (jaati) is in play.
Sense 1 : ethnic, relating to a people or community
adjective
Synonyms: नस्लीय (nasliiy, often used for race/ethnicity in a broader or political sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in Indian news coverage of communal or ethnic conflict, both within India and abroad -- जातीय संघर्ष (jaatiiy sangharsh, 'ethnic conflict') is a stock phrase you will see in headlines. It sits one register above everyday chat, at home in journalism and political commentary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for जातीय (jaatiiy) when they mean 'national' -- राष्ट्रीय (raashtriiy) is the word for that; जातीय (jaatiiy) stays at the level of community or ethnic group, not the whole nation. 2) In news Hindi, जातीय हिंसा (jaatiiy hinsaa, 'ethnic violence') and जातीय तनाव (jaatiiy tanaav, 'ethnic tension') are set collocations worth learning as chunks rather than building word by word.

Sense 2 : caste-related, pertaining to caste
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Caste (जाति, jaati) remains a live social and political reality in India, and this adjective surfaces in serious conversations about reservation policy, discrimination, and social justice -- not small talk. Knowing both this sense and the ethnic sense above matters because context alone tells you which जाति (jaati) is meant.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with जातिगत (jaatigat), which is actually the more common word for 'caste-based' in everyday and policy Hindi (जातिगत भेदभाव (jaatigat bhedbhaav, 'caste-based discrimination'), जातिगत आरक्षण (jaatigat aarakshan, 'caste-based reservation')); learners should recognize जातीय (jaatiiy) in this sense but lean on जातिगत (jaatigat) when producing it themselves.

(bhaarat men jaatiiy bhedbhaav aaj bhii ek gambhiir samasyaa hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Habitual -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
