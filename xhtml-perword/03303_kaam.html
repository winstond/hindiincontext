काम (kaam)

noun The core meanings are 'work/job/task' and, in a narrower everyday sense, 'use/purpose' (as in 'this is useful to me'); a separate, more restrained sense refers to sexual desire. All three share the same spelling but split cleanly by meaning.
Sense 1 : work, job, task
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कार्य (kaary, formal), धंधा (dhandhaa, colloquial, business/trade)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: काम (kaam) is one of the first words every learner needs -- क्या काम करते हो? (kyaa kaam karte ho, 'what work do you do') is a standard get-to-know-you question in Delhi. It also appears in the common brush-off कोई काम नहीं (koii kaam nahiin, 'no big deal, not important') and in घर का काम (ghar kaa kaam, 'housework').
Common Mistakes: 1) काम (kaam) is masculine, so past-tense agreement and adjectives go masculine: मेरा काम (meraa kaam), not मेरी काम. 2) काम करना (kaam karnaa, 'to do work') is the everyday phrase for 'to work' -- learners sometimes reach for काम होना (kaam honaa) or a literal 'to job' construction instead. 3) काम पर जाना (kaam par jaanaa, 'to go to work') uses पर (par), not को, which trips up learners expecting a destination-style को.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : use, purpose, utility
noun, masculine This sense typically appears in the dative-subject frame X के काम आना (ke kaam aanaa, 'to be of use to X') or काम का होना (kaam kaa honaa, 'to be useful').
Synonyms: फ़ायदा (faaydaa, benefit/advantage), उपयोग (upyog, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up constantly in practical advice: यह किताब तुम्हारे काम आएगी (yah kitaab tumhaare kaam aaegii, 'this book will be useful to you'). It is the same root idea as sense 1 -- work as something that 'serves' a purpose -- just extended metaphorically to objects and information.
Common Mistakes: 1) यह किसी काम का नहीं (yah kisii kaam kaa nahiin, 'this is useless') is a fixed idiom -- learners often try to translate 'useless' word-for-word instead of using this frame. 2) The construction X के काम आना (ke kaam aanaa) takes के, not को, which surprises learners used to को-marked recipients.

Sense 3 : sexual desire, lust
noun, masculine
Synonyms: वासना (vaasnaa, literary/formal), कामेच्छा (kaamechchhaa, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In classical thought काम (kaam) is one of the four purusharthas (life-goals) alongside धर्म (dharm), अर्थ (arth), and मोक्ष (moksh) -- so it carries a philosophical weight beyond mere 'lust' in Hindu tradition, roughly 'desire' as a legitimate part of life. Most learners will meet it in this dignified, textual context rather than in slang.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is far less common in casual speech than senses 1 and 2 -- learners should not assume every काम (kaam) they hear carries this meaning; context (often religious, philosophical, or literary) makes it clear. 2) It survives mainly in set compounds like कामदेव (kaamdev, the god of desire) rather than in everyday standalone use.

(kaam manushy ke chaar purushaarthon men se ek hai।)
