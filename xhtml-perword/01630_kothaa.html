कोठा (kothaa)

noun Primarily a noun meaning an upper room/terrace-room or a large room in a traditional house, but the word carries a strong secondary sense referring to a brothel or courtesan's establishment -- context makes clear which is meant.
Sense 1 : brothel, courtesan's house (historically, a place of music and dance patronized by nobility)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तवायफ़खाना (tavaayafkhaanaa, rare), चकला (chaklaa, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Historically the कोठा (kothaa) referred to the salons of courtesans (तवायफ़, tavaayaf) who were skilled in classical music and dance and patronized by nobility in cities like Lucknow and old Delhi; over time the word narrowed in popular usage to mean simply 'brothel,' losing much of that artistic association -- a shift you'll notice if you watch older versus newer films that use the word.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is the sense most learners pick up first from Hindi cinema (tawaif films set in old Lucknow or Delhi), but using कोठा (kothaa) casually about someone's house without context can be genuinely offensive -- it is not a neutral synonym for 'room' in modern usage unless context clearly supports that reading.

Sense 2 : upper room, large room, or terrace room in a traditional house
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कमरा (kamraa), छत का कमरा (chhat kaa kamraa, descriptive phrase)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: In old havelis and village houses across the Delhi-NCR belt, the कोठा (kothaa) was traditionally the upper room, often used for storage of grain or as a guest room -- you will still hear older people use it this way without any of the film-world connotation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often only know the brothel sense from films and are surprised to hear कोठा (kothaa) used innocently for an ordinary upper-floor room in older havelis and village houses -- context (who is speaking, what is being described) is everything here.
