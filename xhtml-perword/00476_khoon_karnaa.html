ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa)

verb A verb phrase meaning 'to murder, to kill' -- literally 'to do blood.' Built from ख़ून (khoon, 'blood/murder') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do').
Sense 1 : to murder, to kill (someone)
verb, transitive Takes the person killed marked with का (kaa) or की (kii) as part of a possessive-style object phrase -- ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa) pairs with X का (kaa) ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa, 'to murder X'), not a plain accusative object.
Synonyms: क़त्ल करना (qatl karnaa), हत्या करना (hatyaa karnaa, formal/written), मार डालना (maar daalnaa, less formal, 'to kill/do away with')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase is everywhere in Hindi crime dramas and newspaper headlines -- ख़ून (khoon, 'blood') stands in for the act of murder itself, much like English 'bloodshed' evokes violence. You'll often hear it paired with the compound verb form ख़ून कर दिया (khoon kar diyaa) using दे (de) to mark the finality and suddenness of the act -- a classic use of the completive compound verb construction.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to mark the victim directly with को (ko) as if ख़ून करना (khoon karnaa) worked like a normal transitive verb with a direct object -- instead the victim is expressed with का/की (kaa/kii) before ख़ून (khoon), e.g. उसका ख़ून कर दिया (uskaa khoon kar diyaa, 'he killed him'), literally 'did his blood.' 2) Don't confuse this with ख़ून बहाना (khoon bahaanaa, 'to shed blood'), which describes violence or bloodshed more broadly, not necessarily a completed killing.
Sample Sentences:
उसने अपने पड़ोसी का ख़ून कर दिया। He murdered his neighbor.
(usne apne padosii kaa khoon kar diyaa।)
