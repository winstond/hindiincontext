फेफड़ा (phephdaa)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'lung', almost always encountered in the plural फेफड़े (phephde) since humans have two; also used figuratively for stamina or loud voice.
Sense 1 : lung (the organ)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Doctors and health campaigns (anti-smoking ads, pollution warnings during Delhi's winter smog season) constantly use फेफड़े (phephde) -- it is one of the first body-organ words a learner in North India will hear outside a biology class, thanks to how often air quality comes up in daily conversation here.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use the singular फेफड़ा (phephdaa) when talking about a person's lungs in general; native speakers default to the plural फेफड़े (phephde) since we have two, e.g. मेरे फेफड़े कमज़ोर हैं (mere phephde kamzor hain, 'my lungs are weak'), even when the underlying point could be about lung health in a general sense.

(dhoomrpaan se phephde kharaab ho jaate hain।)

Sense 2 : stamina/lung capacity, loud voice (figurative)
noun, masculine Used in set phrases like फेफड़े फाड़ना/फाड़कर बोलना (phephde phaadnaa / phephde phaadkar bolnaa, 'to shout at the top of one's lungs') and फेफड़ों में दम होना (phephdon men dam honaa, 'to have the stamina/guts').
Synonyms: दम (dam, overlaps for stamina/breath)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: फेफड़े फाड़कर चिल्लाना (phephde phaadkar chillaanaa, 'to scream at the top of one's lungs') is a vivid, very physical image Hindi reaches for -- much like English 'lung power', but the Hindi phrase leans harder into the sense of the lungs literally tearing from effort, which gives it a slightly more dramatic, almost comic flavor in everyday storytelling.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is idiom-bound -- it does not extend freely; you can't casually say 'my lungs are strong' to mean 'I'm brave' outside these set expressions.

(vo phephde phaadkar chillaayaa, magar aavaaz bhiid ke shor men kho gaii।)
