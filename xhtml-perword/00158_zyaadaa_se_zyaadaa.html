ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा (zyaadaa se zyaadaa)

adverb A fixed comparative frame built from ज़्यादा (zyaadaa, 'more/much') repeated with से (se) in between; used to mean 'at most' (a ceiling) or 'to the greatest extent possible' (a maximizing push), both senses being everyday and closely related.
Sense 1 : at most, at the maximum (upper limit)
adverb Typically precedes a quantity, time, or number to cap it: zyaadaa se zyaadaa दस मिनट (zyaadaa se zyaadaa das minat, 'at most ten minutes').
Synonyms: अधिकतम (adhiktam, formal), बस इतना (bas itnaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear shopkeepers and auto-rickshaw drivers use this constantly when quoting a limit -- ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा पचास रुपये (zyaadaa se zyaadaa pachaas rupye, 'fifty rupees at most') -- it is the go-to phrase for capping an estimate in everyday bargaining.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes place it after the number instead of before, e.g. saying दस मिनट ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा (das minat zyaadaa se zyaadaa) instead of the natural ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा दस मिनट (zyaadaa se zyaadaa das minat) -- native speakers put the phrase first as a ceiling marker.

Sense 2 : to the greatest possible extent, as much as possible
adverb Used to push a quality or effort to its maximum, often with an adjective or verb: ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा कोशिश करो (zyaadaa se zyaadaa koshish karo, 'try as hard as possible').
Synonyms: यथासंभव (yathaasambhav, formal), जितना हो सके (jitnaa ho sake)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in encouragement and advice -- a teacher or parent saying ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा पढ़ो (zyaadaa se zyaadaa padho, 'study as much as you can') -- the doubling of ज़्यादा (zyaadaa) itself gives the sense of pushing toward a maximum, a common Hindi pattern of repeating a word with से (se) to intensify it.
