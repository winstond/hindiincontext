मुश्किल (mushkil)

adjective/noun Mainly an adjective meaning 'difficult' and a feminine noun meaning 'difficulty, trouble' -- the two senses share the same word and are used together constantly, e.g. talking about a मुश्किल (mushkil) situation versus facing a मुश्किल (mushkil) itself.
Sense 1 : difficult, hard
adjective
Synonyms: कठिन (kathin), दुष्कर (dushkar, literary, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear मुश्किल है (mushkil hai) constantly in everyday Delhi speech as a stand-alone remark, roughly 'that's tough' or 'good luck with that,' often with a small head-shake or sigh -- it carries a resigned, sympathetic tone rather than sounding clinical like 'difficult' can in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect मुश्किल (mushkil) like a regular -आ adjective; it is invariant and stays मुश्किल (mushkil) with masculine, feminine, singular, and plural nouns alike. 2) With काम (kaam) or बात (baat), Hindi often prefers the construction X करना मुश्किल है (X karnaa mushkil hai, 'it is difficult to do X') over trying to directly translate an English 'difficult thing.'

Sense 2 : difficulty, trouble, problem
noun, feminine
Synonyms: परेशानी (pareshaanii), दिक्कत (dikkat), तकलीफ़ (takliif)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मुश्किल से (mushkil se) is a very handy phrase meaning 'barely, with great effort,' as in मुश्किल से पहुँचा (mushkil se pahunchaa, 'I barely made it') -- it's one of those little adverbial idioms that instantly makes your Hindi sound more native than a literal word-for-word translation would.
Common Mistakes: 1) As a noun मुश्किल (mushkil) is feminine, so it takes feminine agreement in phrases like बड़ी मुश्किल (badii mushkil, 'a big difficulty') even though the adjective सense of the same word never inflects at all -- learners sometimes carry the invariance of the adjective over to the noun by mistake. 2) मुश्किल में होना (mushkil men honaa, 'to be in trouble') and मुश्किल से (mushkil se, 'with difficulty, barely') are set phrases worth learning as whole units rather than building word by word.
