बाँधना (baandhnaa)

verb The core physical verb 'to tie/bind', with common extended senses covering securing something in place and figuratively binding through emotion, obligation, or organization.
Sense 1 : to tie, bind (with rope, string, cloth, etc.)
verb, transitive Takes an object with से (se) marking the instrument/material used to tie: रस्सी से बाँधना (rassii se baandhnaa, 'to tie with a rope').
Synonyms: जोड़ना (jodnaa, to join/attach, not identical but overlaps for tying together)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a kite string being tied to a spool, or a buffalo tied to a post outside a village home -- this is the most physical, literal sense and the one learners meet first.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use से (se) where को (ko) is needed for the thing being tied to a fixed point -- compare पेड़ से बाँधना (ped se baandhnaa, 'to tie to a tree') where से (se) marks the anchor, not the material, so context decides the role of से (se) each time.

Sense 2 : to fasten, wrap, put on (something worn wrapped around the body)
verb, transitive Used for items that are wrapped or wound rather than simply worn, e.g. पगड़ी बाँधना (pagdii baandhnaa, 'to tie a turban') or पट्टी बाँधना (pattii baandhnaa, 'to bandage').
Synonyms: पहनना (pahannaa, to wear, broader and used for clothes generally)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: A father tying his son's पगड़ी (pagdii, 'turban') before a wedding, or a mother tying a rakhi (raakhii) on her brother's wrist -- this sense is deeply tied to ceremony and daily dressing alike.

Sense 3 : to bind emotionally, form an attachment or bond
verb, transitive Common in reflexive-feeling constructions like रिश्ते में बाँधना (rishte men baandhnaa, 'to bind in a relationship') and in the passive/compound form बँधना (bandhnaa) for 'to become bound/attached'.
Synonyms: जोड़ना (jodnaa, to connect, broader emotional sense too)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi treats emotional connection as a kind of tying -- दिल बाँधना (dil baandhnaa) or रिश्ता बाँधना (rishtaa baandhnaa) both picture the heart or relationship as something literally knotted together, the same imagery English keeps only in fossilized phrases like 'tie the knot.'

Sense 4 : to compose or set (a tune, rhythm, or verse)
verb, transitive Used with musical or poetic objects: धुन बाँधना (dhun baandhnaa, 'to compose a tune'), ताल बाँधना (taal baandhnaa, 'to set a rhythm').
Synonyms: रचना (rachnaa, to compose, more literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will hear this from musicians and singers -- a tabla player 'ties' the ताल (taal, 'rhythm') the way a tailor ties a knot, framing composition as a kind of careful binding together of notes.

Sense 5 : to hold or fix (attention, hope, expectation) on something
verb, transitive Common in fixed collocations like आशा बाँधना (aashaa baandhnaa, 'to pin one's hopes') and निगाहें बाँधना (nigaahen baandhnaa, 'to fix one's gaze').
Synonyms: टिकाना (tikaanaa, to fix/rest, close overlap)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same 'binding' logic extended once more -- your hopes or your eyes get tied onto a target, so you cannot look away or stop expecting, much like a boat tied to a dock.
