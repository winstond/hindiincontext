आदी (aadii)

adjective आदी (aadii) is an adjective meaning 'habituated' or 'used to' something, always paired with की (kii) marking the thing one is accustomed to (आदी होना, aadii honaa).
Sense 1 : accustomed, habituated, used to (something)
adjective Takes the pattern X की आदी/आदी होना (kii aadii honaa, 'to be used to X') -- the thing one is habituated to is marked with की (kii), not से (se) as an English speaker might guess by analogy with 'used to.'
Synonyms: अभ्यस्त (abhyast, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll often hear this in the context of adjusting to new environments -- मैं यहाँ के मौसम की आदी हो गई हूँ (main yahaan ke mausam kii aadii ho gaii hoon, 'I have gotten used to the weather here') is the kind of thing a newcomer to Delhi says after their first monsoon or winter fog. It pairs very naturally with होना (honaa) and पड़ना (padnaa) to express the process of becoming accustomed, not just the state.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the habituated-to noun with से (se) by analogy with English 'used to something,' but Hindi requires की (kii): आदत की आदी (aadat kii aadii) not आदत से आदी. 2) Confusing this with आदत (aadat, 'habit,' a noun) -- आदी (aadii) describes the person, आदत (aadat) names the habit itself.
Sample Sentences:
मुझे सुबह जल्दी उठने की आदत है, मैं इसका आदी हूँ। I'm used to waking up early in the morning.
(mujhe subah jaldii uthne kii aadat hai, main iskaa aadii hoon।)
