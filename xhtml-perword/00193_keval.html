केवल (keval)

adverb A single-sense adverb meaning 'only, merely, just' -- a slightly more formal/written cousin of सिर्फ़ (sirf).
Sense 1 : only, merely, just (restricting a quantity, item, or scope)
adverb Placed directly before the word or phrase it restricts, just like सिर्फ़ (sirf): केवल दस रुपये (keval das rupye, 'only ten rupees').
Synonyms: सिर्फ़ (sirf), मात्र (maatr, formal/literary), बस (bas, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see केवल (keval) a lot on official signage and forms -- 'केवल सदस्यों के लिए' (keval sadasyon ke lie, 'only for members') outside clubs or reserved train compartments -- while सिर्फ़ (sirf) dominates everyday spoken Hindi. Knowing both lets you read formal notices and still sound natural when talking.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to सिर्फ़ (sirf) in speech and forget केवल (keval) exists; both are correct, but केवल (keval) sounds a touch more formal or written, like something you'd see on a sign or hear in a news report rather than casual chat. 2) Word order mistake: it must go right before the item it limits, not at the end of the sentence as 'only' sometimes can in English.

(yah tikat keval aaj ke lie maany hai।)
