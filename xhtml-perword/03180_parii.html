परी (parii)

noun A noun meaning 'fairy' -- both the literal magical being of folk stories and, very commonly, an affectionate way to call someone (especially a girl) beautiful or angelic.
Sense 1 : fairy (mythical/folkloric being)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: अप्सरा (apsraa, celestial nymph, mythological)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: परी (parii) is the stock character of Hindi fairy tales -- think of the winged, beautiful being who grants wishes or gets trapped by a jinn in a बच्चों की कहानी (bachchon kii kahaanii, 'children's story'). Grandmothers telling bedtime stories reach for this word constantly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes equate परी (parii) directly with 'angel' -- an angel in the religious sense is usually फ़रिश्ता (farishtaa), while परी (parii) belongs more to fairy-tale (jinn/fairy-tale) imagery than to religious cosmology.

Sense 2 : a beautiful or angelic girl/woman (affectionate term)
noun, feminine Used vocatively or descriptively to praise someone's looks or sweetness, e.g. calling a little girl or a loved one मेरी परी (merii parii, 'my little angel').
Synonyms: हसीना (hasiinaa, beautiful woman), गुड़िया (gudiyaa, term of endearment for a girl, lit. 'doll')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear Delhi parents and grandparents call a little girl मेरी परी (merii parii) all the time -- it carries the same doting warmth as English 'my little angel,' with no religious weight attached.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is almost always applied to females; using it for a man sounds odd or joking rather than complimentary. 2) It is warm and affectionate, not flirtatious by default -- fathers call daughters this all the time, so it is not restricted to romantic contexts.
