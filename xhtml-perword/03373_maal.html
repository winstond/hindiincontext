माल (maal)

noun Primarily a noun meaning goods/merchandise/wealth (माल maal), with a very common colloquial extension to 'stuff' -- including slang for drugs or an attractive person; also survives in a few fixed idioms.
Sense 1 : goods, merchandise, cargo
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सामान (saamaan), वस्तु (vastu, more formal, singular-item usage)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask a shopkeeper दुकान में माल कब आएगा (dukaan men maal kab aaegaa, 'when will the stock arrive at the shop')? and everyone understands you mean fresh inventory. You will also hear it shouted at loading docks and mandis where trucks are being loaded with माल (maal).
Common Mistakes: 1) माल (maal) leans commercial/bulk -- shopkeepers, traders, and truck drivers talk about माल (maal) arriving or being loaded, whereas सामान (saamaan) is the everyday word for personal belongings or household items; swapping them sounds odd in context.

Sense 2 : wealth, riches, money (as property)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धन (dhan), दौलत (daulat), सम्पत्ति (sampatti, formal, especially real estate/assets)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The set phrase माल-असबाब (maal-asbaab, 'belongings/property') and the proverb-like use of माल (maal) alongside जान (jaan, 'life') -- as in जान-माल (jaan-maal, 'life and property') -- show up constantly in news reports about accidents, riots, or disasters.

Sense 3 : colloquial: drugs, contraband, or an attractive person (slang)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चीज़ (chiiz, slang, also used for an attractive person)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Bollywood-flavored slang, कैसा माल है (kaisaa maal hai, 'what a hottie') is a phrase you will hear in edgy dialogue or street banter, but it is objectifying and best understood passively rather than used casually by a learner.
Common Mistakes: 1) This slang sense is entirely tone-dependent -- the same word that means innocent 'merchandise' at a shop can mean 'drugs' or comment on someone's looks on the street; learners should recognize it but use it cautiously since it can come across as crude or objectifying, especially when applied to a person.
