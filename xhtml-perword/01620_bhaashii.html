भाषी (bhaashii)

adjective/noun A bound suffix-like adjective meaning 'speaking (a language)' or 'speaker of', almost always attached after a language name to describe who speaks it, e.g. हिंदी-भाषी (hindii-bhaashii, Hindi-speaking/Hindi speaker).
Sense 1 : speaking (a given language); belonging to speakers of that language
adjective Almost always used compounded directly after a language name (no space or a hyphen), as in अंग्रेज़ी-भाषी (angrezii-bhaashii, English-speaking) or पंजाबी-भाषी (panjaabii-bhaashii, Punjabi-speaking); it does not stand alone as a free adjective.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see this constantly in Indian news and census talk -- हिंदी-भाषी राज्य (hindii-bhaashii raajy, 'Hindi-speaking states'), तमिल-भाषी लोग (tamil-bhaashii log, 'Tamil-speaking people') -- since language is such a live marker of regional identity in India. It pairs naturally with भाषा (bhaashaa, 'language'), from which it derives.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to use भाषी (bhaashii) as a standalone word meaning 'speaker' -- it almost never appears without a language name attached before it. 2) It is easy to confuse with भाषा (bhaashaa, 'language') itself; भाषी (bhaashii) describes the person/group, not the language.

Sense 2 : a speaker of a given language (as a person/category)
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun when used substantively, e.g. मराठी-भाषी (maraathii-bhaashii, 'a Marathi speaker') referring to the person rather than describing another noun.
Synonyms: बोलनेवाला (bolnevaalaa, more literal 'one who speaks')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The line between the adjective and noun use is soft in practice -- हिंदी-भाषी (hindii-bhaashii) can mean 'Hindi-speaking' (describing a state or region) or 'a Hindi speaker' (a person) depending on context, much like 'English speaker' works in English.
