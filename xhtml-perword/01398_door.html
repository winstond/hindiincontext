दूर (door)

adjective/adverb/verb Mainly an adjective/adverb meaning 'far, distant', with an important idiomatic extension in the compound verb दूर करना (door karnaa, 'to remove/get rid of') and दूर होना (door honaa, 'to be resolved/go away').
Sense 1 : far, distant (physical or figurative distance)
adjective
Synonyms: दूरस्थ (doorasth, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: दूर (door) is one of the first spatial words learners meet, often paired with पास (paas, 'near') as a natural opposite pair when giving directions or describing distance in daily conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to inflect दूर (door) like a regular -आ adjective; it is invariant and never changes form for gender or number. 2) Distance 'from' a place uses से (se), as in स्कूल से दूर (skool se door, 'far from school'), not से दूर होना with का (kaa) as some learners guess.
Sample Sentences:
मेरा घर स्कूल से बहुत दूर है। My house is very far from school.
(meraa ghar skool se bahut door hai।)

Sense 2 : far away, at a distance (used adverbially with motion/location verbs)
adverb Functions adverbially in phrases like दूर जाना (door jaanaa, 'to go far away') or दूर रहना (door rahnaa, 'to stay away'), where दूर modifies the verb rather than a noun.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear parents and elders say बुरी आदतों से दूर रहो (burii aadton se door raho, 'stay away from bad habits') -- दूर रहना (door rahnaa) is a very common piece of everyday advice-giving in Hindi households.
Common Mistakes: 1) दूर रहना (door rahnaa) meaning 'to stay away/keep distance' is often used figuratively for avoiding people, habits, or trouble, not just physical distance -- learners sometimes miss this extended sense.

Sense 3 : to remove, get rid of, dispel (a problem, doubt, pain, etc.)
verb, transitive Used as दूर करना (door karnaa), a compound verb where दूर supplies the meaning and करना (karnaa) carries the grammar; takes ने (ne) marking in the perfective like any transitive verb with करना (karnaa).
Synonyms: मिटाना (mitaanaa), खत्म करना (khatm karnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will often hear this in advice or advertising: गरीबी दूर करना (gariibii door karnaa, 'to eliminate poverty') or शक दूर करना (shak door karnaa, 'to clear up a doubt') -- it is the go-to verb for 'solving' an intangible problem in Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) दूर करना (door karnaa) is used for abstract removal -- worries, doubts, poverty, darkness -- much more often than for physically moving an object away; for physically moving something, हटाना (hataanaa) is the natural choice instead.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 4 : to be removed, resolved, or to go away (of a problem, doubt, illness, etc.)
verb, intransitive Used as दूर होना (door honaa); the subject is typically an abstract noun like गलतफहमी (galatphahmii, 'misunderstanding'), दर्द (dard, 'pain'), or शक (shak, 'doubt') rather than a physical object moving away.
Synonyms: मिटना (mitnaa), समाप्त होना (samaapt honaa, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the natural intransitive partner of दूर करना (door karnaa) below: उसकी परेशानी दूर हो गई (uskii pareshaanii door ho gaii, 'his worry went away') frames the problem as something that departs on its own, a common Hindi pattern of describing change as happening *to* the subject.
Common Mistakes: 1) दूर होना (door honaa) is intransitive and takes no ने (ne) marking even though its transitive counterpart दूर करना (door karnaa) does -- learners sometimes carry ने over by mistake.
Sample Sentences:
