मगर (magar)

conjunction/noun A common conjunction meaning 'but'; also has a less common noun sense meaning 'crocodile' (usually written मगर (magar) as well, though मगरमच्छ (magarmachchh) is the everyday word for the animal).
Sense 1 : but, however
conjunction
Synonyms: लेकिन (lekin), पर (par, shorter, very common), किंतु (kintu, more formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear मगर (magar) constantly in everyday arguments and Bollywood dialogues, often carrying a little extra feeling, like someone pausing before delivering the 'but' that changes everything. It sits alongside पर (par) and लेकिन (lekin) as one of three near-synonyms every learner ends up using daily.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat मगर (magar) and लेकिन (lekin) as fully interchangeable, which is true in most sentences, but मगर (magar) can sound slightly more emphatic or emotional in speech, almost like 'but still...'. 2) पर (par) is the go-to casual choice in fast conversation; overusing मगर (magar) in every sentence can sound a bit heavy or dramatic.

Sense 2 : crocodile
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मगरमच्छ (magarmachchh, everyday word for the animal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: As a standalone noun for the animal, मगर (magar) is much less common in daily speech than the compound मगरमच्छ (magarmachchh); you will meet it mostly in folk tales, riverside place names, or set phrases rather than casual conversation about zoos.
