हर (har)

adjective/noun Mainly the everyday adjective meaning 'every, each' (har); it also appears as a bound element in a few fixed words meaning 'defeat' and shows up in the divine name हर (har, an epithet of Shiva), which is literary/devotional rather than conversational.
Sense 1 : every, each
adjective Always precedes a singular noun even though the meaning is distributive/plural in sense, e.g. हर दिन (har din, 'every day'), हर बार (har baar, 'every time').
Synonyms: प्रत्येक (pratyek, formal), सब (sab, meaning scope: 'all' rather than 'each individually')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear हर (har) constantly in daily talk -- हर रोज़ (har roz, 'every day'), हर जगह (har jagah, 'everywhere'), हर कोई (har koii, 'everyone') -- it's one of the first 'every/each' words learners should own cold, well before प्रत्येक (pratyek), which feels bookish by comparison.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often pluralize the noun after हर (har), saying हर दिनों (har dinon) instead of the correct हर दिन (har din) -- the noun stays singular. 2) हर एक (har ek, 'each and every one') is a common intensified variant; learners sometimes use it interchangeably with हर (har) when just 'every' is meant, which sounds slightly emphatic/redundant in plain contexts.

(main har roz subah yog kartaa hoon।)

Sense 2 : defeat, loss (in a fixed expression)
noun, masculine Survives mainly in the fixed pair हार-जीत (haar-jiit, 'win and loss') and in हर-जीत (har-jiit) as an alternate rendering; the everyday word for 'defeat' as a standalone noun is हार (haar), not हर (har).
Synonyms: हार (haar, variant form/related noun; more common in this sense)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense is quite marginal in modern conversation -- it's really the shadow of हार (haar) surviving in a compound; don't spend much active-vocabulary effort on हर (har) as 'defeat' itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is easy to confuse with हार (haar, 'defeat' or 'necklace') -- learners should default to हार (haar) for 'defeat' in normal speech and treat हर (har) in this sense as something they'll mainly recognize in the compound हार-जीत (haar-jiit) rather than produce on its own.

Sense 3 : Shiva (an epithet)
noun, masculine Used as a proper-noun epithet, chiefly in devotional or literary contexts, e.g. हर हर महादेव (har har mahaadev, 'Hail Shiva, the great god') as a devotional chant.
Synonyms: शिव (shiv), शंकर (shankar)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in the chant हर हर महादेव (har har mahaadev) at Shiva temples and during festivals like Mahashivratri -- it's a name every Hindi speaker recognizes instantly even if they never use हर (har) this way in daily conversation.
