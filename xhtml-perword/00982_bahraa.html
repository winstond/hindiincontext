बहरा (bahraa)

adjective/noun An adjective/noun meaning 'deaf'; describes a person who cannot hear, and by extension can be used figuratively for someone who won't listen.
Sense 1 : deaf (unable to hear)
adjective
Synonyms: बधिर (badhir, formal/medical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In daily conversation, calling someone बहरा (bahraa) to their face can come across as impolite -- much like calling someone 'deaf' bluntly in English -- so people often soften it or switch to बधिर (badhir) in polite company. You will also hear it in the common scolding phrase कान में बहरा हो गया है क्या? (kaan men bahraa ho gayaa hai kyaa, 'have your ears gone deaf or what?') aimed at someone who isn't listening.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners forget this adjective inflects like कड़ा (kadaa) -- बहरा (bahraa) for masculine singular, बहरी (bahrii) for feminine, बहरे (bahre) for plural/oblique -- rather than staying invariant. 2) बधिर (badhir) is the word used in official, medical, or polite contexts (e.g. signage, schools for the deaf); बहरा (bahraa) is the everyday spoken word but can sound blunt or even rude if used directly to or about someone in a formal setting.

Sense 2 : a deaf person
noun, masculine/feminine Used as a noun by dropping any following noun, e.g. वह बहरा है (vah bahraa hai, 'he is a deaf person/he is deaf') -- the same word doubles as noun and adjective, as with many Hindi adjectives of this type.
Synonyms: बधिर (badhir, formal/medical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear this paired with गूँगा (goongaa, 'mute') in the set phrase बहरा-गूँगा (bahraa-goongaa), used both literally and, jokingly, for someone who neither listens nor speaks up when they should.

Sense 3 : figuratively deaf; willfully not listening
adjective Commonly used in the extended sense of 'turning a deaf ear,' often in the phrase बहरे कानों तक (bahre kaanon tak, 'to deaf ears') describing pleas or advice that go unheeded.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same emotional move English makes with 'falling on deaf ears' -- Hindi speakers say किसी की बात बहरे कानों तक पहुँचना (kisii kii baat bahre kaanon tak pahunchnaa, 'for someone's words to reach deaf ears') to describe advice or complaints that are completely ignored, often about a stubborn family member or an unresponsive official.
