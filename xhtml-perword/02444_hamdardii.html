हमदर्दी (hamdardii)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'sympathy' or 'compassion' for another's suffering; built from हम (ham, 'same/co-') plus दर्द (dard, 'pain') plus the abstract-noun suffix ी.
Sense 1 : sympathy, compassion, empathy for someone's suffering
noun, feminine Commonly used with जताना (jataanaa, 'to express') or दिखाना (dikhaanaa, 'to show') and takes से (se) marking the person it's felt for, as in किसी से हमदर्दी (kisii se hamdardii, 'sympathy for someone').
Synonyms: सहानुभूति (sahaanubhooti), करुणा (karunaa, more about pity/compassion), दयालुता (dayaalutaa, focuses on kindness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The word's build is lovely and transparent once pointed out: हम (ham) means 'shared' or 'together' and दर्द (dard) means 'pain' -- so हमदर्दी (hamdardii) is literally 'shared-pain-ness,' the feeling of hurting along with someone else. It's a common, warm, everyday word you'll hear used about a friend's troubles, a neighbor's loss, or in the context of political rhetoric about the poor.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use हमदर्दी (hamdardii) interchangeably with सहानुभूति (sahaanubhooti), but हमदर्दी (hamdardii) carries a warmer, more personal, almost 'fellow-feeling' tone (from हम, 'shared'), while सहानुभूति (sahaanubhooti) can sound more formal or clinical. 2) The person you sympathize with is marked with से (se), not के लिए (ke lie) -- so it's उससे हमदर्दी है (usse hamdardii hai, 'I feel sympathy for him'), not उसके लिए हमदर्दी.

मुझे उसके हालात पर बहुत हमदर्दी है। I feel a lot of sympathy for his situation.
(mujhe uske haalaat par bahut hamdardii hai।)
