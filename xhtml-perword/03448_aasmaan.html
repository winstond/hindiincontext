आसमान (aasmaan)

noun Means 'sky.' Mostly literal, but it also anchors a handful of common figurative expressions about vastness, height, or extreme states.
Sense 1 : sky, heavens (the physical sky overhead)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आकाश (aakaash), गगन (gagan, literary), नभ (nabh, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: आसमान (aasmaan) is the word you'll hear in weather talk -- आसमान में बादल हैं (aasmaan men baadal hain, 'there are clouds in the sky') -- and in monsoon-season chatter across Delhi-NCR balconies. आकाश (aakaash) sits a notch higher in register, more common in writing, school textbooks, and place names.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for आकाश (aakaash) as the default, but in everyday speech आसमान (aasmaan) is the word people actually say when pointing up at clouds, rain, or stars.

Sense 2 : figurative: boundless height, greatness, or an extreme/unreachable degree
noun, masculine Used mainly inside fixed expressions -- e.g. आसमान छूना (aasmaan chhoonaa, 'to touch the sky', meaning to soar or achieve greatly) and आसमान सर पर उठाना (aasmaan sar par uthaanaa, 'to lift the sky onto one's head', meaning to make an uproar/commotion).
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ambitious parents and coaches love आसमान छूना (aasmaan chhoonaa) -- 'touch the sky' -- as a way to talk about a child's or team's potential. On the flip side, आसमान सर पर उठाना (aasmaan sar par uthaanaa) is what you say about neighbors having a loud fight or kids making a racket -- literally 'lifting the sky onto your head' with noise.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost always appears bound inside a set phrase with a specific verb (छूना (chhoonaa), उठाना (uthaanaa), टूटना (tootnaa)) -- swapping in a different verb won't sound natural to a native speaker.
