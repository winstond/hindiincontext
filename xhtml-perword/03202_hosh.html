होश (hosh)

noun Primarily means 'consciousness/senses' (as in being awake and aware, or in one's right mind) and by extension appears in common idioms about awareness, composure, and sanity.
Sense 1 : consciousness, senses (physical awareness)
noun, masculine/feminine Almost always used with verbs like आना (aanaa, 'to come [back]') or खोना (khonaa, 'to lose') to describe gaining or losing consciousness -- होश आना (hosh aanaa, 'to regain consciousness'), होश खोना (hosh khonaa, 'to lose consciousness').
Synonyms: चेतना (chetnaa, more formal/clinical), सुध (sudh)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture someone fainting in the Delhi heat during summer and their friends splashing water on their face saying होश में आओ (hosh men aao, 'come back to consciousness') -- this is the everyday physical sense, distinct from the more figurative 'sanity/composure' sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) होश (hosh) is grammatically plural-only in this sense (it has no singular form in use) -- learners sometimes try to treat it as a regular singular noun and get the agreement wrong. 2) Don't confuse with साँस (saans, 'breath') -- होश (hosh) is about mental awareness, not breathing, even though both appear in 'fainting' contexts.

Sense 2 : presence of mind, composure, senses (figurative -- being sane/alert/in control)
noun, masculine/feminine Common in the fixed phrase होश में रहना/आना (hosh men rahnaa, 'to stay/come to one's senses') used scoldingly or warningly, and होश उड़ जाना (hosh ud jaanaa, 'to be shocked out of one's wits').
Synonyms: समझ (samajh, understanding/sense), अकल (akal, practical sense, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear a Delhi mother snap होश में आओ, क्या कर रहे हो? (hosh men aao, kyaa kar rahe ho, 'come to your senses, what are you doing?') at a child about to do something reckless -- this figurative sense of होश (hosh) is really about mental clarity and self-control, closely tied to the literal consciousness sense above but extended to judgment and composure.
Common Mistakes: 1) होश उड़ जाना (hosh ud jaanaa) literally means 'senses to fly away' but is used for shock or fear, not literal fainting -- learners sometimes take it too literally. 2) होश में आओ (hosh men aao) can be an angry rebuke ('wake up and think straight!') rather than a gentle request, depending on tone -- register matters here.
