नाश्ता करना (naashtaa karnaa)

verb A common verb-noun collocation meaning 'to have breakfast/a snack' -- नाश्ता (naashtaa) is the food item and करना (karnaa) is the light verb that turns it into an action. Only one sense: eating breakfast or a light snack.
Sense 1 : to have breakfast / to snack
verb, transitive
Synonyms: नाश्ता खाना (naashtaa khaanaa, less common variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian homes, नाश्ता (naashtaa) is a substantial affair -- think पराठे (paraathe, 'stuffed flatbreads'), पोहा (pohaa), or आलू के पराठे (aaloo ke paraathe, 'potato-stuffed flatbreads') with चाय (chaay) -- so a Delhi mother asking बेटा, नाश्ता कर लो (betaa, naashtaa kar lo, 'son, go have your breakfast') is a near-daily household refrain. The word also stretches informally to cover a mid-afternoon snack, unlike the more meal-bound English 'breakfast'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat नाश्ता (naashtaa) as the direct object needing ने-marking on the subject in the past tense, which is correct here since करना (karnaa) is transitive -- e.g. मैंने नाश्ता किया (mainne naashtaa kiyaa, 'I had breakfast') -- but they forget that नाश्ता (naashtaa) being masculine singular controls the verb agreement (किया (kiyaa), not की (kii)), which surprises those used to feminine nouns like रोटी (rotii) in similar food collocations. 2) नाश्ता (naashtaa) is not limited to Western-style breakfast foods -- it covers any light morning meal or between-meal snack, so learners shouldn't assume it always means cereal-and-toast type food.
Sample Sentences:
बेटा, नाश्ता कर लो, स्कूल के लिए देर हो रही है। Son, go have your breakfast, you're getting late for school.
(betaa, naashtaa kar lo, skool ke lie der ho rahii hai।)
