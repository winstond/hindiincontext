सुधारना (sudhaarnaa)

verb A transitive verb meaning 'to correct, fix, or improve' something -- a mistake, a situation, a habit, or a broken object. Related to the intransitive/passive-leaning verb सुधरना (sudharnaa, 'to improve, mend oneself'), which describes the same change happening on its own or to the subject itself.
Sense 1 : to correct or fix a mistake/error
verb, transitive
Synonyms: ठीक करना (thiik karnaa), दुरुस्त करना (durust karnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word your teacher uses when marking homework: गलतियाँ सुधारो (galtiyaan sudhaaro, 'correct the mistakes') is a classic red-pen instruction in Indian classrooms.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सुधरना (sudharnaa) when they mean सुधारना (sudhaarnaa) -- सुधरना (sudharnaa) is what happens when something improves by itself or someone reforms themselves, while सुधारना (sudhaarnaa) is used when you actively correct or fix something/someone else. 2) With ने (ne) marking, remember this verb is transitive, so the past tense takes ने (ne) on the subject: उसने गलती सुधारी (usne galtii sudhaarii, 'he/she corrected the mistake').

Sense 2 : to improve or fix a situation, system, or object
verb, transitive
Synonyms: बेहतर बनाना (behtar banaanaa), मरम्मत करना (marammat karnaa, for physical objects)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in civic and political talk constantly -- हालात सुधारना (haalaat sudhaarnaa, 'to improve the situation') or व्यवस्था सुधारना (vyavasthaa sudhaarnaa, 'to fix the system') are staples of news debates and neighborhood chai-time complaints about the government.

Sense 3 : to reform or correct someone's behavior/character
verb, transitive
Synonyms: सही रास्ते पर लाना (sahii raaste par laanaa, to set right/reform)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic worried-parent verb -- उसे सुधारना पड़ेगा (use sudhaarnaa padegaa, 'he'll have to be straightened out') is something you might overhear about a child who's fallen into bad company. It carries a firm, almost disciplinary tone, distinct from gentler words for guidance.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense takes a person as the object, which can feel presumptuous in English but is completely natural in Hindi: बेटे को सुधारना (bete ko sudhaarnaa, 'to reform/correct one's son') -- the को (ko) marks the person being corrected, not a location or recipient.

(maan-baap hameshaa yahii sochte hain ki ve apne bachche ko sudhaar sakte hain।)
