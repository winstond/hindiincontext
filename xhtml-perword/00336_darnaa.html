डरना (darnaa)

verb A basic intransitive verb meaning 'to fear, to be afraid.' Its causative partner डराना (daraanaa, 'to scare, frighten') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to be afraid, to fear
verb, intransitive Takes से (se) for the thing feared: किसी चीज़ से डरना (kisii chiiz se darnaa, 'to fear something'), not को.
Synonyms: भयभीत होना (bhaybhiit honaa, formal), घबराना (ghabraanaa, to be nervous/scared -- overlapping but not identical), सहमना (sahamnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Indian parents love to weaponize this verb: मत डरो, कुछ नहीं होगा (mat daro, kuchh nahiin hogaa, 'don't be afraid, nothing will happen') is a classic reassurance line at the doctor's or dentist's office. Note the family link to डराना (daraanaa, 'to frighten someone') -- the causative you'd use for a scary movie or a threatening uncle.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for को (ko) by analogy with dative-subject verbs like लगना, but डरना (darnaa) marks the object of fear with से (se): साँप से डरना (saamp se darnaa, 'to be afraid of a snake'), not साँप को डरना. 2) डरना (darnaa) is intransitive and does not take ने (ne) in the perfective: वह डरा (vah daraa, 'he got scared'), not उसने डरा.

Sense 2 : to be intimidated by, to be in awe of (someone's authority)
verb, intransitive Same से (se)-marked frame as the core sense, but used for social/hierarchical fear rather than physical danger: बॉस से डरना (bos se darnaa, 'to be intimidated by the boss').
Synonyms: डरना-सहमना (darnaa-sahamnaa, compound idiom form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is less about literal fear and more about respect-tinged wariness -- the kind of feeling Indian kids describe having for a strict teacher or a stern grandfather. It overlaps with the core sense enough that some teachers wouldn't split it, but the emotional register (deference, not danger) is distinct enough to flag for a learner building real conversational instinct.
