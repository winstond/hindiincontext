होना (honaa)

verb होना (honaa) is Hindi's core verb 'to be' -- it covers plain copula/identity statements, existence and location, events happening, change of state ('become'), a dative-subject use for feelings and ailments, and a very common presumptive/inferential mood built on its future tense. Compare रहना (rahnaa, 'to stay/reside') and बनना (bannaa, 'to become/be made').
Sense 1 : to be (identity/description, copula)
verb, intransitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind the very first Hindi sentences learners build: यह मेरी किताब है (yah merii kitaab hai, 'this is my book'), मैं ठीक हूँ (main thiik hoon, 'I am fine'). Its present-tense forms (हूँ/है/हैं) are so central that they get memorized before almost anything else.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to exist, be present, be located
verb, intransitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: होना (honaa) is also the backbone of possession sentences like उसके पास पैसा है (uske paas paisaa hai, 'he has money') -- Hindi frames having as something existing near you, not something you 'hold.'

Sense 3 : to happen, occur, take place
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: घटना (ghatnaa, formal/written)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense constantly around weddings, meetings, and daily plans -- शादी कब होगी? (shaadii kab hogii?, 'when's the wedding [happening]?') is a classic nosy-relative question.
Common Mistakes: 1) क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?, 'what happened?') is the everyday way to ask what's wrong or what occurred -- learners often overreach for more elaborate phrasing when this simple question is what native speakers actually use. 2) Scheduling and events lean on this sense too: पार्टी कल होगी (paartii kal hogii, 'the party is [happening] tomorrow') -- not a verb like 'करना.'

Sense 4 : to become (change of state)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: बनना (bannaa, for deliberate/effortful transformation)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Mothers everywhere say बड़े हो जाओगे तो समझोगे (bade ho jaaoge to samjhoge, 'you'll understand once you've grown up') -- होना (honaa) here quietly carries the whole idea of maturing over time.
Common Mistakes: 1) होना (honaa) and बनना (bannaa) both translate as 'become' but split differently: बड़ा होना (badaa honaa, 'to grow up') and ठीक होना (thiik honaa, 'to recover/get well') describe natural change, while डॉक्टर बनना (doktar bannaa, 'to become a doctor') describes a deliberately achieved role -- mixing them up is a common learner slip.

Sense 5 : to happen to someone; to come over (dative-subject: feelings, illness)
verb, intransitive Takes a को-marked experiencer subject rather than a normal subject: मुझे खुशी हुई (mujhe khushii huii, 'I felt happy', lit. 'happiness happened to me'), मुझे बुखार हुआ (mujhe bukhaar huaa, 'I got a fever').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same body-as-recipient logic behind नींद आना (niind aanaa, 'sleep comes'): Hindi treats feelings and sicknesses as events that arrive and happen to you, not actions you perform.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मैं खुश हुआ (main khush huaa) when native speakers would say मुझे खुशी हुई (mujhe khushii huii) -- emotions and ailments 'happen to' you (को + होना) rather than being something you actively 'become.'

Sense 6 : must be / probably is (presumptive/inferential mood)
verb, intransitive Built on होना's future forms (होगा/होगी/होंगे), used either as the main verb -- वह घर पर होगा (vah ghar par hogaa, 'he must be/is probably at home') -- or as an auxiliary on another verb's perfective participle to guess about the past -- उसने खाना खाया होगा (usne khaanaa khaayaa hogaa, 'he must have eaten'). The tense form is future, but the meaning is a present-or-past guess, not literal future time.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is Hindi's built-in 'must be' modal -- no extra word needed, just ride the future tense of होना (honaa) itself. Delhi speakers use this constantly for everyday guesses: बारिश होगी (baarish hogii, 'it's probably raining') said while glancing at a cloudy sky, not planning ahead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners default to reading होगा as plain future 'will be' and miss that in context it often means 'probably is/must be right now' -- क्या टाइम होगा? (kyaa taaim hogaa?, 'what time might it be?') is a guess, not a question about a future clock. 2) For past inference, learners reach for शायद (shaayad, 'maybe') plus a plain past tense instead of the more natural participle+होगा pattern, e.g. preferring शायद उसने खाना खाया over the smoother उसने खाना खाया होगा (usne khaanaa khaayaa hogaa).
Sample Sentences:
बाहर बारिश हो रही होगी। It must be raining outside.
(baahar baarish ho rahii hogii।)
