स्थायी (sthaayii)

adjective/noun An adjective meaning 'permanent, lasting, stable'; it also functions as a noun for the recurring refrain/first line of a song, especially in classical or semi-classical music.
Sense 1 : permanent, lasting
adjective
Synonyms: पक्का (pakkaa), टिकाऊ (tikaaoo, especially of things/materials), चिरस्थायी (chirasthaayii, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will meet this word constantly in job contexts -- नौकरी पक्की (naukrii pakkii) is the everyday version of 'permanent job,' but स्थायी नौकरी (sthaayii naukrii) is what appears on offer letters and in government notices, since it carries a more official, written register. It is also the opposite of अस्थायी (asthaayii, 'temporary'), a pairing worth learning together since both show up on forms, contracts, and news reports alike.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पक्का (pakkaa) in casual speech where स्थायी (sthaayii) is the word actually used in formal or official contexts, like नौकरी स्थायी है (naukrii sthaayii hai, 'the job is permanent'). 2) Don't confuse with स्थिर (sthir), which means 'stable/still/unmoving' (like a stable price or a steady hand) rather than 'permanent in duration'.

Sense 2 : refrain/recurring line of a song
noun, masculine Refers to the fixed opening line/theme of a khayal, bhajan, or film song that returns between verses (antara); a technical term from Hindustani classical and semi-classical music.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense comes straight from classical music theory: a bandish (composition) is built from a स्थायी (sthaayii) -- the anchoring opening phrase -- and an अंतरा (antraa), the contrasting verse that follows. If you've ever noticed a Hindi film song circle back to its opening line after each verse, that returning line is functioning exactly like a स्थायी (sthaayii). It's a word you'll mostly meet if you get into music, not everyday conversation.
