लीपना (liipnaa)

verb A traditional verb meaning to smear or plaster a surface, most often with mud, cow dung, or a similar paste -- the everyday word for the age-old rural practice of coating a floor or wall this way.
Sense 1 : to plaster/smear (a floor, wall, etc.) with mud or dung
verb, transitive
Synonyms: पोतना (potnaa), थोपना (thopnaa, to slap on/apply thickly)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This word carries the image of a village courtyard at dawn, a woman bent over smoothing wet cow-dung-and-mud paste (gobar) across a mud floor with her bare hand -- a scene still common in rural North India and one every Hindi speaker recognizes even if they've only seen it in films or at a grandparent's house. It pairs naturally with पोतना (potnaa) in the common phrase लीपना-पोतना (liipnaa-potnaa, 'to plaster and coat'), used for general housekeeping upkeep of a mud house.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for पोतना (potnaa) as a casual synonym for painting, but लीपना (liipnaa) specifically evokes the traditional mud/dung-plaster technique, not painting with a brush and color. 2) The object is the surface being coated (the floor, the wall), not the mud itself -- so a sentence like फर्श को लीपना (pharsh ko liipnaa, 'to plaster the floor') takes फर्श (pharsh) as the direct object, with the coating material marked separately, often with से (se).
