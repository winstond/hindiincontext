उल्लू (ulloo)

noun Literally 'owl'; the far more common everyday use is figurative, calling someone a fool or dupe, and it also anchors the idiom उल्लू बनाना (ulloo banaanaa, 'to make a fool of someone').
Sense 1 : fool, dupe, simpleton (used as an insult/tease about a person)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), गधा (gadhaa, also an animal-insult, 'donkey'), मूर्ख (moorkh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi banter: दोस्तों में उल्लू के पट्ठे कहना (doston men ulloo ke patthe kahnaa, 'calling each other 'you owl's kid' among friends') is completely normal and even affectionate, much like calling a friend an 'idiot' in English. The tone -- teasing versus insulting -- depends entirely on context and relationship, so listen for how it's said before using it yourself.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a real insult, not a mild joke like English 'silly goose' -- calling a stranger or elder उल्लू (ulloo) can genuinely offend, though it's used affectionately among close friends or family. 2) Learners sometimes miss that the common expression is उल्लू का पट्ठा (ulloo kaa patthaa, 'son of an owl,' i.e. 'you fool') rather than उल्लू alone, which is also heard but hits differently -- the पट्ठा version is more colloquial and playful.

Sense 2 : owl (the bird)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Unlike in English folklore, the owl in Hindi/Indian culture is not wise -- it is seen as dim-witted and easily fooled at night, which is exactly why the word doubles as an insult. This negative association is also why उल्लू (ulloo) is considered the vaahan (mount) of Lakshmi in some folk depictions, yet still carries a comic, foolish image in daily speech rather than a wise one.
