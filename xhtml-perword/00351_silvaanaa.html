सिलवाना (silvaanaa)

verb The causative of सीना (siinaa, 'to sew') -- means 'to have (something) sewn/stitched,' i.e. getting a tailor to do it for you.
Sense 1 : to have (clothes etc.) stitched by someone else
verb, transitive Doubly causative in structure: सीना (siinaa, 'to sew') -> सिलाना (silaanaa, 'to have sewn') -> सिलवाना (silvaanaa, 'to get someone to have it sewn, i.e. via a tailor'). In practice सिलाना and सिलवाना are used almost interchangeably by native speakers for 'getting clothes tailored,' with सिलवाना being the more common everyday choice.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word comes up constantly around festivals and weddings, when families take fabric to a दर्जी (darjii, 'tailor') to get outfits सिलवाना (silvaanaa) rather than buying ready-made. Saying कपड़े सिलवाने हैं (kapde silvaane hain, 'clothes need to be gotten stitched') is a very ordinary thing to hear before a big occasion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सीना (siinaa) when they mean 'get stitched,' but if a tailor is doing the work rather than the speaker, सिलवाना (silvaanaa) is the natural choice: मैंने कुर्ता सिलवाया (mainne kurtaa silvaayaa, 'I got a kurta stitched'), not मैंने कुर्ता सिला (mainne kurtaa silaa) unless you sewed it yourself. 2) The agent (the tailor) is marked with से (se): दर्जी से सिलवाया (darjii se silvaayaa, 'got it stitched by the tailor'), not को (ko).

मुझे शादी के लिए कपड़े सिलवाने हैं। I need to get clothes stitched for the wedding.
(mujhe shaadii ke lie kapde silvaane hain।)
