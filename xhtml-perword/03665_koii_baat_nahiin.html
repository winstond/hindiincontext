कोई बात नहीं (koii baat nahiin)

phrase A fixed, everyday expression used to dismiss an apology or brush off a minor problem -- the closest match to English 'no problem' or 'never mind'.
Sense 1 : no problem, never mind, it's okay
phrase Functions as a stand-alone social formula, not a literal statement -- it does not describe an actual 'matter' but performs the act of reassuring someone.
Synonyms: कोई फ़र्क नहीं (koii fark nahiin, 'no difference/matters not'), ठीक है (thiik hai, 'it's fine'), चलता है (chaltaa hai, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the reflexive, warm reply you'll hear the instant someone says सॉरी (sorii) or माफ़ कीजिए (maaf kiijie) -- said quickly, often with a small wave of the hand, to smooth over a bumped shoulder, a spilled cup of chai, or someone arriving late. It carries genuine social warmth in Delhi-NCR: waving away an apology this way is considered gracious, not just polite.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too literally in their heads as 'there is no matter,' missing that it is a fixed formula, like 'no worries,' used specifically in response to an apology, a mistake, or an offer of thanks. 2) It is not used to mean 'it doesn't matter' in the sense of indifference to a choice -- for that, कोई फ़र्क नहीं पड़ता (koii fark nahiin padtaa) is more natural. 3) Saying this in response to bad news (rather than an apology or minor mishap) can sound dismissive rather than comforting -- it's calibrated for small stuff, not real problems, where सब ठीक हो जाएगा (sab thiik ho jaaegaa) fits better.
Sample Sentences:
सॉरी, मुझे थोड़ी देर हो गई। कोई बात नहीं, बैठो। Sorry, I'm a little late. No problem, sit down.
(sorii, mujhe thodii der ho gaii। koii baat nahiin, baitho।)
