तख़्त (takht)

noun A borrowed noun meaning a raised wooden platform or seat -- ranging from a simple wooden plank/cot to a royal throne. One core meaning, with the 'throne' sense being an extension used in political and historical contexts.
Sense 1 : wooden platform, plank-bed, or divan
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चौकी (chaukii), पलंग (palang, for a bed-like तख़्त (takht))
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of the low wooden platform you still see outside old Delhi shops or in a village courtyard -- a तख़्त (takht) is where the shopkeeper sits cross-legged, or where the family gathers in summer evenings. It's a very tactile, everyday word for a piece of household or shop furniture.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse with तख़्ती (takhtii), which is a small board/plaque/nameplate, not furniture to sit or lie on -- तख़्त (takht) is bigger and meant for sitting, sleeping, or displaying goods (like a shopkeeper's wooden platform).

Sense 2 : throne (seat of royal or political power)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राजगद्दी (raajgaddii), सिंहासन (sinhaasan, more formal/literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense in the fixed phrase तख़्त पर बैठना (takht par baithnaa, 'to ascend the throne') -- common in history lessons, news about monarchies, and political commentary where 'तख़्त' (takht) stands for supreme power itself, much like 'the throne' does in English.
