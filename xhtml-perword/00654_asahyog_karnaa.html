असहयोग करना (asahyog karnaa)

verb A compound built from असहयोग (asahyog, 'non-cooperation') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means to refuse cooperation, most famously in the historical/political sense of Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement, but also usable for everyday refusal to cooperate.
Sense 1 : to refuse to cooperate, to boycott/withhold cooperation (esp. politically)
verb, intransitive Takes से (se) to mark the person or authority being refused cooperation, e.g. सरकार से असहयोग करना (sarkaar se asahyog karnaa, 'to non-cooperate with the government').
Synonyms: बहिष्कार करना (bahishkaar karnaa, boycott -- stronger, implies active rejection), विरोध करना (virodh karnaa, oppose -- broader, not specifically about withholding cooperation)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase is inseparable in the Indian mind from the असहयोग आन्दोलन (asahyog aandolan, Non-Cooperation Movement) of 1920-22, when Gandhi urged Indians to withdraw cooperation from British institutions -- schools, courts, jobs, titles. Even today, calling something असहयोग (asahyog) rather than विरोध (virodh) carries a faint echo of that Gandhian, disciplined, non-violent register, so it tends to appear in political and civic contexts more than in casual complaints about people.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use के साथ (ke saath) where से (se) is the natural choice with this verb -- सरकार से असहयोग किया (sarkaar se asahyog kiyaa) sounds more native than सरकार के साथ असहयोग किया (sarkaar ke saath asahyog kiyaa). 2) This is almost always used about organized or deliberate withholding of cooperation, not casual unhelpfulness -- for a colleague who just won't help with a small task, native speakers would more likely say मदद नहीं करना (madad nahiin karnaa, to not help) rather than reaching for this heavier term.

1920 में गांधीजी ने अंग्रेज़ों से असहयोग करने की अपील की। In 1920, Gandhiji appealed to people to non-cooperate with the British.
(1920 men gaandhiijii ne angrezon se asahyog karne kii apiil kii।)
