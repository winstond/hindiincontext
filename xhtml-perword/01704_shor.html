शोर (shor)

noun A single-sense everyday noun meaning 'noise' or 'racket' -- covers everything from traffic din to a classroom getting loud, and appears in several common fixed phrases.
Sense 1 : noise, din, racket
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हल्ला (hallaa), शोर-शराबा (shor-sharaabaa, emphatic/compound form), आवाज़ (aavaaz, meaning scope: general sound, not necessarily unwanted)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Any Delhi parent will say बच्चों, शोर मत करो (bachchon, shor mat karo, 'kids, don't make noise') on a near-daily basis -- शोर मचाना (shor machaanaa) is the go-to verb pairing, using मचाना (machaanaa, 'to stir up/create a commotion') rather than a plain 'to make.' You'll also hear it in civic contexts like ध्वनि प्रदूषण (dhvani pradooshan, 'noise pollution') debates about Delhi traffic and loudspeakers, though that compound uses ध्वनि (dhvani) rather than शोर (shor) in the formal term itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) शोर (shor) is used with मत करो (mat karo, 'don't make') or मचाना (machaanaa, 'to kick up') more often than with बनाना (banaanaa) -- learners sometimes reach for बनाना (banaanaa) by analogy with English 'make noise,' but शोर मचाना (shor machaanaa, 'to kick up a racket') is the natural collocation. 2) शोर (shor) is uncountable in the way English 'noise' is -- there's no natural plural use for 'several noises'; a specific sound is आवाज़ (aavaaz) instead.

बच्चों, शोर मत करो! Kids, don't make noise!
(bachchon, shor mat karo!)
