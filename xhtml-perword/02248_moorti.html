मूर्ति (moorti)

noun Mainly means a statue or idol (as in a temple or museum), with an extended sense of 'embodiment/personification' of a quality.
Sense 1 : statue, idol
noun, feminine
Synonyms: प्रतिमा (pratimaa, formal), बुत (but)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the neighborhood mandir (mandir) with its मूर्ति (moorti) of Ganesh or Durga, carefully dressed and garlanded -- the word carries a sense of a crafted, honored form, not just any carved object. During Durga Puja or Ganesh Chaturthi, you will hear मूर्ति विसर्जन (moorti visarjan, 'immersion of the idol') on the news, which is the ritual immersion of these clay idols in a river or tank.
Common Mistakes: 1) मूर्ति (moorti) is not limited to religious idols -- a garden statue, a museum sculpture, or a leader's statue in a park is also मूर्ति (moorti). 2) Learners sometimes reach for बुत (but) as the default word, but मूर्ति (moorti) is the everyday, unmarked term in Hindi.

Sense 2 : embodiment, personification (figurative)
noun, feminine Used in fixed phrases like शांति की मूर्ति (shaanti kii moorti, 'the embodiment of peace') to say someone is a living picture of a quality.
Synonyms: प्रतीक (pratiik, symbol/emblem, slightly different shade)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in praise or eulogy -- calling someone सादगी की मूर्ति (saadgii kii moorti, 'the picture of simplicity') is a classic, slightly formal compliment, the kind an uncle might pay a respected elder at a family function.
