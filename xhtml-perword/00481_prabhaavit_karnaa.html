प्रभावित करना (prabhaavit karnaa)

verb A compound verb built from the borrowed adjective प्रभावित (prabhaavit, 'affected/influenced') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'); it means 'to affect' or 'to influence' something or someone.
Sense 1 : to influence, to affect (someone's thinking, behavior, decisions)
verb, transitive The person or thing influenced is the direct object, marked with को (ko) when it is a specific person: उसे प्रभावित करना (use prabhaavit karnaa, 'to influence him/her').
Synonyms: असर करना (asar karnaa), असर डालना (asar daalnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This verb shows up constantly in news, essays, and school compositions -- 'फिल्मों का युवाओं पर प्रभाव' type discussions -- so it has a slightly bookish, exam-answer flavor even though everyone understands and uses it. In warm daily chatter, Delhi folks are more likely to say किसी पर असर डालना (kisii par asar daalnaa) or दिल पर असर करना (dil par asar karnaa) for emotional influence.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for असर करना (asar karnaa) or असर पड़ना (asar padnaa) instead, which are more common in casual speech; प्रभावित करना (prabhaavit karnaa) sounds slightly more formal or written, so overusing it in chatty conversation can sound stiff. 2) Since करना (karnaa) is transitive, the subject takes ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने मुझे प्रभावित किया (usne mujhe prabhaavit kiyaa, 'he/she influenced me') -- learners sometimes forget ने (ne) here because प्रभावित (prabhaavit) itself feels adjective-like.

(vigyaapan bachchon ko bahut prabhaavit karte hain।)

Sense 2 : to affect, to have an impact on (physically, materially, or in outcome)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: असर डालना (asar daalnaa), नुक़सान पहुँचाना (nuqsaan pahunchaanaa, narrower: to harm)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this sense constantly in news reports -- बारिश ने फसल को प्रभावित किया (baarish ne phasal ko prabhaavit kiyaa, 'the rain affected the crop') -- it is the go-to verb for cause-and-effect statements in journalism and official language.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate English 'affect' too literally in emotional contexts (like being moved to tears) where native speakers prefer छू जाना (chhoo jaanaa, 'to touch/move') or दिल पर असर करना (dil par asar karnaa) rather than प्रभावित करना (prabhaavit karnaa), which leans toward measurable or logical impact (health, economy, results) rather than raw emotion.
