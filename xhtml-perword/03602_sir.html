सिर (sir)

noun Primarily 'head' (the body part), with a rich extension into idioms about burden, responsibility, and stubbornness; also used for 'top/summit' of an object.
Sense 1 : head (body part)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सर (sar, variant form), शीश (shiish, literary), मस्तक (mastak, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: सिर दर्द (sir dard, headache) is the everyday complaint word, and you'll hear it constantly in Indian households, often followed by someone reaching for a बाम (baam, medicated balm) like Amrutanjan or Vicks. Head-touching gestures also carry weight here: touching an elder's feet while they place a hand on your सिर (sir) is a common blessing gesture.
Common Mistakes: 1) सिर (sir) and सर (sar) are both common in speech; सर (sar) is not the same word as सर meaning 'Sir' (English loanword used for teachers/bosses) -- context makes it clear, but learners sometimes confuse them in writing. 2) Body-part idioms with सिर (sir) often use दर्द (dard) and दुखना (dukhnaa) rather than direct 'my head hurts' phrasing borrowed from English.
Sample Sentences:

(meraa sir dard kar rahaa hai।)

Sense 2 : burden, responsibility (figurative 'on one's head')
noun, masculine Occurs almost exclusively in the fixed frame X के सिर (ke sir) होना/आना/डालना -- 'to fall on X's head/shoulders,' i.e., to become X's responsibility or burden.
Synonyms: जिम्मेदारी (jimmedaarii, different register, more literal 'responsibility')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in family contexts: सारा काम उसके सिर पड़ गया (saaraa kaam uske sir pad gayaa, 'all the work fell on him/her') is a classic complaint about being overloaded, especially the eldest child or daughter-in-law in a household.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कंधों पर (kandhon par, on the shoulders) by analogy with English 'on someone's shoulders,' but Hindi idiom puts the burden on the सिर (sir), not the shoulders. 2) The postposition is के सिर (ke sir), not पर सिर (par sir) -- it patterns like a possessive-plus-locative, not a simple 'on' construction.
Sample Sentences:

(saaraa kaam uske sir pad gayaa।)

Sense 3 : top, summit, tip (of an object)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चोटी (chotii, mainly for mountains/peaks), ऊपरी हिस्सा (ooprii hissaa, more literal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This extension is natural and mirrors English 'head' of a pin or table -- पहाड़ के सिर पर (pahaad ke sir par, 'at the head/top of the mountain') is a fairly literary usage, while more concrete objects like a nail or pin also take सिर (sir) for their tip in everyday speech.
