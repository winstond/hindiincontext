सेवा (sevaa)

noun Mainly means 'service' in the sense of caring for or attending to someone, and by extension 'service' in a job/professional or devotional sense; also appears in common compound expressions.
Sense 1 : care/attending to someone
noun, feminine Often used with करना (karnaa) as सेवा करना (sevaa karnaa, 'to serve/take care of'), taking की (kii) before the object: किसी की सेवा करना (kisii kii sevaa karnaa, 'to take care of someone').
Synonyms: देखभाल (dekhbhaal), टहल (tahal, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense sits at the heart of Indian family life -- बुज़ुर्गों की सेवा (buzurgon kii sevaa, 'service to elders') is a phrase every Hindi-speaking child grows up hearing, tied to the idea that looking after aging parents is a duty and a virtue, not a burden.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मदद (madad, 'help') when सेवा (sevaa) is meant -- सेवा carries a stronger sense of devoted, ongoing care (like tending to an elderly parent or a sick person), not a one-off favor. 2) The verb pairing is सेवा करना (sevaa karnaa), not सेवा देना (sevaa denaa), when talking about personally attending to someone.

Sense 2 : professional/institutional service
noun, feminine Common in compounds like सरकारी सेवा (sarkaarii sevaa, 'government service') and ग्राहक सेवा (graahak sevaa, 'customer service').
Synonyms: नौकरी (naukrii, job context), सर्विस (sarvis, colloquial English loan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this sense everywhere in formal contexts -- job ads for सरकारी सेवा (sarkaarii sevaa) are a national obsession in India because of the stability and prestige government jobs carry.

Sense 3 : devotional/religious service
noun, feminine Used for acts of devotion to a deity or guru, as in ईश्वर की सेवा (iishvar kii sevaa, 'service/devotion to God') or गुरु सेवा (guru sevaa, 'service to one's teacher').
Synonyms: पूजा (poojaa, meaning overlaps only in devotional contexts), भक्ति (bhakti, devotion, broader sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In gurdwaras, सेवा (sevaa) specifically means the selfless volunteer work devotees do -- cooking in the langar, cleaning, serving food -- and this sense of सेवा as spiritual practice through humble action is one of the most beautiful ideas in Indian religious life.
