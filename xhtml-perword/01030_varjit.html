वर्जित (varjit)

adjective A formal, Sanskrit-derived adjective meaning 'forbidden' or 'prohibited' -- used mainly in signage, rules, and formal announcements rather than casual speech.
Sense 1 : forbidden, prohibited
adjective Common in the fixed public-notice frame X वर्जित है (X varjit hai, 'X is forbidden'), as seen on signs.
Synonyms: मना (manaa), निषिद्ध (nishiddh, formal/literary), प्रतिबंधित (pratibandhit, formal, used for official bans)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see वर्जित (varjit) everywhere on signs in Delhi -- धूम्रपान वर्जित है (dhoomrpaan varjit hai, 'smoking is forbidden') outside offices and hospitals, or प्रवेश वर्जित (pravesh varjit, 'entry forbidden') on gates. In spoken Hindi among friends or family, people almost always switch to मना (manaa) instead -- यहाँ धूम्रपान मना है (yahaan dhoomrpaan manaa hai, 'smoking is not allowed here') -- so think of वर्जित (varjit) as the word you read, not the word you say.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मना (manaa) in speech and वर्जित (varjit) in writing without realizing the difference is register, not meaning -- वर्जित (varjit) sounds stiff if used in casual conversation, like saying 'prohibited' instead of 'not allowed' to a friend. 2) It is an invariant adjective and does not agree in gender/number with the noun it describes, unlike many other Hindi adjectives.
