दाम (daam)

noun दाम (daam) is an everyday word for 'price' or 'cost,' used constantly in shops and markets; it also has a rarer literary/idiomatic sense of 'a snare or trap.'
Sense 1 : price, cost
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कीमत (kiimat), मूल्य (mooly, formal), भाव (bhaav, often for market/commodity rates)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear दाम (daam) everywhere in a Delhi bazaar -- दाम बताओ (daam bataao, 'tell me the price') is the opening line of every bit of haggling. भाव (bhaav) is the word you'll hear more from vendors quoting daily rates for vegetables or gold, while दाम (daam) and कीमत (kiimat) are more general-purpose.
Common Mistakes: 1) दाम (daam) is usually treated as plural in set phrases like दाम बढ़ गए (daam badh gae, 'the prices went up') even when talking about a single item's cost -- learners often keep it singular by mistake. 2) Don't confuse with नाम (naam, 'name') -- similar sound but unrelated meaning.

Sense 2 : snare, trap, net (figurative/literary)
noun, masculine Usually appears in fixed expressions like दाम में फँसना (daam men phansnaa, 'to be caught in a snare') rather than as a freestanding everyday noun.
Synonyms: जाल (jaal), फंदा (phandaa)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense shares its spelling with the 'price' word but comes from a different root; you'll mostly meet it in older poetry or proverbs rather than in daily conversation, so don't expect a shopkeeper to use it this way.
