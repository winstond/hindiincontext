छाता (chhaataa)

noun Mainly means 'umbrella'; also has a less common figurative sense of 'shelter/protection' and a rare sense as an umbrella-shaped stand or cover.
Sense 1 : umbrella
noun, masculine
Synonyms: छत्री (chhatrii, smaller or feminine variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: During Delhi's monsoon or the fierce June heat, you'll see छाता (chhaataa) everywhere -- as much a sunshade as a rain shield, unlike the English 'umbrella,' which people mostly associate with rain alone. The smaller, often more decorative छत्री (chhatrii) shares the same root and is common in folk and classical contexts (like the little domed pavilions called छतरी in Rajasthani architecture, itself a cousin word).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse छाता (chhaataa) with छत (chhat, 'roof/ceiling') because of the similar root -- they are related in origin but not interchangeable. 2) The verb usually paired with it is लगाना (lagaanaa) or खोलना (kholnaa) for 'to open/put up an umbrella,' not खड़ा करना (khadaa karnaa).

Sense 2 : shelter, protective cover (figurative)
noun, masculine Used metaphorically for someone or something that shields another from trouble, often in phrases like किसी का छाता बनना (kisii kaa chhaataa bannaa, 'to become someone's shield/protector').
Synonyms: आड़ (aad, protective cover), सहारा (sahaaraa, support/shelter)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense when someone describes a powerful relative or boss shielding a person from consequences -- much like saying someone has an 'umbrella of protection' in English, the imagery carries over quite naturally into Hindi.
