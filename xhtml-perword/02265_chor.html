चोर (chor)

noun/adjective Primarily a concrete noun meaning 'thief', with a common figurative/adjectival extension describing someone sneaky or evasive.
Sense 1 : a person who steals
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चोर (chor)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चोर चोर मौसेरे भाई (chor chor mausere bhaaii, 'thieves are cousins') is a widely used proverb about people with shared guilt covering for each other -- you will hear it in everyday banter about office politics or family disputes. चोर (chor) is also the classic villain in children's games and old Bollywood plots, so it carries a slightly folkloric, larger-than-life flavor beyond just 'criminal'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume चोर (chor) is invariant like दोस्त (dost), but it actually inflects as a regular -आ (aa) ending masculine noun in the oblique and plural (see morphology). 2) डाकू (daakoo) is a more violent 'bandit/dacoit', not interchangeable with चोर (chor) for petty theft.

Sense 2 : sneaky, deceitful, evasive (of a person or their manner)
adjective Used attributively or predicatively about a person's demeanor, e.g. describing shifty eyes or a guilty, evasive look -- चोर नज़रें (chor nazren, 'shifty/guilty eyes') is a fixed, very common collocation.
Synonyms: चालाक (chaalaak), धूर्त (dhoort, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear चोर नज़र (chor nazar) or आँखें चुराना (aankhen churaanaa, 'to steal one's eyes/avoid eye contact') used together -- Hindi frames guilt and evasiveness visually, through the eyes, much more than English does.
Common Mistakes: 1) This adjectival sense is invariant in practice -- it does not take the usual -आ/-ई (aa/ii) gender agreement the way ordinary adjectives do; learners sometimes wrongly feminize it to चोरी (chorii) by confusing it with the noun चोरी (chorii, 'theft').
