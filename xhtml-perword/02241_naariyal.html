नारियल (naariyal)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'coconut' -- the fruit, the tree, or the water/flesh inside; also carries a specific ritual sense as an offering in Hindu worship.
Sense 1 : coconut (the fruit)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: नारियल का फल (naariyal kaa phal, the fruit specifically), गरी (garii, the dried flesh only)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In any Delhi-NCR market you will see thelewale (thelevaale, cart vendors) hacking the top off a green नारियल (naariyal) with a heavy knife and handing it to you with a straw -- this is नारियल पानी (naariyal paanii), a favorite summer cooler believed to cool the body and settle the stomach.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes forget नारियल (naariyal) is masculine and pair it with feminine agreement, saying अच्छी नारियल (achchhii naariyal) instead of अच्छा नारियल (achchhaa naariyal). 2) नारियल पानी (naariyal paanii, coconut water) is the common way to ask for the drink at a roadside stall -- just saying नारियल (naariyal) alone usually gets you the whole fruit, not the drink.

Sense 2 : coconut offered in Hindu ritual/worship, or given as a ceremonial gift
noun, masculine
Synonyms: श्रीफल (shriiphal, literary/ritual term)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A whole नारियल (naariyal) is offered at temples, broken to inaugurate a new shop, ship, or building, and tucked into the sari or hands of a bride's family as a token of respect -- it stands in for purity and auspiciousness across Hindu ritual life, quite apart from being food.
Common Mistakes: 1) नारियल फोड़ना (naariyal phodnaa, to break a coconut) is the fixed expression for the ritual act of cracking it before a deity or at the start of a venture -- learners sometimes reach for a more literal verb like तोड़ना (todnaa) instead, which sounds odd in this context.
