साला (saalaa)

interjection/noun Literally 'wife's brother' (a real kinship term), but the word most learners encounter is its extremely common use as a coarse filler/abuse word -- these two senses are worlds apart in register.
Sense 1 : coarse filler/mild abuse (roughly 'bastard', 'damn')
interjection Used both as a standalone exclamation and as an adjective-like intensifier before a noun, e.g. साला मौसम (saalaa mausam, 'this damn weather') -- in this use it has drifted entirely away from its literal kinship meaning.
Synonyms: कमीना (kamiinaa, stronger insult), हरामी (haraamii, stronger insult)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi cinema's most iconic mild curses -- practically every masala film has a character muttering साला (saalaa) in frustration. It sits at the 'safe for friends, risky for strangers' end of Hindi swearing, much milder than हरामी (haraamii) but still not polite speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) This word is genuinely rude in mixed company, formal settings, or with elders -- it is common in male peer speech and films but can seriously offend if used with or about someone's actual family in the literal sense. 2) Learners sometimes think it always targets a person; it is very often used about things or situations (साला ट्रैफिक, saalaa traiphik, 'this damn traffic') with no insult to any person at all.
Sample Sentences:
साला, आज फिर बस छूट गई! Damn, I missed the bus again today!
(saalaa, aaj phir bas chhoot gaii!)

Sense 2 : wife's brother (brother-in-law)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In North Indian families, the साला-जीजा (saalaa-jiijaa) relationship is famously teasing and informal -- a man's साला (saalaa) is often his closest friend for joking, banter, and good-natured insults, which is part of why the word slid so easily into casual abuse (see the next sense).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use साला (saalaa) for any 'brother-in-law,' but Hindi kinship terms are precise: साला (saalaa) is specifically the wife's brother, while जीजा (jiijaa) is the sister's husband and देवर (devar) is the husband's younger brother -- they are not interchangeable.
Sample Sentences:
यह मेरा साला है, राजेश। This is my brother-in-law, Rajesh.
(yah meraa saalaa hai, raajesh।)
