दिशा (dishaa)

noun A common noun meaning 'direction' -- both the literal compass-direction sense and the figurative sense of a direction or path something takes (a project, a life, a conversation).
Sense 1 : compass direction
noun, feminine
Synonyms: ओर (or), तरफ़ (taraf)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi speech, if someone gives you directions to a shop, they will more likely say उस तरफ़ (us taraf, 'that way') than उस दिशा में (us dishaa men, 'in that direction') -- दिशा (dishaa) tends to surface in slightly more formal, geographic, or written contexts, like weather reports or Vaastu discussions about which direction a house should face.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for तरफ़ (taraf) or ओर (or) in every context, but दिशा (dishaa) is the word used for the four cardinal directions -- उत्तर (uttar, north), दक्षिण (dakshin, south), पूर्व (poorv, east), पश्चिम (pashchim, west) -- and in more formal or spatial descriptions, whereas तरफ़ (taraf) and ओर (or) are the everyday 'this way/that way' words.

Sense 2 : figurative direction, course, orientation
noun, feminine Common in the phrase दिशा देना (dishaa denaa, 'to give direction/guidance to') and दिशा में (dishaa men, 'in the direction of') used abstractly, as in बात दूसरी दिशा में चली गई (baat doosrii dishaa men chalii gaii, 'the conversation went in a different direction').
Synonyms: रुख़ (rukh)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the दिशा (dishaa) you hear in speeches and news commentary -- देश की दिशा (desh kii dishaa, 'the country's direction') or ज़िंदगी को दिशा देना (zindgii ko dishaa denaa, 'to give one's life direction/purpose') -- it carries a slightly weighty, purposeful tone, often used when someone is talking about guidance, mentorship, or a turning point.
