द्रव्य (dravy)

noun A learned/technical word meaning 'substance, matter' in a physical or philosophical sense, and by extension 'wealth, money' in older or formal registers. Not a word learners will reach for in everyday chat -- more likely to be met in science textbooks, philosophy, or classical contexts.
Sense 1 : substance, matter (physical/chemical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पदार्थ (padaarth), वस्तु (vastu, broader: 'object/thing')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the word used in school-level chemistry and in classical Indian philosophy (Nyaya-Vaisheshika) for 'substance' as a category of reality, alongside qualities and actions. If you've heard पदार्थ (padaarth) used similarly, the two overlap heavily in technical contexts, but द्रव्य (dravy) leans more textbook/philosophical.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for द्रव्य (dravy) to mean an everyday 'thing,' but in modern speech पदार्थ (padaarth) or चीज़ (chiiz) is the natural word; द्रव्य (dravy) belongs to science/philosophy registers, e.g. describing a chemical substance.

Sense 2 : wealth, money, riches
noun, masculine
Synonyms: धन (dhan), पैसा (paisaa, everyday word for money), संपत्ति (sampatti, more 'property/assets')
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense in older texts, legal or economic writing, and set phrases about wealth and worldly goods -- it carries a slightly formal, almost moralizing tone, as in discussions of धन-द्रव्य (dhan-dravy, 'wealth and riches') contrasted with spiritual values.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is formal/literary; in daily conversation about money, धन (dhan) or पैसा (paisaa) is what people actually say, so using द्रव्य (dravy) in casual speech can sound stiff or bookish.
