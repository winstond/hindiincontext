पशु (pashu)

noun A single core sense: an animal, especially a four-legged domestic or wild beast, with a common figurative extension for brutish/inhuman human behavior.
Sense 1 : animal, beast (especially quadruped)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जानवर (jaanvar), जीव (jiiv, broader: living being), मृग (mrig, literary, deer/wild animal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of पशु (pashu) as the word you'd see on a government sign like पशु चिकित्सालय (pashu chikitsaalay, 'animal hospital/veterinary clinic') or hear from a farmer talking about his पशुधन (pashudhan, 'livestock, animal wealth') -- cows and buffaloes are a real economic asset in rural Indian households, so this word carries a slightly more official or agricultural flavor than the everyday जानवर (jaanvar).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for जानवर (jaanvar) by default; पशु (pashu) is the word you will actually see on signboards, in farming/veterinary contexts, and in formal or written registers -- जानवर (jaanvar) is the everyday spoken default. 2) पशु (pashu) leans toward domesticated or farm animals (cows, goats, buffaloes) in everyday use, even though it technically covers wild animals too.

Sense 2 : brute, beast (figurative, of a person)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as a direct insult, e.g. calling someone पशु (pashu) for acting without decency or restraint.
Synonyms: जानवर (jaanvar, figurative use also common), दरिंदा (darindaa, stronger: predator/monster), हैवान (haivaan, stronger: monster, inhuman)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in a moral or scolding tone -- a parent or teacher might say तुम पशु जैसे व्यवहार कर रहे हो (tum pashu jaise vyavahaar kar rahe ho, 'you're behaving like an animal') to shame bad manners, tying back to the cultural idea that पशु (pashu) lack buddhi (buddhi, reason/discernment), the trait that supposedly separates humans from beasts.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative insult is milder than हैवान (haivaan) or दरिंदा (darindaa), which imply real cruelty or violence -- पशु (pashu) is closer to 'brute' or 'animal' used for someone who is crude, uncultured, or lacking self-control, not necessarily violent.
