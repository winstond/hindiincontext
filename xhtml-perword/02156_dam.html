दम (dam)

noun Basic meaning is 'breath' (dam), which extends metaphorically to strength/stamina, courage/nerve, and life-force; it also appears as the name of a slow-cooking technique in food (dum cooking) and inside many common idioms.
Sense 1 : breath
noun, masculine
Synonyms: साँस (saans)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of दम (dam) as breath-as-life-force rather than just the mechanical act of breathing -- that is why so many idioms about stamina, courage, and even dying (दम तोड़ना, dam todnaa, 'to breathe one's last') build on this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for साँस (saans) in every context, but दम (dam) is preferred in fixed idioms like दम घुटना (dam ghutnaa, 'to suffocate/feel stifled') and दम लेना (dam lenaa, 'to take a breather') -- the two are not freely interchangeable everywhere.

Sense 2 : stamina, strength, energy

Sense 3 : courage, nerve, boldness
noun, masculine
Synonyms: हिम्मत (himmat), हौसला (hauslaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: उसमें दम नहीं है (usmen dam nahiin hai, 'he doesn't have the guts') is a very common putdown in casual conversation, and it overlaps closely with हिम्मत (himmat) -- but दम (dam) leans slightly more toward raw gutsiness/capability than हिम्मत's (himmat) moral courage.
Common Mistakes: 1) दम (dam) in this sense almost always appears with होना (honaa) in the pattern X में दम होना (X men dam honaa, 'to have it in X'), so learners should get comfortable with the में...होना (men...honaa) frame rather than trying to use दम (dam) as a direct object of a verb like 'to have'.

उसमें दम नहीं है कि वह अपने बॉस के सामने खड़ा हो सके। He doesn't have the guts to stand up to his boss.

Sense 4 : dum cooking (slow-cooking technique, sealed-pot steam method)
noun, masculine Used attributively before a dish name, as in दम आलू (dam aaloo, 'potatoes cooked dum-style') or बिरयानी दम पर पकाना (biryaanii dam par pakaanaa, 'to cook biryani on dum').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This दम (dam) comes from the same root idea of trapped 'breath'/steam -- the pot is sealed (often with dough) so the dish cooks in its own steam and aroma, which is exactly what gives dum biryani (dam biryaanii) and दम आलू (dam aaloo) their signature depth of flavor.
