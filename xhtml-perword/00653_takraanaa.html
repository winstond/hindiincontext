टकराना (takraanaa)

verb Basic meaning is 'to collide/strike against' physically, extending naturally to figurative clashes -- between people, opinions, or interests (takraanaa).
Sense 1 : to collide, crash, strike against (physical)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: भिड़ना (bhidnaa), टक्कर खाना (takkar khaanaa, phrase variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in traffic-accident news and everyday chatter about Delhi's chaotic roads -- दो गाड़ियाँ टकरा गईं (do gaadiyaan takraa gaiin, 'two cars collided') is a classic news-report line. The compound with जाना (jaanaa) (टकरा जाना) is very common and adds a sense of suddenness or completion to the collision.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often forget the से (se) marking the thing collided with: गाड़ी पेड़ से टकराई (gaadii ped se takraaii, 'the car crashed into the tree') -- not पेड़ को (ped ko). 2) This verb is intransitive despite describing a forceful action, so it does not take ने (ne) in the perfective -- गाड़ी टकराई (gaadii takraaii), not गाड़ी ने टकराया (gaadii ne takraayaa).

Sense 2 : to clash, come into conflict (figurative -- interests, opinions, people)
verb, intransitive Used with से (se) for the party or thing one clashes with: सरकार से टकराना (sarkaar se takraanaa, 'to clash with the government').
Synonyms: भिड़ना (bhidnaa), उलझना (ulajhnaa, more about getting entangled in a dispute)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up a lot in political and news commentary -- हित टकराना (hit takraanaa, 'interests clashing') or दोनों पार्टियाँ टकरा गईं (donon paartiyaan takraa gaiin, 'the two parties clashed'). It carries a slightly more dramatic, head-on flavor than a plain disagreement.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for लड़ना (ladnaa) when they mean a clash of interests or ideas rather than a physical fight or argument -- टकराना (takraanaa) suits interests, policies, egos colliding, not just people shouting at each other.
