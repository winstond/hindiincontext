बात (baat)

noun One of the most versatile words in everyday Hindi: covers 'thing said,' 'matter/topic,' 'point/gist,' and lives inside a huge number of set phrases and idioms.
Sense 1 : a thing said; remark, words
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कथन (kathan, formal), टिप्पणी (tippnii, remark)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बात करना (baat karnaa, 'to talk') is the everyday verb for chatting or having a conversation, as in फ़ोन पर बात करना (fon par baat karnaa, 'to talk on the phone') -- you'll hear this constantly in daily life, far more than बातचीत करना (baatchiit karnaa) in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शब्द (shabd) when they mean बात (baat) -- शब्द (shabd) is an individual word, while बात (baat) is a whole remark, utterance, or piece of speech. 2) बात करना (baat karnaa) means 'to talk/converse,' not literally 'to do a thing' -- learners sometimes misparse it word-by-word.
Sample Sentences:

(main usse fon par baat kar rahaa thaa।)

Sense 2 : a matter, topic, or issue under discussion
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मामला (maamlaa), मसला (maslaa, formal), विषय (vishay, topic)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: कोई बात नहीं (koii baat nahiin, 'no problem, it's nothing') is one of the most reassuring phrases in Hindi -- said constantly to wave away an apology or a small mishap, much like 'no worries' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) In questions like क्या बात है? (kyaa baat hai, 'what's the matter?'), learners sometimes translate too literally as 'what is the thing' instead of hearing it as the natural Hindi way to ask 'what's wrong?' or 'what's going on?'

Sense 3 : the point, gist, or crux of something
noun, feminine Common in the frame बात यह है कि (baat yah hai ki, 'the point is that...') to introduce an explanation or excuse.
Synonyms: मुद्दा (muddaa, point/issue), सार (saar, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear बात सुनो (baat suno, 'listen to this') used to grab someone's attention before making a point -- it works almost like 'listen' or 'look' in English, softening into a conversational opener rather than a literal command.
Common Mistakes: 1) बात तो यह है (baat to yah hai) and असल बात (asal baat, 'the real point') both signal 'here's the actual point' -- learners often miss that यह (yah) here refers forward to a clause, not to a physical thing.

Sense 4 : an idiomatic core inside fixed expressions (e.g. self-respect, promise, no big deal)
noun, feminine This sense covers the many frozen idioms built on बात (baat) rather than one literal meaning: बात रखना (baat rakhnaa, 'to keep one's word/honor a promise'), बात मानना (baat maannaa, 'to heed/obey what someone says'), बात बनाना (baat banaanaa, 'to make excuses / spin a story'), and बातों में आना (baaton men aanaa, 'to be taken in by someone's talk').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बात रखना (baat rakhnaa) carries real social weight in Hindi-speaking culture -- refusing to 'keep someone's बात (baat)' can be read as a slight against their izzat (honor), so the phrase shows up often in family and community contexts, not just casual promises.
Common Mistakes: 1) बात रखना (baat rakhnaa) is not about 'placing a thing' -- it means to honor a commitment or someone's request, tied to izzat/self-respect; learners who parse it literally miss the emotional weight. 2) बातों में आना (baaton men aanaa) uses the plural oblique बातों (baaton) even though it describes being fooled by a single conversation's worth of sweet talk.
Sample Sentences:
मेरे पापा हमेशा अपनी बात रखते हैं। My dad always keeps his word.
