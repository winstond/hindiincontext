साबुत (saabut)

adjective An adjective describing something whole, intact, or unbroken -- most often heard in the kitchen (whole spices, whole grains) but also used for objects or bodies that have come through undamaged.
Sense 1 : whole, unbroken (of spices, grains, food items)
adjective
Synonyms: अखंड (akhand, literary), पूरा (pooraa, meaning scope: whole/complete, less specific to intactness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Walk into any Indian kitchen and you will hear साबुत (saabut) constantly on spice packets and recipes: साबुत जीरा (saabut jiiraa, 'whole cumin seeds'), साबुत लाल मिर्च (saabut laal mirch, 'whole red chilies'). It signals that the spice has not been ground, which matters a lot for how a dish is tempered (तड़का, tadkaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पूरा (pooraa) when they mean साबुत (saabut) -- पूरा (pooraa) means 'complete/entire' (पूरा दिन (pooraa din, 'the whole day')) while साबुत (saabut) specifically means 'not broken into pieces,' as in साबुत धनिया (saabut dhaniyaa, 'whole coriander seeds').

Sense 2 : intact, undamaged, coming through safely (of a body or object)
adjective
Synonyms: सही सलामत (sahii salaamat, safe and sound), सुरक्षित (surakshit, safe)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in relief after an accident or a fall: वह गिर गया लेकिन साबुत बच गया (vah gir gayaa lekin saabut bach gayaa, 'he fell but came out unharmed'). It carries a sense of having escaped breakage or injury entirely, not just being 'fine' in a general sense.
