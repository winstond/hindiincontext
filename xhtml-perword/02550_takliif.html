तकलीफ़ (takliif)

noun A common everyday word covering physical pain/illness, trouble or inconvenience, and (in polite requests) the effort someone takes on your behalf; all three senses share the same noun.
Sense 1 : pain, ailment, physical discomfort
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दर्द (dard), पीड़ा (piidaa, literary), कष्ट (kasht)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: When someone asks आपको क्या तकलीफ़ है (aapko kyaa takliif hai, 'what seems to be the problem'), it's the standard phrase a doctor uses to ask a patient what's wrong -- much gentler and vaguer than naming a specific dard (dard).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for दर्द (dard) only, but तकलीफ़ (takliif) is broader and can describe a vague ailment or discomfort, not just sharp physical pain. 2) With this sense, the pattern is X को तकलीफ़ है/होना (X ko takliif hai, 'X has some ailment') -- the sufferer is marked with को (ko), not the subject of a normal transitive sentence.
Sample Sentences:
आपको क्या तकलीफ़ है? What seems to be the problem?
(aapko kyaa takliif hai?)

Sense 2 : trouble, inconvenience, difficulty
noun, feminine
Synonyms: परेशानी (pareshaanii), मुश्किल (mushkil), दिक्कत (dikkat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the very common host's line तकलीफ़ मत करो (takliif mat karo, 'don't go to any trouble') -- said when a guest is being fussed over with tea and snacks, echoing the polite-effort sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) तकलीफ़ होना (takliif honaa) is used for a general hardship or inconvenience someone faces, while तकलीफ़ देना (takliif denaa) means to cause someone trouble -- learners sometimes mix up which verb goes with which direction of the trouble.
Sample Sentences:
तकलीफ़ मत करो, मैं खुद चाय बना लूँगा। Don't go to any trouble, I'll make the tea myself.

Sense 3 : the bother/effort someone takes on your behalf (in polite requests)
noun, feminine Commonly appears in the polite frame तकलीफ़ करना/देना (takliif karnaa, 'to go to the trouble of...') when asking someone to do something for you, or apologizing for having caused them effort.
Synonyms: ज़हमत (zahmat, formal), कष्ट (kasht, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is classic Delhi hospitality-speak: a guest might say तकलीफ़ मत लीजिए (takliif mat liijie, 'please don't trouble yourself') while the host is already busy laying out food -- it's a ritual politeness exchange, not usually meant to be taken literally.
Common Mistakes: 1) माफ़ करना, तकलीफ़ हुई (maaf karnaa, takliif huii, 'sorry for the trouble') is a fixed apologetic phrase; learners sometimes construct something more literal and stiff instead of using this natural set phrase.
Sample Sentences:

(are, aap kyon takliif kar rahe hain, main khud le letaa।)
