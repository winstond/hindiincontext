इरादा (iraadaa)

noun A single core sense: 'intention' or 'resolve' -- the plan or purpose behind an action. It appears constantly in the everyday frame X का इरादा है (X kaa iraadaa hai).
Sense 1 : intention, purpose, resolve
noun, masculine Commonly used with का (kaa) plus होना (honaa) in the pattern [someone] का इरादा है (kaa iraadaa hai, 'intends to') followed by an infinitive, e.g. जाने का इरादा है (jaane kaa iraadaa hai, 'intend to go').
Synonyms: मंशा (manshaa, formal), मक़सद (maqsad, purpose/goal), नीयत (niiyat, intent/motive, often about good or bad intent), इच्छा (ichchhaa, wish/desire, softer than इरादा)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear इरादा (iraadaa) a lot in dramatic contexts too -- तुम्हारा इरादा क्या है? (tumhaaraa iraadaa kyaa hai, 'what are your intentions?') can carry a suspicious or confrontational edge, much like its English equivalent when a parent asks it about a daughter's suitor. It is a Persian-origin word, at home in both everyday planning talk and slightly theatrical accusations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say मुझे इरादा है (mujhe iraadaa hai) by analogy with दातिव-subject verbs like लगना, but इरादा takes का/मेरा (kaa/meraa), not को (ko): the correct frame is मेरा इरादा है (meraa iraadaa hai, 'I intend to') or जाने का इरादा है (jaane kaa iraadaa hai). 2) इरादा (iraadaa) suggests a firmer, more deliberate plan than इच्छा (ichchhaa), which is closer to a wish or preference; don't use them interchangeably when translating 'I want to.'
