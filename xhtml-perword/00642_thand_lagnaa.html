ठंड लगना (thand lagnaa)

verb A dative-subject construction meaning 'to feel cold' -- literally 'cold applies/strikes (to someone)'. Uses को (ko) marking on the person who feels cold, following the same pattern as भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa) and डर लगना (dar lagnaa).
Sense 1 : to feel cold
verb, intransitive Person who feels cold is marked with को (ko): मुझे ठंड लगती है (mujhe thand lagtii hai, 'I feel cold') -- the subject is grammatically the cold (thand, feminine), so the verb agrees with it, not with the person.
Synonyms: सर्दी लगना (sardii lagnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same grammatical family as भूख लगना (bhookh lagnaa, 'to feel hungry') and प्यास लगना (pyaas lagnaa, 'to feel thirsty') -- Hindi treats bodily sensations as things that happen *to* you rather than actions you perform, which is why the person takes को (ko) instead of being the subject. In Delhi winters you will hear this constantly: mothers telling kids ठंड लग जाएगी, स्वेटर पहनो (thand lag jaaegii, svetar pahno, 'you'll catch a chill, wear a sweater'), where लग जाना (lag jaanaa) adds a sense of it happening/catching suddenly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make the person the subject, saying something like मैं ठंड लगता हूँ (main thand lagtaa hoon) -- wrong; the person must be marked with को (ko) and the verb agrees with ठंड (thand): मुझे ठंड लग रही है (mujhe thand lag rahii hai, 'I am feeling cold'). 2) Confusing ठंड लगना (thand lagnaa) with ठंडा होना (thandaa honaa, 'to become/be cold' -- describes an object's temperature, not a person's sensation). 3) Overusing perfective ठंड लगी (thand lagii) when the ongoing/habitual forms (लगती है (lagtii hai), लग रही है (lag rahii hai)) are what native speakers reach for to describe the present feeling.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to catch a chill / fall ill from cold
verb, intransitive Often appears with जाना (jaanaa) as a compound -- ठंड लग जाना (thand lag jaanaa) -- to mark the onset as sudden or unwanted, distinct from the steady-state 'feeling cold' sense.
Synonyms: सर्दी लग जाना (sardii lag jaanaa), ठंड लग जाना (thand lag jaanaa, with compound verb जाना emphasizing suddenness)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Parents and grandparents in Delhi-NCR use this warningly all winter -- बाहर मत जाओ, ठंड लग जाएगी (baahar mat jaao, thand lag jaaegii, 'don't go outside, you'll catch a chill') -- treating cold exposure as a direct cause of illness, a very common folk-health framing in North India.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this interchangeably with the simple 'feel cold' sense, but here the meaning shifts toward 'got sick from cold exposure', closer to English 'caught a chill' -- context (often past tense with जाना (jaanaa)) signals which sense is meant. 2) This sense is often followed by इसलिए, and physical symptoms (बुखार, खांसी) in the same breath -- learners may not realize the phrase alone implies illness, not just discomfort.
