स्वर (svar)

noun स्वर (svar) covers two closely related senses: a linguistic 'vowel' (in grammar/phonetics discussions) and a broader 'sound, tone, or voice/pitch' (especially in music and speech).
Sense 1 : sound, tone, voice, musical note
noun, masculine
Synonyms: आवाज़ (aavaaz), ध्वनि (dhvani, more formal/technical), सुर (sur, music-specific)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll often hear this word in the context of classical or devotional music -- a singer's स्वर (svar) is praised for being sweet or steady. It also shows up in the phrase अपना स्वर बदलना (apnaa svar badalnaa, 'to change one's tone,' e.g. when someone shifts from angry to conciliatory), reflecting how Hindi treats 'tone of voice' as tightly linked to attitude or stance.
Common Mistakes: 1) स्वर (svar) leans toward the quality, pitch, or musical character of a sound (a singer's tone, the tenor of someone's speech), whereas आवाज़ (aavaaz) is the more general everyday word for 'sound' or 'voice' -- learners often reach for स्वर (svar) when आवाज़ (aavaaz) would sound more natural in casual speech. 2) In set phrases like स्वर मिलाना (svar milaanaa, 'to join one's voice/agree with') the word means 'agreement in tone/opinion,' not literally 'sound' -- this idiomatic layer surprises learners who only know the literal sense.

Sense 2 : vowel (linguistic term)
noun, masculine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear in a Hindi or Sanskrit classroom when the teacher explains that अ, आ, इ, ई... are स्वर (svar, vowels) and क, ख, ग... are व्यंजन (vyanjan, consonants). It's a foundational term if you ever study Devanagari script formally.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse स्वर (svar, 'vowel') with व्यंजन (vyanjan, 'consonant') -- these are opposite terms in the same grammar lesson, so mixing them up is the most common slip. 2) In this sense स्वर (svar) is a grammar term used when talking *about* Hindi/Sanskrit script, not a word you'd casually drop into daily conversation.
