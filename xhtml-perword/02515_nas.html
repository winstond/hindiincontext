नस (nas)

noun A concrete anatomical noun meaning 'vein/nerve/sinew,' with a common figurative extension into idioms about someone's innate temperament or 'nature.'
Sense 1 : vein, nerve, sinew (anatomical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रग (rag), शिरा (shiraa, formal/medical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this word a lot in casual health talk -- someone pressing a sore shoulder might say नस चढ़ गई (nas chadh gaii, 'a nerve/muscle got pulled or knotted up'), a very common household complaint. Related body-part vocabulary like रग (rag) overlaps closely with this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume नस (nas) maps cleanly to English 'nerve' in the sense of courage or boldness -- it doesn't; that figurative use belongs to a separate idiom (see the other sense) and does not apply here. 2) It is used loosely in everyday speech for veins, nerves, and even tendons/sinews without much anatomical precision -- a doctor and a grandmother might both say नस (nas) for different structures.

Sense 2 : innate nature, deep-seated trait or tendency (idiomatic, as in 'it's in his blood')
noun, feminine Lives mainly in the fixed idiom नस-नस में (nas-nas men, 'in every vein,' i.e. 'in one's very blood/bones') and phrases like उसकी नस पहचानना (uskii nas pahchaannaa, 'to know someone's true nature/weak spot').
Synonyms: फ़ितरत (fitrat), आदत (aadat, meaning scope: habit, narrower)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the Hindi equivalent of saying something 'runs in someone's blood' -- नस-नस में बसा है (nas-nas men basaa hai, 'it has settled into every vein') is a favorite way to describe a passion or trait someone was simply born with, whether it's a love of music or a family business.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is idiom-bound -- you won't substitute नस (nas) freely for 'nature' or 'personality' outside these set phrases; use स्वभाव (svabhaav) for that general meaning instead.
