सौतेला (sautelaa)

adjective Adjective describing a step-relationship (step-brother, step-mother, etc.); always used attached to a kinship noun, never standing alone.
Sense 1 : step- (kinship relation, e.g. step-brother, step-mother)
adjective Always precedes a kinship noun: सौतेला भाई (sautelaa bhaaii, 'step-brother'), सौतेली माँ (sautelii maan, 'step-mother'), सौतेला बाप (sautelaa baap, 'step-father'). Agrees in gender/number with the kinship noun it modifies.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The word carries real emotional weight in Hindi the way 'stepmother' does in English fairy tales -- सौतेली माँ (sautelii maan) is practically a stock villain figure in older Bollywood films and folk stories, so the phrase often shows up loaded with connotations of unfairness or neglect even outside literal family contexts, as in सौतेला व्यवहार (sautelaa vyavahaar, 'unfair/discriminatory treatment') used for any situation where someone is treated as a lesser priority.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use सौतेला (sautelaa) as a standalone noun meaning 'step-relative' -- it only works glued to a kinship word like भाई (bhaaii) or माँ (maan). 2) Do not confuse with चचेरा/ममेरा/मौसेरा (chacheraa/mameraa/mauseraa), which describe cousins through specific relatives (paternal uncle, maternal uncle, maternal aunt) -- सौतेला (sautelaa) specifically marks a step-relationship arising from a parent's remarriage, not a cousin relationship.

Sense 2 : treated unfairly, discriminated against, neglected (figurative extension)
adjective Common in the fixed phrase सौतेला व्यवहार (sautelaa vyavahaar, 'stepmotherly/unfair treatment') and सौतेलापन (sautelaapan, the abstract noun for such treatment); also used to describe policies, departments, or children within a family being favored less than others.
Synonyms: उपेक्षित (upekshit, more formal, 'neglected')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in everyday complaints -- a person might say their locality gets सौतेला व्यवहार (sautelaa vyavahaar) from the municipal corporation, or a sibling might joke that they got सौतेला प्यार (sautelaa pyaar, 'stepchild-like love') compared to a favored brother, extending the stepmother stereotype into ordinary favoritism talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners may not realize this figurative sense exists independently of literal step-relations -- a government scheme, a company department, or even a city can be described as getting सौतेला व्यवहार (sautelaa vyavahaar), meaning it is under-resourced or unfairly sidelined compared to others.
