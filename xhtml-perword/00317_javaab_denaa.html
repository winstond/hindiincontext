जवाब देना (javaab denaa)

verb A compound formed from जवाब (javaab, 'answer/reply') plus देना (denaa, 'to give'); covers giving a verbal reply as well as extended senses like responding to a challenge or a machine/body part 'giving out.'
Sense 1 : to answer, to reply
verb, transitive Takes को for the person being answered: उसे जवाब देना (use javaab denaa) 'to answer him.' The thing said can be marked with का or expressed as a direct object.
Synonyms: उत्तर देना (uttar denaa, formal), जवाब में कहना (javaab men kahnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a classroom or office setting, teacher जवाब माँगते हैं (javaab maangte hain, 'ask for an answer') and students जवाब देते हैं (javaab dete hain) -- this pairing of माँगना (maangnaa) and देना is very natural in Hindi, mirroring how Hindi likes to frame communication as things exchanged rather than actions performed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say जवाब बोलना (javaab bolnaa) by analogy with बोलना (bolnaa, 'to speak'), but जवाब always pairs with देना (denaa), never बोलना. 2) The person being answered takes को (ko), not से (se) -- सवाल के जवाब में (savaal ke javaab men, 'in reply to the question') uses में, but the person answered is marked with को.

Sense 2 : to respond, retaliate, counter (a challenge, an attack, a rival's move)
verb, transitive Common in sports, politics, and rivalry contexts: टीम ने अच्छा जवाब दिया (tiim ne achchhaa javaab diyaa, 'the team gave a good response'). The object of confrontation is usually marked with को.
Synonyms: मुकाबला करना (mukaablaa karnaa, 'to compete/confront'), जवाब में टक्कर देना (javaab men takkar denaa, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in cricket commentary and political news -- विरोधियों को जवाब देना (virodhiyon ko javaab denaa, 'to give a reply to one's opponents') is a stock phrase for coming back strong after criticism or a setback.

Sense 3 : to fail, give out, stop working (of a body part, machine, or system)
verb, intransitive Used intransitively with an inanimate or bodily subject: दिल ने जवाब दे दिया (dil ne javaab de diyaa, 'the heart gave out'); ब्रेक ने जवाब दे दिया (brek ne javaab de diyaa, 'the brakes failed'). Despite the subject being non-human, the verb still takes ने because देना is inherently transitive in form even in this figurative extension.
Synonyms: काम न करना (kaam n karnaa), जवाब दे जाना (javaab de jaanaa, with दे जाना emphasizing suddenness)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a lovely example of Hindi's love of personifying the body: instead of saying a knee 'stopped working,' you say घुटनों ने जवाब दे दिया (ghutnon ne javaab de diyaa, 'the knees gave out') -- as if the knees were replying 'no more' to your demands. You'll hear this a lot from older relatives describing aches and pains.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes expect an intransitive construction without ने since the meaning feels like 'failing' rather than 'giving,' but the ने-marking still applies because grammatically this is still देना (denaa) in its transitive form -- the body part or machine is treated as the one 'giving' a (negative) answer.
