सरल (saral)

adjective A common adjective meaning simple/easy, and by extension used to describe an uncomplicated, straightforward personality.
Sense 1 : simple, easy (of a task, text, or process)
adjective
Synonyms: आसान (aasaan), सहज (sahaj), सुगम (sugam, somewhat formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the go-to word on the cover of every Hindi grammar or self-help book promising to make some hard subject सरल (saral) -- 'सरल हिंदी व्याकरण' (saral hindii vyaakran, 'Simple Hindi Grammar') is a familiar genre title. It sits a notch more bookish than आसान (aasaan), which dominates casual speech, though both are used constantly.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for आसान (aasaan) by default since it is learned earlier; सरल (saral) is just as common in speech and very common in writing, especially describing language, methods, or explanations -- सरल भाषा (saral bhaashaa, 'simple language') is a set phrase you will see constantly on textbook covers. 2) सरल (saral) describes the task or thing itself as simple, not a person's ability to do it -- for 'I find it easy' you still need a dative construction like मुझे यह आसान/सरल लगता है (mujhe yah aasaan/saral lagtaa hai, 'this seems easy to me'), not a sentence with सरल as the main predicate of a person.
Sample Sentences:

(yah savaal bahut saral hai।)

Sense 2 : simple, unaffected, guileless (of a person's nature)
adjective
Synonyms: सीधा (siidhaa), निष्कपट (nishkpat, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone सरल स्वभाव का (saral svabhaav kaa, 'of a simple/gentle nature') is warm praise in Hindi -- it paints someone as unpretentious and easy to get along with, the kind of person your parents approve of as a match or a friend.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense सरल (saral) is a compliment about character -- open, honest, without cunning -- and is not interchangeable with आसान (aasaan), which never describes personality this way.
