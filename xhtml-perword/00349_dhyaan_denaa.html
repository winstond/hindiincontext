ध्यान देना (dhyaan denaa)

verb A compound verb built from ध्यान (dhyaan, 'attention/focus') plus देना (denaa, 'to give'), literally 'to give attention.' Covers paying attention/heed to something and, by extension, taking care of someone or something.
Sense 1 : to pay attention, to heed
verb, transitive The thing attended to is marked with पर (par) or की ओर (kii or): ध्यान देना पर (dhyaan denaa par) is the standard frame, e.g. बात पर ध्यान देना (baat par dhyaan denaa, 'to pay attention to the matter').
Synonyms: गौर करना (gaur karnaa), तवज्जो देना (tavajjo denaa, somewhat formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday phrase a teacher, parent, or boss uses to tell someone to focus: ध्यान दो! (dhyaan do!, 'Pay attention!') is heard constantly in Indian classrooms and homes. It pairs naturally with ध्यान से (dhyaan se, 'carefully/attentively'), which describes the manner of doing something rather than the act of attending itself.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try को (ko) for the object of attention, but the natural marker is पर (par) (or की ओर (kii or)), not को (ko). 2) As a transitive compound verb, it takes ने (ne) in the perfective: मैंने ध्यान दिया (mainne dhyaan diyaa, 'I paid attention'), which learners sometimes forget since ध्यान (dhyaan) itself feels abstract/non-object-like.
Sample Sentences:
ज़रा ध्यान दो, मैं क्या कह रहा हूँ! Pay attention for a second, I'm telling you something!
(zaraa dhyaan do, main kyaa kah rahaa hoon!)

Sense 2 : to take care of, look after
verb, transitive Here the person or thing cared for is also marked with पर (par) or की ओर (kii or), as in अपनी सेहत पर ध्यान देना (apnii sehat par dhyaan denaa, 'to take care of one's health').
Synonyms: देखभाल करना (dekhbhaal karnaa), ख्याल रखना (khyaal rakhnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: अपने ऊपर ध्यान दो (apne oopar dhyaan do, 'take care of yourself') is a common thing to say when someone is overworked or stressed -- it frames self-care as a matter of redirecting one's own attention, a very Hindi way of talking about wellbeing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for देखभाल करना (dekhbhaal karnaa) exclusively for 'taking care of' and miss that ध्यान देना (dhyaan denaa) covers this sense too, especially for health, studies, or one's own well-being rather than nursing a person directly.
