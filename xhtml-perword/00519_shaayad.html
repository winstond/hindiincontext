शायद (shaayad)

adverb A single-sense adverb meaning 'perhaps, maybe' -- used to express doubt or possibility about a statement.
Sense 1 : perhaps, maybe
adverb Typically placed at the start of the sentence, though it can also appear before the verb; it does not trigger any change in verb mood on its own, but is very often paired with the subjunctive when the speaker wants to underscore genuine uncertainty, e.g. शायद वह आए (shaayad vah aae, 'maybe he'll come') rather than the plain future.
Synonyms: मुमकिन है (mumkin hai, phrase: 'it's possible'), संभवतः (sambhavtah, literary/formal), कदाचित (kadaachit, literary, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear शायद (shaayad) constantly at the start of casual sentences in Delhi -- it's the Hindi speaker's natural hedge, much like English 'maybe' or 'I guess'. Pairing it with the subjunctive (शायद वह आए shaayad vah aae, 'maybe he'll come') rather than a flat future tense is what makes the doubt sound genuine and idiomatic rather than textbook-ish.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often pair शायद (shaayad) only with the future tense (जायेगा jaayegaa) when native speakers frequently prefer the subjunctive (जाए jaae) to sound more natural and genuinely uncertain. 2) Do not confuse with ज़रूर (zaroor, 'certainly'), which expresses the opposite level of confidence -- learners sometimes mix up these two common sentence-openers.
