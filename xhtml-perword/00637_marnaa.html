मरना (marnaa)

verb The basic intransitive verb 'to die', used literally as well as in a wide range of everyday figurative and idiomatic expressions (dying of hunger, love, boredom, embarrassment, etc.).
Sense 1 : to die
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: गुज़रना (guzarnaa, euphemistic), गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa, euphemistic), स्वर्गवास होना (svargvaas honaa, formal/euphemistic), चल बसना (chal basnaa, euphemistic), देहांत होना (dehaant honaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Talking directly about death with मरना (marnaa) can feel abrupt or even rude in Hindi, much like bluntly saying 'died' in English when 'passed away' would be gentler. You will hear मरना (marnaa) used freely for animals, insects, or in angry/casual speech (मर जा! mar jaa, 'drop dead!'), but for a family member or respected person, Delhi speakers almost always soften it.
Common Mistakes: 1) मरना (marnaa) is blunt; in polite conversation about someone's death, native speakers usually reach for a softer expression like गुज़र गए (guzar gae) or स्वर्गवास हो गया (svargvaas ho gayaa) instead. 2) Learners sometimes add ने (ne) by analogy with other verbs, but मरना (marnaa) is intransitive and never takes ने (ne) in the perfective -- वह मर गया (vah mar gayaa, 'he died'), not वह ने मरा।
Sample Sentences:

(kal raat ek choohaa jahar khaakar mar gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to suffer intensely / be overwhelmed by a feeling (figurative 'dying of...')
verb, intransitive Common frame: [noun/oblique] + के मारे (ke maare) or से (se) + मरना (marnaa), e.g. भूख से मरना (bhookh se marnaa, 'to be dying of hunger'), प्यार में मरना (pyaar men marnaa, 'to be dying for love').
Synonyms: तड़पना (tadapnaa, to writhe/pine), घुटना (ghutnaa, to suffocate/stifle)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi loves this dramatic exaggeration just as English does with 'I'm dying of laughter' or 'starving to death' -- a mother saying भूख से मर रहा हूँ (bhookh se mar rahaa hoon, 'I'm dying of hunger') to demand a snack is everyday household theater, not a medical emergency.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is hyperbole, not literal death -- शर्म के मारे मर गई (sharm ke maare mar gaii, 'I nearly died of embarrassment') describes intense feeling, not an actual event, so don't translate it flatly. 2) Learners often miss that मरे जा रहे हैं (mare jaa rahe hain) type continuous forms intensify the exaggeration further, common in complaints and teasing.
Sample Sentences:
मैं भूख से मर रहा हूँ, कुछ खाने को दो। I'm dying of hunger, give me something to eat.
(main bhookh se mar rahaa hoon, kuchh khaane ko do।)

Sense 3 : to be infatuated with / crazy about (someone or something)
verb, intransitive Frame: [subject] + [person/thing] + पर (par) + मरना (marnaa), e.g. वह उस पर मरता है (vah us par martaa hai, 'he's crazy about her').
Synonyms: फ़िदा होना (fidaa honaa, to be smitten), दीवाना होना (diivaanaa honaa, to be infatuated)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: मरना पर (par marnaa) is a favorite in Bollywood-style banter and gossip about crushes -- दोस्त कहते हैं 'यह तो उस पर मरता है' (yah to us par martaa hai, 'this guy's totally smitten with her') with a teasing grin.
Common Mistakes: 1) The postposition here is पर (par), not के लिए (ke lie) or को (ko) as an English speaker might expect -- उस पर मरना (us par marnaa), not उसे मरना।
Sample Sentences:

(vah us ladkii par martaa hai।)
