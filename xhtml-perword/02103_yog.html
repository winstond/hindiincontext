योग (yog)

noun Covers the spiritual/physical discipline of yoga, the mathematical sense of 'sum/addition', and a more literary sense of 'union, combination, conjunction'.
Sense 1 : yoga (the discipline)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR you'll see योग (yog) everywhere from park sessions at dawn (सुबह की सैर (subah kii sair) crowds doing योग together) to the annual International Yoga Day, 21 June, which is a genuine civic event here with government-organized sessions. The word carries real weight beyond fitness -- it's tied to a whole philosophical tradition in Hindu thought, not just stretching.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say योगा (yogaa) borrowing the English pronunciation; the correct Hindi word is योग (yog), pronounced with a short final vowel, not an 'ah' sound. 2) करना (karnaa) is the verb learners reach for, and it's correct here: योग करना (yog karnaa, 'to do yoga') -- but note it's करना, not खेलना (khelnaa) or कोई और verb learners might guess.

Sense 2 : sum, total (mathematics)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जोड़ (jod)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll meet in a child's math textbook: दो संख्याओं का योग (do sankhyaaon kaa yog, 'the sum of two numbers'). It sits one register above kitchen-table Hindi -- much like 'sum' versus 'total' in English classroom vs. casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) In school-math contexts योग (yog) is the formal/textbook word for 'sum'; in everyday speech जोड़ (jod) or जोड़ना (jodnaa, 'to add') is far more common. Learners who only know the everyday word may not recognize योग on a math worksheet, or vice versa overuse योग in casual speech where जोड़ sounds more natural.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : union, combination, conjunction (of forces, stars, elements)
noun, masculine Common in astrology and formal/literary contexts to describe a favorable combination of planets or circumstances, e.g. शुभ योग (shubh yog, 'an auspicious conjunction').
Synonyms: मिलाप (milaap), संयोग (sanyog, overlapping sense of 'coincidence/conjunction')
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll hear this sense from a pandit (pandit) or in astrology talk -- ग्रहों का योग (grahon kaa yog, 'a conjunction of planets') -- when discussing whether the stars are aligned for a wedding date or a new venture. It's a more literary/formal register than the fitness or math senses.
