बुरा (buraa)

adjective/noun The everyday word for 'bad,' with a strong idiomatic side: it is the word Hindi reaches for when someone's feelings get hurt (बुरा लगना (buraa lagnaa)) or when a habit or company turns out to be harmful.
Sense 1 : bad, unpleasant, of poor quality
adjective
Synonyms: गलत (galat, wrong/incorrect), खराब (kharaab), निकृष्ट (nikrisht, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: बुरा (buraa) carries a moral or emotional weight that खराब (kharaab) usually doesn't -- a खराब (kharaab) फ्रिज (phrij) is just a broken fridge, but a बुरा (buraa) आदमी (aadmii) is a bad person in character. Parents and teachers use it constantly to warn against बुरी संगत (burii sangat, 'bad company').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for खराब (kharaab) in every context, but बुरा (buraa) is the better choice for moral or emotional badness (बुरी आदत (burii aadat, 'bad habit'), बुरा आदमी (buraa aadmii, 'bad man')), while खराब (kharaab) leans toward things being broken, spoiled, or malfunctioning.

Sense 2 : offended, hurt, upset (in the fixed idiom बुरा लगना/मानना)
adjective Occurs almost exclusively inside the dative-subject frame X को बुरा लगना (X ko buraa lagnaa, 'X feels hurt/offended') and the related बुरा मानना (buraa maannaa, 'to take offense'), where बुरा (buraa) stays in its default masculine singular form regardless of the subject.
Synonyms: नाराज़ (naaraaz, mainly describes anger rather than hurt feelings)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most useful emotional-register phrases in everyday Hindi: बुरा मत मानना (buraa mat maannaa, 'don't take this the wrong way') is what you say right before delivering a mildly critical or awkward remark, much like English 'no offense, but...'. It softens social friction the way Delhi conversations often do, with a polite cushion before the real point.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners familiar with को-लगना constructions correctly build मुझे बुरा लगा (mujhe buraa lagaa, 'I felt bad/hurt'), but then mistakenly inflect बुरा (buraa) to agree with a feminine or plural subject -- it stays बुरा (buraa) here because it's functioning adverbially with लगना (lagnaa), not agreeing with a noun. 2) मत बुरा मानना (mat buraa maannaa, 'please don't be offended') is a fixed politeness formula worth learning as a chunk, not building word-by-word.
Sample Sentences:
बुरा मत मानना, लेकिन तुम्हारी शर्ट का रंग अजीब है। Don't take this the wrong way, but the color of your shirt is weird.
(buraa mat maannaa, lekin tumhaarii shart kaa rang ajiib hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : evil, harm, ill (as an abstract noun)
noun, masculine Used in set phrases like किसी का बुरा चाहना (kisii kaa buraa chaahnaa, 'to wish ill on someone') and किसी का बुरा करना (kisii kaa buraa karnaa, 'to do harm to someone'), where बुरा (buraa) functions as a noun meaning 'ill/harm' rather than as an adjective modifying anything.
Synonyms: नुकसान (nuksaan, harm/loss, broader everyday use), अनिष्ट (anisht, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: किसी का बुरा मत सोचो (kisii kaa buraa mat socho, 'don't wish ill on anyone') is the kind of moral advice grandparents give constantly -- बुरा (buraa) here slides from 'bad' the adjective into 'harm' the noun, much like English 'wish someone ill.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to make बुरा (buraa) agree with the object of चाहना (chaahnaa) or करना (karnaa) as if it were a normal adjective; in these fixed expressions it stays invariant as an abstract noun.
