चोट लगना (chot lagnaa)

verb A compound expression built from चोट (chot, 'injury/hurt') plus लगना (lagnaa, the involuntary 'to strike/affect' verb); it covers both physical injury and emotional hurt, always framing the hurt as something that happens to the person rather than something they do.
Sense 1 : to get physically hurt/injured
verb, intransitive The person who gets hurt is marked with को (ko): मुझे चोट लगी (mujhe chot lagii, 'I got hurt'). The body part or cause can be added with पर (par) or से (se): पैर पर चोट लगी (pair par chot lagii, 'got hurt on the leg').
Synonyms: ज़ख्मी होना (zakhmii honaa), घायल होना (ghaayal honaa, slightly more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic 'involuntary verb' pattern in Hindi -- injury is treated like hunger or sleep, something that arrives and befalls you (को (ko) + verb), not something you actively do. A worried mother's first question after a fall is almost always चोट तो नहीं लगी? (chot to nahiin lagii?, 'you didn't get hurt, did you?').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to make the hurt person the subject with ने (ne), producing मैंने चोट लगी (mainne chot lagii) -- wrong; the correct dative-subject frame is मुझे चोट लगी (mujhe chot lagii), since the injury happens *to* you, you don't perform it. 2) Confusing चोट लगना (chot lagnaa) with चोट पहुँचाना (chot pahunchaanaa) or चोट देना (chot denaa) -- the latter two are transitive, 'to cause hurt to someone else.'

Sense 2 : to feel emotionally hurt/wounded
verb, intransitive Same को (ko)-marked frame as the physical sense: उसकी बात से मुझे चोट लगी (uskii baat se mujhe chot lagii, 'his words hurt me'), with the cause marked by से (se).
Synonyms: बुरा लगना (buraa lagnaa), दुःख होना (duhkh honaa, somewhat more formal/literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Here चोट (chot) extends the 'wound' metaphor from body to heart -- Hindi speakers talk about दिल पर चोट लगना (dil par chot lagnaa, 'to be struck on the heart') the same way English speakers say something 'wounded' them, showing how naturally the physical injury frame carries over to emotional pain.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बुरा लगना (buraa lagnaa) as a blanket substitute, but चोट लगना (chot lagnaa) implies a deeper, more wounding hurt (like betrayal or insult), not mere mild annoyance.
