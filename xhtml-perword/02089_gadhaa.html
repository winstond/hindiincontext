गधा (gadhaa)

noun Literally 'donkey' (the animal); the same word doubles as a common, mild insult for a foolish or stubborn person.
Sense 1 : fool, dimwit (used as an insult)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), उल्लू (ulloo, 'owl', also used as fool), मूर्ख (moorkh, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic taane (taane, 'taunt') word every Delhi kid has heard from a frustrated teacher or parent -- 'गधा हो क्या?' (gadhaa ho kyaa?, 'are you a donkey or what?') -- treating stubbornness and stupidity as the donkey's defining traits, much like English uses 'ass' or 'dumb ox.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Calling someone गधा (gadhaa) is genuinely rude, not a gentle tease like English 'silly goose' -- Hindi speakers reserve it for real irritation or scolding, most often from an elder to a child or between close friends where the familiarity softens it. 2) Feminine referents take गधी (gadhii), not गधा (gadhaa) -- the insult still agrees in gender like the animal noun does.
Sample Sentences:
गधा हो क्या? Are you a donkey or what?
(gadhaa ho kyaa?)

Sense 2 : donkey (the animal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: गदहा (gadahaa, rural variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will still see गधे (gadhe) carrying bricks and sand at small construction sites and in villages around Delhi-NCR -- the animal is a familiar working presence, not just a storybook character, which is part of why it slid so easily into the insult sense below.
