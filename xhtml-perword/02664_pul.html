पुल (pul)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'bridge,' used both for physical structures and, less commonly, in figurative extensions like connecting people or ideas.
Sense 1 : bridge (physical structure)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सेतु (setu, formal/literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, पुल (pul) shows up constantly in traffic talk -- रेलवे पुल (relve pul, 'railway bridge/overpass') and पुल के नीचे (pul ke niiche, 'under the bridge') are common landmarks people use when giving directions, much like Americans might say 'past the overpass.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for सेतु (setu) in everyday speech because it looks textbook-familiar, but पुल (pul) is what you will actually hear when someone gives directions or talks about traffic, like पुल पार करना (pul paar karnaa, 'to cross the bridge').

Sense 2 : bridge (figurative: connection between people, ideas, or groups)
noun, masculine Used in phrases like पुल बनना (pul bannaa, 'to become a bridge/link') or पुल का काम करना (pul kaa kaam karnaa, 'to serve as a link'), describing a person or thing that connects two sides.
Synonyms: कड़ी (kadii, connecting link)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in speeches or news commentary -- someone might be called दोनों देशों के बीच पुल (donon deshon ke biich pul, 'a bridge between the two countries'), the same metaphor English uses, so it transfers easily for learners.
