खड़ा होना (khadaa honaa)

verb A compound formed from the adjective खड़ा (khadaa, 'standing/upright') plus होना (honaa, 'to be/become'); its core sense is 'to stand up / be standing,' with figurative extensions to something 'coming up/arising' (a problem, a queue) and to a vehicle 'stopping/parking.'
Sense 1 : to stand up, rise to a standing position
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: उठना (uthnaa, to get up)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a crowded Delhi metro or bus, you will constantly hear खड़े हो जाइए (khade ho jaaie, 'please stand up') or जगह दो, खड़ा होना है (jagah do, khadaa honaa hai, 'make room, I need to stand') -- this is one of the most physically enacted verbs in daily commuting life.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with उठना (uthnaa): उठना (uthnaa) is the general act of 'getting up' from lying/sitting, while खड़ा होना (khadaa honaa) specifically emphasizes ending up in an upright, standing position. 2) Since खड़ा (khadaa) is an adjective here, it agrees in gender/number with the subject rather than staying fixed -- learners sometimes leave it as खड़ा (khadaa) even with a feminine or plural subject.

Sense 2 : to be in a standing position (state)
verb, intransitive Often appears in the perfect participle form खड़ा है (khadaa hai, 'is standing') to describe an ongoing stance rather than the motion of standing up; the distinction between the inceptive action and the resulting state is carried by aspect, not by a different verb.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of a rickshaw-wallah waiting at a stand: वह वहाँ खड़ा है (vah vahaan khadaa hai, 'he is standing there') describes his ongoing state, while वह खड़ा हो गया (vah khadaa ho gayaa, 'he stood up') describes the moment he rose. Hindi keeps these conceptually distinct even though English blurs them.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for a continuous tense (खड़ा हो रहा है) when they mean the simple state 'is standing' (खड़ा है (khadaa hai)) -- the perfect form, not the progressive, is what natives use for the static state.

Sense 3 : for a vehicle to stop or be parked
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: रुकना (ruknaa, to stop), पार्क होना (paark honaa, colloquial, for cars)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see hand-painted signs in Delhi markets reading यहाँ गाड़ी खड़ी करना मना है (yahaan gaadii khadii karnaa manaa hai, 'parking a vehicle here is forbidden') -- खड़ा करना (khadaa karnaa) is the transitive causative counterpart, 'to park/stand something,' which pairs naturally with this intransitive sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners assume this only applies to people, but गाड़ी खड़ी है (gaadii khadii hai, 'the car is parked/stopped') is extremely common -- the vehicle 'stands' the way a person does. 2) Note the gender agreement: गाड़ी (gaadii) is feminine, so it is खड़ी (khadii), not खड़ा (khadaa).

Sense 4 : for a problem, situation, or need to arise/come up
verb, intransitive Common with abstract subjects like समस्या (samasyaa, 'problem'), सवाल (savaal, 'question'), or मुश्किल (mushkil, 'difficulty').
Synonyms: उठना (uthnaa, to arise, of an issue), पैदा होना (paidaa honaa, to be created/arise)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Office and family conversations alike are full of lines like एक नई समस्या खड़ी हो गई है (ek naii samasyaa khadii ho gaii hai, 'a new problem has come up') -- Hindi visualizes an abstract problem as literally 'standing up' in front of you, blocking your path, which is a vivid, physical way of framing trouble that differs from English's more abstract 'arise.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'a problem arose' too literally and reach for उत्पन्न होना (utpann honaa, very formal) when the everyday spoken choice is मुश्किल खड़ी हो गई (mushkil khadii ho gaii, 'a difficulty came up').
