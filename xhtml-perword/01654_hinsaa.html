हिंसा (hinsaa)

noun A single core meaning: violence or physical harm, both as a general concept and as a specific act. Note the near-homophone अहिंसा (ahinsaa, 'nonviolence'), the famous Gandhian concept, which is simply this word with the negating prefix अ- (a).
Sense 1 : violence (general concept or specific acts of harm)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मार-पीट (maar-piit, physical beating/scuffle), ज़ुल्म (zulm, oppression/cruelty), हिंसात्मक कार्य (hinsaatmak kaary, violent act, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is inseparable from its opposite, अहिंसा (ahinsaa), the principle of nonviolence that Gandhi placed at the center of India's freedom struggle -- you'll see both words together constantly in civics textbooks, political speeches, and news coverage of protests. In everyday conversation हिंसा (hinsaa) shows up often in news contexts: सांप्रदायिक हिंसा (saamprdaayik hinsaa, 'communal violence') and घरेलू हिंसा (ghareloo hinsaa, 'domestic violence') are two very common collocations worth learning as set phrases.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse हिंसा (hinsaa) with गुस्सा (gussaa, 'anger') -- हिंसा is the physical act or its threat, not the emotion behind it. 2) The negated form अहिंसा (ahinsaa) is written and pronounced as one word, not हिंसा with a separate अ (a) attached loosely -- learners sometimes write them as two words.

(pulis ne shahar men badhtii hinsaa par kaaboo paane kii koshish kii।)
