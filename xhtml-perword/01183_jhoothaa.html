झूठा (jhoothaa)

adjective/noun Primarily an adjective meaning 'false/lying' (of statements) or 'lying/dishonest' (of people), with a variable-gender noun sense meaning 'a liar', and a distinct culturally important adjective sense describing food/drink that has touched someone's mouth (hence 'contaminated by another's touch').
Sense 1 : false, untrue (of a statement, claim, promise)
adjective
Synonyms: मिथ्या (mithyaa, literary), बेबुनियाद (bebuniyaad, baseless)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in everyday arguments: यह बात झूठी है (yah baat jhoothii hai, 'this thing/statement is false') -- note the agreement with बात (baat, feminine), a common trip-up since the base form झूठा (jhoothaa) looks masculine by default.
Common Mistakes: 1) झूठा (jhoothaa) describes the statement or claim as false, while a person who habitually lies is called झूठा (jhoothaa) in the separate noun/person sense -- learners sometimes reach for असत्य (asaty) in speech, which sounds bookish; झूठा (jhoothaa) is the everyday word.
Sample Sentences:

(yah baat bilkul jhoothii hai।)

Sense 3 : a liar
noun, masculine/feminine This noun sense is the same adjective used substantively (e.g. वह झूठा है (vah jhoothaa hai, 'he is a liar')) and takes m/f agreement matching the person referred to, rather than being a fixed-gender noun.
Synonyms: फरेबी (pharebii, deceiver), मक्कार (makkaar, deceitful person)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kids trade this insult on the playground just like English-speaking kids say 'liar, liar' -- तू झूठा है (too jhoothaa hai, 'you're a liar') is the classic schoolyard line.

Sense 4 : touched by someone's mouth, hence ritually/socially contaminated (of food or a vessel)
adjective Used almost always attributively with food/drink nouns, e.g. झूठा खाना (jhoothaa khaanaa, 'food someone has already put their mouth to'), and inflects to agree with that noun.
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a real, live idea in Indian households: parents will say मत खाओ, यह झूठा है (mat khaao, yah jhoothaa hai, 'don't eat it, it's already been bitten into') about food someone else has taken a bite of, tied to older ideas about saliva and purity that still shape sharing food and drink at the table.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense has nothing to do with lying -- it describes food, a glass, or a plate that has touched someone else's mouth or saliva, a concept with no single-word English equivalent, so learners often miss it entirely and default to the 'false' meaning; 2) Confusable with जूठा (joothaa), an alternate spelling without the ‘झ’ softening some speakers use for exactly this sense -- both are heard in Delhi-NCR.
Sample Sentences:
मत खाओ, यह झूठा है। Don't eat it, it's already been bitten into.
(mat khaao, yah jhoothaa hai।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active
