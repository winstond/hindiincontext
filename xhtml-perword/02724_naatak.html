नाटक (naatak)

noun One core meaning ('play/drama') that extends naturally into a very common figurative sense: a fake show of emotion or a dramatic fuss. Both senses share the same noun.
Sense 1 : melodrama, fuss, fake show of emotion
noun, masculine Very commonly used with करना (karnaa) as नाटक करना (naatak karnaa, 'to make a scene / put on an act') to describe someone exaggerating emotions or pretending.
Synonyms: ड्रामा (draamaa), तमाशा (tamaashaa, colloquial), बहाना (bahaanaa, meaning scope: excuse, not identical)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you will hear constantly at home: a mother telling a child रोना बंद करो, नाटक मत करो (ronaa band karo, naatak mat karo, 'stop crying, stop making a scene') is a classic bit of Delhi household speech. It captures a very Hindi idea -- performance and real emotion blurring into one word.
Common Mistakes: 1) नाटक करना (naatak karnaa) does not mean 'to perform a play' in this sense -- it means someone is being fake or overdramatic, e.g. crying for attention or exaggerating an illness. Context (tone, situation) tells you which sense is meant. 2) Learners often miss that this sense can be used affectionately or with mild irritation, both common in family settings.

Sense 2 : play, drama (theatrical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: नाट्य (naaty, literary), ड्रामा (draamaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Think of school and college culture in Delhi: every annual function has a नाटक (naatak) performed by students, often based on mythology or a social message. It is the everyday word you would use for anything from a Shakespeare play to a local street theater piece.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume नाटक (naatak) only means a stage play, but it is also the standard word for a TV drama or serial in casual speech, e.g. टीवी नाटक (tiivii naatak, 'TV drama').
