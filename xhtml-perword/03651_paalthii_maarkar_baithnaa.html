पालथी मारकर बैठना (paalthii maarkar baithnaa)

verb A common expression meaning to sit cross-legged on the floor, typically the way one sits on a durrie (darii) or takhat (takht) for a meal, prayer, or a relaxed chat.
Sense 1 : to sit cross-legged (on the floor)
verb, intransitive पालथी (paalthii) is the noun for the cross-legged posture itself; मारना (maarnaa) here is a light verb (like in ताली मारना (taalii maarnaa)) giving the sense of 'assuming' the posture, and बैठना (baithnaa) supplies the actual sitting-down action.
Synonyms: पालथी लगाकर बैठना (paalthii lagaakar baithnaa, variant with लगाना), सुखासन में बैठना (sukhaasan men baithnaa, yoga/formal register)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic posture for sitting on the floor in a North Indian home -- around a low table for a meal, on a mat during a puja (poojaa), or on a charpai (chaarpaaii) while chatting with elders. Many households still eat this way at least occasionally, especially during festivals, and it is considered a comfortable, grounded, informal way to sit, in contrast to sitting in a chair.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to translate मारना (maarnaa) literally as 'to hit' -- here it is just a light verb bleached of its literal meaning, so 'पालथी मारकर बैठना' (paalthii maarkar baithnaa) simply means 'to sit down cross-legged,' with no violence implied. 2) The कर (kar) form is fixed here: you cannot swap in another connective without sounding odd, since this is a set collocation, not a free combination of two independent clauses.
