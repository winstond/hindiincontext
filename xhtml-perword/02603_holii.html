होली (holii)

noun होली (holii) is primarily the name of the spring festival of colors; it also appears in a couple of fixed idiomatic turns of phrase built on the festival image.
Sense 1 : the Hindu spring festival of colors
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: होली (holii) falls in the Hindu month of Phalgun (roughly March) and marks the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil, commemorated the night before with होलिका दहन (holikaa dahan, 'the burning of Holika') bonfires. In Delhi-NCR you will hear people greet each other with होली मुबारक (holii mubaarak, 'Happy Holi') or बुरा न मानो होली है (buraa n maano holii hai, "don't take offense, it's Holi") as a cheerful excuse for the day's mischief.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say होली मनाना (holii manaanaa, 'to celebrate Holi') but reach for the English-influenced 'do Holi' pattern instead -- मनाना (manaanaa) is the verb that pairs with festival names in Hindi, not करना (karnaa). 2) होली खेलना (holii khelnaa, 'to play Holi') is the everyday way to describe throwing colors and water on people -- खेलना (khelnaa) here does not mean a game in the English sense, it is the fixed idiom for participating in the color-throwing itself.

Sense 2 : used figuratively for a riotous, colorful, or chaotic scene
noun, feminine Occurs mainly in the set phrase खून की होली (khoon kii holii, 'a Holi of blood', i.e. a bloodbath/massacre), extending the image of colors flying everywhere to violence or chaos.
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You will meet this mostly in news commentary or dramatic writing, where खून की होली खेलना (khoon kii holii khelnaa, 'to play a Holi of blood') describes a massacre or violent riot -- the festival's image of color splashed everywhere is turned dark. It is a literary flourish rather than something you would casually say at the dinner table.
