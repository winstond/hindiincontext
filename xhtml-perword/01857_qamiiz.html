क़मीज़ (qamiiz)

noun A borrowed noun meaning 'shirt', used the same way in daily speech as its English counterpart. Often seen paired with a lower garment like पैंट (paint) or पजामा (pajaamaa).
Sense 1 : shirt
noun, feminine
Synonyms: शर्ट (shart)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word came into Hindi from Arabic via Urdu/Persian, and you will hear it constantly in everyday clothes-shopping talk -- at a बाज़ार (baazaar) a shopkeeper might ask आपको कौन से रंग की क़मीज़ चाहिए? (aapko kaun se rang kii qamiiz chaahie?, 'What color shirt do you want?'). It pairs naturally with पैंट (paint) for Western wear, in contrast to the more traditional कुर्ता-पजामा (kurtaa-pajaamaa) combination.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write/say कमीज़ (kamiiz) instead of क़मीज़ (qamiiz) -- both are heard in casual speech, but the nuqta-marked spelling stays closer to the Urdu-derived pronunciation with a uvular 'q' sound. 2) Do not confuse with कुर्ता (kurtaa), which is the traditional loose-fitting Indian shirt worn with पजामा (pajaamaa) or धोती (dhotii) -- क़मीज़ (qamiiz) usually implies a Western-style button-up shirt.
