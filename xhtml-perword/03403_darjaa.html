दर्जा (darjaa)

noun Means 'rank, grade, or status' -- used for social standing, official grades/classes, and hierarchical position. Closely related to दर्जी (darjii, tailor) only by coincidence of sound, not meaning -- no connection.
Sense 1 : rank, status, standing
noun, masculine
Synonyms: स्तर (star), हैसियत (haisiyat), पद (pad, more official/positional)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news and civic contexts -- राज्य का दर्जा (raajy kaa darjaa, 'statehood/state status'), जिस चीज़ को हम रोज़ इस्तेमाल में लाते हैं, उसे प्राथमिकता का दर्जा मिलना चाहिए (jis chiiz ko ham roz istemaal men laate hain, use praathmiktaa kaa darjaa milnaa chaahie, 'the thing we use daily should be given priority status') -- for instance, debates over giving a language or a territory a formal दर्जा (darjaa) come up often in Delhi political conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्थिति (sthiti) when they mean social standing, but स्थिति (sthiti) is about 'situation/condition,' not hierarchical rank -- दर्जा (darjaa) is specifically about where something or someone sits on a scale. 2) The common phrase is दर्जा देना (darjaa denaa, 'to grant/accord status') or दर्जा पाना (darjaa paanaa, 'to attain status') -- learners sometimes force a plain 'hona' construction instead of these set verb pairings.

(dillii ko abhii tak poorn raajy kaa darjaa nahiin milaa hai।)

Sense 2 : grade, class (school), or class/category in a graded system
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कक्षा (kakshaa, school grade specifically), श्रेणी (shrenii, category/class)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will meet this in phrases like पहले दर्जे का डिब्बा (pahle darje kaa dibbaa, 'first-class compartment') on Indian Railways, or पहले दर्जे का बदमाश (pahle darje kaa badmaash, 'a first-rate scoundrel') -- the 'first-class' sense has jumped from train travel into everyday superlative expressions, much like English 'first-rate'.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday school contexts, कक्षा (kakshaa) is far more common for 'which grade a child is in' -- दर्जा (darjaa) here tends to sound slightly old-fashioned or is used more for formal/administrative grading (train classes, quality grades) than for a child's school year.
