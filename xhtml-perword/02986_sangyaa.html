संज्ञा (sangyaa)

noun Primarily the grammatical term for 'noun'; also carries a less common sense of 'consciousness/senses' seen mainly in the fixed idiom for fainting.
Sense 1 : noun (grammatical term)
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word every Hindi-medium schoolkid learns in grammar class, right alongside सर्वनाम (sarvnaam, 'pronoun') and क्रिया (kriyaa, 'verb'). If you're studying Hindi grammar terminology, this is the first rung on that ladder.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with विशेषण (visheshan, 'adjective') when discussing grammar -- संज्ञा (sangyaa) specifically means 'noun.' 2) This word is almost always encountered in grammar lessons and textbooks rather than casual conversation, so it can feel oddly formal if used outside that context.

Sense 2 : consciousness, awareness (of one's senses)
noun, feminine Almost exclusively seen in the set phrase संज्ञा खोना (sangyaa khonaa, 'to lose consciousness, faint').
Synonyms: होश (hosh)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You'll meet this sense in newspaper reports or formal writing describing someone fainting -- वह संज्ञा खो बैठा (vah sangyaa kho baithaa, 'he lost consciousness') -- rather than in casual chat, where होश (hosh) dominates.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is essentially locked into the idiom संज्ञा खोना (sangyaa khonaa) -- learners shouldn't expect to use संज्ञा (sangyaa) alone to mean 'consciousness' in everyday speech; होश (hosh) is the word people actually reach for.
