मानना (maannaa)

verb Covers a cluster of related meanings: to believe/consider something to be true, to accept or agree to something, to obey or heed someone, and to celebrate/observe (a festival or occasion).
Sense 1 : to believe, consider, regard as
verb, transitive Often takes the frame X को Y मानना (X ko Y maannaa) meaning 'to consider X as Y', e.g. treating someone as a friend, god, fool, etc.
Synonyms: समझना (samajhnaa, to consider/understand), विश्वास करना (vishvaas karnaa, to have faith/belief)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मानना (maannaa) sits at the heart of how Hindi expresses personal conviction -- मैं मानता हूँ (main maantaa hoon, 'I believe/hold the view') is the everyday way to voice an opinion, softer and more common in conversation than the heavier विश्वास करना (vishvaas karnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for विश्वास करना (vishvaas karnaa) when मानना (maannaa) alone is more natural for 'I believe/think that...', especially with कि (ki) clauses. 2) In the 'consider as' sense, the structure is X को Y मानना (X ko Y maannaa), not X Y मानना -- dropping को (ko) is a common slip.

मैं मानता हूँ कि यह सही फ़ैसला है। I believe that this is the right decision.
(main maantaa hoon ki yah sahii faislaa hai।)

Sense 2 : to accept, agree to, give in to
verb, transitive Used for accepting a request, condition, or proposal -- बात मानना (baat maannaa) is a common collocation meaning 'to accept what someone says' or 'to comply'.
Synonyms: स्वीकार करना (sviikaar karnaa, more formal), राज़ी होना (raazii honaa, to become willing/agree)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the classic parental complaint मेरी बात नहीं मानता (merii baat nahiin maantaa, 'he doesn't listen to/obey me') -- a phrase every Hindi-speaking parent has uttered about a stubborn child.
Common Mistakes: 1) बात मानना (baat maannaa) means 'to listen to/comply with someone's word', not literally 'to believe a matter' -- learners sometimes misread it through the 'believe' sense. 2) With this sense, the person whose request is accepted is marked with की बात (kii baat) or का कहना (kaa kahnaa), not directly as an object.

Sense 3 : to obey, heed, listen to (a person or their words)
verb, transitive Takes की बात मानना (kii baat maannaa) or का कहना मानना (kaa kahnaa maannaa) as its typical frame when the object is a person being obeyed.
Synonyms: आज्ञा मानना (aagyaa maannaa, to obey an order, more formal), सुनना (sunnaa, colloquial extension meaning 'to listen to/heed')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with the 'accept/agree' sense above -- both trace back to the same root idea of yielding to someone's word, so in real speech the line between 'agreeing' and 'obeying' with मानना (maannaa) is often blurry and context-dependent.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use सुनना (sunnaa) alone for 'obey', but मानना (maannaa) is the stronger, more idiomatic choice when compliance (not just hearing) is meant.

Sense 4 : to celebrate, observe (a festival, occasion, or event)
verb, transitive Used with festival or occasion nouns: त्योहार मानना (tyohaar maannaa, 'to observe a festival'); in everyday speech the causative मनाना (manaanaa) is actually more common for this exact meaning.
Synonyms: मनाना (manaanaa, causative form, to celebrate/please -- often used interchangeably in casual speech for festivals)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Keep मानना (maannaa) and मनाना (manaanaa) firmly distinct in your ear: they look like a minimal pair but मनाना (manaanaa) is the everyday verb for 'to celebrate' or 'to persuade/coax', while this sense of मानना (maannaa) for celebration is the less common, more literary cousin.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners frequently confuse this with the causative मनाना (manaanaa) -- 'we celebrate Diwali' is far more often हम दिवाली मनाते हैं (ham divaalii manaate hain) than ...मानते हैं (maante hain); this मानना (maannaa) sense for 'celebrate' is comparatively less common and can sound slightly formal or old-fashioned.
