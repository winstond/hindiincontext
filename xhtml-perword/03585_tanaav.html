तनाव (tanaav)

noun Primarily means 'stress' or 'tension' -- both the psychological/emotional sense and the physical/political sense of strain between two things. One core sense with wide application, rather than several unrelated meanings.
Sense 1 : mental/emotional stress, tension
noun, masculine
Synonyms: तनावग्रस्तता (tanaavagrasttaa, rare), दबाव (dabaav, pressure -- overlaps but not identical), चिंता (chintaa, worry -- related but distinct)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: तनाव मत लो (tanaav mat lo, 'don't take tension') is something you'll hear constantly in Delhi -- from friends, from doctors, from worried mothers. The idea of 'taking' tension, as if it's a burden you pick up, is very much how Hindi frames stress: it's something that comes to you or that you take on, not something you simply 'are'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for स्ट्रेस (stres) as a borrowed English word in casual speech, which is common, but तनाव (tanaav) is the standard word you'll see in writing, on doctors' forms, and in more careful speech. 2) तनाव लेना (tanaav lenaa, 'to take tension') is the natural collocation for 'to get stressed' -- not तनाव करना (tanaav karnaa).
Sample Sentences:
तनाव मत लो, सब ठीक हो जाएगा। Don't take stress, everything will be fine.
(tanaav mat lo, sab thiik ho jaaegaa।)

Sense 2 : physical or political tension/strain
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खिंचाव (khinchaav, physical pulling/strain), तनातनी (tanaatnii, strained relations, informal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll see this sense in the news constantly -- सीमा पर तनाव (siimaa par tanaav, 'tension at the border') is a stock phrase in Hindi journalism, paralleling how English news uses 'tensions'. It's the same underlying idea as the emotional sense: something stretched taut, whether a nerve, a rope, or a relationship.
Sample Sentences:
सीमा पर तनाव बढ़ रहा है। Tension is increasing at the border.
(siimaa par tanaav badh rahaa hai।)

(donon deshon ke biich tanaav kaii saalon se banaa huaa hai।)
Register: Formal -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
