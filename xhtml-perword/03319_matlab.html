मतलब (matlab)

noun/conjunction Mainly a noun meaning 'meaning/point/purpose', but also used constantly as a conversational filler meaning 'I mean...' and in the fixed pattern मतलब यह है कि (matlab yah hai ki) 'the point is that'.
Sense 1 : meaning, sense (of a word or statement)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: अर्थ (arth), मायने (maayne, often used in plural form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most useful nouns in the language because it also becomes a verb-like construction: X का मतलब है (X kaa matlab hai, 'X means...') is the everyday way to ask or explain meaning, much more common than अर्थ (arth) in spoken Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often ask क्या मतलब है? (kyaa matlab hai?, 'what does it mean?') without किसका (kiskaa) or इसका (iskaa) to specify what is being asked about; native speakers usually say इसका क्या मतलब है? (iskaa kyaa matlab hai?, 'what does this mean?').

Sense 2 : purpose, intention, motive (often self-interested)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: इरादा (iraadaa), उद्देश्य (uddeshy, formal), स्वार्थ (svaarth, self-interest specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear उसका मतलब क्या है? (uskaa matlab kyaa hai?, 'what's his angle?') used suspiciously about someone's real intentions -- Hindi leans on मतलब (matlab) the way English leans on 'angle' or 'agenda' in this sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) मतलबी (matalbii, 'selfish, self-serving') is a common derived adjective learners meet separately -- it builds on this sense of मतलब (matlab) as 'personal motive', not the 'meaning' sense.

Sense 3 : conversational filler / clarifier: 'I mean', 'that is'
conjunction Used mid-sentence to pause, rephrase, or clarify, much like English 'I mean' -- functions as discourse filler rather than a true grammatical connector.
Synonyms: यानी (yaanii), मेरा मतलब है (meraa matlab hai, fuller phrase version)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Listen for मतलब (matlab) sprinkled through casual Delhi speech almost like a verbal tic -- it buys thinking time the same way English speakers say 'like' or 'I mean'.
Common Mistakes: 1) Overusing this as a direct translation of English 'I mean' in formal writing sounds too casual; it belongs in speech. 2) Learners sometimes confuse this filler use with यानी (yaanii), which is more for restating/defining something precisely rather than casually backtracking mid-thought.
