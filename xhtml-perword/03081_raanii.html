रानी (raanii)

noun One core meaning: queen, in both the literal royal sense and the affectionate/figurative sense used for a beloved girl or woman.
Sense 1 : queen (royal ruler or king's wife)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: महारानी (mahaaraanii, empress/great queen)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: रानी (raanii) is a fixture of Hindi folk tales and children's stories -- राजा-रानी (raajaa-raanii, 'king and queen') is the classic opening pair of every ऐसे थे राजा और रानी (aise the raajaa aur raanii) style story. The word also survives in Indian chess and card games as the term for the 'queen' piece/card.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume रानी (raanii) always means a ruling monarch, but historically it most often referred to the king's wife rather than a female sovereign in her own right -- context (as in a folk tale vs. a history lesson) decides which sense is meant.

Sense 2 : affectionate term for a girl or woman (like 'princess' or 'my queen')
noun, feminine Used as a direct address or affectionate nickname, often for a daughter or young girl, similar to calling someone 'princess' in English.
Synonyms: गुड़िया (gudiyaa, used for young girls, 'doll'), राजकुमारी (raajkumaarii, closer to 'princess')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Delhi-NCR parents and grandparents often call a small daughter मेरी रानी बेटी (merii raanii betii, 'my little queen/darling daughter') or just रानी (raanii) as a pet name -- it carries the same doting warmth as 'sweetheart' or 'princess' in English, with no royal connotation at all.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is warm and casual, not literal -- calling a little girl मेरी रानी (merii raanii, 'my queen/darling') has nothing to do with royalty, so learners should not read it as a claim about status.
