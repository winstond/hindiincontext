कहावत (kahaavat)

noun A single-sense word meaning 'proverb, saying' -- the fixed, traditional turns of phrase Hindi speakers reach for to make a point memorably.
Sense 1 : proverb, saying
noun, feminine
Synonyms: लोकोक्ति (lokokti, formal), मुहावरा (muhaavraa, not a true synonym -- means 'idiom', often confused)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Hindi conversation, especially from grandparents and elders, is seasoned with कहावतें (kahaavten) -- proverbs about crows, wells, camels, and needles that carry village wisdom into everyday city talk. Starting a sentence with कहते हैं (kahte hain, 'they say') or एक कहावत है (ek kahaavat hai, 'there is a saying') is a classic way to lend authority to advice, much like invoking 'as they say' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mix this up with मुहावरा (muhaavraa): a कहावत (kahaavat) is a complete saying with its own wisdom or moral (like 'a stitch in time saves nine'), while a मुहावरा (muhaavraa) is an idiom, a fixed phrase that needs to be plugged into a sentence (like 'to pull someone's leg'). 2) The natural verb pairing is कहावत है (kahaavat hai, 'the saying goes') or कहावत के अनुसार (kahaavat ke anusaar, 'according to the saying') -- learners sometimes try to force other verbs where these set phrases are expected.
