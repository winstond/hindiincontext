मातृभाषा (maatribhaashaa)

noun A compound noun meaning 'mother tongue' -- mातृ (maatri, 'mother,' a bound Sanskrit form) plus भाषा (bhaashaa, 'language'). It has just one everyday sense.
Sense 1 : mother tongue, native language
noun, feminine
Synonyms: मादरी ज़बान (maadrii zabaan, colloquial, Urdu-flavored equivalent), निज भाषा (nij bhaashaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a country with over twenty official languages, asking आपकी मातृभाषा क्या है (aapkii maatribhaashaa kyaa hai, 'What is your mother tongue?') is a completely normal getting-to-know-you question in India, unlike in many English-speaking contexts where it might seem intrusive. It often comes right after someone's name and hometown in casual introductions, since it signals region, community, and sometimes caste or background all at once.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say अपनी भाषा (apnii bhaashaa) to mean 'mother tongue,' but that just means 'one's own language' (which could be a second or third language one has adopted) -- मातृभाषा (maatribhaashaa) specifically means the language learned from birth/family. 2) The gender is feminine because भाषा (bhaashaa) is feminine -- adjectives and verbs agreeing with this word must take feminine forms, e.g. मेरी मातृभाषा हिंदी है (merii maatribhaashaa hindii hai, 'My mother tongue is Hindi'), not मेरा.
