मुँह (munh)

noun Literally 'mouth/face,' but its real value is in the huge number of everyday idioms built on it -- from politeness ('keep your mouth shut') to emotion ('face fell') to attitude ('mouth on him'). Also spelled मुंह (munh) with a regular nasal instead of the chandrabindu -- both are common.
Sense 1 : mouth (body part)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Elders in North India will often scold a child with मुँह बंद रखो (munh band rakho, 'keep your mouth shut') -- मुँह (munh) stands in for 'speaking' as much as for the physical mouth in daily speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुँह खोलना (munh kholnaa) literally 'to open the mouth' is often used for speaking up rather than just the physical act -- context decides which sense is meant.
Sample Sentences:
उसके मुँह में छाला हो गया है। He has got a sore in his mouth.
(uske munh men chhaalaa ho gayaa hai।)

Sense 2 : face (as the seat of expression)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चेहरा (chehraa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Where English says 'his face fell,' Hindi says मुँह उतर गया (munh utar gayaa, 'the face came down') -- the body-as-agent framing is very typical of how Hindi talks about emotion showing on the face.
Common Mistakes: 1) मुँह उतरना (munh utarnaa, 'the face falls') describes a visible change of expression from disappointment -- learners sometimes reach for चेहरा (chehraa) here, but मुँह (munh) is the natural choice in this idiom.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी बात सुनकर मेरा मुँह उतर गया। Hearing what he said, my face fell.
(uskii baat sunkar meraa munh utar gayaa।)

Sense 3 : opening or mouth of an object (figurative extension)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मुँहाना (munhaanaa, used specifically for a river's mouth)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English calls the opening of a bottle its 'mouth,' Hindi extends मुँह (munh) the same way -- बोतल का मुँह (botal kaa munh, 'the mouth of the bottle') is completely natural and not at all poetic-sounding.
Sample Sentences:
बोतल का मुँह बंद करो, सारा पानी गिर जाएगा। Close the mouth of the bottle, otherwise all the water will spill.
(botal kaa munh band karo, saaraa paanii gir jaaegaa।)

Sense 4 : cheek, audacity, nerve (idiomatic, of a person's attitude)
noun, masculine Common in the pattern तेरी/उसकी यह मुँह (yah munh, 'the nerve of you/him'), often continued with और... ('and you dare...').
Synonyms: हिम्मत (himmat, covers 'courage' more than 'audacity')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense lives almost entirely inside fixed put-downs and taunts rather than free composition -- it is one reason मुँह (munh) shows up in so many idioms about pride, shame, and cheekiness.
Common Mistakes: 1) The idiom यह मुँह और यह बात (yah munh aur yah baat, roughly 'this mouth and this talk,' meaning 'you have some nerve saying that') is a fixed put-down -- learners shouldn't try to build fresh sentences with मुँह (munh) meaning 'audacity' outside of set idioms like this.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी यह कहने की हिम्मत कैसे हुई, मुँह तो देखो! How dare he say that, look at the nerve of him!
(uskii yah kahne kii himmat kaise huii, munh to dekho!)
