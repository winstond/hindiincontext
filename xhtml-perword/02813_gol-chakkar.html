गोल-चक्कर (gol-chakkar)

noun A compound noun literally meaning 'round-circle', used mainly for a traffic roundabout/traffic circle; it can also be used figuratively for beating around the bush or going in circles.
Sense 1 : traffic roundabout
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चौराहा (chauraahaa, intersection/crossing, not exactly the same but often confused), गोलचक्कर (golchakkar, variant spelling without hyphen)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, well-known गोल-चक्कर (gol-chakkar) landmarks (like the ones near Connaught Place) are used as navigation reference points -- people give directions like 'गोल-चक्कर से सीधे चले जाना' (gol-chakkar se siidhe chale jaanaa, 'go straight from the roundabout') rather than using street names.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use चौराहा (chauraahaa) interchangeably, but that word means a four-way crossing/intersection, not a circular roundabout -- the two are physically different road structures in Indian cities. 2) The word is often written without the hyphen as गोलचक्कर (golchakkar); both forms are seen on signboards and in speech.

Sense 2 : figurative: going around in circles, beating around the bush
noun, masculine Used with verbs like खाना (khaanaa) or लगाना (lagaanaa) to mean 'to go in circles' -- e.g. गोल-चक्कर खाना (gol-chakkar khaanaa, to go around in circles/get confused going nowhere).
Synonyms: आना-कानी (aanaa-kaanii, evasiveness, not identical but related), टालमटोल (taalamtol, stalling/evasion)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative use draws directly on the physical image of driving round and round a traffic circle without ever reaching your destination -- a vivid, very Delhi way of describing bureaucratic runaround or someone dodging a straight answer.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense only really works in fixed expressions like गोल-चक्कर खाना (gol-chakkar khaanaa) or गोल-चक्कर में डालना (gol-chakkar men daalnaa, to lead someone in circles/confuse them) -- learners shouldn't use the bare noun alone to mean 'evasion.'
