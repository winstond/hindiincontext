चमचा (chamchaa)

noun Literally a spoon, but the word carries a very lively figurative sense in everyday speech: a sycophant or yes-man (chamchaa) who flatters someone powerful.
Sense 1 : sycophant, flatterer, yes-man
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खुशामदी (khushaamdii), जीहुजूर (jiihujoor, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of Hindi's great political and office-culture insults -- a नेता का चमचा (netaa kaa chamchaa, 'a politician's flunkey') is a stock phrase in Indian news and everyday gossip about people who flatter bosses or leaders to gain favor. The image is vivid: a spoon just scoops up and serves whatever the powerful person wants.
Common Mistakes: 1) This word is mildly insulting -- calling someone चमचा (chamchaa) to their face is a jab, not a neutral description, so learners should be careful using it in polite company. 2) It is almost always used about someone (third person) rather than as a self-description.

Sense 2 : spoon (eating/serving utensil)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चम्मच (chammach)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a Delhi kitchen you will hear both चमचा (chamchaa) and चम्मच (chammach) used interchangeably for a spoon -- there is no real difference in meaning, just personal or regional habit.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often meet चम्मच (chammach) first in textbooks and don't realize चमचा (chamchaa) is an equally common everyday variant for the physical spoon, not just the slang sense.
