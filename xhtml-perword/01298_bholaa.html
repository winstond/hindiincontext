भोला (bholaa)

adjective/noun An adjective meaning innocent, naive, or simple-hearted, describing someone (often a child) who is guileless -- it also lives on as a common personal name and as a familiar nickname for Lord Shiva.
Sense 1 : innocent, naive, simple-hearted
adjective
Synonyms: मासूम (maasoom), सीधा (siidhaa, can also mean 'straightforward/simple'), निष्कपट (nishkpat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic word Indian parents and grandparents use for a wide-eyed child -- भोला बच्चा (bholaa bachchaa, 'innocent child') is a very common phrase. It is also a hugely popular boy's name in North India, often paired with भोला भाला (bholaa bhaalaa) as a set phrase meaning 'utterly innocent and harmless.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use भोला (bholaa) as a compliment for adults meaning 'trustworthy,' but it can also carry a gentle edge of 'easily fooled' -- context and tone decide whether it is affectionate or mildly pitying. 2) Remember this is a variable adjective: भोला (bholaa) for masculine singular, भोली (bholii) for feminine, भोले (bhole) for masculine plural/oblique.

Sense 2 : Bhola, an epithet of Lord Shiva
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भोलेनाथ (bholenaath), भोले बाबा (bhole baabaa)
Frequency: devotional
Cultural Note: भोला (bholaa) or भोलेनाथ (bholenaath) is an affectionate epithet for Shiva, capturing his reputation as a guileless god who is easily pleased by simple devotion -- you will hear this especially around Shivratri and among Shiva devotees, sometimes shouted as बम भोले! (bam bhole, a devotional chant/greeting).
