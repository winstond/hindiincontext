कसर (kasar)

noun A noun mostly encountered in fixed phrases about something being 'lacking' or 'left undone' -- कसर (kasar) rarely stands alone in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : deficiency, shortfall, something left incomplete or lacking
noun, feminine Most common in the pattern कोई कसर नहीं छोड़ना (koii kasar nahiin chhodnaa, 'to leave no stone unturned') and कसर बाकी रहना (kasar baakii rahnaa, 'for something to remain incomplete').
Synonyms: कमी (kamii), त्रुटि (truti, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly at family gatherings when a host insists कोई कसर नहीं छोड़ी (koii kasar nahiin chhodii, 'no effort was left undone') about the food or arrangements -- it's a classic way to signal generous hospitality, almost a ritual phrase at Indian weddings and dinners.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use कसर (kasar) as a general word for 'mistake' -- it specifically means something is incomplete or falling short of full effort/quantity, not an error in the sense of ग़लती (ghaltii). 2) The verb पूरी करना (poorii karnaa) is idiomatic here -- Hindi speakers say कसर पूरी करना (kasar poorii karnaa, 'to make up the shortfall') rather than a literal translation of 'complete the deficiency.'

मेहमानों को खुश करने में उन्होंने कोई कसर नहीं छोड़ी। They left no shortcoming in making the guests happy -- they went all out.
(mehmaanon ko khush karne men unhonne koii kasar nahiin chhodii।)
