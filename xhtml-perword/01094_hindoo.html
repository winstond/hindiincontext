हिन्दू (hindoo)

noun/adjective Both a noun (a person who follows Hinduism) and an adjective (relating to Hinduism or Hindus); also spelled हिंदू (hindoo) with anusvara instead of the half-न, both equally common.
Sense 1 : a follower of Hinduism
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday conversation in Delhi-NCR, हिन्दू (hindoo) is used casually and matter-of-factly to describe religious identity, the way you might mention someone's name or hometown -- it usually doesn't carry the weight it can carry in political speech. You'll often hear it paired with other community names like मुसलमान (musalmaan, Muslim), सिख (sikh, Sikh), and ईसाई (iisaaii, Christian) when people are simply describing India's religious diversity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume हिन्दू (hindoo) is a linguistic or national label like हिन्दी (hindii, Hindi language) or भारतीय (bhaartiiy, Indian) -- it refers strictly to religious identity, not language or nationality. 2) A Hindu person is not automatically a हिन्दी-भाषी (hindii-bhaashii, Hindi-speaker); the two words look similar but are unrelated in meaning.

Sense 2 : relating to Hinduism or Hindus
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll see this adjective everywhere in compound phrases like हिन्दू धर्म (hindoo dharm, 'Hinduism, lit. Hindu religion'), हिन्दू मंदिर (hindoo mandir, 'Hindu temple'), and हिन्दू परिवार (hindoo parivaar, 'Hindu family') -- it functions much like English 'Hindu' does as a modifier before a noun.
