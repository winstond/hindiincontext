आतंकवादी (aatankvaadii)

noun/adjective This word covers two related uses: as a noun meaning 'terrorist' and as an adjective meaning 'terrorist-related' or 'relating to terrorism'. Both come from the same root, आतंक (aatank, terror/panic), plus the suffix -वादी (vaadii, one who follows/advocates).
Sense 1 : terrorist (a person)
noun, masculine/feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a word you hear constantly on Hindi news channels (आतंकवादी हमला (aatankvaadii hamlaa, terrorist attack) is a near-daily headline phrase), so learners pick up its adjectival use almost before its plain noun use. It carries real emotional weight in India given the country's history with cross-border terrorism, so it is not thrown around casually in conversation the way some English speakers use 'terrorist' hyperbolically.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use this word loosely for any violent criminal; in everyday Hindi it is reserved specifically for politically or ideologically motivated violence, much like in English -- a mugger or dacoit (डकैत (dakait)) would not be called this. 2) The plural often looks identical to the singular in casual speech (आतंकवादी लोग (aatankvaadii log, terrorist people/terrorists) or just आतंकवादी (aatankvaadii) with a plural verb) -- the ending does not change the way यह/ये (yah/ye) do.

Sense 2 : terrorist-related, relating to terrorism
adjective Used attributively before a noun, as in आतंकवादी संगठन (aatankvaadii sangthan, 'terrorist organization') or आतंकवादी गतिविधि (aatankvaadii gatividhi, 'terrorist activity').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This adjectival sense is essentially the noun sense doing double duty, which is common for -वादी (vaadii) words in Hindi (compare साम्प्रदायिक (saamprdaayik, communal) and साम्प्रदायिकता (saamprdaayiktaa, communalism)-type pairs). You will meet it constantly in news Hindi, paired with words like हमला (hamlaa, attack), संगठन (sangthan, organization), and गुट (gut, faction/group).
