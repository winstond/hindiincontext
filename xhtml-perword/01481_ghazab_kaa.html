ग़ज़ब का (ghazab kaa)

adjective/adverb An intensifying phrase used before a noun to mean 'amazing/incredible', built from ग़ज़ब (ghazab, 'wonder/calamity') plus the genitive postposition का (kaa).
Sense 1 : amazing, incredible, extraordinary (used attributively before a noun)
adjective Functions like an adjective phrase inflecting through का/के/की agreeing with the following noun, e.g. ग़ज़ब की बात (ghazab kii baat, 'an amazing thing'), ग़ज़ब के लोग (ghazab ke log, 'amazing people').
Synonyms: अद्भुत (adbhut), असाधारण (asaadhaaran), बेमिसाल (bemisaal), कमाल का (kamaal kaa, close synonym with same construction)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic Delhi-style exclamation of admiration or disbelief -- you will hear shopkeepers, cricket commentators, and friends alike say things like ग़ज़ब का स्वाद है (ghazab kaa svaad hai, 'the taste is incredible'). It shares its intensifying flavor with कमाल का (kamaal kaa), and the two are often used interchangeably in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often leave का (kaa) unchanged; it must agree in gender/number with the noun that follows, like किताब (kitaab) taking की (kii) in ग़ज़ब की किताब (ghazab kii kitaab, 'an amazing book'). 2) This phrase describes the noun itself, not an action, so it cannot directly modify a verb the way an adverb would.

(yaar, yah to ghazab kaa svaad hai!)

Sense 2 : amazingly, incredibly (as an intensifier before an adjective)
adverb Used before an adjective to intensify it, as in ग़ज़ब का सुंदर (ghazab kaa sundar, 'incredibly beautiful'); the का (kaa) here stays fixed in this frozen adverbial use rather than agreeing with a following noun.
Synonyms: बेहद (behad), बहुत (bahut, plainer, less colorful), कमाल का (kamaal kaa, same colloquial flavor)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same phrase as the attributive sense, just shifted to sit before an adjective instead of a noun -- a very natural, punchy way to say 'incredibly' in spoken Hindi, as in ग़ज़ब का तेज़ है (ghazab kaa tez hai, 'he's incredibly fast').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect का (kaa) here as if a noun follows, but before an adjective it stays invariant since there is no noun to agree with.
