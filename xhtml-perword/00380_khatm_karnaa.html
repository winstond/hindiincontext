ख़त्म करना (khatm karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to finish/end' something (transitive) -- the adjective ख़त्म (khatm, 'finished') paired with करना (karnaa, 'to do'). See also ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa) for the intransitive counterpart, 'to end/run out' on its own.
Sense 1 : to finish, complete (a task, food, book, etc.)
verb, transitive Takes ने in the perfective since it is transitive: मैंने काम ख़त्म किया (mainne kaam khatm kiyaa, 'I finished the work'). The adjective ख़त्म agrees with the object like a normal predicate adjective in this construction, so it shows up as ख़त्म किया / ख़त्म की / ख़त्म किए depending on the object's gender and number.
Synonyms: समाप्त करना (samaapt karnaa, formal), पूरा करना (pooraa karnaa, 'to complete')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the everyday, casual way to say 'finish' in spoken Hindi -- you will hear it constantly for finishing food, homework, a movie, a bottle of water. समाप्त करना (samaapt karnaa) means the same thing but sounds like a news anchor or a formal announcement, so ख़त्म करना (khatm karnaa) is what people actually say at the dinner table or in the office.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for ख़त्म होना (khatm honaa) when they actually mean the transitive sense -- 'I finished my tea' needs करना (karnaa) because you are acting on the tea, not the tea ending by itself. 2) Because ख़त्म is an adjective here, learners sometimes forget it must agree with the object in gender/number, saying ख़त्म किया (khatm kiyaa) even when the object is feminine (should be ख़त्म की (khatm kii)).

Sense 2 : to end, terminate, kill off (a relationship, a person's life, someone's problem) -- figurative/extended use
verb, transitive Used with abstract or animate objects to mean 'put an end to' -- a relationship, someone's career, a rivalry, or, bluntly, a person's life. The literal completion sense above is about tasks and objects; this sense is about erasing or terminating something that had ongoing existence.
Synonyms: ख़त्म कर देना (khatm kar denaa, with completive nuance), मिटा देना (mitaa denaa, 'to wipe out'), मार देना (maar denaa, 'to kill', when referring to a person)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This figurative extension is everywhere in Hindi film dialogue and news reporting -- गैंग को ख़त्म करना (gaing ko khatm karnaa, 'to finish off the gang') has that same dramatic finality as English 'take out' or 'finish off.' It is worth learning early because the leap from 'finish a sandwich' to 'finish a rival' is smaller in Hindi than it feels in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) In news/crime contexts and in everyday tough talk, उसे ख़त्म कर दिया (use khatm kar diyaa) means 'they killed him,' not 'they finished him off a task' -- learners sometimes miss this darker figurative reading and get confused by headlines or dramatic dialogue. 2) With relationships, रिश्ता ख़त्म करना (rishtaa khatm karnaa) sounds more final and decisive than तोड़ना (todnaa, 'to break'), so swapping them changes the emotional weight of the sentence.
