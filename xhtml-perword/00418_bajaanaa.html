बजाना (bajaanaa)

verb The causative of बजना (bajnaa, 'to sound/ring on its own'): बजाना (bajaanaa) means to make something sound -- playing an instrument, ringing a bell, or (idiomatically) sounding someone out or making a mess of things.
Sense 1 : to play a musical instrument
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Hindi speaker and they'll tell you तबला बजाना (tablaa bajaanaa, 'to play the tabla') or ढोलक बजाना (dholak bajaanaa, 'to play the dholak') before they mention a guitar -- this verb is deeply at home in the world of classical and folk music, weddings, and bhajans.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use खेलना (khelnaa, 'to play a game/sport') for instruments -- Hindi keeps the two strictly separate: games use खेलना (khelnaa), instruments use बजाना (bajaanaa). 2) The instrument is the direct object taking ने (ne) in the perfective: उसने गिटार बजाया (usne gitaar bajaayaa, 'he played the guitar'), not an intransitive-style sentence.
Sample Sentences:

(vah har shaam tablaa bajaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : to ring/sound (a bell, alarm, horn)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: घंटी बजाना (ghantii bajaanaa, 'to ring the doorbell/bell') is what you do at a temple or at someone's front door -- the same verb covers a school bell, a car horn (हॉर्न बजाना, horn bajaanaa), and a temple bell, all framed as 'making it sound' rather than just 'ringing.'

Sense 3 : to mess up, ruin, expose (idiomatic, often reflexive/negative)
verb, transitive Common in fixed idiomatic phrases like बैंड बजाना (baind bajaanaa, colloquial, 'to thrash/mess someone up') and अपनी इज़्ज़त बजाना (apnii izzat bajaanaa, 'to ruin one's own reputation').
Synonyms: बिगाड़ना (bigaadnaa), भांडा फोड़ना (bhaandaa phodnaa, idiomatic, 'to expose')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in casual banter among friends -- something like उसकी तो बजा दी (uskii to bajaa dii, 'we really messed him up/showed him up') -- it's got that same cheeky, informal energy as English slang like 'we wrecked him.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is heavily idiomatic and colloquial -- learners should recognize it in set phrases rather than trying to construct new sentences with it, since the literal 'play/sound' meaning doesn't map onto 'ruin' in any predictable way.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी तो बजा दी यार! We really wrecked him, man!
(uskii to bajaa dii yaar!)
