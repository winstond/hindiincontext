शहीद (shahiid)

noun/adjective A borrowed word (from Arabic via Persian/Urdu) meaning 'martyr' -- someone who dies for a cause, country, or faith. Used as a noun and as an adjective describing the person or their sacrifice.
Sense 1 : martyr (person who dies for a cause)
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: बलिदानी (balidaanii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word carries deep emotional and patriotic weight in India -- shahiid diwas (shahiid divas, 'Martyrs' Day') is observed on January 30th and March 23rd for different sets of freedom fighters, and you will see शहीद स्मारक (shahiid smaarak, 'martyrs' memorial') in towns across the country. It is not a neutral clinical term like 'deceased' -- using it confers real honor, so Hindi speakers are careful about who they call शहीद (shahiid).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use शहीद (shahiid) loosely for anyone who simply dies, but it is reserved for death in service of a cause, country, or belief -- a soldier, freedom fighter, or activist, not an ordinary death. 2) The common verb pairing is शहीद होना (shahiid honaa, 'to be martyred') for the person dying, and शहीद करना (shahiid karnaa, 'to martyr someone') for causing it -- learners sometimes reach for मरना (marnaa) instead, which loses the honorific weight.

Sense 2 : martyred, having died a martyr's death
adjective Most often seen predicatively with होना (honaa), as in वह शहीद हो गया (vah shahiid ho gayaa, 'he was martyred/died a martyr').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in news reports about soldiers -- सैनिक शहीद हो गया (sainik shahiid ho gayaa, 'the soldier was martyred') is a standard headline phrase, carrying respect that a plain 'died' (mar gayaa) would not.
