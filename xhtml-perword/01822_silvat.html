सिलवट (silvat)

noun A concrete noun meaning a crease, wrinkle, or fold -- used for cloth, skin, paper, and by extension for troubles or complications in a situation.
Sense 1 : crease or wrinkle (in cloth, paper, skin)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: शिकन (shikan)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi mother getting ready for a wedding and she'll tell you to iron out the सिलवटें (silavten) before you leave the house -- a crumpled kurta is a small social embarrassment here. The same word covers the fine lines that appear on skin with age, so you'll hear it in beauty-product ads too, promising to smooth away चेहरे की सिलवटें (chehre kii silavten, 'wrinkles on the face').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for शिकन (shikan) only -- both words are common, but सिलवट (silvat) is the more everyday choice for clothes fresh out of a bag or suitcase.

Sense 2 : figurative: a complication, snag, or wrinkle in a plan/situation
noun, feminine Used in phrases like मामले में सिलवट पड़ना (maamle men silvat padnaa, 'for a snag to appear in a matter') -- the physical image of a crumpled cloth extended to a plan or relationship running into trouble.
Synonyms: उलझन (uljhan), पेच (pech, informal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just as in English we speak of 'ironing out' a problem, Hindi speakers picture a difficulty as a सिलवट (silvat) that needs smoothing -- so you'll hear रिश्ते में सिलवट आ गई (rishte men silvat aa gaii, 'a wrinkle has appeared in the relationship') when things get slightly tense between two people.

उनके रिश्ते में एक सिलवट आ गई है। A wrinkle has appeared in their relationship.
