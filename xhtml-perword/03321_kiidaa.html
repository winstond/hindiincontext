कीड़ा (kiidaa)

noun Basic meaning is a small crawling creature -- insect, worm, or bug. The word branches into a very common figurative sense (an obsession or fixation someone has) and a couple of set idiomatic expressions.
Sense 1 : insect, worm, bug (the literal creature)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कीट (kiit, formal/scientific)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every Indian household has had the moment of finding a कीड़ा (kiidaa) in the rice or daal and the ensuing minor domestic crisis. Mothers also routinely ask if a child has पेट में कीड़े (pet men kiide, 'worms in the stomach') as a first guess for tummy troubles -- it is practically a reflex diagnosis before the doctor's visit.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used loosely for almost anything small and crawling -- ants, caterpillars, maggots, worms in fruit -- English speakers may want separate words but Hindi speakers default to कीड़ा (kiidaa) unless being precise. 2) कीड़े (kiide, 'worms/bugs') is the everyday way to talk about intestinal worms in children, e.g. at the doctor -- not a euphemism, just the normal term.

Sense 2 : an obsession, fixation, or mania for something (figurative)
noun, masculine Occurs almost always in the frame X को Y का कीड़ा होना/काटना (X ko Y kaa kiidaa honaa/kaatnaa, 'X has a bug/obsession for Y'), with a dative-marked subject like लगना.
Synonyms: जुनून (junoon, stronger, 'passion/madness'), धुन (dhun, a fixed idea or tune in one's head)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you'll hear about the neighborhood uncle who is endlessly tinkering with his car, or the cousin who reads every cricket stat available -- उसे क्रिकेट का कीड़ा है (use kriket kaa kiidaa hai, 'he's got the cricket bug'). It frames obsession as something that gets under your skin, almost literally, echoing the English idiom 'bitten by the bug.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners translate 'bug' literally and miss that कीड़ा काटना (kiidaa kaatnaa, 'the bug bit [someone]') is the idiom for suddenly catching an obsession or urge -- it is always figurative here, never about an actual insect bite. 2) The person affected is को (ko)-marked, not the subject of an active verb: उसे लिखने का कीड़ा है (use likhne kaa kiidaa hai, 'she has a bug for writing'), not वह लिखने का कीड़ा है.

Sense 3 : a contemptible, worthless, or insignificant person (insult)
noun, masculine Used predicatively or as a dismissive label, e.g. में तुझे कीड़े की तरह कुचल दूँगा (main tujhe kiide kii tarah kuchal doongaa, 'I'll crush you like a worm').
Synonyms: कीड़ा-मकोड़ा (kiidaa-makodaa, 'creepy-crawly', emphatic doubled form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense leans on the same logic as English 'worm' or 'insect' as an insult -- something beneath notice, easily crushed. It shows up in dramatic dialogue and quarrels more than in mild everyday complaints.
