उधर (udhar)

adverb A directional/locational adverb meaning 'there, that way, over there' -- pairs naturally with इधर (idhar, 'here, this way') in everyday speech.
Sense 1 : over there, that way (location/direction)
adverb
Synonyms: वहाँ (vahaan, that place, more static/specific)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of उधर (udhar) as pointing with your chin or a hand gesture -- it's the word Delhi shopkeepers and rickshaw drivers use to wave you toward a general direction rather than naming an exact spot. The pair इधर-उधर (idhar-udhar, 'here and there') is extremely common in casual speech for aimless movement or scattered things.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use वहाँ (vahaan) and उधर (udhar) interchangeably, but उधर (udhar) leans toward a general direction or side ('that way, that side') while वहाँ (vahaan) points to a specific place ('at that spot'). 2) उधर (udhar) is frequently paired with इधर (idhar) as a set expression meaning 'here and there' or 'on one side... on the other side' -- learners sometimes miss this idiomatic pairing.

Sense 2 : on that side, in that matter (figurative/topical use)
adverb Used to shift topic or refer to a different matter/party under discussion, similar to English 'meanwhile, over there' or 'on that front.'
Synonyms: उस तरफ़ (us taraf, that side/direction, more explicit phrase)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In conversation or storytelling, उधर (udhar) often works like 'meanwhile' in English -- shifting attention to another person, place, or situation, as in news reports or family gossip: उधर उसने कुछ नहीं कहा (udhar usne kuchh nahiin kahaa, 'meanwhile/for his part, he said nothing').
