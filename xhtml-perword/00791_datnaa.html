डटना (datnaa)

verb One core meaning: to stand firm, hold one's ground, or stay put with determination -- physically or figuratively (in an argument, task, or fight).
Sense 1 : to stand firm / hold one's ground
verb, intransitive Often used with पर (par) marking the position or front held -- मोर्चे पर डटना (morche par datnaa, 'to hold one's position at the front').
Synonyms: जमे रहना (jame rahnaa), टिके रहना (tike rahnaa), अड़ना (adnaa, more about stubborn refusal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in news reports about soldiers or protesters: सैनिक सीमा पर डटे रहे (sainik siimaa par date rahe, 'the soldiers stood firm at the border'). It carries a heroic, dogged-persistence flavor -- someone who refuses to budge despite pressure, tiredness, or danger.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse डटना (datnaa) with अड़ना (adnaa): डटना (datnaa) is about steadfast presence or resistance under pressure (staying put, holding ground), while अड़ना (adnaa) is about stubborn insistence in an argument or demand. 2) डटना (datnaa) is intransitive -- it takes पर (par) or में (men) for the place/front, never a direct object.
Sample Sentences:
सैनिक सीमा पर डटे रहे। The soldiers stood firm at the border.
(sainik siimaa par date rahe।)

Sense 2 : to apply oneself persistently / stick to a task
verb, intransitive Common in the compound-verb form डटे रहना (date rahnaa), stressing continued, unwavering effort -- पढ़ाई में डटे रहना (padhaaii men date rahnaa, 'to stay dedicated to one's studies').
Synonyms: जुटे रहना (jute rahnaa), लगे रहना (lage rahnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: A parent encouraging a child before exams might say मेहनत में डटे रहो (mehnat men date raho, 'keep at your hard work') -- it's a word of encouragement toward grit and follow-through, similar in spirit to 'keep pushing.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense overlaps with the 'stand firm' sense -- both describe not giving up, but here the context is effort/work rather than physical or confrontational ground-holding, so the object of में (men) is typically an activity (study, work) rather than a place or fight.
Sample Sentences:

(mehnat men date raho, saphaltaa zaroor milegii।)
