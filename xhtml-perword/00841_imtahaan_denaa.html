इम्तहान देना (imtahaan denaa)

verb A compound verb phrase meaning 'to take/sit an exam' -- built from the Urdu-origin noun इम्तहान (imtahaan, 'exam') plus देना (denaa, 'to give'). Hindi frames the test-taker as the one 'giving' the exam, the mirror image of the English idiom.
Sense 1 : to take/sit an exam
verb, transitive इम्तहान (imtahaan) takes ने (ne) marking on the subject in the perfective, following the transitive behavior of देना (denaa).
Synonyms: परीक्षा देना (pariikshaa denaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This give/take reversal trips up almost every English-speaking learner at first -- in Hindi, the exam is a thing you 'give' (देना, denaa) to prove yourself, while the examiner is the one who 'takes' (लेना, lenaa) it from you, almost like handing in your performance for judgment. Once you internalize this, you'll hear it constantly around exam season in Delhi -- proud parents asking बच्चे ने इम्तहान कैसा दिया? (bachche ne imtahaan kaisaa diyaa?, 'How did the child do on the exam?')
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners instinctively say इम्तहान लेना (imtahaan lenaa) by analogy with English 'take an exam,' but इम्तहान लेना (imtahaan lenaa) actually means the teacher/examiner is administering the exam, not the student sitting it -- the roles are swapped from English. 2) The student is the subject of देना (denaa), not लेना (lenaa): मैंने इम्तहान दिया (mainne imtahaan diyaa, 'I took the exam') is correct; मैंने इम्तहान लिया (mainne imtahaan liyaa) would mean 'I administered the exam.'
Sample Sentences:
मैं कल गणित का इम्तहान देता हूँ। I take my math exam tomorrow.
(main kal ganit kaa imtahaan detaa hoon।)
