बरसाती (barsaatii)

noun/adjective Two main uses: as a noun for a rain-related item (raincoat, or the covered rooftop room typical of North Indian houses), and as an adjective meaning 'of the rainy season.' Related to बरसना (barasnaa, 'to rain') and बारिश (baarish, 'rain').
Sense 1 : raincoat
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रेनकोट (renkot)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In much of North India, बरसाती (barsaatii) is the everyday word for a raincoat, especially the simple plastic ones sold on roadside carts the moment the monsoon clouds gather -- you will hear vendors shouting बरसाती ले लो! (barsaatii le lo!, 'get your raincoat!') on the first rainy day.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume बरसाती (barsaatii) means 'umbrella' -- that word is छतरी (chhatrii) or छाता (chhaataa); बरसाती (barsaatii) is the coat itself, worn over clothes.

Sense 2 : rooftop room/barsati (a covered room built on a flat roof)
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is a very Delhi word: a बरसाती (barsaatii) is the small covered structure built on the flat roof of a house, originally meant to give shelter from monsoon rain, but for decades now rented out cheaply as a bachelor pad or studio apartment. Ads for flats in old Delhi neighborhoods still routinely list 'barsati available' as a housing category in its own right, distinct from a normal flat.

Sense 3 : of or relating to the rainy season
adjective Used to describe things tied to the monsoon, such as बरसाती बीमारियाँ (barsaatii biimaariyaan, 'monsoon-season illnesses') or बरसाती मौसम (barsaatii mausam, 'rainy weather').
Synonyms: मानसूनी (maansoonii)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this adjective a lot around July-August in phrases like बरसाती बीमारियाँ (barsaatii biimaariyaan, 'monsoon illnesses') -- doctors and news reports use it constantly to talk about the seasonal spike in fevers and infections.
