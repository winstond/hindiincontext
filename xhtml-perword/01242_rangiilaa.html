रँगीला (rangiilaa)

adjective A colorful, vibrant variant spelling of रंगीला (rangiilaa); describes something bright and colorful, and by extension a person who is fun-loving, flamboyant, or pleasure-seeking.
Sense 1 : colorful, brightly colored
adjective
Synonyms: रंगीला (rangiilaa, more common spelling), बहुरंगी (bahurangii), रंग-बिरंगा (rang-birangaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this used for anything from a colorful dupatta (dupattaa) at a market stall to a colorful description of a mela (melaa). The spelling with the chandrabindu (rangiilaa) is a bit less common in print than रंगीला (rangiilaa), but both are said the same way.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often see this with the chandrabindu (ँ) and assume it is a different word from रंगीला (rangiilaa) -- it is the same word, just with the nasalization marked explicitly on the vowel rather than folded into the following nasal consonant.

Sense 2 : fun-loving, flamboyant, pleasure-seeking (of a person)
adjective Applied to a person's personality or lifestyle -- someone who enjoys life's pleasures, parties, romance, or a carefree, colorful way of living, often with a mildly playful or teasing undertone.
Synonyms: रंगीला (rangiilaa, more common spelling), मौज-मस्ती वाला (mauj-mastii vaalaa, descriptive phrase), रसिया (rasiyaa, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the sense behind the common phrase रंगीला मिज़ाज (rangiilaa mizaaj, 'a colorful/pleasure-loving temperament'), often used about someone who loves music, dance, good food, and a bit of drama in life -- think of an uncle who is always the life of the family wedding.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not a neutral compliment like 'cheerful' -- calling someone रँगीला (rangiilaa) can hint at a taste for indulgence or romance, so tone matters; it can be affectionate teasing among friends or a gentle jab depending on context.
