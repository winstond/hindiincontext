पतला (patlaa)

adjective Basic adjective meaning 'thin' -- covers physical thinness of people/things and thinness of liquids/consistency; also extends to a figurative 'weak/flimsy' sense for arguments or excuses.
Sense 1 : thin (of a person or physical object)
adjective
Synonyms: दुबला (dublaa, of a person's build), छरहरा (chharahraa, slender/lithe, complimentary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear दुबला-पतला (dublaa-patlaa, 'thin, skinny') as a fixed pair describing someone's build -- Hindi loves these rhyming binomials, and this one is the default way to say 'he's a thin fellow' without sounding clinical.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दुबला (dublaa) and पतला (patlaa) interchangeably, but दुबला (dublaa) is used only for people/animals (body build), while पतला (patlaa) works for people and objects alike (a thin book, a thin wire). 2) पतला (patlaa) describing a person is neutral to positive; it does not carry the slightly negative 'too skinny' connotation that दुबला (dublaa) can carry in casual speech.
Sample Sentences:
वह बहुत पतला लड़का है। He is a very thin boy.
(vah bahut patlaa ladkaa hai।)

Sense 2 : thin, watery (of a liquid or consistency)
adjective Used for the consistency of liquids like tea, daal, or batter -- पतली चाय (patlii chaay, 'weak/watery tea') is a common complaint, not a compliment.
Synonyms: गाढ़ा (gaadhaa, opposite: thick)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask for चाय थोड़ी कड़क बनाना, पतली मत बनाना (chaay thodii kadak banaanaa, patlii mat banaanaa, 'make the tea strong, not watery') at a dhaba and you'll get instant respect -- consistency of tea and daal is a real point of household pride in North Indian kitchens.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this sense पतला (patlaa) is often a mild criticism -- पतली दाल (patlii daal, 'watery daal') implies it needed more lentils or was rushed, so don't use it thinking it's neutral the way 'thin soup' might sound in English.
Sample Sentences:
चाय थोड़ी कड़क बनाना, पतली मत बनाना। Make the tea a bit strong, don't make it watery.
(chaay thodii kadak banaanaa, patlii mat banaanaa।)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Imperative -- Voice: Active

Sense 3 : flimsy, weak (of an excuse, argument, or plan)
adjective Used figuratively for excuses, arguments, or plots that don't hold up -- पतला बहाना (patlaa bahaanaa, 'a flimsy excuse').
Synonyms: कमज़ोर (kamzor, weak)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: यह बहाना बहुत पतला है (yah bahaanaa bahut patlaa hai, 'this excuse is very thin') is exactly the sarcastic tone an Indian parent or boss uses when they've caught you out -- the physical image of thinness (easy to see through) carries over neatly into English's own 'thin excuse,' so this figurative leap feels intuitive once pointed out.
Sample Sentences:

Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
