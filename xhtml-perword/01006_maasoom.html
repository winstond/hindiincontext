मासूम (maasoom)

adjective A borrowed Persian-Urdu adjective meaning 'innocent,' used both literally (a child's innocence) and affectionately/figuratively (a naive or guileless look or person).
Sense 1 : innocent, guileless (of a person, especially a child)
adjective
Synonyms: निर्दोष (nirdosh), भोला (bholaa, also naive), निष्पाप (nishpaap, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word Hindi speakers reach for when cooing over a baby's face or describing a child's wide-eyed innocence -- मासूम बच्चा (maasoom bachchaa, 'innocent child') is an extremely common pairing. It also shows up often in film and song titles and lyrics describing an innocent heart or face, reflecting how central this idea is to Hindi's emotional vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for निर्दोष (nirdosh) when they mean legally 'not guilty' -- मासूम (maasoom) is broader and warmer, describing someone's whole nature or face, not just their innocence in a specific incident. 2) Don't confuse with भोला (bholaa), which leans more toward 'naive/simple' and can sometimes carry a teasing undertone; मासूम (maasoom) is almost always tender or sympathetic.

Sense 2 : naive, unaware of wrongdoing (extended/figurative use)
adjective
Synonyms: अनजान (anjaan, unaware), भोला-भाला (bholaa-bhaalaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Used of adults too, this sense softens a criticism -- calling someone's mistake मासूम (maasoom) suggests they erred out of naivety rather than malice, e.g. मासूम सी गलती (maasoom sii galtii, 'an innocent little mistake'). It's a gentle, forgiving word.
