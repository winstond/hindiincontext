अगरबत्ती (agarbattii)

noun A single-sense everyday noun: the incense stick burned in homes, shops, and temples across India.
Sense 1 : incense stick
noun, feminine
Synonyms: धूपबत्ती (dhoopbattii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll find अगरबत्ती (agarbattii) burning at the household पूजा (poojaa) shelf every morning and evening in most Hindu homes, as well as in shops and offices for its calming fragrance and to ward off mosquitoes. The word itself comes from अगर (agar, agarwood/aloeswood) plus बत्ती (battii, stick/wick), reflecting the traditional aromatic wood used in incense-making.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say जलाना (jalaanaa, 'to light/burn') for lighting an incense stick, which is correct, but many native speakers also use लगाना (lagaanaa) in this context, e.g. अगरबत्ती लगाना (agarbattii lagaanaa, 'to light/put on an incense stick') -- both are natural, but लगाना (lagaanaa) can surprise learners who only know it as 'to apply.' 2) The compound is often shortened in casual speech to बत्ती (battii) when context makes it clear incense is meant, though बत्ती (battii) alone usually just means 'light/bulb' -- don't assume this shortening works in all contexts.
