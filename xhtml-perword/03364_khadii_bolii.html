खड़ी बोली (khadii bolii)

noun A proper name for the dialect base of modern standard Hindi (and Urdu), used mainly in discussions of language and linguistic history rather than in everyday chit-chat.
Sense 1 : the Khari Boli dialect/register underlying standard Hindi
noun, feminine
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: You will meet this term in school textbooks and language-history discussions around Delhi, Meerut, and western Uttar Pradesh, the historical home of this dialect. It is worth knowing as background: modern standard Hindi, as taught and used in news, schools, and this very course, is built on खड़ी बोली (khadii bolii), not on the more poetic dialects like ब्रज भाषा (braj bhaashaa) that older Hindi/Urdu poetry often used.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes think this is just another word for "Hindi" in general -- it specifically names the Delhi-area dialect that standard Hindi (and Urdu) grew out of, as opposed to other regional dialects like ब्रज (braj) or अवधी (avdhii). 2) खड़ी (khadii) here does not mean "standing" in the everyday sense; in this fixed name it is usually explained as "straightforward/plain" (as opposed to more ornate literary dialects), so do not try to translate the phrase literally word-by-word.
