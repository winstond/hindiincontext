छलकना (chhalaknaa)

verb A single core meaning: liquid spilling or brimming over the edge of a container because it is too full or being jostled. Often extended figuratively to emotions overflowing, especially in eyes/tears.
Sense 1 : to spill over, brim over (of liquid)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: उफनना (uphannaa, of boiling/rising liquid), बहना (bahnaa, to flow, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture carrying a full glass of chai across a crowded room and a little sloshes over the rim at every jerky step -- that visual is exactly what छलकना (chhalaknaa) captures. It is a very physical, everyday verb you will hear in kitchens and while pouring drinks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for बहना (bahnaa) here, but छलकना (chhalaknaa) specifically means liquid spilling over an edge because the container is too full or shaken, not liquid simply flowing or running (like a river or tap). 2) The subject is the liquid itself, not the container: चाय छलक गई (chaay chhalak gaii, 'the tea spilled over'), not the cup.

(gilaas itnaa bharaa thaa ki paanii chhalak gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to well up, brim over (of emotion, especially in the eyes)
verb, intransitive Most commonly paired with आँखें (aankhen, eyes) as the subject: आँखों में आँसू छलक आए (aankhon men aansoo chhalak aae, 'tears welled up in the eyes').
Synonyms: भर आना (bhar aanaa, of eyes filling with tears)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same mental image as the liquid sense, just applied to the eyes as a brimming vessel -- Hindi loves treating the body as a container that fills up with feeling. You will often see it paired with the compound verb form छलक आना (chhalak aanaa) when tears suddenly well up at a tender or emotional moment.
