भैया (bhaiyaa)

noun An affectionate, everyday term for 'elder brother' that has expanded into a very common respectful/friendly address for men in general, especially in service contexts (shopkeepers, drivers, waiters).
Sense 1 : elder brother (affectionate)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भाई (bhaaii), बड़ा भाई (badaa bhaaii, more explicit)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word most Indian kids actually grow up calling their older brother; भाई (bhaaii) can feel a touch more formal or generic by comparison. It also pairs with दीदी (diidii) for 'elder sister' as the standard sibling-address pair in North Indian families.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat भैया (bhaiyaa) and भाई (bhaaii) as fully interchangeable, but भैया (bhaiyaa) carries a warmer, more childlike or family-affectionate tone, closer to 'bhaiya' as an endearment than the plainer भाई (bhaaii).
Sample Sentences:
भैया, मेरी साइकिल ठीक कर दो ना। Bhaiya, please fix my bicycle.
(bhaiyaa, merii saaikil thiik kar do naa।)

Sense 2 : general polite/friendly address for a man
noun, masculine Used vocatively to address or get the attention of an unrelated man, typically of similar or slightly younger age, in everyday transactions.
Synonyms: भाई साहब (bhaaii saahab, more formal), भाई (bhaaii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Walk through any Delhi market and you will hear "भैया, ज़रा रुकना" (bhaiyaa, zaraa ruknaa, 'brother, wait a second') called out to autorickshaw drivers and shopkeepers all day long -- it is the default friendly-but-respectful way to hail a man you don't know.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often hesitate to use भैया (bhaiyaa) with strangers, but it is the completely normal, expected way to call out to a rickshaw driver, vendor, or waiter -- it is not overly familiar or rude. 2) It is not appropriate for addressing someone clearly older or of higher status than a peer; चाचा (chaachaa) or जी (jii)-suffixed address may fit better there.
