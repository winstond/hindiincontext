महल (mahal)

noun A single-sense everyday noun: a palace or grand mansion, used both literally and as a figurative description of a lavish home.
Sense 1 : palace, grand mansion
noun, masculine
Synonyms: राजभवन (raajabhvan, formal), किला (kilaa, fort, not a true synonym but often confused)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Delhi-NCR is full of living reminders of this word -- the Red Fort (laal kilaa) and Purana Qila both house what locals still call महल (mahal) inside their walls, and taking a family outing to see one is a classic weekend plan. In everyday speech, calling someone's house a महल (mahal) is a warm, slightly teasing compliment about its size or decor, similar to English 'it's like a mansion in there.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse महल (mahal) with किला (kilaa, 'fort') -- a महल is a residence built for comfort and grandeur, while a किला (kilaa) is a fortified structure for defense; many historic sites in India are actually किले (kile) that contain a महल (mahal) inside them, like the Red Fort's palace quarters. 2) In casual speech, महल (mahal) is also used loosely and humorously for any unusually large or fancy house, e.g. calling a rich relative's new house एक महल है (ek mahal hai, 'it's a palace') -- learners often reserve it only for literal royal palaces and miss this exaggerated everyday use.
