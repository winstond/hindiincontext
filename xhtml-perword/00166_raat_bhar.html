रात भर (raat bhar)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'all night, the whole night through' -- built from रात (raat, 'night') plus भर (bhar, 'full/entire'), a pattern that also works with other time words like दिन भर (din bhar, 'all day').
Sense 1 : throughout the night, all night long
adverb
Synonyms: पूरी रात (poorii raat), सारी रात (saarii raat)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in complaints and confessions: बच्चा रात भर रोता रहा (bachchaa raat bhar rotaa rahaa, 'the child kept crying all night') or मैं रात भर सो नहीं पाया (main raat bhar so nahiin paayaa, 'I couldn't sleep all night') -- it is the go-to way to describe a sleepless or eventful night. The same भर (bhar) pattern extends naturally: दिन भर (din bhar, 'all day'), साल भर (saal bhar, 'all year'), उम्र भर (umr bhar, 'one's whole life') -- worth learning as a family once you have this one.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to add a postposition after this phrase (e.g. treating it like रात भर में, raat bhar men) when a plain adverbial 'all night' is meant; रात भर (raat bhar) alone already functions as a complete time adverb without needing को (ko) or में (men) unless you specifically mean 'within the night' or 'over the course of the night.' 2) The word order is fixed as रात भर (raat bhar), not भर रात (bhar raat) -- भर (bhar) always follows the noun it attaches to.
