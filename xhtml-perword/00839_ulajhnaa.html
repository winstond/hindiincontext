उलझना (ulajhnaa)

verb An intransitive verb meaning to get entangled or tangled (like thread or hair), which extends naturally to the figurative sense of getting caught up in a confusion, argument, or problem. Its transitive causative partner उलझाना (uljhaanaa, 'to entangle someone/something') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to get caught up in confusion, an argument, or a problem
verb, intransitive Very commonly used with बात (baat), बहस (bahas), या झगड़े (jhagde) as the thing one gets tangled in, often with में (men): उलझना (men ulajhnaa) 'to get entangled in [something]'.
Synonyms: फंसना (phansnaa, of being stuck in a bad situation), झगड़ना (jhagadnaa, narrower, specifically to quarrel)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word for that very Indian experience of getting stuck going back and forth in a needless बहस (bahas, argument) or उलझन (uljhan, a mental knot/confusion, the related noun) -- a mother might tell her child दिमाग़ मत उलझाओ (dimaagh mat uljhaao, 'don't tangle your brain [in this]') using the causative, while the child himself is बात में उलझ गया (baat men ulajh gayaa, 'got tangled up in the matter') using this intransitive form.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is about mental or situational entanglement -- confusion, a pointless argument, overthinking -- not physical danger, so it is a poor fit for translating 'trapped' in a literal sense (use फंसना (phansnaa) there instead). 2) Learners often want to use it transitively ('to entangle someone in an argument') but that causative meaning belongs to उलझाना (uljhaanaa), not this word.

(vah chhotii-chhotii baaton men ulajh jaataa hai।)

Sense 2 : to get entangled, tangled up (physically)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: फंसना (phansnaa, overlaps in the getting-stuck sense), गुत्थी बनना (gutthii bannaa, of thread/hair specifically)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture a kite string (dor) or a dupatta (dupattaa) getting caught in a fan or a bicycle chain -- that visual of threads winding around each other is the mental anchor for this word, and it's exactly this image that carries over into the figurative sense below.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for फंसना (phansnaa) here, but फंसना (phansnaa) is about being trapped or stuck (like in a snare or a bad situation), while उलझना (ulajhnaa) specifically evokes strands winding around each other -- hair, thread, wires, string.
