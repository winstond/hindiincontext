दिवस (divas)

noun A formal, written-register word for 'day,' mostly reserved for named occasions and observances rather than everyday talk about days.
Sense 1 : day (formal, esp. a designated/commemorative day)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दिन (din)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll meet this word constantly on calendars, banners, and news headlines for official and commemorative days -- गणतंत्र दिवस (gantantr divas, 'Republic Day'), स्वतंत्रता दिवस (svatantrtaa divas, 'Independence Day'), मातृ दिवस (maatri divas, 'Mother's Day'). In ordinary conversation about days of the week or daily life, Delhi speakers reach for दिन (din) instead; दिवस (divas) carries a formal, almost ceremonial weight, much like 'day' in 'Labor Day' versus casually saying 'what a day.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use दिवस (divas) where दिन (din) is expected in everyday speech -- 'कौन सा दिन है?' (kaun saa din hai?, 'what day is it?') sounds natural, but 'कौन सा दिवस है?' (kaun saa divas hai?) sounds oddly formal or out of place. 2) दिवस (divas) is almost always used in fixed compound names (गणतंत्र दिवस, gantantr divas, 'Republic Day') rather than as a free-standing word for 'a day' the way दिन (din) is used.

(har saal chhabbiis janavrii ko gantantr divas manaayaa jaataa hai।)
