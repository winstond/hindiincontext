कोई नहीं (koii nahiin)

pronoun/phrase A fixed two-word phrase built from कोई (koii, 'someone/any') plus नहीं (nahiin, 'not'), used to mean 'no one' or, in a common conversational aside, 'it's nothing / never mind.'
Sense 1 : no one, nobody
pronoun Functions as the negative counterpart of कोई (koii, 'someone') when answering or making statements about people; takes the same oblique/case behavior as कोई.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in answer to घर में कौन है? (ghar men kaun hai, 'who's home?') -- the reply कोई नहीं (koii nahiin) simply means 'nobody.' It pairs naturally with कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahiin, 'nothing') as the other half of Hindi's basic negative-pronoun set.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes double-negate by adding another नहीं (nahiin) later in the sentence out of habit from English 'no one doesn't...'; in Hindi the negation is already complete in कोई नहीं (koii nahiin) itself. 2) Word order surprises: कोई नहीं आया (koii nahiin aayaa, 'no one came') keeps कोई नहीं together right before the verb, rather than splitting it apart.
Sample Sentences:
घर में कोई नहीं है। There's no one home.
(ghar men koii nahiin hai।)

Sense 2 : it's nothing, never mind, don't worry about it
phrase Used as a stand-alone conversational response, often shortened from the fuller कोई बात नहीं (koii baat nahiin), to wave off an apology or thanks.
Synonyms: कोई बात नहीं (koii baat nahiin), कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahiin)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Said with a casual wave of the hand, कोई नहीं (koii nahiin) is the everyday Delhi shortcut for 'forget it, it's fine' -- you will hear it constantly after someone bumps into you or apologizes for being late.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for सॉरी इट्स फाइन-style literal translations; कोई नहीं (koii nahiin) here is idiomatic and should be learned as a fixed response, not built up word by word. 2) Confusing this reassuring sense with the 'no one' sense: context (a reply to sorry/thanks vs. a reply to 'who') is what tells them apart.
