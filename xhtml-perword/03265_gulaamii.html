गुलामी (gulaamii)

noun One main sense: slavery/servitude, both as the literal institution and as a common metaphor for being under someone's or something's control. Related to गुलाम (gulaam, 'slave'), from which it is derived.
Sense 1 : slavery, servitude, bondage
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दासता (daastaa, formal/literary), बंधन (bandhan, meaning scope: bondage/constraint more broadly), परतंत्रता (partantrtaa, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian schoolchild meets in the phrase अंग्रेजों की गुलामी (angrejon kii gulaamii, 'servitude under the British'), so it carries a strong charge of colonial history alongside its literal sense. It is also used loosely and often self-deprecatingly, as when someone says नौकरी में गुलामी है (naukrii men gulaamii hai, 'the job is basically slave labor') about a demanding boss or job, extending the word from literal bondage to any felt loss of freedom or dignity.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दास (daas) or दासता (daastaa) as the default, but गुलामी (gulaamii) is the word actually used in everyday and political speech, especially about historical/colonial subjugation. 2) गुलामी करना (gulaamii karnaa) means 'to live/work in servitude' or 'to be servile toward someone,' not literally 'to do slavery' as a one-off act -- it describes an ongoing state or habitual submission.
Sample Sentences:
भारत ने दो सौ साल तक अंग्रेजों की गुलामी झेली। India endured servitude under the British for two hundred years.
(bhaarat ne do sau saal tak angrejon kii gulaamii jhelii।)
