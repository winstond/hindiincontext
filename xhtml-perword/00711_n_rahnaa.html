न रहना (n rahnaa)

verb A euphemistic phrase built from न (n, 'not') plus रहना (rahnaa, 'to stay/remain'), used mainly to say that someone has passed away, i.e. is 'no longer' with us.
Sense 1 : to pass away, to no longer be alive (euphemism for dying)
verb, intransitive Almost always used in a completed/perfective form about a person, typically with an oblique possessor construction: किसी के न रहने पर (kisii ke n rahne par, 'when someone is no longer [with us]') or वे अब नहीं रहे (ve ab nahiin rahe, 'he/she is no more'), where नहीं rather than न often appears in the finite past tense, with न reserved more for infinitival/subordinate uses.
Synonyms: गुजर जाना (gujar jaanaa), चल बसना (chal basnaa, gentle/literary), मर जाना (mar jaanaa, direct, less euphemistic), गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa, spelling variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the phrase you will hear when someone gently breaks the news of a death: वे अब नहीं रहे (ve ab nahiin rahe, 'he is no longer with us'). It softens the blow compared to मर गया (mar gayaa), much like English speakers prefer 'passed away' over 'died' in polite company. You will often hear it paired with respectful plural verb agreement even for one person, as a mark of respect for the deceased.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to मर गया (mar gayaa) in all contexts; न रहना (n rahnaa) is the softer, more respectful way to announce a death, especially to family or in formal settings. 2) The negator shifts: in the actual finite past tense Hindi speakers usually say नहीं रहे (nahiin rahe) rather than न रहे (n rahe), even though the dictionary/infinitive form of this phrase is written with न. 3) This phrase is almost never used for objects or animals in casual speech; use it primarily for people.

Sense 2 : to no longer exist, remain, or be present (of things, situations, relationships)
verb, intransitive Used for abstract or inanimate subjects -- a relationship, a system, a feeling -- that has ceased to exist or continue, e.g. अब वह रिश्ता नहीं रहा (ab vah rishtaa nahiin rahaa, 'that relationship no longer exists').
Synonyms: खत्म हो जाना (khatm ho jaanaa), बचना नहीं (bachnaa nahiin)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same underlying phrase as the 'passing away' sense, just applied to things instead of people -- Hindi treats a relationship, a job, or an era 'ceasing to remain' with the same verb used for a life ending, which gives the phrase a slightly wistful, reflective color even when talking about non-human subjects.
