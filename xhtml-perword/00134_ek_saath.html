एक साथ (ek saath)

adverb A common adverbial phrase built from एक (ek, 'one') plus साथ (saath, 'company/togetherness'), meaning 'together' or 'at the same time.' Both senses -- physical/social togetherness and simultaneity -- are heard constantly in daily speech.
Sense 1 : together (in one place, as a group)
adverb
Synonyms: मिलकर (milkar, 'jointly/by coming together'), साथ साथ (saath saath)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a Delhi household calling everyone to eat: सब लोग एक साथ खाना खाएंगे (sab log ek saath khaanaa khaaenge, 'everyone will eat together') -- the phrase carries a real cultural weight around family meals, festivals, and group outings, where doing things 'as one' is valued over doing them individually.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say केवल साथ (keval saath) when they mean 'together,' but साथ (saath) alone usually needs एक (ek) or a possessive/oblique pronoun before it (मेरे साथ (mere saath, 'with me')) to make sense -- एक साथ (ek saath) is the fixed way to say 'all together, as one group' without naming who is with whom. 2) Confusing एक साथ (ek saath) with साथ साथ (saath saath) -- the latter leans towards 'side by side / in parallel' over a stretch of time or space, while एक साथ (ek saath) leans towards 'all at once, as a single unit.'
Sample Sentences:

(ham sab ek saath khaanaa khaate hain।)

Sense 2 : at the same time, simultaneously
adverb
Synonyms: एक ही समय में (ek hii samay men), साथ साथ (saath saath)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Very handy for describing multitasking or coincidence: वह पढ़ते हुए एक साथ गाना भी सुनता है (vah padhte hue ek saath gaanaa bhii suntaa hai, 'he listens to music while studying, at the same time') -- a phrase you will hear often when someone describes juggling two things at once.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for एक ही समय पर (ek hii samay par) for 'at the same time,' which is correct but more formal/explicit -- एक साथ (ek saath) is the everyday, more natural choice for the same idea in casual speech.
