झुकना (jhuknaa)

verb Basic meaning is physical bending/bowing down; extends naturally into the figurative sense of yielding or submitting to someone's will. The causative झुकाना (jhukaanaa, 'to bend something') is a separate lemma.
Sense 1 : to bend, to bow (physically)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: नमना (namnaa, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly around touching elders' feet: बड़ों के सामने झुकना (badon ke saamne jhuknaa, 'to bow before elders') is a real, everyday gesture of respect in Delhi-NCR households, not just a book phrase. It is also physically what your body does when you sit on the floor to eat or pray -- a low stool or thali means you naturally झुकना (jhuknaa) forward.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मोड़ना (modnaa) here, but that word is for bending an object (a wire, an arm) with your hands -- झुकना (jhuknaa) is intransitive and describes a body or structure bending itself, like touching one's own head down or a branch sagging under weight.
Sample Sentences:
पेड़ की एक शाखा हवा में झुक रही थी। A branch of the tree was bending in the wind.
(ped kii ek shaakhaa havaa men jhuk rahii thii।)

Sense 2 : to yield, to give in, to submit
verb, intransitive Often used with दबाव (dabaav, 'pressure') or किसी की बात (kisii kii baat, 'someone's word/wish') to describe caving in under someone else's will.
Synonyms: हार मानना (haar maannaa, phrase, to admit defeat), झुक जाना (jhuk jaanaa, compound-verb variant)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this in political and family arguments alike: सरकार को झुकना पड़ा (sarkaar ko jhuknaa padaa, 'the government was forced to give in') is a stock news phrase, and a parent might say the same about finally giving in to a child's demand. The dative-subject flavor with पड़ना (padnaa) -- झुकना पड़ा (jhuknaa padaa) -- captures the sense of being compelled rather than choosing to yield.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is not the verb to use for losing a game or contest (use हारना haarnaa for that) -- झुकना (jhuknaa) specifically implies caving in to pressure, demand, or authority, even without any formal contest taking place.
