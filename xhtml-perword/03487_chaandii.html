चाँदी (chaandii)

noun Primarily the metal 'silver'; also lives in a common idiom about sudden good fortune.
Sense 1 : the metal silver
noun, feminine
Synonyms: रजत (rajat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: चाँदी (chaandii) is everywhere in Indian households -- silver anklets (paayal) for babies, silver coins gifted at weddings, and silver-plated (chaandii kaa vark) sweets at festivals. Jewelers (sunaar) and utensil shops both deal heavily in it, so it is a very practical, real-world noun to know.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse चाँदी (chaandii) with सोना (sonaa, gold) when learning jewelry vocabulary together -- keep the pair straight since both are common market words. 2) The chandrabindu (nasalization) is often dropped in typing as चादी, but the nasal sound is standard in speech.

Sense 2 : sudden good fortune, windfall (idiomatic)
noun, feminine Mainly appears in the fixed idiom चाँदी होना (chaandii honaa, 'to strike it rich/have a windfall') and चाँदी काटना (chaandii kaatnaa, 'to rake in money'), where चाँदी stands metaphorically for easy profit or good fortune rather than the literal metal.
Synonyms: किस्मत (kismat, broader meaning: fate/luck in general)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear शॉपकीपर्स (dukaandaar, shopkeepers) and businessmen say मेरी तो चाँदी हो गई (merii to chaandii ho gaii, 'I really struck it rich') after an unexpectedly good sale -- it captures that shiny, almost magical feeling of easy money, much like English 'to strike gold,' except Hindi reaches for silver instead.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use चाँदी alone to mean 'luck,' but it almost always needs the verb होना (honaa) or काटना (kaatnaa) attached to carry this idiomatic sense -- on its own it just means the metal.
