सीमा (siimaa)

noun Mainly means a boundary or border, extending naturally into the figurative sense of a 'limit' -- both are the same underlying concept of an edge that should not be crossed.
Sense 1 : border, boundary (geographical/physical)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: सरहद (sarahad), हद (had), बॉर्डर (bordar, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly on Hindi news channels in phrases like सीमा पर तनाव (siimaa par tanaav, tension at the border), since India's borders with Pakistan and China are a near-daily topic. It carries a slightly more formal, official weight than सरहद (sarahad), which has a more emotive, almost poetic flavor (think of border-town songs and stories).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for बॉर्डर (bordar) in speech, but सीमा (siimaa) is the word used in news, official, and written contexts for national or state borders -- e.g. भारत-पाकिस्तान सीमा (bhaarat-paakistaan siimaa, the India-Pakistan border).

Sense 2 : limit, extent (figurative)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: हद (had), सीमा-रेखा (siimaa-rekhaa, compound form, less common)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word behind useful everyday phrases like सीमा से बाहर (siimaa se baahar, 'beyond the limit') and सीमा पार करना (siimaa paar karnaa, 'to cross a limit/line', also used literally for crossing a border). Notice how the same physical image of a border gets reused for describing patience, endurance, or propriety running out.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this figurative sense सीमा (siimaa) is largely interchangeable with हद (had), but हद (had) is the word that shows up in the common exclamation हद है! (had hai, 'that's too much!') -- सीमा (siimaa) does not work in that idiom.
