ऐसे (aise)

adverb The oblique/adverbial form of ऐसा (aisaa, 'such, like this') -- used as an adverb meaning 'like this/that' or 'in this way', and also as a filler/discourse word meaning 'just so, for no reason'.
Sense 1 : in this/that manner
adverb Often paired with a demonstrated action or gesture; can point forward or backward to something just shown or said.
Synonyms: इस तरह (is tarah), इस प्रकार (is prakaar, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly with a hand gesture, like a shopkeeper showing you ऐसे करो (aise karo, 'do it like this') while demonstrating how to fold something or fix a strap.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse ऐसे (aise) with ऐसा (aisaa) -- use ऐसे (aise) when modifying a verb/action (adverbial), and ऐसा (aisaa) (agreeing in gender/number) when modifying a noun. 2) ऐसे (aise) is also the correct oblique plural/oblique form of ऐसा (aisaa) before postpositions, e.g. ऐसे लोगों से (aise logon se, 'from such people') -- this is not a separate word, just the expected inflection.
Sample Sentences:
ऐसे करो, तो काम आसान हो जाएगा। Do it like this, and the work will become easy.
(aise karo, to kaam aasaan ho jaaegaa।)

Sense 2 : just because, for no particular reason
adverb Used alone as a dismissive or casual answer to 'why', implying there's no real reason worth stating.
Synonyms: बस (bas), बेवजह (bevjah)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the classic evasive answer in Hindi -- a mother asking क्यों रो रही हो? (kyon ro rahii ho?, 'why are you crying?') might get ऐसे ही (aise hii) as the deliberately vague reply. It signals 'don't push further' as much as it states a fact.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often miss this idiomatic standalone use and try to translate it literally as 'like this' when a native speaker means 'no particular reason' -- e.g. answering क्यों? (kyon?, 'why?') with ऐसे ही (aise hii, 'just because').
Sample Sentences:
क्यों रो रही हो? -- ऐसे ही। Why are you crying? -- No reason.
(kyon ro rahii ho? -- aise hii।)
