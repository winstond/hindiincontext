रेत (ret)

noun A common everyday noun meaning 'sand'; it also appears in a few extended/figurative uses tied to the image of sand slipping or shifting.
Sense 1 : sand (the granular material)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: बालू (baaloo)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of riverbanks like the Yamuna's, or the sand pits (रेत के गड्ढे, ret ke gaddhe) where construction sand is dug up around Delhi-NCR -- रेत (ret) is the word you'd use for both a beach and a truckload of construction material. Its close cousin बालू (baaloo) is interchangeable in most contexts, though रेत (ret) is the more common everyday choice in the Delhi belt.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat रेत (ret) as masculine by default; it is feminine, so adjectives and verbs agreeing with it take feminine forms, e.g. रेत गरम है (ret garam hai, 'the sand is hot').

Sense 2 : something fleeting, ungraspable, or slipping away (figurative)
noun, feminine Used mainly in fixed images like हाथ से रेत की तरह फिसलना (haath se ret kii tarah phisalnaa, 'to slip away like sand from the hand'), describing time, opportunities, or relationships that cannot be held onto.
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Hindi shares this image with English (sand through the fingers), so it usually feels intuitive to learners -- you'll hear it in songs and heartfelt speech about time or youth passing, e.g. वक्त रेत की तरह फिसल गया (vakt ret kii tarah phisal gayaa, 'time slipped away like sand').
