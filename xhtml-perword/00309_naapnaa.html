नापना (naapnaa)

verb A concrete transitive verb meaning to measure -- length, distance, weight, or other quantities -- with a figurative extension to sizing someone up or scrutinizing them. English inline: measure (naapnaa).
Sense 1 : to measure (length, distance, quantity)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: मापना (maapnaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly at a tailor's shop or fabric market -- दर्जी कपड़ा नापता है (darjii kapdaa naaptaa hai, 'the tailor measures the cloth') -- and its close cousin मापना (maapnaa) is largely interchangeable in everyday speech, though नापना (naapnaa) feels slightly more hands-on and physical.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for माँगना (maangnaa, 'to ask for') by mistake because of the similar sound -- नापना (naapnaa) has nothing to do with asking or requesting. 2) The tool or unit used to measure is typically marked with से (se), e.g. फीते से नापना (phiite se naapnaa, 'to measure with a tape'), which can trip up learners expecting a different postposition.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to size someone up, scrutinize/judge (figurative)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: जाँचना (jaanchnaa, to inspect/check), आँकना (aanknaa, to gauge/assess)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Picture someone giving a newcomer a slow, appraising once-over -- Hindi captures that with उसने मुझे सिर से पैर तक नाप लिया (usne mujhe sir se pair tak naap liyaa, 'he sized me up from head to toe'). It carries a slightly judgmental or wary edge, the kind of look a shopkeeper or a strict relative might give a stranger.
Common Mistakes: 1) In this figurative sense the object is a person, often with सिर से पैर तक (sir se pair tak, 'from head to toe') added -- learners sometimes miss that the literal measuring-tool phrases (फीते से, गज़ से) don't belong here since no physical instrument is involved.
Sample Sentences:
