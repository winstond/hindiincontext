भाभी (bhaabhii)

noun A kinship term meaning 'brother's wife' (sister-in-law) -- one core sense, but with an extended everyday sense used as a friendly/respectful address for an older married woman in one's social circle.
Sense 1 : brother's wife, sister-in-law
noun, feminine
Synonyms: भाभी जी (bhaabhii jii, more respectful form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In a joint or extended Indian family, भाभी (bhaabhii) is often a warm, teasing relationship -- especially between a भाभी (bhaabhii) and her husband's younger siblings (देवर/dewar and ननद/nanad), who may joke and banter with her in a way they wouldn't with an elder. This dynamic (देवर-भाभी, devar-bhaabhii) shows up constantly in TV serials and Bollywood family comedies.
Common Mistakes: 1) English 'sister-in-law' also covers husband's sister and wife's sister, but भाभी (bhaabhii) is specifically the wife of one's brother (or male cousin treated as a brother) -- other relations have their own separate terms. 2) The vocative use ('Bhabhi!') doesn't need the possessive -- you don't usually say मेरी भाभी (merii bhaabhii) when addressing her directly, just भाभी (bhaabhii) or भाभी जी (bhaabhii jii).

Sense 2 : friendly/respectful term of address for an older married woman (not a blood relative)
noun, feminine Used as a direct address or to refer to a married woman close to the speaker's family or neighborhood, without any actual sibling-in-law relationship.
Synonyms: दीदी (diidii, used for an older woman more generally, not necessarily married), आंटी (aantii, more distancing/formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Calling a neighbor's wife or a close friend's wife भाभी (bhaabhii) instantly signals affection and near-family status -- it's a very common feature of mohalla (neighborhood, mohallaa) life in Delhi-NCR, where social relationships often borrow kinship vocabulary.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes default to आंटी (aantii) for every older woman, but भाभी (bhaabhii) is warmer and implies more closeness or familiarity, often used for a neighbor's or friend's wife rather than a stranger.
