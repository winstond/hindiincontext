चला जाना (chalaa jaanaa)

verb A compound verb built from चलना (chalnaa, 'to move/walk') plus जाना (jaanaa, 'to go') as the completive vector verb; it means 'to leave/go away' with a sense of departure being completed or set in motion. Closely related to just जाना (jaanaa) but adds a nuance of setting off or moving away decisively.
Sense 1 : to leave, go away
verb, intransitive The compound uses जाना (jaanaa) as a completive vector verb on the root चल- (chal), adding a sense of decisive departure or motion getting underway, compared to plain चलना (chalnaa).
Synonyms: निकल जाना (nikal jaanaa), रवाना होना (ravaanaa honaa, formal), खिसक जाना (khisak jaanaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the most common ways to say someone 'left' or 'took off' in everyday Hindi -- वह चला गया (vah chalaa gayaa, 'he left') is what you'd say about a guest who's gone home or a bus that's already pulled away. It pairs naturally with time or suddenness words like अचानक (achaanak, 'suddenly') to convey an abrupt departure.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use plain चलना (chalnaa) when they mean 'left/went away' -- चला गया (chalaa gayaa, 'he left') is the natural way to report a completed departure, while चलना (chalnaa) alone leans toward 'to walk/move' or 'to function.' 2) Agreement is with the subject as with जाना (jaanaa): वह चली गई (vah chalii gaii, 'she left') needs both चली and गई to agree in gender, which learners forget when the subject is feminine.
Sample Sentences:
वह चला गया। He left.
(vah chalaa gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to pass away, die (euphemistic)
verb, intransitive
Synonyms: गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa), स्वर्ग सिधारना (svarg sidhaarnaa, literary/euphemistic), गुज़र जाना (guzar jaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just as English softens death with 'passed away' or 'is gone,' Hindi speakers often reach for चला जाना (chalaa jaanaa) instead of मरना (marnaa, 'to die'), especially when speaking respectfully about an elder -- दादाजी चले गए (daadaajii chale gae, 'grandfather has passed away') is far gentler than saying he died outright.
Common Mistakes: 1) This euphemistic sense overlaps heavily with the literal 'leave/go away' sense, so context (illness, age, funeral talk) is what signals death rather than any special grammar -- वह चले गए (vah chale gae, 'he passed away') looks identical in form to a simple departure sentence.
