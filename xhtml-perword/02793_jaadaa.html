जाड़ा (jaadaa)

noun Means winter/cold season, and by extension the physical sensation of cold; commonly used in its plural form जाड़े (jaade) to mean 'the winter months.'
Sense 1 : winter, the cold season
noun, masculine Often used in the plural जाड़े (jaade) to refer to 'the winter season' as a stretch of time, e.g. जाड़ों में (jaadon men, 'in winter').
Synonyms: सर्दी (sardii), शीत (shiit, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In Delhi-NCR, जाड़ा (jaadaa) evokes a very specific seasonal picture: thick रज़ाइयों (razaaiyon, 'quilts'), morning fog, and steaming cups of चाय (chaay) on the roadside. Older speakers and rural/Hindi-heartland families often prefer जाड़ा (jaadaa) over the more urban-neutral सर्दी (sardii), so it carries a slightly homely, old-fashioned warmth.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use only the singular जाड़ा (jaadaa) where native speakers default to the plural जाड़े (jaade) when talking about the season generally, as in जाड़ों में (jaadon men, 'in winter'). 2) Do not confuse with ठंड (thand), which refers more to the coldness/chill itself rather than the season as a whole, though the two overlap heavily in casual speech.

दिल्ली में जाड़ा नवंबर के आखिर में शुरू होता है। Winter starts in Delhi towards the end of November.
(dillii men jaadaa navambar ke aakhir men shuroo hotaa hai।)

Sense 2 : the feeling of cold, chilliness
noun, masculine Used with लगना (lagnaa) in dative-subject constructions, e.g. मुझे जाड़ा लगता है (mujhe jaadaa lagtaa hai, 'I feel cold').
Synonyms: सर्दी (sardii), ठंड (thand)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with the winter-season sense above -- both come from the same root idea of 'cold.' In everyday speech, ठंड (thand) is actually more common than जाड़ा (jaadaa) for describing the physical feeling of cold, so listen for both.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes say मैं जाड़ा हूँ (main jaadaa hoon) by analogy with English 'I am cold' -- the correct pattern is the दाता-subject frame मुझे जाड़ा लगता है (mujhe jaadaa lagtaa hai), literally 'cold feels to me.'
