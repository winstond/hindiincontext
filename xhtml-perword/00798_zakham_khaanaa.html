ज़ख़म खाना (zakham khaanaa)

verb A compound expression built from ज़ख़म (zakham, 'wound') and खाना (khaanaa, 'to eat'), meaning 'to suffer a wound/injury' -- both a literal physical sense and a common figurative one for emotional hurt.
Sense 1 : to be wounded, injured (literal)
verb, transitive खाना (khaanaa) here works as a light verb meaning 'to receive/undergo,' with the subject as the one who receives the harm -- compare चोट खाना (chot khaanaa) and मार खाना (maar khaanaa) built on the same pattern.
Synonyms: ज़ख़मी होना (zakhmii honaa), घाव खाना (ghaav khaanaa, less common), चोट खाना (chot khaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi uses the 'eating' pattern (खाना, khaanaa) for a whole family of things one passively receives or endures -- मार खाना (maar khaanaa, 'to get beaten'), डांट खाना (daant khaanaa, 'to get scolded'), धोखा खाना (dhokhaa khaanaa, 'to be deceived') -- so once a learner internalizes ज़ख़म खाना (zakham khaanaa), the whole pattern of 'खाना (khaanaa) = to be at the receiving end of something unpleasant' clicks into place.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try को-marking on the wound (ज़ख़म को खाना, zakham ko khaanaa) as if 'eating' the wound were a direct transitive action on an object of focus, but the phrase is used as a fixed idiom describing something happening *to* the subject, so the wound stays a plain object, not marked specially. 2) Don't confuse with ज़ख़म देना (zakham denaa, 'to inflict a wound'), which reverses the roles -- देना (denaa) is the giver's side, खाना (khaanaa) is the receiver's side.

(sipaahii ladaaii men zakham khaa gayaa।)

Sense 2 : to be emotionally hurt, wounded (figurative)
verb, transitive Often appears with दिल पर (dil par, 'on the heart') or a source marked with से (se) to specify what caused the hurt, e.g. उसकी बात से (uskii baat se, 'from his/her words').
Synonyms: दिल पर चोट खाना (dil par chot khaanaa), आहत होना (aahat honaa, more formal), दुखी होना (dukhii honaa, broader meaning)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative sense leans into Hindi/Urdu poetic tradition, where the heart (दिल, dil) is imagined as a body that can be wounded just like flesh -- you'll meet this same imagery constantly in ghazals and film lyrics, where प्यार में ज़ख़म खाना (pyaar men zakham khaanaa, 'to be wounded in love') is a stock romantic image.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for hurt (hurt) as a literal translation and use घायल होना (ghaayal honaa), which sounds oddly clinical/physical for emotional pain -- ज़ख़म खाना (zakham khaanaa) is the natural figurative choice in everyday speech and writing about feelings.
