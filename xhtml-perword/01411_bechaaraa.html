बेचारा (bechaaraa)

adjective/noun An adjective/noun meaning 'poor thing, helpless one' -- an expression of pity or mild condescension toward someone in an unfortunate situation.
Sense 1 : pitiable, helpless (poor thing)
adjective
Synonyms: गरीब (gariib, used loosely for 'pitiable' rather than 'poor in money'), मजबूर (majboor, helpless/compelled)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you reach for when clicking your tongue in sympathy -- a friend who got soaked in the rain, a stray dog shivering in winter, a kid who failed an exam despite trying hard. It carries genuine warmth, but be aware it can also tip into gentle condescension, as in बेचारा, उसे कुछ नहीं आता (bechaaraa, use kuchh nahiin aataa, 'poor guy, he doesn't know anything'), where the pity has a slight edge of dismissal.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this with गरीब (gariib), which primarily means financially poor; बेचारा (bechaaraa) is about being pitiable or helpless, regardless of wealth -- a rich person can be बेचारा (bechaaraa) if they're in a sad situation. 2) It agrees in gender/number like a regular -आ adjective (बेचारी (bechaarii) for feminine, बेचारे (bechaare) for plural/oblique), which learners sometimes forget to adjust when describing a woman or multiple people.

Sense 2 : a pitiable/helpless person (used as a noun)
noun, masculine/feminine Functions as a noun when used standalone to refer to the person, without a following noun it modifies -- e.g. बेचारा क्या करता (bechaaraa kyaa kartaa, 'what could the poor fellow do').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll often hear this used almost like an interjection tacked onto a story -- someone describes a mishap and finishes with बेचारा! (bechaaraa), the way an English speaker might sigh 'poor thing!' This noun use is really the same word as the adjective, just standing on its own rather than describing a following noun.
