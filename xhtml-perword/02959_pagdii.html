पगड़ी (pagdii)

noun A traditional cloth turban worn wound around the head; also used figuratively for honor and prestige, as seen in common idioms.
Sense 1 : turban (headwear)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: साफ़ा (saafaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The पगड़ी (pagdii) is worn very differently across India -- a Rajasthani, a Punjabi, and a Sikh पगड़ी (pagdii) differ in color, shape, and style, and each carries its own regional or religious identity. In Sikh tradition it is an article of faith, not just clothing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse पगड़ी (pagdii) with टोपी (topii) -- a टोपी (topii) is a simple cap, while a पगड़ी (pagdii) is a long cloth specifically wound around the head, often with ceremonial or regional significance.

Sense 2 : honor, prestige, dignity (figurative)
noun, feminine Mainly encountered inside the fixed idiom पगड़ी उछालना (pagdii uchhaalnaa, 'to throw someone's turban', i.e. to publicly disgrace or humiliate them) and related phrases like पगड़ी उतारना (pagdii utaarnaa, to strip someone of honor).
Synonyms: इज़्ज़त (izzat), मान-सम्मान (maan-sammaan)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Because the turban sits visibly on the head, it became Hindi's natural symbol for a person's public honor -- to knock it off (पगड़ी उछालना (pagdii uchhaalnaa)) is to shame someone in front of everyone. This mirrors how English speakers might say someone was 'brought low' or had their name dragged through the mud.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense almost never stands alone -- learners should expect to meet it inside set phrases about honor being 'placed on' or 'thrown off' someone's head, not as a free-standing noun for 'honor' in ordinary sentences.
