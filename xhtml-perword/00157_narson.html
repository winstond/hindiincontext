नरसों (narson)

adverb A time adverb meaning either 'the day before yesterday' or 'the day after tomorrow' (two days from the reference point, in either direction) -- context and tense tell you which.
Sense 1 : two days from now (day after tomorrow)
adverb
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Think of नरसों (narson) as a local cousin of परसों (parson) -- you will hear it from older speakers or in Haryanvi-inflected Delhi speech, but if you say परसों (parson) instead, everyone will understand you and you will sound more textbook-standard.
Common Mistakes: 1) नरसों (narson) covers both directions in time -- 'day after tomorrow' and 'day before yesterday' -- unlike English, which uses two different phrases. Only the surrounding verb tense tells you which one is meant, so learners often reach for a separate word when none exists. 2) Do not confuse with परसों (parson), which is the far more common word for the same two meanings; नरसों (narson) is a regional/colloquial variant heard in and around Delhi-NCR and Haryana, not the standard form taught in most textbooks.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : two days ago (day before yesterday)
adverb
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the future-facing sense; Hindi (like several other South Asian languages) often lets one word do double duty for 'two days away' in either direction, leaning entirely on the verb tense to disambiguate.
Common Mistakes: 1) The past-tense reading only becomes clear from the verb in the sentence -- नरसों (narson) itself gives no signal of direction, so learners should listen for the tense rather than the word.
