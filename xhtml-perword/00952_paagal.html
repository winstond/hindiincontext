पागल (paagal)

adjective/noun One core meaning -- 'mad/crazy' -- used literally for mental illness and, far more often in daily speech, as a light figurative jab (foolish, obsessed, or wildly enthusiastic).
Sense 1 : foolish, crazy, acting senselessly
adjective
Synonyms: बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof), मूर्ख (moorkh, formal), सनकी (sankii, eccentric)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the sense you hear constantly in banter -- दोस्तों में मज़ाक (doston men mazaak, joking among friends) -- said with a laugh, not an insult, much like calling a friend 'crazy' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used casually among friends it is affectionate teasing, but calling a stranger or elder पागल (paagal) can land as genuinely rude -- tone and relationship matter more than the dictionary meaning. 2) Learners sometimes reach for पागल (paagal) where बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof) fits better -- पागल (paagal) leans toward 'acting irrationally/crazily' while बेवकूफ़ (bevkoof) is plain 'stupid'.
Sample Sentences:
तू पागल है क्या, इतनी रात में फ़ोन करेगा? Are you crazy, calling at this hour of the night?
(too paagal hai kyaa, itnii raat men fon karegaa?)

Sense 2 : obsessed with, wildly enthusiastic about (someone/something)
adjective This sense typically appears with के पीछे (ke piichhe, 'after/behind') or पर (par) marking the object of obsession, e.g. 'क्रिकेट के पीछे पागल (kriket ke piichhe paagal, 'crazy about cricket')'.
Synonyms: दीवाना (diivaanaa, especially for love/passion), जुनूनी (junoonii, obsessive)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly about fandoms -- वह क्रिकेट के पीछे पागल है (vah kriket ke piichhe paagal hai, 'he's crazy about cricket') is the everyday shape of Indian sports and film devotion.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to के लिए (ke lie) for 'crazy about X', but के पीछे (ke piichhe) or पर (par) is the natural frame here, not 'for'.

Sense 3 : mentally ill, insane (clinical/literal)
adjective
Synonyms: विक्षिप्त (vikshipt, formal/clinical), दीवाना (diivaanaa, poetic, love-crazed rather than clinical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: पागलखाना (paagalkhaanaa, 'madhouse', i.e. a mental asylum) is built directly on this literal sense -- a useful anchor for remembering it is the 'serious' meaning underneath the joke.
Common Mistakes: 1) This literal medical sense is real but far less common in daily conversation than the teasing sense above; overusing it in that heavier meaning can sound blunt or even cruel about a real person's condition, where पागलपन (paagalpan, madness) or a softer phrase is preferred in polite talk.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 4 : a crazy/mad person
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: दीवाना (diivaanaa, lovestruck madman), सनकी (sankii, eccentric person)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The same word doubles as noun and adjective without any change in form -- वह पागल है (vah paagal hai, 'he is crazy') versus एक पागल (ek paagal, 'a madman') -- so context alone tells you which role it is playing.
