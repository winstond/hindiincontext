डर के मारे (dar ke maare)

adverb A fixed idiomatic expression meaning 'out of fear' or 'because of fear' -- it explains a reaction or action as being caused by fright. Built from डर (dar, 'fear') plus के मारे (ke maare, a postpositional pattern meaning 'because of, on account of').
Sense 1 : out of fear, because of fear
adverb Modifies the whole clause it precedes, explaining the cause of the action that follows: डर के मारे वह भाग गया (dar ke maare vah bhaag gayaa, 'out of fear, he ran away').
Synonyms: डर से (dar se), भय के कारण (bhay ke kaaran, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The pattern x के मारे (ke maare) shows up with other intense emotions too, like गुस्से के मारे (gusse ke maare, 'out of anger') or भूख के मारे (bhookh ke maare, 'out of hunger') -- it is Hindi's way of saying someone was so overcome by a feeling that it drove their action, similar to English 'out of' but with a stronger sense of being overwhelmed.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try to use डर के मारे (dar ke maare) as if मारे (maare) were the verb 'to hit' in this phrase -- here मारे (maare) has bleached into a fixed postposition meaning 'because of, overcome by,' and has no literal hitting sense at all. 2) This phrase always precedes the clause it explains, not the noun डर (dar) alone -- learners sometimes stop after डर के मारे (dar ke maare) expecting it to stand as a full thought, but it needs a following result clause to complete the sense.
Sample Sentences:
डर के मारे उसकी आवाज़ काँप रही थी। Out of fear, his voice was trembling.
(dar ke maare uskii aavaaz kaamp rahii thii।)
