रुपया (rupyaa)

noun The name of India's currency unit, the rupee; also used loosely and figuratively to mean 'money' in general, the way English speakers say 'cash' or 'bucks'.
Sense 1 : the rupee (currency unit)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear रुपया (rupyaa) constantly in markets and taxis -- bargaining almost always happens in rupees, paise (paisaa) being so small now that it barely enters spoken transactions. The word itself comes from Sanskrit रूप्य (roopy, 'wrought silver'), a nice bit of history behind a coin that hasn't been silver for a long time.
Common Mistakes: 1) The plural for counting is रुपये (rupye) not रुपया (rupyaa) -- 'do sau rupaye' (do sau rupye, 'two hundred rupees'), never 'रुपया' after a number greater than one. 2) In casual speech the sum is often quoted with no explicit word for 'rupees' at all when the context is obvious, e.g. just a number, which can throw learners expecting the noun every time.

(ek kilo pyaaz kitne rupye kaa hai?)

Sense 2 : money, cash (in general)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: पैसा (paisaa), धन (dhan, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Just as English speakers say 'I don't have the cash for that' without meaning literal paper bills, Hindi speakers say मेरे पास रुपया नहीं है (mere paas rupyaa nahiin hai, 'I don't have the money') to mean funds in general, not a specific rupee note.
Common Mistakes: 1) Used generically, रुपया (rupyaa) overlaps heavily with पैसा (paisaa) -- both can mean 'money' broadly, and learners often assume one is 'wrong,' but in everyday speech people swap them freely for this general sense.
