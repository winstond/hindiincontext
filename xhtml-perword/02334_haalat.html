हालत (haalat)

noun Means 'condition' or 'state' -- of health, of a situation, or of an object -- and also shows up in the fixed phrase इस हालत में (is haalat men) meaning 'in this condition/state.' Closely related to हाल (haal, 'state/condition/news'), which a learner will also meet.
Sense 1 : condition, state (of health or circumstance)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: हाल (haal), दशा (dashaa), स्थिति (sthiti)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in the context of health: उसकी हालत ठीक नहीं है (uskii haalat thiik nahiin hai, 'his/her condition is not good') is what a family member or doctor says outside a hospital room. It also covers a country's economy, a road's disrepair, or a relationship gone bad -- Hindi treats all of these as 'conditions' the same way English does with 'state.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use it only for physical health, but हालत (haalat) is equally common for financial, emotional, or situational states -- माली हालत (maalii haalat, 'financial condition') is a very frequent collocation. 2) Confused with हाल (haal): हाल (haal) leans toward 'news/recent state' (हाल-चाल haal-chaal), while हालत (haalat) leans toward the state itself, often a difficult or serious one.
Sample Sentences:
उसकी हालत ठीक नहीं है। His condition is not good.
(uskii haalat thiik nahiin hai।)

Sense 2 : a bad or dire state (implied by context, not the word alone)
noun, feminine This is the same word as the neutral 'condition' sense, but used bare -- without an adjective -- it very often implies a negative state by conversational default, especially in exclamations.
Synonyms: दुर्दशा (durdshaa), बुरा हाल (buraa haal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the हालत (haalat) of everyday complaint -- a mother scolding a muddy child might say यह क्या हालत बना ली है? (yah kyaa haalat banaa lii hai, 'what state have you gotten yourself into?'). The word itself is neutral, but Delhi speech loads it with exasperation through tone and the verbs around it (बना देना banaa denaa, हो जाना ho jaanaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes add ठीक (thiik) or अच्छी (achchhii) redundantly when the negative meaning is already clear from tone or context; native speakers often just say क्या हालत बना दी! (kyaa haalat banaa dii, 'what a state you have made [of it/yourself]!') without any explicit negative adjective.
Sample Sentences:
यह क्या हालत बना ली है तुमने? What state have you gotten yourself into?
(yah kyaa haalat banaa lii hai tumne?)
Register: Casual -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Present -- Voice: Active
