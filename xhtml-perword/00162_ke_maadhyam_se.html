के माध्यम से (ke maadhyam se)

postposition A postpositional phrase meaning 'through' or 'by means of' -- used to indicate a medium, channel, or intermediary through which something happens. Built from माध्यम (maadhyam, 'medium'), it is a more formal alternative to से (se) alone.
Sense 1 : through, by means of, via (a medium or intermediary)
postposition Governs the oblique of the preceding noun via के (ke); the noun sits between के (ke) and माध्यम से (maadhyam se), e.g. 'दोस्त के माध्यम से' (dost ke maadhyam se, 'through a friend').
Synonyms: द्वारा (dvaaraa, formal), से (se, plainer alternative)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase has a formal, written-register flavor -- you will see it constantly in news reports, official notices, and essays ('सरकार के माध्यम से', 'through the government'), but in relaxed daily chat, most Delhi speakers reach for the shorter से (se) or through द्वारा (dvaaraa) in writing. Recognizing it is more important than actively producing it in casual speech.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop के (ke) and just say माध्यम से (maadhyam se) after a noun in the direct case -- the noun must be in the oblique-triggering के (ke) construction, like other के-postpositions (के लिए (ke lie), के बारे में (ke baare men)). 2) Overused in spoken conversation where से (se) alone would sound more natural -- यह खबर मुझे रेडियो से मिली (yah khabar mujhe rediyo se milii, 'I got this news from the radio') is more natural in casual speech than inserting के माध्यम से (ke maadhyam se).
