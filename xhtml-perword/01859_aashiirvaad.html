आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad)

noun A single-sense noun meaning 'blessing' -- the formal good wishes an elder gives a younger person, often as part of a set social gesture (touching feet, bowing the head).
Sense 1 : blessing (given by an elder or respected person)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: दुआ (duaa, often from elders/religious figures, overlaps in use), कृपा (kripaa, grace/favor, broader sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This word is tied to a very real gesture in Hindi-speaking households: younger people touch the feet of elders (पैर छूना, pair chhoonaa) and the elder responds by placing a hand on their head and saying something like खुश रहो (khush raho, 'be happy') -- that whole exchange is called लेना/देना आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad lenaa / aashiirvaad denaa). It carries a weight that the English word 'blessing' doesn't fully capture, since it's less about religion and more about the social hierarchy of respect between generations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad) interchangeably with दुआ (duaa), but आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad) specifically flows from someone senior or respected (a grandparent, a guru, a god) to someone junior -- you would not say you give आशीर्वाद (aashiirvaad) to your boss or an elder. 2) The common verb pairing is आशीर्वाद देना (aashiirvaad denaa, 'to give a blessing') for the giver and आशीर्वाद लेना (aashiirvaad lenaa, 'to take/receive a blessing') for the receiver -- learners sometimes default to लेना (lenaa) or देना (denaa) inconsistently with who is doing the blessing versus receiving it.

(shaadii se pahle usne apne maataa-pitaa kaa aashiirvaad liyaa।)
