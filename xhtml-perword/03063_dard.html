दर्द (dard)

noun Mainly means physical pain/ache as a noun, but extends naturally to emotional pain/anguish, and appears in a common adjective/compound-forming role meaning 'sympathetic, feeling another's pain' (as in दर्दमंद (dardmand)) -- here treated as the core noun sense plus its figurative extension.
Sense 1 : physical pain, ache
noun, masculine Used with होना (honaa) in dative-subject constructions: मुझे दर्द है (mujhe dard hai, 'I have pain') or सिर में दर्द है (sir men dard hai, 'there is a headache') -- the person feeling it takes को/मुझे, not ने.
Synonyms: पीड़ा (piidaa, slightly formal/literary), तकलीफ़ (takliif)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday speech, Hindi treats pain as something that arrives or happens to you rather than something you actively do -- दर्द होना (dard honaa) literally 'pain is happening,' mirroring the same dative-subject logic as नींद आना (niind aanaa) or पसंद आना (pasand aanaa). Doctors and patients alike default to कहाँ दर्द है? (kahaan dard hai, 'where does it hurt') as the standard clinical question.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often try को करना (ko karnaa) or a subject-verb pattern like 'I hurt' instead of the correct दर्द होना (dard honaa) or दर्द करना (dard karnaa) construction, e.g. पेट दर्द कर रहा है (pet dard kar rahaa hai, 'the stomach is aching'). 2) दर्द is masculine, so adjectives and verb agreement follow that even when the sentence is 'about' the (possibly female) speaker's body part.

Sense 2 : emotional pain, anguish, heartache
noun, masculine
Synonyms: ग़म (gham), पीड़ा (piidaa, literary), टीस (tiis, a sharper, more literary pang)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the दर्द (dard) of a thousand Bollywood ghazals and sad songs -- दिल का दर्द (dil kaa dard) is a stock phrase for heartbreak, and dard-bhare geet (sorrow-filled songs) are a whole genre unto themselves. It sits comfortably between casual conversation ('sunte hain dard hai') and deeply emotional, almost poetic registers.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for दुख (dukh) by default for all sadness; दर्द carries a more visceral, felt-in-the-chest quality often used in songs and heartfelt speech, e.g. दिल का दर्द (dil kaa dard, 'the heart's pain').
