नशा (nashaa)

noun Primarily means intoxication or a drug/substance that causes it, and extends easily into a figurative sense of being 'high' on power, love, success, or pride.
Sense 1 : intoxication, the state of being drunk or drugged
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मद (mad, literary), सुरूर (suroor)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear नशा चढ़ गया (nashaa chadh gayaa, 'the intoxication has risen/hit') at a dhaba table as often as in a doctor's warning against नशाखोरी (nashaakhorii, 'substance abuse') -- the same word moves comfortably between a casual joke and a serious public-health poster.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for शराब (sharaab) when they mean the drunken *state* -- नशा (nashaa) is the intoxication itself, not the drink. 2) The common verb pairing is नशा होना (nashaa honaa, 'to become intoxicated') or नशा चढ़ना (nashaa chadhnaa, 'for intoxication to rise/take hold') -- learners sometimes force पीना (piinaa) alone to carry this meaning.

Sense 2 : intoxicant, drug, or alcoholic substance itself
noun, masculine Used as a countable/mass noun for the substance, as in नशा करना (nashaa karnaa, 'to take drugs/consume an intoxicant').
Synonyms: मादक पदार्थ (maadak padaarth, formal, 'narcotic substance'), ड्रग्स (drags, colloquial loanword)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Public campaigns against addiction almost always use this sense: नशा मुक्ति केंद्र (nashaa mukti kendr, 'de-addiction center') signs are common outside Delhi hospitals and government clinics.
Common Mistakes: 1) नशा करना (nashaa karnaa) is the standard phrase for 'to use drugs' or 'to drink' habitually -- learners sometimes translate this too literally as 'do intoxication', which sounds odd; think of it as a fixed collocation.

Sense 3 : figurative intoxication -- being 'high' on power, love, pride, success, etc.
noun, masculine Takes a genitive complement naming the source: सत्ता का नशा (sattaa kaa nashaa, 'the intoxication of power'), जवानी का नशा (javaanii kaa nashaa, 'the intoxication of youth').
Synonyms: जुनून (junoon, 'obsession/passion', broader meaning), सुरूर (suroor)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the नशा (nashaa) of Bollywood lyrics and political commentary alike -- सत्ता का नशा (sattaa kaa nashaa) is a stock phrase for describing politicians drunk on power, and प्यार का नशा (pyaar kaa nashaa) for someone besotted with love. It is a favorite word for Hindi writers because it lets abstract feelings behave like alcohol -- something that rises, fades, and can be 'broken' (नशा टूटना, nashaa tootnaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often default to खुशी (khushii, 'happiness') for a feeling of elation, missing that नशा (nashaa) specifically implies a heady, almost dangerous loss of perspective, not simple joy.
