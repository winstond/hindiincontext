पदार्थ (padaarth)

noun A Sanskrit-origin noun meaning 'substance' or 'matter' in a general physical/philosophical sense, and also 'thing, object, item' more loosely; commonly seen in science contexts and in older or formal usage for everyday 'thing.'
Sense 1 : substance, matter (physical/chemical)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: द्रव्य (dravy, more technical/scientific), तत्व (tatv, element or essence, not identical)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the word you will meet in school science textbooks -- रासायनिक पदार्थ (raasaaynik padaarth, 'chemical substance') or पदार्थ की अवस्था (padaarth kii avasthaa, 'state of matter') -- so it carries a slightly bookish, classroom flavor even outside of science.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use पदार्थ (padaarth) for any random 'thing' in casual speech, when चीज़ (chiiz) is what native speakers reach for in everyday conversation -- पदार्थ (padaarth) leans formal or scientific. 2) In chemistry class contexts (rasaayan padaarth, raasaaynik padaarth, 'chemical substance'), the word is standard and not overly formal -- so register shifts by context, not just by word choice.

Sense 2 : thing, object, item (general, somewhat formal)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चीज़ (chiiz), वस्तु (vastu, slightly more formal, common in shops/labels)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will still hear this sense in fixed formal phrases like खाद्य पदार्थ (khaady padaarth, 'food item/foodstuff'), which shows up often on packaging and in official or nutritional contexts -- a good anchor for remembering this sense.
Common Mistakes: 1) Native speakers overwhelmingly prefer चीज़ (chiiz) for 'thing' in daily conversation -- reaching for पदार्थ (padaarth) here can sound stiff or bookish rather than wrong.
