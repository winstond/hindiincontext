पक्का (pakkaa)

adjective/adverb Covers the physical sense 'ripe/cooked/solid/permanent' and the extended sense 'certain, confirmed, reliable' -- both trace back to the idea of something having reached a firm, finished state. Also functions as a common colloquial adverb/particle meaning 'definitely.'
Sense 1 : certain, confirmed, sure (of a fact, plan, or person's reliability)
adjective
Synonyms: निश्चित (nishchit), तय (tay), यकीनी (yakiinii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you'll hear constantly when making plans in Delhi -- "पक्का?" (pakkaa?, 'for sure?') and "हाँ, पक्का!" (haan, pakkaa!, 'yes, definitely!') are practically a ritual exchange when confirming a meetup, a promise, or a piece of gossip.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for निश्चित (nishchit) in speech where a native speaker would just say पक्का (pakkaa) -- it's the everyday word for 'sure/definite,' while निश्चित (nishchit) leans slightly more formal/written. 2) पक्का है? (pakkaa hai?, 'is that for sure?') is a very common standalone confirmation question -- learners tend to build a fuller sentence when this clipped form is what natives actually say.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : ripe (of fruit); cooked through; solid, sturdy, or permanent (of a structure, material, or arrangement)
adjective Contrasts with कच्चा (kachchaa, 'raw/unripe/temporary/flimsy'), its natural opposite across all these physical meanings -- fruit, construction, and roads are all commonly described with this पक्का (pakkaa) / कच्चा (kachchaa) pair.
Synonyms: स्थायी (sthaayii, for 'permanent' sense), मज़बूत (mazboot, for 'sturdy' sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: The पक्का (pakkaa) / कच्चा (kachchaa) contrast is everywhere in daily life -- from पक्की सड़क (pakkii sadak, 'paved road') versus a dirt path, to पक्का मकान (pakkaa makaan, 'brick house') versus a mud one, this pair maps a whole worldview of 'finished/durable' versus 'raw/temporary' onto very concrete things.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss that पक्का मकान (pakkaa makaan, 'a solid/brick house') describes construction material and permanence (brick/concrete) as opposed to कच्चा मकान (kachchaa makaan, 'a mud/temporary house') -- it's not about size or quality in the way 'solid house' might suggest in English. 2) For fruit, पक्का (pakkaa) means ripe, not merely 'ready' -- an unripe mango is कच्चा आम (kachchaa aam, 'raw/unripe mango'), a common snack in its own right, so don't assume unripe fruit is undesirable or inedible.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : definitely, for sure (as a colloquial intensifying response or promise)
adverb Used on its own as an emphatic one-word reply, functioning like an adverb modifying an understood verb rather than modifying a noun.
Synonyms: ज़रूर (zaroor), निश्चित रूप से (nishchit roop se, more formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: As a one-word promise, पक्का! (pakkaa!) carries real social weight in casual Delhi conversation -- saying it is treated almost like a handshake, a small verbal contract that you'll actually show up or do the thing.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often construct a full sentence (e.g. with ज़रूर (zaroor)) when a native speaker would simply answer पक्का! (pakkaa!, 'for sure!') to confirm a promise or plan -- this bare usage is extremely common and worth adopting directly.
