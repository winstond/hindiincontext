विदेशी (videshii)

adjective/noun Built from विदेश (videsh, 'foreign country') plus the adjectival suffix -ई (ii); covers both the adjective 'foreign' and the noun 'foreigner' derived from it.
Sense 1 : foreign
adjective
Synonyms: परदेसी (pardesii, less common), बाहरी (baahrii, 'outside/external', broader meaning)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see this word everywhere in Delhi -- विदेशी मुद्रा (videshii mudraa, 'foreign currency') at money-changers near Connaught Place, विदेशी निवेश (videshii nivesh, 'foreign investment') in news headlines, and विदेशी पर्यटक (videshii parytak, 'foreign tourists') around monuments. It carries a neutral, official tone rather than any warmth or coldness.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse विदेशी (videshii) with बाहरी (baahrii) -- बाहरी just means 'outside/external' (an outsider to a group or place), while विदेशी (videshii) specifically means 'from another country.' 2) As an adjective it is invariant -- it does not change form for gender or number, so विदेशी किताब (videshii kitaab, 'foreign book') and विदेशी किताबें (videshii kitaaben, 'foreign books') both use the same form.

Sense 2 : a foreigner
noun, masculine/feminine Same word as the adjective sense, used as a noun on its own, e.g. वह विदेशी है (vah videshii hai, 'he/she is a foreigner').
Synonyms: परदेसी (pardesii, less common, also carries a poetic 'far from home' feel), गोरा (goraa, informal, specifically 'white/Western foreigner')
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday chatter -- say, a shopkeeper in Paharganj sizing up customers -- विदेशी (videshii) is the polite, standard word for a foreign visitor, while गोरा (goraa) is the casual street term you will overhear more often but wouldn't necessarily use yourself in front of the person being described.
Common Mistakes: 1) गोरा (goraa) is often used colloquially to mean 'foreigner' but literally means 'fair-skinned/white person' -- it is not a neutral equivalent of विदेशी (videshii) and can sound reductive depending on context.
