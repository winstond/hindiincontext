पीटना (piitnaa)

verb A physical verb meaning to hit or beat someone/something; also extends to the idiomatic sense of 'beating' a drum or one's chest, and figuratively to 'flogging' or overusing something like a topic.
Sense 1 : to beat, hit, thrash (a person or animal)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: मारना (maarnaa), धुनना (dhunnaa, colloquial), ठोकना (thoknaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear in stories about strict school teachers or angry parents -- बच्चे को पीटना (bachche ko piitnaa, 'to beat a child') is a phrase that comes up constantly in conversations about old-school Indian discipline, often followed by a rueful joke about how common it used to be.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for मारना (maarnaa) for any kind of hitting; पीटना (piitnaa) specifically implies repeated or sustained blows -- like a thrashing -- rather than a single strike, so it fits contexts like parents beating children or a mob beating someone. 2) The person being beaten takes को (ko), e.g. उसे पीटा (use piitaa, 'beat him'), which learners sometimes forget in favor of a direct object without a postposition.

Sense 2 : to beat/bang (a drum, door, etc.)
verb, transitive Commonly used with ताली (taalii, 'clap') and ढोल (dhol, 'drum') as objects -- ताली पीटना (taalii piitnaa, 'to clap loudly') and ढोल पीटना (dhol piitnaa) both literally invoke repeated striking.
Synonyms: बजाना (bajaanaa, used for musical instruments specifically), ठोकना (thoknaa, for banging on a surface)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: ढोल पीटना (dhol piitnaa, 'to beat the drum') is also used figuratively to mean loudly announcing or publicizing something, much like the English 'to trumpet' -- you'll hear it in sentences like अपनी तारीफ़ का ढोल पीटना (apnii taariif kaa dhol piitnaa, 'to sing one's own praises').
Common Mistakes: 1) ताली पीटना (taalii piitnaa) sounds more forceful and folksy than the more neutral ताली बजाना (taalii bajaanaa) -- both mean 'to clap' but पीटना (piitnaa) suggests vigorous or sustained clapping, which learners often use interchangeably without noticing the difference in intensity.

Sense 3 : to harp on, go on about (a topic), figuratively
verb, transitive Used with एक ही बात (ek hii baat, 'the same thing') or a topic as the object, in a mildly exasperated or dismissive tone about someone who keeps repeating themselves.
Synonyms: रटना (ratnaa, more about repeating/memorizing), दोहराना (dohraanaa, to repeat)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This figurative extension mirrors the English idiom 'to beat a dead horse' -- both languages reach for physical beating to describe tiresome repetition, which is a nice parallel to point out to learners.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense -- as in वही बात पीटना (vahii baat piitnaa, 'to keep beating the same point') -- is casual and slightly critical in tone; learners sometimes use it in neutral contexts where दोहराना (dohraanaa) would sound more appropriate and less judgmental.
