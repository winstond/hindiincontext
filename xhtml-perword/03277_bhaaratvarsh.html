भारतवर्ष (bhaaratvarsh)

noun A formal/literary compound name for India, joining भारत (bhaarat) with वर्ष (varsh, here meaning 'land, realm' rather than 'year'); used mainly in elevated, patriotic, or historical registers rather than everyday speech.
Sense 1 : India (formal/traditional name)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: भारत (bhaarat), हिन्दुस्तान (hindustaan), इंडिया (indiyaa, colloquial)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This is the grand, old-fashioned name for India you meet in school textbooks, patriotic songs, and speeches by leaders on Independence Day or Republic Day, evoking the ancient civilizational idea of the land rather than the modern nation-state. Think of it as sitting alongside भारत माता (bhaarat maataa, 'Mother India') in tone: emotionally resonant and formal, but not something you'd say while giving directions or chatting over chai.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat this as a everyday synonym for भारत (bhaarat); in real conversation, news, or casual writing, भारत (bhaarat) or इंडिया (indiyaa) is used instead -- भारतवर्ष (bhaaratvarsh) shows up in speeches, textbooks, anthems, and historical or devotional contexts, not in ordinary sentences like 'I live in India.' 2) The वर्ष (varsh) here does not mean 'year' as it usually does elsewhere; it is an old sense meaning 'region' or 'realm,' so learners should not read this word as 'year of Bharat.'
