मज़ाक़ (mazaaq)

noun A noun meaning 'joke' or 'fun,' central to a cluster of everyday expressions about joking around, teasing, or not being taken seriously. Also commonly spelled मज़ाक (mazaak) without the nuqta under क.
Sense 1 : a joke; a funny remark
noun, masculine
Synonyms: चुटकुला (chutkulaa, a told/scripted joke), हँसी (hansii, laughter/fun, broader sense), ठिठोली (thitholii, playful teasing)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: मज़ाक़ करना (mazaaq karnaa) is one of the most useful social phrases in Hindi -- Delhi friend groups run on gentle ragging, and knowing how to say 'मैं तो मज़ाक़ कर रहा था' (main to mazaaq kar rahaa thaa, 'I was just joking') can smooth over a comment that landed wrong. The phrase क्या मज़ाक़ है! (kyaa mazaaq hai, 'what a joke!') is used exactly like English, to mock something absurd or unfair.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for चुटकुला (chutkulaa) for any joke, but चुटकुला (chutkulaa) is specifically a narrated/told joke (like a joke with a setup and punchline), while मज़ाक़ (mazaaq) covers a wider range including teasing remarks, pranks, and banter. 2) The common phrase मज़ाक़ करना (mazaaq karnaa, 'to joke/tease') takes से (se) for the person being teased -- मुझसे मज़ाक़ मत करो (mujhse mazaaq mat karo, 'don't joke with me') -- not को (ko), which trips learners used to marking the object of an action.
Sample Sentences:
उसने एक मज़ाक़ सुनाया और सब हँस पड़े। He told a joke and everyone burst out laughing.
(usne ek mazaaq sunaayaa aur sab hans pade।)

Sense 2 : something not to be taken seriously; a trivial or laughable matter
noun, masculine Appears mainly in negative or rhetorical frames like यह कोई मज़ाक़ नहीं है (yah koii mazaaq nahiin hai, 'this is no joke/laughing matter') and क्या तुम्हें मेरी बात मज़ाक़ लगती है? (kyaa tumhen merii baat mazaaq lagtii hai, 'does what I said seem like a joke to you?').
Synonyms: खेल (khel, 'child's play,' figurative), हँसी-मज़ाक़ (hansii-mazaaq, lighthearted fun)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This sense shows up whenever someone wants to be taken seriously: यह कोई मज़ाक़ नहीं है (yah koii mazaaq nahiin hai, 'this is not a joke') is a stock line parents, bosses, and cops alike use to signal 'I mean business.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss that मज़ाक़ लगना (mazaaq lagnaa) uses a dative-subject construction (को + लगना (lagnaa)) to mean 'to seem/feel like a joke to someone' -- उसे यह मज़ाक़ लगा (use yah mazaaq lagaa, 'it seemed like a joke to him'), paralleling other लगना (lagnaa) constructions they already know.
