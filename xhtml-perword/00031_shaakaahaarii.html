शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii)

adjective/noun शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii) means 'vegetarian' -- built from शाक (shaak, vegetable/greens) + आहार (aahaar, diet) + the ई (ii) suffix. It works both as a describing word (शाकाहारी भोजन (shaakaahaarii bhojan, vegetarian food)) and as a noun for a person who eats that way.
Sense 1 : vegetarian (describing food, restaurants, or diet)
adjective
Synonyms: वेज (vej, colloquial, borrowed from English)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Look for the small square symbol on Indian packaged food: a green dot means शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii) and a brown/maroon dot means मांसाहारी (maansaahaarii) -- this red-vs-green coding is second nature to Indian shoppers and appears on every restaurant menu too. The word pair शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii) / मांसाहारी (maansaahaarii) is one of the first food distinctions any visitor to India learns, often before they learn much else.
Common Mistakes: 1) In everyday Indian usage शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii) usually still includes dairy like दूध (doodh, milk) and दही (dahii, yogurt) -- it is not the same as the English 'vegan.' 2) Eggs are almost always excluded, so अंडा (andaa, egg) is generally treated as मांसाहारी (maansaahaarii, non-vegetarian) in India, which surprises many Western learners.

Sense 2 : a vegetarian (a person who does not eat meat, fish, or eggs)
noun, masculine/feminine Used exactly like the English noun 'vegetarian': मैं शाकाहारी हूँ (main shaakaahaarii hoon, 'I am a vegetarian') -- no separate word for the person versus the food quality.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: When ordering at a dhaba or asking about a new friend's food habits, कोई शाकाहारी है क्या? (koii shaakaahaarii hai kyaa?, 'is anyone a vegetarian?') is a completely normal, non-awkward question in India -- unlike in many Western settings, checking everyone's diet before a meal is just good hosting.
Common Mistakes: 1) Strict Jain vegetarians (जैन शाकाहारी, jain shaakaahaarii) also avoid onion and garlic, so simply saying शाकाहारी (shaakaahaarii) at a Jain host's table may not fully convey your dietary needs -- you may need to specify further.
