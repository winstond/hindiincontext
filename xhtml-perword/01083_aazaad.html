आज़ाद (aazaad)

adjective A single core sense meaning 'free/independent', used for people, nations, and situations. Related to the abstract noun आज़ादी (aazaadii, 'freedom').
Sense 1 : free, independent (of a country, person, or situation)
adjective
Synonyms: स्वतंत्र (svatantr, formal), मुक्त (mukt, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Indian schoolchild learns alongside 15 अगस्त (15 agast) and भारत छोड़ो (bhaarat chhodo, 'Quit India') -- आज़ादी (aazaadii) is the everyday word for the freedom movement and Independence Day itself, not स्वतंत्रता (svatantrtaa), which sounds more like a textbook term. You'll also hear it used loosely and warmly, as when a parent tells a grown child तुम आज़ाद हो (tum aazaad ho, 'you're free to do as you like') -- meaning permission, not politics.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for मुक्त (mukt) in everyday speech about a country or person being free -- native speakers overwhelmingly say आज़ाद (aazaad) in this everyday sense, saving मुक्त (mukt) for more formal or abstract contexts (like 'freed from suffering'). 2) As an invariant adjective, it does not change form before masculine or feminine nouns -- आज़ाद औरत (aazaad aurat, 'free woman') and आज़ाद मुल्क (aazaad mulk, 'free country') both use the same form.
