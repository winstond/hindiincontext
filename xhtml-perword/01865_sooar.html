सूअर (sooar)

noun Means 'pig, hog, swine' -- one core sense as a literal animal name, plus a common figurative use as a harsh insult. Also spelled सुअर (suar) without the long vowel.
Sense 1 : the animal (pig, hog, boar)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: सुअर (suar, variant form), बाराह (baaraah, literary), वराह (varaah, literary)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Pigs are not part of mainstream Hindu or Muslim diets in most of North India, so सूअर (sooar) is a word people use to talk *about* an animal they rarely eat or keep at home, unlike गाय (gaay, 'cow') or भैंस (bhains, 'buffalo') which are common in daily rural life. In Delhi-NCR you are more likely to hear this word used to complain about stray pigs near a colony or in the news than in everyday kitchen talk.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes assume सूअर (sooar) is only a swear word and are surprised it is the plain, neutral term for the animal in contexts like farming, zoology, or the wild boar seen near fields. 2) The wild boar is often called जंगली सूअर (janglii sooar, 'wild pig') -- do not drop जंगली (janglii) when specifically meaning 'boar' rather than 'domestic pig.'

Sense 2 : insult meaning a filthy, greedy, or disgusting person
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कमीना (kamiinaa, harsher insult), गंदा इंसान (gandaa insaan, 'dirty person', descriptive not a slur)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Calling someone सूअर (sooar) is a genuine gaali (gaalii, 'curse word') in Hindi, on par with calling someone filthy or morally low, and it can start a real fight if said in the street or in traffic. It draws on the same cultural association as the literal-animal sense -- pigs are seen as unclean scavengers -- so the insult leans on 'dirty, gluttonous, low' rather than on 'fat' the way English insults sometimes do.
Common Mistakes: 1) This is a strong, rude insult in Hindi -- much more cutting than the mild English 'you pig' used jokingly about messiness or overeating. Learners should not use it casually with friends the way English speakers might. 2) It is almost always used vocatively or predicatively about a person's character or hygiene/greed, e.g. साले सूअर (saale sooar, 'you swine') -- not used for describing objects or situations.
