दबे पाँव (dabe paanv)

adverb A fixed adverbial phrase meaning 'stealthily, softly, on tiptoe' -- literally 'with pressed/suppressed feet.' It has one core meaning, used both literally (moving quietly) and figuratively (something arriving unnoticed).
Sense 1 : stealthily, quietly, on tiptoe
adverb Formed from दबा (dabaa, pressed/suppressed) agreeing with पाँव (paanv, feet) in the oblique plural, functioning together as a frozen adverbial phrase -- it does not vary or decline further as a unit.
Synonyms: चुपके से (chupke se), खामोशी से (khaamoshii se, quietly/silently, broader use)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This phrase is a lovely example of body-image idiom in Hindi -- feet (paanv) doing the 'pressing' paints a vivid picture of someone sneaking past a sleeping family member at night, which is exactly the kind of household scene Hindi speakers have in mind when they use it. It is also used poetically for abstract things that creep up on you, like बुढ़ापा दबे पाँव आता है (budhaapaa dabe paanv aataa hai, 'old age comes stealthily') -- so don't be surprised to see it applied to time or fate as much as to footsteps.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to use it as an adjective describing a person ('a stealthy person') -- it only describes the manner of movement or arrival, so it pairs with a verb like आना (aanaa) or चलना (chalnaa), not with a noun directly. 2) It is easy to under-translate as just 'quietly' -- the phrase specifically evokes careful, deliberate softness, like tiptoeing so as not to be heard, not just a low volume of sound.
