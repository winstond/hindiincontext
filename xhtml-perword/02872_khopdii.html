खोपड़ी (khopdii)

noun Literally the skull or scalp; extends colloquially to mean 'brain/head' in the sense of intellect or cunning, and shows up in a few lively idioms about someone being stubborn or short-tempered.
Sense 1 : head/brain as seat of intellect or stubbornness (colloquial)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: दिमाग़ (dimaagh), भेजा (bhejaa, colloquial/slangy)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you reach for when someone's being stubborn or acting foolishly -- उसकी खोपड़ी खराब है (uskii khopdii kharaab hai, 'his head/brain is messed up') is a classic exasperated remark from an elder about a headstrong younger relative. It's affectionate-annoyed more often than genuinely insulting, the tone Delhi aunties perfect.
Common Mistakes: 1) खोपड़ी (khopdii) in this sense is casual and often teasing or exasperated in tone -- it's not a neutral way to say 'mind' the way दिमाग़ (dimaagh) can be; using it in a formal or respectful context about someone's intelligence would sound rude. 2) The common idiom खोपड़ी खराब होना (khopdii kharaab honaa, 'to go crazy/act senselessly') uses it with होना, not other verbs learners might guess.

Sense 2 : skull, cranium
noun, feminine
Synonyms: कपाल (kapaal, literary), सिर की हड्डी (sir kii haddii, descriptive)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this in casual, even slightly rough speech -- a mother might scold a child with सिर पर मत मारो, खोपड़ी फूट जाएगी (sir par mat maaro, khopdii phoot jaaegii, 'don't hit him on the head, his skull will crack'). It carries a physical, almost blunt bluntness that सिर (sir) lacks.
Common Mistakes: 1) Don't confuse with सिर (sir), which is the general word for 'head' -- खोपड़ी (khopdii) specifically evokes the skull/scalp, often with a slightly blunt or informal edge even in this literal sense.
