हरा (haraa)

adjective Mainly the color adjective 'green' (haraa); the same form also occurs as a masculine verb form of हारना (haarnaa, 'to lose') and हरना (harnaa, 'to defeat/take away'), which are separate lemmas covered by their own morphology tables, not here.
Sense 1 : the color green
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हरा (haraa) is the color of freshness and growth in everyday speech -- हरी सब्ज़ी (harii sabzii, 'green vegetables') and हरा धनिया (haraa dhaniyaa, 'fresh coriander') are kitchen staples you will hear constantly at the सब्ज़ी वाला (sabzii vaalaa, 'vegetable vendor')'s cart. It is also one of the three colors of the Indian flag, so it carries a mild patriotic association alongside its literal meaning.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often leave हरा (haraa) unmarked before a feminine or plural noun; like other variable adjectives it must agree: हरी साड़ी (harii saadii, 'green sari'), हरे पत्ते (hare patte, 'green leaves').

Sense 2 : unripe, raw, inexperienced (figurative)
adjective Used of fruit not yet ripe, or figuratively of a person still green/inexperienced, much like English 'green' in this sense.
Synonyms: कच्चा (kachchaa, 'unripe/raw', overlaps closely in the unripe-fruit sense), अनुभवहीन (anubhvahiin, 'inexperienced', formal)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: Just as in English, हरा (haraa) can describe a person who is naive or new to something, though कच्चा (kachchaa) is more common for literally unripe fruit -- the figurative 'green/inexperienced' sense of हरा (haraa) is heard less often in casual speech than its color sense.
