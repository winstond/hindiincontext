ठेकेदार (thekedaar)

noun Primarily 'contractor' in the building/business sense, with a common figurative extension to someone who acts as if they alone are in charge of something (self-appointed authority).
Sense 1 : contractor (building/works/labor contractor)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi-NCR life, ठेकेदार (thekedaar) usually conjures the guy overseeing a construction site or a road repair crew, often with a mix of respect (he brings jobs) and suspicion (corner-cutting, unpaid laborers). The word ठेका (thekaa) itself just means 'contract,' so ठेकेदार (thekedaar) is literally 'contract-holder.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse ठेकेदार (thekedaar) with दुकानदार (dukaandaar) or व्यापारी (vyaapaarii); ठेकेदार (thekedaar) specifically refers to someone who takes on a ठेका (thekaa, 'contract') for construction or supply work, not general trade.

Sense 2 : self-appointed authority; someone who acts like they own/control something (figurative)
noun, masculine Common in the fixed phrase X का ठेकेदार (kaa thekedaar, 'the self-appointed guardian/authority of X'), e.g. संस्कृति का ठेकेदार (sanskriti kaa thekedaar, 'self-appointed guardian of culture').
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this in political and social commentary a lot: 'ये लोग नैतिकता के ठेकेदार बन गए हैं' (ye log naitiktaa ke thekedaar ban gae hain, 'these people have made themselves the self-appointed guardians of morality') -- the image is of someone acting as if they hold an exclusive contract on deciding what's right, which Hindi speakers find both vivid and a bit funny.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is almost always used critically or sarcastically -- calling someone ठेकेदार (thekedaar) in this sense is not a compliment, unlike the neutral literal 'contractor' sense.
Sample Sentences:
