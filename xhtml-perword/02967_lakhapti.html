लखपति (lakhapti)

noun A person of considerable wealth -- literally 'lord of a lakh (100,000)'; used loosely for 'rich person' rather than as a precise financial benchmark.
Sense 1 : a wealthy person, one with lakhs of rupees
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: धनी (dhanii), अमीर (amiir), मालदार (maaldaar, colloquial), करोड़पति (karodapti, wealthier tier -- 'multi-millionaire')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word is built from लाख (laakh, 'one hundred thousand') plus पति (pati, 'lord/master' -- the same पति as in 'husband'), so it literally means 'master of a lakh.' In everyday Delhi conversation it's often used affectionately or teasingly, as in calling a friend who just got a bonus a लखपति (lakhapti) -- and these days, with inflation, people often joke that being a mere लखपति (lakhapti) isn't as impressive as it used to be, gesturing instead toward करोड़पति (karodapti) status.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat लखपति (lakhapti) as a precise, official term of wealth measurement, but in everyday speech it is used loosely to mean simply 'rich' or 'well-off,' much like calling someone a 'millionaire' in casual English even when the exact sum is unclear. 2) Don't confuse it with करोड़पति (karodapti), which signals a distinctly higher tier of wealth (crorepati vs. lakhpati) -- the two are not interchangeable when precision matters, such as in a joking comparison of someone's riches.
