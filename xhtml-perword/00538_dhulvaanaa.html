धुलवाना (dhulvaanaa)

verb The double-causative of धोना (dhonaa, 'to wash') -- it means to get something washed by someone else, i.e. to have a third party do the washing.
Sense 1 : to have (something) washed by someone else
verb, transitive Causative frame: subject उससे कपड़े धुलवाए (usse kapde dhulvaae, 'had the clothes washed by him') marks the person who actually does the washing with से (se), while the thing washed is the direct object.
Synonyms: धोने के लिए देना (dhone ke lie denaa, descriptive phrase, not a true synonym)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic double-causative built on धोना (dhonaa) -> धुलाना (dhulaanaa) -> धुलवाना (dhulvaanaa), reflecting how household chores in India are often outsourced -- to a धोबी (dhobii, washerman), a maid, or a laundromat -- so this verb comes up constantly in conversations about domestic life: गाड़ी धुलवानी है (gaadii dhulvaanii hai, 'the car needs to be gotten washed') is something you will hear said to a driver or car-wash boy rather than 'I will wash the car myself.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use धुलाना (dhulaanaa) instead, but धुलवाना (dhulvaanaa) is the more common everyday causative in Delhi-NCR speech for arranging for someone else to do the washing -- both exist, but धुलवाना (dhulvaanaa) is heard far more in daily life, especially for laundry, cars, and dishes sent out to be cleaned. 2) The agent who actually washes must take से (se), not को (ko) -- learners transitioning from causatives like सिखाना (sikhaanaa) sometimes wrongly reach for को (ko).
Sample Sentences:
मुझे कल गाड़ी धुलवानी है। I need to get the car washed tomorrow.
(mujhe kal gaadii dhulvaanii hai।)
