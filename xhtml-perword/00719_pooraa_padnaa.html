पूरा पड़ना (pooraa padnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to be enough, to suffice' -- built from पूरा (pooraa, 'complete/full') plus पड़ना (padnaa, the same 'befall' verb that gives Hindi many of its involuntary/circumstantial constructions).
Sense 1 : to be sufficient, to be enough (of money, time, resources)
verb, intransitive The thing that falls short is the grammatical subject and agrees with पूरा (pooraa) in gender/number (पैसे पूरे नहीं पड़े (paise poore nahiin pade, 'the money wasn't enough')); the person affected is usually marked with को (ko), e.g. मुझे पैसे पूरे नहीं पड़े (mujhe paise poore nahiin pade, 'I didn't have enough money').
Synonyms: काफ़ी होना (kaafii honaa), बस होना (bas honaa, colloquial)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in markets and households in Delhi-NCR -- a shopkeeper or a mother managing the monthly budget saying पैसे पूरे नहीं पड़ रहे (paise poore nahiin pad rahe, 'the money isn't stretching far enough'). It captures a very Hindi way of thinking about scarcity: the resource itself 'falls short' rather than the person 'lacking' it, similar in spirit to दाल-रोटी में गुज़ारा होना (daal-rotii men guzaaraa honaa) style constructions where circumstances act upon you.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for काफ़ी होना (kaafii honaa) in every context, but पूरा पड़ना (pooraa padnaa) is the more natural everyday choice when talking about running out of money, time, or supplies mid-task, e.g. while shopping or budgeting. 2) The subject of agreement is the thing that is/isn't enough, not the person -- learners sometimes wrongly try to make पूरा (pooraa) agree with the को (ko)-marked person instead.
Sample Sentences:
इस महीने पैसे पूरे नहीं पड़ रहे हैं। The money isn't stretching far enough this month.
(is mahiine paise poore nahiin pad rahe hain।)
