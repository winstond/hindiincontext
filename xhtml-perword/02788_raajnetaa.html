राजनेता (raajnetaa)

noun A compound noun meaning 'politician' or 'statesman', formed from राज (raaj, 'rule/kingdom') plus नेता (netaa, 'leader'). One core sense, though it carries a spectrum from respectful to sarcastic depending on tone.
Sense 1 : politician, statesman
noun, masculine
Synonyms: नेता (netaa, broader term, also 'leader' generally), सियासतदान (siyaasatdaan, less common)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word sits right at the intersection of respect and cynicism in everyday Hindi -- much depends on tone and context. Say it flatly in a news broadcast and it means 'statesman'; say it with a raised eyebrow at the dinner table and it drips with the assumption that all राजनेता (raajnetaa) are corrupt. This double edge mirrors how नेता (netaa) itself is used colloquially, often as a mild insult ('वो तो बड़ा नेता बनता है' vo to badaa netaa bantaa hai, 'he acts like such a big shot leader').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use नेता (netaa) and राजनेता (raajnetaa) interchangeably, but नेता (netaa) is broader -- it covers any kind of leader (a student leader, a union leader, even a family's de facto decision-maker), while राजनेता (raajnetaa) specifically means someone in electoral or state politics. 2) In casual, critical speech, native speakers often just say नेता (netaa) even about a politician -- राजनेता (raajnetaa) tends to sound slightly more formal or written, like something you'd read in a newspaper rather than say while complaining about corruption over chai.
