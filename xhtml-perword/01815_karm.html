कर्म (karm)

noun Primarily a noun meaning 'action, deed, work' -- both in a plain everyday sense and in the deeper philosophical/religious sense of karma, the moral law of action and consequence. The two senses are closely linked; the religious sense is an extension of the everyday one.
Sense 1 : action, deed, work (in the moral/religious sense of karma)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कर्तव्य (kartavy, duty), कृत्य (krity, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the Sanskrit-origin word behind the English loanword 'karma,' and it carries the same idea in Hindi: what you do comes back to you, in this life or beyond. You will hear it in phrases like अपना कर्म भोगना (apnaa karm bhognaa, 'to bear the fruits of one's own deeds') and करम का फल (karam kaa phal, 'the fruit of one's actions') -- both reflecting a deep-rooted belief that action and consequence are morally linked, not separate from fate.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use कर्म (karm) where काम (kaam, work/task) is meant in everyday contexts -- कर्म (karm) carries a moral or philosophical weight (as in fate, destiny, past deeds) that plain काम (kaam) does not. 2) अच्छे कर्म (achchhe karm, good deeds) and बुरे कर्म (bure karm, bad deeds) are the common everyday phrases -- learners often reach for a more literal or clinical translation instead of these set expressions.

Sense 2 : work, occupation, one's proper duty or task
noun, masculine
Synonyms: काम (kaam), पेशा (peshaa, occupation), धर्म (dharm, one's duty, closely related concept)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense shows up in the famous Gita line कर्म करो, फल की चिंता मत करो (karm karo, phal kii chintaa mat karo, 'do your duty, do not worry about the result') -- here कर्म (karm) means one's proper work or role in life, not just any random task. It is a step up in register from काम (kaam), which is the word you would actually use to ask someone what they do for a living.
