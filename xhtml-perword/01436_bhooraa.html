भूरा (bhooraa)

adjective A basic color adjective meaning 'brown'; it also extends to describe hazel or light-colored eyes and, in some contexts, greyish/dun-colored animals.
Sense 1 : brown (color)
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Think of the color of chai without enough milk, or of roasted peanuts (भुना हुआ (bhunaa huaa)) -- that earthy tone is the mental anchor for भूरा (bhooraa), and the resemblance to भुनना (bhunnaa, 'to roast') is not an accident; both share the sense of something browned or toasted.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for भूरा (bhooraa) for any warm dark shade, but for reddish-brown or maroon shades Hindi speakers usually prefer मैरून (mairoon) or लाल-भूरा (laal-bhooraa); भूरा (bhooraa) alone tends to mean a plain, earthy brown.

Sense 2 : hazel or light-colored (eyes, hair)
adjective
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: भूरी आँखें (bhoorii aankhen, 'hazel/light-brown eyes') is a common descriptive phrase in Hindi -- unlike English, where 'hazel' feels like a special or fancy word, भूरा (bhooraa) is just the everyday, unremarkable word Hindi speakers reach for when describing lighter eyes or hair.
