पोता (potaa)

noun/verb Primarily a family-relations noun meaning 'grandson' (son's son); also has a separate, much less common verb sense related to 'wiping/mopping', derived from the same root as पोंछना (ponchhnaa).
Sense 1 : grandson (son's son)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In joint-family culture, the arrival of a पोता (potaa) is traditionally treated as a big event for grandparents, especially the dादा-दादी (daadaa-daadii, 'paternal grandparents') who often dote openly on this particular grandchild. The पोता/नाती (potaa/naatii) distinction reflects how Hindi kinship terms track lineage (paternal vs. maternal) far more precisely than English 'grandson' does.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use पोता (potaa) for any grandson, but Hindi distinguishes the paternal line: पोता (potaa) is specifically the son's son, while नाती (naatii) is the daughter's son -- mixing these up is a common error. 2) The feminine counterpart is पोती (potii, 'granddaughter'), not a different word entirely -- learners sometimes reach for an unrelated term.

Sense 2 : to wipe, mop, plaster (a surface, e.g. with a cloth or with cow-dung/mud)
verb, transitive Used especially for wiping a surface clean or plastering a floor/wall with a wet cloth, mud, or cow-dung paste, as in traditional home upkeep.
Synonyms: पोंछना (ponchhnaa)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense survives strongly in rural and older domestic vocabulary, e.g. फर्श पोतना (pharsh potnaa, 'to plaster/mop the floor'), often with a mixture of mud and cow-dung used traditionally to coat courtyard floors.
Common Mistakes: 1) This verb is far less common in casual city speech than पोंछना (ponchhnaa); learners who have only met पोता (potaa) as 'grandson' may not recognize this verb sense when it appears in rural or older-generation contexts.
