मिलकर (milkar)

adverb The conjunctive-participle form of मिलना (milnaa, 'to meet/combine'), used adverbially to mean 'together' or 'jointly' -- describing an action done in combination with others.
Sense 1 : together, jointly (doing something in combination)
adverb Formed from मिल (mil, stem of मिलना) plus the conjunctive participle marker -कर (kar); it attaches to a following main verb to show that the action was done jointly, e.g. मिलकर काम करना (milkar kaam karnaa, 'to work together').
Synonyms: साथ में (saath men), एक साथ (ek saath), संयुक्त रूप से (sanyukt roop se, formal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in calls to collective action -- चलो, मिलकर करते हैं (chalo, milkar karte hain, 'come on, let's do it together') -- it carries a warm, cooperative flavor, often used in family, community, or team contexts to stress 'we're in this together.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use साथ (saath) alone where मिलकर (milkar) is more natural for emphasizing joint action or collaboration, not just physical proximity -- सब मिलकर गाएँगे (sab milkar gaaenge, 'everyone will sing together') stresses unity of effort, not just being in the same place.
Sample Sentences:
चलो, मिलकर करते हैं। Come on, let's do it together.
(chalo, milkar karte hain।)
