उड़ना (udnaa)

verb The basic intransitive verb 'to fly'; it also extends figuratively to things vanishing, spreading (like rumors), or colors fading, and appears in a few vivid idioms about shock and fear.
Sense 1 : to fly (through the air)
verb, intransitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Kite season (especially around Makar Sankranti/Independence Day) is when Delhi kids shout पतंग उड़ रही है (patang ud rahii hai, 'the kite is flying') -- but note that flying the kite yourself is उड़ाना (udaanaa), the causative partner of this verb.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to make this transitive to mean 'fly a kite/plane' -- that meaning uses the causative उड़ाना (udaanaa), not उड़ना (udnaa) itself. 2) उड़ना (udnaa) is intransitive, so its perfective forms agree with the subject without ने (ne) -- e.g. चिड़िया उड़ी (chidiyaa udii, 'the bird flew'), not चिड़िया ने उड़ा (chidiyaa ne udaa).
Sample Sentences:

(panchhii aasmaan men ud rahe hain।)

Sense 2 : to vanish, disappear suddenly
verb, intransitive Often used for things that seem to 'fly away' on their own -- sleep, color, money, patience.
Synonyms: गायब होना (gaayab honaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll hear रंग उड़ गया (rang ud gayaa, 'the color faded') for washed-out clothes, and नींद उड़ गई (niind ud gaii, 'sleep vanished') when scary news jolts someone awake at 2 a.m. -- very common dinner-table drama phrase.
Common Mistakes: 1) नींद उड़ जाना (niind ud jaanaa, 'sleep flew away/vanished') is a set expression for suddenly losing sleepiness (e.g. from shock) -- learners often reach for सोना (sonaa) negated instead, which misses the idiomatic flavor.

Sense 3 : to spread rapidly (news, rumor)
verb, intransitive Used with ख़बर (khabar) or अफ़वाह (afvaah) as subject: ख़बर उड़ गई (khabar ud gaii, 'the news spread fast').
Synonyms: फैलना (phailnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: In a mohalla, गली में ख़बर उड़ गई (galii men khabar ud gaii, 'the news flew through the lane') captures exactly how fast gossip travels between balconies and shared courtyards.

Sense 4 : to have one's wits scattered from fear or shock (idiomatic, with होश)
verb, intransitive Fixed idiom होश उड़ना (hosh udnaa, 'senses fly away') -- the experiencer takes के (ke): उसके होश उड़ गए (uske hosh ud gae, 'he was scared out of his wits').
Synonyms: घबरा जाना (ghabraa jaanaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: होश उड़ना (hosh udnaa) is a favorite in Hindi film dialogue for comic or dramatic shock -- picture someone opening a door to a ghost and the narrator saying उसके होश उड़ गए (uske hosh ud gae).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often mark the frightened person with को (ko) by analogy with लगना (lagnaa)-type verbs, but this idiom actually takes के होश (ke hosh) as the possessor construction, not a dative subject.
Sample Sentences:
साँप देखकर उसके होश उड़ गए। Seeing the snake, he was terrified out of his senses.
(saamp dekhkar uske hosh ud gae।)
Register: Neutral -- Mood: Indicative -- Aspect: Perfective -- Tense: Past -- Voice: Active
