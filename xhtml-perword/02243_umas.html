उमस (umas)

noun A single-sense noun naming the sticky, humid, oppressive heat felt before rain -- one of Hindi's precise weather words with no clean one-word English match.
Sense 1 : sultry, humid heat
noun, feminine Almost always used with होना (honaa) or लगना (lagnaa), e.g. उमस होना (umas honaa, 'to be sultry/humid') or उमस लगना (umas lagnaa, 'to feel the sultriness') -- the feeling is described as something that happens to a person or a place, not an action anyone performs.
Synonyms: गर्मी (garmii, covers heat generally, not specifically humid heat), भाप (bhaap, rare, evokes steaminess more than weather)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word every Delhi-NCR household reaches for in July and August, right before the monsoon breaks -- the air feels thick, fans stop helping, and people say बहुत उमस है (bahut umas hai, 'it's very sultry/humid') on the balcony while waiting for rain. It captures a very specific sensory experience that English speakers usually have to describe with a whole phrase like 'sticky heat' or 'humidity.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for गर्मी (garmii) to mean 'it's so hot and sticky,' but गर्मी (garmii) just means heat/summer in general -- उमस (umas) specifically points to that heavy, sweaty, can't-breathe humidity, usually right before monsoon rain. 2) Because the discomfort is felt by a person, sentences often use a को-marked experiencer, e.g. मुझे उमस लग रही है (mujhe umas lag rahii hai, 'I'm feeling the sultriness') -- learners sometimes try to make themselves the subject with a verb like 'feel' when the dative-experiencer frame is more native.
Sample Sentences:

(aaj bahut umas hai।)
