ख़ैर (khair)

particle/noun An Arabic-origin loanword used mainly as a discourse particle meaning 'anyway/well' to move a conversation along, with a related noun sense meaning 'welfare, well-being' seen mostly in fixed expressions.
Sense 1 : anyway, well (discourse filler to change or close a topic)
particle Typically placed at the start of a sentence or clause to signal that the speaker is moving past a point, wrapping up a digression, or softening a shift in topic.
Synonyms: अच्छा (achchhaa), चलो (chalo, moves the topic forward rather than closing it), वैसे (vaise)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear this constantly in Delhi chai-shop conversation when someone wants to gracefully drop a sensitive or tiring subject: ख़ैर, छोड़ो (khair, chhodo, 'anyway, forget it') is a classic move to end an argument without conceding it. It carries a slight air of resignation or philosophical shrug, as if to say 'whatever the case may be.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often skip this word entirely since it has no fixed one-word English equivalent, but leaving it out makes speech sound abrupt when changing topics. 2) It is not used to mean 'well' in the sense of physical health here (that is the noun sense below); this is purely a conversational connector. 3) It's often paired with तो (to) as ख़ैर तो (khair to) or followed by a comma-pause in speech, functioning much like English 'anyway,' or 'well then,' at the start of a sentence.

Sense 2 : welfare, well-being, good (as in someone's safety or benefit)
noun, feminine Mostly encountered in the set phrase ख़ैर मनाना (khair manaanaa, 'to be thankful that things turned out okay') and in ख़ैरियत (khairiyat, a related derived noun meaning 'well-being/news of welfare'), rather than used freely as a standalone noun in everyday speech.
Synonyms: भलाई (bhalaaii), कल्याण (kalyaan, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: The phrase अपनी ख़ैर मनाओ (apnii khair manaao, 'be grateful you got off easy') is a common half-joking warning, said with a smile after someone narrowly avoids trouble, like a scolding parent letting a child off the hook.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse this with ख़ैरियत (khairiyat) -- ख़ैर (khair) itself is rarely used alone to ask 'how are you'; that job belongs to ख़ैरियत (khairiyat) or सब ठीक है? (sab thiik hai, 'is everything fine?').
