खेत (khet)

noun A concrete, everyday noun meaning a field or plot of farmland; also lives in a few common figurative expressions about death/battle.
Sense 1 : agricultural field
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खेती (khetii, farming/the cultivated land more broadly), मैदान (maidaan, open ground, not specifically farmed)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Say खेत (khet) and most Delhi-NCR folks picture wheat or mustard fields just outside the city, the kind you drive past on the way to a village wedding or a farmhouse party. It is a word that carries real emotional weight in Indian public life, given how central land and farming are to rural identity and politics.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use मैदान (maidaan) for 'field' generally, but मैदान (maidaan) is an open ground (for playing, gathering) while खेत (khet) specifically means land under cultivation. 2) खेत (khet) is masculine, so possessives and adjectives agreeing with it take the masculine forms, e.g. मेरा खेत (meraa khet).

Sense 2 : battlefield (in the fixed idiom 'khet rahnaa', to fall in battle)
noun, masculine Chiefly encountered in the fixed idiom खेत रहना (khet rahnaa, 'to die in battle') -- the noun itself doesn't otherwise mean 'battlefield' outside this expression.
Synonyms: रणभूमि (ranbhoomi, literary/formal), युद्धभूमि (yuddhbhoomi, literary/formal)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: This sense survives almost entirely inside the old idiom खेत रहना (khet rahnaa, 'to be left [dead] on the field'), a phrase you meet in history books, ballads, and patriotic songs about soldiers rather than in daily conversation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not use खेत (khet) alone to mean 'battlefield' in ordinary speech -- outside the idiom खेत रहना (khet rahnaa), it just means a farm field, and reaching for it as a general word for 'battlefield' will confuse a listener.
