जेब (jeb)

noun A concrete noun meaning 'pocket' -- the cloth pouch in clothing -- with a common extended sense referring to one's personal money or finances, as in English 'out of pocket.'
Sense 1 : pocket (in clothing)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: जेब (jeb) comes from Persian and is the everyday word every Hindi speaker uses for a pocket -- there is no competing native term in common use. Kids checking their जेब (jeb) for coins on the way to the corner shop is a scene every Delhi household knows well.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes use में (men) correctly but forget that जेब (jeb) takes the oblique before postpositions like any feminine noun, e.g. जेब में (jeb men, 'in the pocket') -- not a special exception, just easy to forget with a short word.
Sample Sentences:
मेरी जेब में सिर्फ बीस रुपये हैं। There are only twenty rupees in my pocket.
(merii jeb men sirph biis rupye hain।)

Sense 2 : one's own money, personal funds
noun, feminine Common in fixed expressions: जेब से (jeb se, 'out of one's own pocket'), जेब ढीली होना (jeb dhiilii honaa, 'to have to spend money, lit. pocket becomes loose'), जेब पर भारी (jeb par bhaarii, 'heavy on the pocket, expensive').
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Just like English, Hindi extends 'pocket' to mean personal money -- जेब ढीली करनी पड़ी (jeb dhiilii karnii padii, 'had to loosen the pocket, i.e. spend money') is a phrase you will hear constantly when someone complains about wedding season expenses or a big bill at a restaurant.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पैसा (paisaa) or पैसे (paise) when they actually mean 'my own money' in a sentence like 'it came out of my own pocket' -- native speakers reach for जेब (jeb) here instead, e.g. मेरी जेब से गया (merii jeb se gayaa, 'it came out of my pocket').
