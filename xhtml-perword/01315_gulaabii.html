गुलाबी (gulaabii)

adjective Primarily the color adjective 'pink,' with a common figurative extension describing a pleasant chill in the air or a rosy/tipsy glow -- both senses trace back to the same rose-tinted image.
Sense 1 : pink (color)
adjective
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गुलाबी (gulaabii) comes from गुलाब (gulaab, rose), so Hindi literally calls the color 'rose-colored' rather than naming it independently the way English does. Jaipur is nicknamed गुलाबी शहर (gulaabii shahar, the Pink City) for its rose-tinted old-city buildings, which is a handy cultural peg for this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to inflect गुलाबी (gulaabii) like a variable adjective (expecting a masculine form), but it stays गुलाबी (gulaabii) regardless of the noun's gender or number -- गुलाबी कमीज़ (gulaabii kamiiz, pink shirt) and गुलाबी दुपट्टा (gulaabii dupattaa, pink scarf) both use the same form.

Sense 2 : pleasantly mild/crisp (of cold weather)
adjective Almost always modifies ठंड (thand, cold) or जाड़ा (jaadaa, winter) in the fixed phrase गुलाबी ठंड (gulaabii thand, pleasant/crisp cold) -- it is not used to describe heat or general weather.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गुलाबी ठंड (gulaabii thand) describes that lovely early-winter chill in Delhi-NCR -- sweater weather, morning fog, evening bonfires -- and carries real affection; people look forward to it rather than dreading it, the opposite of how 'cold' usually reads in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Do not extend this to heat or rain -- गुलाबी (gulaabii) in this sense is reserved for a mild, invigorating chill, not warmth or humidity; 2) it praises the cold rather than complaining about it, which surprises learners used to English default-negative framing of cold weather.

Sense 3 : tipsy, mildly drunk (figurative, colloquial)
adjective Used with होना (honaa) or a form describing a person's state, e.g. आँखें गुलाबी होना (aankhen gulaabii honaa, 'eyes to turn pink/rosy,' implying mild intoxication).
Synonyms: नशे में (nashe men, phrase, describes the same tipsy state)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense plays on the same rosy-glow image as the color and the weather senses -- a slight flush in the cheeks or eyes after a drink -- so all three meanings of गुलाबी (gulaabii) share one underlying picture of a soft pink tint.
Common Mistakes: 1) This sense is colloquial and mostly describes a mild, pleasant buzz rather than heavy drunkenness -- learners should not use it for someone seriously intoxicated, where नशे में धुत (nashe men dhut) is more appropriate.
