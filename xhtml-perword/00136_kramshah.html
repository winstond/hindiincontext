क्रमशः (kramshah)

adverb A formal adverb meaning 'respectively' or 'successively/in order' -- used mainly in written, academic, or careful spoken Hindi when listing items in a matching sequence.
Sense 1 : respectively
adverb Used to match two or more lists item-by-item, e.g. 'राम, श्याम और मोहन को क्रमशः पहला, दूसरा और तीसरा स्थान मिला (raam, shyaam aur mohan ko kramshah pahlaa, doosraa aur tiisraa sthaan milaa, 'Ram, Shyam, and Mohan got first, second, and third place respectively')' -- the order of the second list maps onto the order of the first.
Synonyms: क्रम से (kram se, two-word variant)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will see क्रमशः (kramshah) constantly in newspapers, exam results, and official announcements listing names against ranks or scores, but it rarely comes up in casual chat -- a Delhi shopkeeper would just say the numbers, not reach for this word.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use क्रमशः (kramshah) loosely to mean 'gradually,' but that sense belongs to its other reading (see next sense) -- this 'respectively' sense only works when pairing off two parallel lists. 2) It sounds bookish in casual speech; native speakers giving a spoken list often just say the items in order without any linking word, or use क्रम से (kram se) instead.

Sense 2 : successively, in due order
adverb Marks a sequence unfolding one stage after another, often in narration of events or processes, e.g. 'क्रमशः स्थिति सुधरती गई (kramshah sthiti sudhartii gaii, 'the situation kept improving, stage by stage')'.
Synonyms: धीरे-धीरे (dhiire-dhiire, gradually, meaning scope), क्रम से (kram se, two-word variant)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps with the 'respectively' sense because both come from the same root idea of क्रम (kram, 'order/sequence') -- here the emphasis is on unfolding over time rather than pairing lists, so it shows up more in formal narration, reports, and news writing than in daily conversation.
