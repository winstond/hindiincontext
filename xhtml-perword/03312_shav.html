शव (shav)

noun A single-sense formal/clinical noun meaning 'dead body, corpse.' It is the word you hear in news reports, hospitals, and official contexts, as distinct from more everyday or emotionally colored alternatives.
Sense 1 : dead body, corpse
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मृत शरीर (mrit shariir), लाश (laash, everyday word), मृतक (mritak, refers to the deceased person, formal)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You will hear this word constantly on the news -- शव बरामद (shav baraamad, 'the body was recovered') is a standard headline phrase -- and see it on hospital and mortuary signage, but ordinary people talking about a death in the family or neighborhood almost always say लाश (laash) instead. Reaching for शव (shav) in casual conversation can come across as oddly formal, almost like saying 'the deceased' instead of 'the body' in English.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for लाश (laash) as the default word for 'dead body' since it is far more common in speech; शव (shav) is the word you will see in newspapers, police reports, and hospital settings, and sounds noticeably more formal or clinical if used in casual conversation. 2) शव (shav) refers only to the physical body, not to the deceased person as a social/legal entity -- for that, मृतक (mritak) is used instead.
Sample Sentences:
पुलिस को नदी के किनारे एक शव मिला। The police found a corpse on the riverbank.
(pulis ko nadii ke kinaare ek shav milaa।)
