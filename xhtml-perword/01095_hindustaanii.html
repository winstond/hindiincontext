हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii)

adjective/noun Covers the noun/adjective for 'Indian' (person or thing of the subcontinent), and the language sense referring to the shared spoken base of Hindi-Urdu.
Sense 1 : Indian (of/from Hindustan)
adjective
Synonyms: भारतीय (bhaartiiy)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: हिन्दुस्तान (hindustaan) is the older, warmer name for India, rooted in Persian usage going back centuries, and हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) inherits that emotional weight -- it is the word you reach for when speaking from the heart about the country (its food, its music, its people) rather than filling out a form. Contrast that with भारतीय (bhaartiiy), which is the term you will see stamped on a passport.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes treat हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) and भारतीय (bhaartiiy) as fully interchangeable in formal writing, but हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) carries a more emotional, everyday feel, while भारतीय (bhaartiiy) is the neutral, official term used in government, news, and constitutional contexts. 2) As an adjective it does not change form for gender or number, so पहनावा हिन्दुस्तानी है (pahnaavaa hindustaanii hai, 'the clothing is Indian') and वह हिन्दुस्तानी है (vah hindustaanii hai, 'she is Indian') use the identical word.

Sense 3 : Hindustani (the shared spoken Hindi-Urdu language base)
noun, feminine
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Linguists and old-school film lyricists alike use हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) to mean the everyday, unpretentious language that ordinary people actually speak on the street -- neither the heavily Sanskritized Hindi of All India Radio nor the heavily Persianized Urdu of classical poetry, but the comfortable middle that most North Indians grow up speaking at home.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often assume हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) simply means 'Hindi', but it specifically names the common colloquial base shared by Hindi and Urdu before they diverge in script and high-register vocabulary -- useful when discussing Bollywood dialogue or old Hindi film songs, which are often praised for being in pure हिन्दुस्तानी (hindustaanii) rather than heavily Sanskritized Hindi or Persianized Urdu.
