कामचोर (kaamchor)

noun/adjective A compound of काम (kaam, 'work') and चोर (chor, 'thief') -- literally 'work-thief.' One sense: a lazy, work-shirking person, used as both noun and adjective.
Sense 1 : a lazy person, someone who avoids work
noun, masculine/feminine
Synonyms: आलसी (aalsii), निकम्मा (nikammaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a classic word every Indian kid has heard from an exasperated mother or teacher -- कितने कामचोर हो तुम! (kitne kaamchor ho tum, 'how lazy/work-shy you are!'). The imagery is vivid: you're not just lazy, you're 'stealing' from the work you owe, which is why it lands harder than सुस्त (sust, 'sluggish') or आलसी (aalsii).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate this too softly as just 'lazy' -- कामचोर (kaamchor) carries a sharper accusatory edge, implying someone actively dodges or steals away from work rather than merely being sluggish by nature. 2) It is commonly used to scold, especially by parents, teachers, or bosses, so tone matters -- calling a stranger this can sound genuinely insulting, not playful.

Sense 2 : lazy, work-shy (describing a person)
adjective Functions as an invariant adjective describing a person's habitual avoidance of work, e.g. कामचोर आदमी (kaamchor aadmii, 'a lazy man') or कामचोर लड़की (kaamchor ladkii, 'a lazy girl') -- the form itself does not change with gender.
Synonyms: आलसी (aalsii), निकम्मा (nikammaa, colloquial)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Note the related word कामचोरी (kaamchorii), the abstract noun for 'shirking, slacking off,' often heard in workplace complaints like दफ़्तर में बहुत कामचोरी होती है (daftar men bahut kaamchorii hotii hai, 'there's a lot of slacking off in the office').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to inflect it like a regular -आ (aa) adjective (e.g. attempting कामचोरी or कामचोरा for feminine/plural agreement) -- it stays कामचोर (kaamchor) regardless of the noun's gender or number.
