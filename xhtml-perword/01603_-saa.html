-सा (-saa)

adjective A bound suffix attached to adjectives, adverbs, and some nouns/pronouns to soften or approximate the quality being described, roughly 'somewhat / -ish / -like'; it inflects like a regular adjective agreeing with the noun it modifies.
Sense 1 : approximation/softening suffix -- 'somewhat, -ish, sort of'
adjective, masculine/feminine Attaches directly to the preceding word with no space in casual handwriting, though it is standardly printed with a hyphen or small gap: छोटा-सा (chhotaa-saa, somewhat small/a little small). It agrees in gender and number with the noun being modified, just like an ordinary adjective, regardless of the gender of the word it is attached to.
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is a favorite Hindi texture for softening description -- a mother might call a portion of food थोड़ा-सा (thodaa-saa, just a little) to make asking (or refusing) sound gentler, and a child's toy might be described as छोटा-सा खिलौना (chhotaa-saa khilaunaa, a cute little toy), where -सा adds an affectionate diminutive color English doesn't have a single suffix for.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often skip agreeing -सा (-saa) with the noun's gender/number, leaving it stuck in the masculine singular: it should be छोटी-सी लड़की (chhotii-sii ladkii, a somewhat small girl), not छोटा-सा लड़की. 2) It softens rather than negates -- बड़ा-सा (badaa-saa) means 'a bit big/biggish', not 'not big'. 3) Learners sometimes treat it as a separate word and add a space with no hyphen; while spacing varies, treating -सा as fully detached changes the feel from a fused descriptive tinge to two separate adjectives.

Sense 2 : resemblance suffix -- 'like, -like, resembling'
adjective, masculine/feminine Here -सा attaches to a noun (rather than an adjective) to mean the thing resembles that noun: फूल-सा चेहरा (phool-saa chehraa, a face like a flower). It still agrees in gender/number with the noun it describes.
Synonyms: जैसा (jaisaa, fuller word for 'like/resembling')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the classic Hindi film-song and poetry device -- चाँद-सा चेहरा (chaand-saa chehraa, a moon-like face) is a stock romantic image, and the same pattern shows up in everyday compliments like गुड़िया-सी बच्ची (gudiyaa-sii bachchii, a doll-like child).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this resemblance use with the softening sense above -- फूल-सा (phool-saa) attached to a noun means 'flower-like', not 'somewhat a flower'; context and whether -सा follows a noun or an adjective usually disambiguates. 2) This sense overlaps with जैसा (jaisaa) but -सा is more compact and poetic, often found in praise or description of appearance.
