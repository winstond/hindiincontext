उलटी करना (ultii karnaa)

verb A common everyday phrase meaning 'to vomit' -- built from उलटी (ultii, 'vomit/reverse') plus करना (karnaa, 'to do'). One core sense.
Sense 1 : to vomit
verb, transitive उलटी (ultii) acts as the object noun in this verb-noun compound; the subject is a plain nominative agent, not को-marked as with उलटी आना (ultii aanaa).
Synonyms: क़ै करना (qai karnaa, more clinical/old-fashioned), उलटी आना (ultii aanaa, involuntary variant -- 'vomit comes')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Hindi speakers often prefer the gentler उलटी आना (ultii aanaa) construction in polite conversation, especially about children or illness, since it doesn't suggest the person did it on purpose. उलटी करना (ultii karnaa) is common in doctor-patient talk and everyday complaints, e.g. describing food poisoning or motion sickness on a hill drive.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for उलटी आना (ultii aanaa) and उलटी करना (ultii karnaa) interchangeably, but they frame the event differently: उलटी आना (ultii aanaa) treats vomiting as something that happens to you (दातिजी को उलटी आ रही है (daatijii ko ultii aa rahii hai, 'she feels like vomiting/is vomiting'), को-marked, involuntary), while उलटी करना (ultii karnaa) frames it as something the subject actively does. 2) In the past tense, this being a transitive-looking compound with करना (karnaa), learners sometimes forget it still takes ने (ne) marking on the subject, e.g. उसने उलटी की (usne ultii kii, 'he/she vomited').
