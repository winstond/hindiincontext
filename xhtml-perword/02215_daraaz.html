दराज़ (daraaz)

noun/adjective Mainly a noun meaning 'drawer' (as in furniture); also survives as a literary/compound adjective meaning 'long' or 'extended,' most familiar to learners through the phrase उम्र दराज़ (umr daraaz, long-lived).
Sense 1 : drawer (of furniture)
noun, feminine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Every almirah (aalmaarii) or study table in a middle-class Indian home has a दराज़ (daraaz) or two -- the word is completely everyday and non-fancy, used exactly like 'drawer' in English. It comes from Persian, part of the large stock of Persian/Urdu vocabulary woven into everyday Hindi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes drop the nuqta and write/say दराज (daraaj) -- both are seen, but the nuqta-form दराज़ (daraaz) is standard for this Persian-origin word. 2) Don't confuse with तख्ता (takhtaa) or आलमारी (aalmaarii), which refer to a shelf/board or a whole cupboard, not a sliding drawer.

Sense 2 : long, extended (literary/formal)
adjective Almost always seen in the fixed phrase उम्र दराज़ (umr daraaz, long life/long-lived), often as part of the blessing उम्र दराज़ हो (umr daraaz ho, 'may your life be long').
Synonyms: लंबा (lambaa), तवील (taviil, literary)
Frequency: literary
Cultural Note: You'll meet this mostly in shayari (shaayrii) or in elders' blessings like उम्र दराज़ हो (umr daraaz ho, 'may you live long') -- a lovely, slightly old-fashioned way of wishing someone a long life, distinct from the everyday drawer sense above despite sharing the same word.
Common Mistakes: 1) This adjective is invariant and won't inflect like लंबा (lambaa) does -- learners sometimes wrongly try to feminize or pluralize it. 2) Outside the set phrase उम्र दराज़ (umr daraaz), this sense is rarely used in casual speech, so don't reach for it as a general everyday word for 'long.'
