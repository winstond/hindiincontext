बाक़ी (baaqii)

adjective/noun One core idea, 'remaining/left over', that splits into an adjective (leftover, remaining) and a noun (the remainder, the rest, or a balance/change amount owed).
Sense 1 : remaining, left over
adjective
Synonyms: शेष (shesh, formal), बचा हुआ (bachaa huaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this constantly in daily life: a shopkeeper says बाक़ी सामान (baaqii saamaan, the remaining goods) or a mother says बाक़ी खाना (baaqii khaanaa, the leftover food) खा लो (khaa lo, eat it up). It sits right alongside the noun sense below, which is really the same idea nominalized.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often treat बाक़ी (baaqii) as fully invariant like a noun-adjective, but it does not change form for gender or number at all -- बाक़ी लड़का (baaqii ladkaa, the remaining boy), बाक़ी लड़की (baaqii ladkii, the remaining girl), and बाक़ी लड़के (baaqii ladke, the remaining boys) all keep the same spelling.
Sample Sentences:
बाक़ी खाना खा लो। Eat up the remaining food.
(baaqii khaanaa khaa lo।)

Sense 2 : the rest, the remainder
noun, feminine Often used alone as a pronoun-like noun standing for 'the rest (of it/them)', without needing an explicit noun after it -- बाक़ी कल करूँगा (baaqii kal karoongaa, 'I'll do the rest tomorrow').
Synonyms: शेष (shesh, formal), बचा हुआ हिस्सा (bachaa huaa hissaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the same word as the adjective sense, just standing on its own as 'the remainder' -- Hindi lets you drop the noun it modifies once context is clear, so बाक़ी सब ठीक है (baaqii sab thiik hai, 'the rest is all fine') is a very common way to wrap up a conversation or a status update.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 3 : balance due, change (money), outstanding amount
noun, feminine Common in shopkeeper/market contexts: बाक़ी दे दो (baaqii de do, 'give me the change/balance') or आपकी बाक़ी (aapkii baaqii, 'your change').
Synonyms: बचत (bachat, meaning scope: savings, not quite the same), उधार (udhaar, meaning scope: refers to credit/debt rather than change)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is the word you will hear at every corner shop in Delhi when the vendor is short on coins: बाक़ी नहीं है (baaqii nahiin hai, 'I don't have change') is practically a ritual phrase of Indian retail life, often followed by an offer to round up or give a toffee instead of coins.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for चेंज (chenj) as a borrowed English word for 'change', but बाक़ी (baaqii) is what a Hindi speaker actually says at a shop counter for money owed back.
Sample Sentences:
