तीसरे पहर (tiisre pahar)

adverb A time-of-day expression meaning 'in the third watch [of the day]', roughly the late afternoon. Built from तीसरा (tiisraa, 'third') in its oblique form plus पहर (pahar), a traditional unit dividing day and night into eight watches of about three hours each.
Sense 1 : in the late afternoon (third watch of the day)
adverb Functions as an adverbial time-phrase, typically placed where a time adverb goes in the sentence; no postposition needed since पहर (pahar) is already in its oblique/locative-like form here.
Synonyms: देर दोपहर (der dopahar, less formal), सायंकाल (saayankaal, literary)
Frequency: low
Cultural Note: This phrase comes from the old system of dividing the day into eight पहर (pahar), each about three hours long, once used for temple bells, guard shifts, and daily routines in traditional Indian life. You will still hear it in older storytelling, folk songs, and rural or historical contexts more than in casual city speech, where लोग अक्सर दोपहर (dopahar) या शाम (shaam) कहते हैं (log aksar dopahar yaa shaam kahte hain, 'people usually just say afternoon or evening instead').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to add a postposition like में (men) after पहर (pahar), but तीसरे पहर (tiisre pahar) already functions as a complete time adverbial on its own, similar to सुबह (subah) or शाम (shaam).
