अदरक (adrak)

noun A single, concrete sense: the kitchen spice/root ginger (adrak). No figurative or idiomatic extensions worth tracking.
Sense 1 : ginger (the root/spice)
noun, feminine
Synonyms: अदरख (adrakh, variant form)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: No Indian kitchen runs without अदरक (adrak) -- it goes into chai, dal, and the अदरक-लहसुन का पेस्ट (adrak-lahsun kaa pest, ginger-garlic paste) that forms the base of countless curries. If you have a cold, someone in a Delhi household will almost certainly hand you adrak wali chai (adrak vaalii chaay, ginger tea) as the first remedy, before any medicine.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes try to assign it masculine gender by default -- अदरक (adrak) is feminine, so it takes अच्छी (achchhii) not अच्छा (achchhaa), e.g. अच्छी अदरक (achchhii adrak, good ginger). 2) In tea contexts, learners often say चाय में अदरक डालो (chaay men adrak daalo, put ginger in the tea) but forget that अदरक (adrak) is usually referred to as a lump/piece -- अदरक का टुकड़ा (adrak kaa tukdaa, a piece of ginger) -- rather than counted individually.
