तौलना (taulnaa)

verb Basic transitive verb meaning 'to weigh' -- used literally for weighing goods on a scale, and figuratively for weighing up options, words, or people before deciding.
Sense 1 : to weigh (a physical object)
verb, transitive
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture a सब्ज़ी वाला (sabzii vaalaa, 'vegetable seller') at a Delhi market putting tomatoes on an old-style तराज़ू (taraazoo, 'balance scale') and calling out the weight -- that everyday scene is exactly where this verb lives. It is a completely ordinary, high-frequency verb for any market or kitchen context involving a scale.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes confuse तौलना (taulnaa, 'to weigh something') with तुलना (tulnaa, 'comparison'), which look and sound similar but are unrelated in everyday use -- one is an action, the other is a noun for comparing things. 2) The shopkeeper doing the weighing is the one who 'तौलता है' (taultaa hai); the customer does not तौलना (taulnaa) the goods, they only watch or ask for weighing -- learners sometimes misassign the subject.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : to weigh up, consider carefully (figurative)
verb, transitive Common object nouns in this figurative sense include बात (baat, 'words/matter'), शब्द (shabd, 'words'), आदमी or इंसान (insaan, 'a person'), as in सोच-समझकर बोलना पड़ता है, हर बात तौल के (har baat taul ke, 'every word must be weighed'), where तौलना (taulnaa) means choosing words carefully.
Synonyms: आँकना (aanknaa, to gauge/assess), जाँचना (jaanchnaa, to check/examine)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the verb behind the common piece of advice given to someone about to speak in a delicate situation: 'अपने शब्दों को तौल कर बोलो' (apne shabdon ko taul kar bolo, 'weigh your words before speaking'). It extends the market image of a scale to weighing a person's character or intentions, much like English 'sizing someone up.'
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is often paired with सोचना (sochnaa, 'to think') or समझना (samajhnaa, 'to understand') in set phrases like सोच-समझकर तौलना (soch-samajhkar taulnaa); using तौलना (taulnaa) alone can sound abrupt where a native speaker would add that cushioning phrase.
