इनकार करना (inkaar karnaa)

verb A compound expression meaning 'to refuse' or 'to deny' -- the noun इनकार (inkaar, 'refusal/denial') paired with करना (karnaa, 'to do'). One core sense covers both refusing an action and denying a statement.
Sense 1 : to refuse (to do something) or deny (a claim/accusation)
verb, transitive Takes से (se) before the thing or person being refused/denied, e.g. किसी बात से इनकार करना (kisii baat se inkaar karnaa, 'to deny something'); with an infinitive, the pattern is X करने से इनकार करना (X karne se inkaar karnaa, 'to refuse to do X').
Synonyms: मुकरना (mukarnaa, to go back on one's word), नकारना (nakaarnaa, formal), मना करना (manaa karnaa, more everyday for simple refusal)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will hear this a lot in news reports and formal contexts -- एक अधिकारी ने आरोपों से इनकार किया (ek adhikaarii ne aaropon se inkaar kiyaa, 'an official denied the allegations') is a classic news-Hindi construction. In daily life, it also shows up when someone digs in their heels, like a child flatly refusing to eat vegetables: उसने खाने से इनकार कर दिया (usne khaane se inkaar kar diyaa, 'he refused to eat').
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often drop से (se) and say इनकार करना (inkaar karnaa) directly with the object, but the noun इनकार (inkaar) grammatically wants से (se) marking what is being refused or denied. 2) Confusing this with मना करना (manaa karnaa): इनकार करना (inkaar karnaa) is stronger and more formal, often used for firm, deliberate refusal or flat denial (e.g. in police statements, negotiations), while मना करना (manaa karnaa) covers everyday, lighter refusals like turning down tea.
