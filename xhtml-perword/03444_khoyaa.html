खोया (khoyaa)

verb/noun Two distinct words that happen to share a spelling: the past-participle form of खोना (khonaa, 'to lose') used in perfective/compound verb constructions, and an unrelated noun meaning the thickened milk solid used in Indian sweets.
Sense 1 : lost (past participle of 'to lose')
verb, transitive This is the masculine singular perfective form of खोना (khonaa); it appears in sentences like मैंने अपना पैसा खोया (mainne apnaa paisaa khoyaa, 'I lost my money') and is also common in the compound/reflexive sense खो जाना (kho jaanaa, 'to become lost'), where खोया (khoyaa) appears as खोया हुआ (khoyaa huaa, 'lost/misplaced') describing a state.
Synonyms: गुम किया (gum kiyaa), गँवाया (ganvaayaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You'll hear खोया हुआ (khoyaa huaa) constantly in everyday complaints -- खोया हुआ फोन (khoyaa huaa phon, 'a lost phone') is practically a national pastime of conversation in Delhi markets and metro stations.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often confuse this खोया (khoyaa) with the milk-sweet noun खोया (khoyaa) -- context (a verb following a subject and object versus a noun after a quantity word or in a sweet shop) is the only way to tell them apart. 2) खो जाना (kho jaanaa) is intransitive ('to get lost') while खोना (khonaa) alone is transitive ('to lose something') -- मैं खो गया (main kho gayaa, 'I got lost') versus मैंने उसे खोया (mainne use khoyaa, 'I lost him/her') take very different subject marking.
Sample Sentences:

Sense 2 : khoya (dried thickened milk solid used in sweets)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: मावा (maavaa, regional variant used more in some sweets)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Khoya (khoyaa) is the soul of North Indian mithai (mithaaii, 'sweets') like gujiya (gujiyaa) and peda (pedaa) -- around Diwali, halwai (halvaaii, 'sweet-maker') shops in old Delhi neighborhoods are stacked high with fresh khoya, and its richness (or adulteration, sadly a real market worry) is a favorite topic of kitchen gossip.
Common Mistakes: 1) This noun is unrelated in meaning to the verb form खोया (khoyaa) despite identical spelling -- a sentence like हलवाई ने खोया मिलाया (halvaaii ne khoyaa milaayaa, 'the sweet-maker mixed in khoya') is about the milk solid, not about losing anything.
