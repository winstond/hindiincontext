चालान (chaalaan)

noun Most commonly known today as the word for a traffic/police 'ticket' or fine, but it also covers the older, still-current sense of an official challan/receipt used in banking, tax, and court paperwork.
Sense 1 : traffic/police fine or ticket
noun, masculine
Synonyms: जुर्माना (jurmaanaa, focuses on the fine amount rather than the paperwork)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi driver about चालान (chaalaan) and you'll get a story -- it's the word that comes up the instant a traffic cop waves you over, especially since e-challans (traffic cameras) became common and people started getting SMS notifications about fines for jumping red lights or not wearing a helmet.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often say जुर्माना (jurmaanaa) when they mean the physical ticket/paperwork itself -- चालान (chaalaan) is the document/act, जुर्माना (jurmaanaa) is specifically the money owed. 2) The common verb pairing is चालान कटना (chaalaan katnaa, 'to get a ticket issued', intransitive, as if the ticket 'cuts itself' on you) versus चालान काटना (chaalaan kaatnaa, 'to issue a ticket', transitive, done by the police).

Sense 2 : official receipt/form for a payment or deposit (bank, tax, court)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: रसीद (rasiid, focuses on the receipt of payment rather than the deposit form)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the older, bureaucratic sense of the word -- the little carbon-copy slip you fill out at a bank counter to deposit money into someone's account, or the paperwork accompanying a police case sent to court (चालान पेश करना (chaalaan pesh karnaa, 'to file the charge sheet')). It shows how one word tracks India's long relationship with official paperwork, from bank deposits to court filings, before finding its now-dominant modern use for traffic fines.
