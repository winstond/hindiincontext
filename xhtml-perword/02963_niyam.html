नियम (niyam)

noun Mainly means 'rule' or 'regulation' (a law or code someone else sets or that governs a system), with a secondary sense of a personal 'principle' or self-imposed discipline/routine.
Sense 1 : rule, regulation
noun, masculine
Synonyms: कानून (kaanoon, law), क़ायदा (qaaydaa)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: You will see नियम (niyam) posted on signs everywhere in Delhi -- पार्किंग नियम (paarking niyam, 'parking rules'), ट्रैफ़िक नियम (traifik niyam, 'traffic rules') -- it is the everyday word for posted or institutional rules, more common in daily speech than the heavier कानून (kaanoon).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for कानून (kaanoon) when they mean a small procedural rule (a game's rules, an office's rules); नियम (niyam) fits everyday rules and regulations, while कानून (kaanoon) leans toward actual law of the land. 2) The common phrase नियमों का पालन करना (niymon kaa paalan karnaa, 'to follow the rules') uses पालन करना (paalan karnaa), not मानना (maannaa), when talking about formally observing rules -- मानना (maannaa) is also fine but sounds slightly more personal/casual.

यहाँ पार्किंग के नियम बहुत सख़्त हैं। The parking rules here are very strict.
(yahaan paarking ke niyam bahut sakht hain।)

Sense 2 : personal principle, self-discipline, routine practice
noun, masculine Often used with रखना (rakhnaa) or बनाना (banaanaa) -- नियम बनाना (niyam banaanaa, 'to make it a rule for oneself') -- to describe a self-imposed habit or discipline rather than an external law.
Synonyms: सिद्धांत (siddhaant, principle), संकल्प (sankalp, resolve, more formal/spiritual)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps with the yogic/spiritual idea of नियम (niyam) as one of the 'observances' in classical yoga philosophy (alongside यम, yam) -- so when someone says सुबह जल्दी उठना मेरा नियम है (subah jaldii uthnaa meraa niyam hai, 'waking up early is my rule/discipline'), it carries a faint echo of virtuous self-discipline, not just a plain habit.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes translate 'I have a rule that...' too literally; the natural Hindi frame is मेरा नियम है (meraa niyam hai, 'it is my rule/principle') or मैंने नियम बना लिया है (mainne niyam banaa liyaa hai, 'I've made it a rule'), not a direct calque of the English sentence structure.
