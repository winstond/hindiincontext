गड्ढा (gaddhaa)

noun A concrete noun for a hole or pit in the ground, with a common figurative extension to a rut or difficult situation.
Sense 1 : hole, pit (in the ground)
noun, masculine
Synonyms: खड्डा (khaddaa, variant form), गड्ढ (gaddh, rare)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Ask any Delhi driver about मानसून (maansoon, monsoon) and गड्ढा (gaddhaa) will come up within seconds -- potholed roads after the rains are a running civic complaint, and 'सड़क में गड्ढे हैं' (sadak men gaddhe hain, 'there are potholes in the road') is something you will hear constantly from July to September.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes reach for सुराख (suraakh) for any 'hole,' but सुराख (suraakh) is a small perforation (like in cloth or a wall), while गड्ढा (gaddhaa) is a dug-out depression in the ground -- a pothole, ditch, or pit. 2) The road-related sense (pothole) uses this same word, not a separate term: सड़क में गड्ढा (sadak men gaddhaa, 'a pothole in the road') is the everyday phrase, not गड्ढा-सड़क or similar compounds.
Sample Sentences:

(sadak men gaddhe hain।)

Sense 2 : figurative pit/trouble, a difficult or ruinous situation
noun, masculine Common in the idiom गड्ढे में गिरना/धकेलना (gaddhe men girnaa/dhakelnaa, 'to fall into/push into a pit') used metaphorically for landing in or causing serious trouble.
Synonyms: मुसीबत (musiibat, trouble/difficulty, not a literal synonym), फँसाव (phansaav, being trapped)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This figurative use leans on the same body-and-space logic Hindi loves: trouble isn't just felt, it's a place you physically fall into or get shoved into, as in किसी को गड्ढे में धकेल देना (kisii ko gaddhe men dhakel denaa, 'to push someone into a pit,' meaning to ruin their prospects).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often translate 'to get someone into trouble' word-for-word, missing that Hindi speakers naturally reach for the physical image of pushing someone into a गड्ढा (gaddhaa) rather than an abstract noun.
