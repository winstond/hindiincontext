व्यापार (vyaapaar)

noun Primarily 'business/trade' as an economic activity, with a common extended sense of 'dealings' or 'occupation' more broadly.
Sense 1 : business, trade, commerce
noun, masculine
Synonyms: व्यवसाय (vyavsaay), कारोबार (kaarobaar), धंधा (dhandhaa, colloquial), तिजारत (tijaarat, literary)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In everyday Delhi-NCR speech, व्यापार (vyaapaar) is the respectable, almost formal word you'd use to describe a shopkeeper's or trader's livelihood -- you'll see it on shop association banners and in news about व्यापार मेला (vyaapaar melaa, 'trade fair'), a well-known annual Delhi event. It carries a slightly more dignified tone than the everyday कारोबार (kaarobaar) or casual धंधा (dhandhaa).
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use धंधा (dhandhaa) and व्यापार (vyaapaar) as if fully interchangeable in all contexts, but धंधा (dhandhaa) can sound casual or even slightly disparaging (as in 'this shady business') depending on tone, while व्यापार (vyaapaar) stays neutral and respectable. 2) The verb usually paired with this noun is करना (karnaa) -- व्यापार करना (vyaapaar karnaa, 'to do business') -- not a direct calque of 'run a business.'

मेरे पिताजी कपड़ों का व्यापार करते हैं। My father does the cloth business.
(mere pitaajii kapdon kaa vyaapaar karte hain।)

Sense 2 : dealings, affair, matter (extended/figurative use)
noun, masculine Used when describing someone's habitual conduct or 'dealings' in a broader, sometimes slightly critical sense, not limited to commerce.
Synonyms: मामला (maamlaa), मसला (maslaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: You'll sometimes hear an elder say something like यह क्या व्यापार है? (yah kyaa vyaapaar hai?, 'what is this business/game going on here?') when mildly scolding someone about odd behavior -- here व्यापार (vyaapaar) has drifted from 'trade' to something closer to 'affair' or 'racket,' much like English speakers stretch 'business' to mean 'goings-on.'
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners sometimes miss this figurative extension entirely and only associate the word with literal commerce, so they're confused when it shows up in sentences with no money or goods involved, e.g. describing someone's 'game' or pattern of behavior.
