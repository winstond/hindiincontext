गाँव (gaanv)

noun A single-sense everyday word: village. Also spelled गांव (gaanv) with the anusvara instead of the chandrabindu -- both are common in print.
Sense 1 : village
noun, masculine
Synonyms: देहात (dehaat, rural area, broader than a single village)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: गाँव (gaanv) carries a strong emotional charge in Hindi the way 'hometown' or 'the old country' might in English -- for many urban Indians whose parents or grandparents migrated to cities, गाँव (gaanv) is still 'home' in a deep sense, the place you go back to for weddings and festivals. You will often hear अपना गाँव (apnaa gaanv, 'one's own village') used with real affection, contrasting with शहर (shahar, 'city') as a simpler, more rooted way of life.
Common Mistakes: 1) गाँव (gaanv) is masculine, but learners sometimes mismark adjectives/verbs agreeing with it as feminine, perhaps by association with English 'village.' 2) The postposition से (se) is used for 'from the village' (गाँव से, gaanv se, 'from the village') -- learners sometimes reach for का (kaa) out of habit when describing origin, e.g. wanting to say 'a person of the village' when 'from the village' is meant.
