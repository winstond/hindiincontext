मसालेदार (masaaledaar)

adjective A single-sense adjective meaning 'spicy/spiced' (full of masala masaalaa), used for food and, by extension, for lively stories or gossip.
Sense 1 : spicy, richly spiced (food)
adjective
Synonyms: तीखा (tiikhaa, hot/pungent, not the same as spiced), चटपटा (chataptaa, tangy and zesty)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Any Delhi cook will tell you मसालेदार (masaaledaar) is high praise for a curry -- it means the masala masaalaa (the onion-tomato-spice base) has been built up properly, with layers of flavor, not just heat. It's the opposite of a bland, watery preparation.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use मसालेदार (masaaledaar) when they mean 'hot/pungent,' but it really means 'full of spices/masala' -- a dish can be मसालेदार (masaaledaar) without being तीखा (tiikhaa, chili-hot). 2) It describes the presence and richness of spicing, not necessarily the heat level, so a mild but richly spiced curry is still मसालेदार (masaaledaar).

Sense 2 : juicy, sensational, spicy (of gossip, news, or a story)
adjective
Synonyms: रसीला (rasiilaa, juicy, more about relish/flavor than scandal), चटपटा (chataptaa, zesty, lighter register)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Just like in English 'spicy gossip,' Hindi extends the kitchen metaphor to talk: a मसालेदार (masaaledaar) story is one with enough scandal or drama to make it worth repeating over chai. You'll hear it a lot describing TV news or neighborhood gossip.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is very common in speech about news and gossip -- मसालेदार खबर (masaaledaar khabar, 'spicy/sensational news') -- and learners who only know the food sense may miss it in conversation.
