थामना (thaamnaa)

verb A transitive verb meaning to hold, grasp, or steady something, physically or emotionally; also extends to figurative senses like 'holding onto' composure or a position.
Sense 1 : to hold, grasp, catch hold of
verb, transitive
Synonyms: पकड़ना (pakadnaa), संभालना (sambhaalnaa, often overlaps in the steadying sense)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: Picture someone grabbing a friend's arm as they stumble on the stairs -- that steadying grip is exactly the flavor of थामना (thaamnaa), distinct from a plain, neutral grab. It shows up often in the phrase हाथ थामना (haath thaamnaa, 'to hold someone's hand'), which carries a slightly tender or protective undertone, as in a parent guiding a child or a partner offering support.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for पकड़ना (pakadnaa) by default; थामना (thaamnaa) is the better choice when the holding is meant to steady or support something (a falling person, a shaking hand, a railing) rather than just grip it. 2) थामना (thaamnaa) takes a direct object with ने in the past (usne haath thaamaa, 'he/she held the hand') -- it behaves as a regular transitive verb, no surprises there, but learners sometimes intransitivize it by analogy with संभलना (sambhalnaa).

Sense 2 : to hold back, restrain, or steady (emotions, tears, a situation)
verb, transitive
Synonyms: रोकना (roknaa), काबू में रखना (kaaboo men rakhnaa)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This is the same physical image of gripping something tightly, just turned inward -- steadying your own emotions as if they were a shaking hand. You'll hear जिगर थामना (jigar thaamnaa, 'to steady one's heart/nerve') in more dramatic or filmy speech when someone braces for bad news.
Common Mistakes: 1) This figurative sense is easy to miss -- आँसू थामना (aansoo thaamnaa, 'to hold back tears') or खुद को थामना (khud ko thaamnaa, 'to steady/control oneself') don't translate literally as 'holding' in English, so learners under-use this verb here and default to रोकना (roknaa) instead.
