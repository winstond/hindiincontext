आटा (aataa)

noun A single, concrete everyday-kitchen sense: wheat/grain flour, as distinct from मैदा (maidaa), the finer refined flour used for breads like नान (naan) or पूरी (poorii).
Sense 1 : flour (usually whole wheat)
noun, masculine
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: In most North Indian homes, आटा (aataa) is bought in large sacks and kneaded fresh every day or two for रोटी (rotii) -- the phrase आटा गूँधना (aataa goondhnaa, 'to knead the dough') is a daily kitchen ritual, not an occasional task. There's also a well-known idiom, आटे-दाल का भाव मालूम होना (aate-daal kaa bhaav maaloom honaa, 'to know the price of flour and lentils'), meaning to finally understand the hard realities of running a household -- a nod to how central आटा is to the idea of domestic economy.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often use आटा (aataa) for any flour, but Hindi distinguishes it from मैदा (maidaa, refined white flour) and बेसन (besan, chickpea flour) -- reach for आटा specifically for the whole-wheat flour used to make रोटी (rotii) or चपाती (chapaatii). 2) In shops and recipes it is almost always used as a mass noun without a plural, so phrases like दो आटे (do aate) sound odd; instead say दो किलो आटा (do kilo aataa, 'two kilos of flour').
