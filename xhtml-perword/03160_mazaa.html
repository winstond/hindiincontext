मज़ा (mazaa)

noun Means 'fun, enjoyment, pleasure' -- the everyday word for a good time, whether from an activity, food, or an experience. Almost always seen in fixed phrases like मज़ा आना (mazaa aanaa) 'to feel enjoyment' rather than standing alone as a subject.
Sense 1 : fun, enjoyment, pleasure
noun, masculine Almost always used with आना (aanaa) in a dative-subject frame: X को मज़ा आता है (X ko mazaa aataa hai, 'X enjoys it') -- the enjoyment 'comes to' the person rather than the person 'having' fun.
Synonyms: आनंद (aanand, formal), मस्ती (mastii)
Frequency: high
Cultural Note: This is one of the clearest everyday examples of Hindi's dative-subject pattern: enjoyment is treated as something that arrives at you, like nींद (niind, sleep) or गुस्सा (gussaa, anger), rather than something you actively do. You'll hear मज़ा आ गया (mazaa aa gayaa, 'that was fun!') constantly after a good meal, movie, or outing in Delhi.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners try to make मज़ा (mazaa) the subject of a normal verb like करना (karnaa); native speakers instead say मज़ा आना (mazaa aanaa) or मज़ा लेना (mazaa lenaa), not मज़ा करना (mazaa karnaa) for 'having fun.' 2) The person enjoying is marked with को (ko), not से (se) or का (kaa): मुझे मज़ा आया (mujhe mazaa aayaa, 'I enjoyed it'), not मैंने मज़ा आया।

Sense 2 : taste, savor (of food or experience)
noun, masculine Used for the enjoyable 'taste' or 'flavor' of an experience more broadly, not just literal food flavor -- e.g. बारिश का मज़ा (baarish kaa mazaa, 'the pleasure of the rain').
Synonyms: स्वाद (svaad)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: Where स्वाद (svaad) is the more literal, physical word for taste, मज़ा (mazaa) leans into the pleasurable, experiential side -- so लस्सी का मज़ा (lassii kaa mazaa) evokes the whole enjoyable experience of drinking lassi on a hot day, not just its flavor on the tongue.

Sense 3 : used in idiom to mean a joke or teasing, mockery (in मज़ाक-related idiomatic use)
noun, masculine Appears in the fixed idiom मज़ा लेना (mazaa lenaa, 'to enjoy someone's discomfort, to relish teasing someone'), often with से (se) marking the target: किसी से मज़ा लेना (kisii se mazaa lenaa).
Synonyms: मज़ाक (mazaak, more common for this specific sense)
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This sense overlaps closely with मज़ाक (mazaak, 'joke'), and the two words are often used almost interchangeably in casual banter -- दोस्तों के बीच मज़ा लेना (doston ke biich mazaa lenaa, 'to have a laugh at a friend's expense among friends') is a normal, affectionate part of Delhi friend-group dynamics, not necessarily unkind.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners confuse this idiomatic मज़ा लेना (mazaa lenaa, 'to relish teasing/mocking someone') with the plain enjoyment sense of मज़ा लेना (mazaa lenaa, 'to enjoy oneself'); context and the presence of a से-marked person distinguish them.
