आदतन (aadtan)

adverb An adverb meaning 'habitually, out of habit' -- built from आदत (aadat, 'habit') with the Persian-style adverbial suffix -अन. Single-sense word.
Sense 1 : habitually, out of habit, as a matter of routine
adverb
Synonyms: अक्सर (aksar, often, broader than just habit), हमेशा (hameshaa, always, broader), स्वभाववश (svabhaavvash, literary, 'due to one's nature')
Frequency: medium
Cultural Note: This word carries a slightly critical or resigned undertone in everyday speech -- Delhi parents and teachers love using it to describe someone's annoying but deeply ingrained tendencies, like वह आदतन देर से आता है (vah aadtan der se aataa hai, 'he habitually comes late'). It is related to आदत (aadat, 'habit') and आदी (aadii, 'accustomed/addicted to'), so once you know one, the others click into place.
Common Mistakes: 1) Learners often reach for अक्सर (aksar) when they mean 'habitually,' but अक्सर (aksar) just means 'often' without the flavor of ingrained habit -- आदतन (aadtan) specifically frames the action as something done almost automatically, sometimes even involuntarily or unconsciously. 2) It usually sits right before the verb it modifies, e.g. वह आदतन झूठ बोलता है (vah aadtan jhooth boltaa hai, 'he habitually lies'), not at the start of the sentence.
Sample Sentences:
वह आदतन देर से आता है। He habitually comes late.
(vah aadtan der se aataa hai।)
